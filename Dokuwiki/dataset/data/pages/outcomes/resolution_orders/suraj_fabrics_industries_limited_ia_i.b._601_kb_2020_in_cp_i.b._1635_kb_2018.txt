## Main Themes
- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**  
  Execution of CIRP for Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

- **Resolution Plan Approval**  
  Successful culmination of the CIRP with the approval of the resolution plan submitted by M/s. Sponge Sales (India) Private Limited.

- **Preferential Transactions**  
  Allegations of preferential transactions during the look-back period leading to an application by the Resolution Professional (RP).

## Key Facts and Ideas
- **Admission of Company Petition**  
  The company petition filed under Section 10 of the IBC was admitted on July 1, 2019, marking the commencement of CIRP.

- **Appointment of RP**  
  Mr. Santosh Choraria was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) and subsequently confirmed as the RP.

- **Financial Distress**  
  The company faced continuous losses since FY 2012-2013, leading to its accounts being declared as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) by lenders like SBI and IOB.  
  > "However, due to continuous losses year after year from the financial year 2012-2013, the Company was unable to repay the debts due to the secured creditors i.e. SBI and IOB and eventually the bank accounts were declared as Non Performing Assets (NPA) by the respective bankers i.e. bank account with SBI on 29th September, 2013 and bank account with IOB on 8th July, 2015."  
  (Source: Amended order admitting company petition and initiating CIRP)

- **Resolution Plan**  
  The CoC approved the resolution plan of M/s. Sponge Sales (India) Private Limited with 100% voting, offering a total payout of ₹10.53 crores.  
  > "Accordingly, I hereby approve the resolution plan of M/s. Sponge Sales (India) Private Limited."  
  (Source: Order approving resolution plan)

- **Preferential Transactions Alleged**  
  The RP filed an application under Section 43 of the IBC, alleging preferential transactions totaling ₹11,83,12,330.  
  > "That the aforesaid payments have put the unsecured creditors in a beneficial position over the Secured Financial Creditors, who were not paid or even if paid, at marginal percentage."  
  (Source: Order dismissing preferential transaction application)

- **Timeline Non-Adherence**  
  The NCLT found that the RP failed to adhere to the prescribed timelines for filing the preferential transaction application, submitted on the 389th day of the CIRP period, exceeding the stipulated 135 days.  
  > "That RP has filed the application on the 389th day of the CIRP period."  
  (Source: Order dismissing preferential transaction application)

- **Dismissal of Preferential Transaction Application**  
  The NCLT dismissed the application due to non-adherence to timelines and failure to form an independent opinion beyond the Forensic Auditor's report. The RP was deemed functus officio after the resolution plan was approved.  
  > "Given the circumstances of the case, we are satisfied that the prayer made in the IA should not be allowed. Therefore, the I.A. (IB) No. 750/KB/2020 shall stand dismissed."  
  (Source: Order dismissing preferential transaction application)

## Overall Summary
This case highlights the successful resolution of a financially distressed company through CIRP while underscoring the importance of strict adherence to prescribed timelines by the RP, particularly concerning preferential transactions. It also raises questions about the RP's actions and their potential impact on maximizing value for creditors.

## Glossary of Key Terms

- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)**: The primary legislation in India governing insolvency resolution and bankruptcy proceedings for individuals and corporate entities.
- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**: A time-bound process (typically 180 days, extendable to 270 days) under the IBC to revive a financially distressed company or, if revival is not feasible, to proceed with liquidation.
- **Resolution Professional (RP)**: A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CIRP of the corporate debtor. They take control of the company's affairs, oversee the resolution process, and report to the CoC.
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC)**: A collective body of financial creditors who have a claim against the corporate debtor. They hold the decision-making power during the CIRP and vote on the approval of resolution plans.
- **Resolution Plan**: A proposal submitted by a prospective resolution applicant outlining the plan to revive the corporate debtor. It includes financial restructuring, operational changes, and payment plans for creditors.
- **Resolution Applicant**: An entity that submits a resolution plan to the RP for consideration by the CoC. They can be existing promoters, investors, or other companies interested in acquiring the corporate debtor's assets.
- **Liquidation Value**: The estimated value of the corporate debtor's assets if they were to be sold in a liquidation scenario. It serves as a benchmark for evaluating the viability of resolution plans.
- **Preferential Transaction**: A transaction made by a debtor before insolvency that gives an unfair advantage to a particular creditor over other creditors. The IBC allows the RP to challenge and reverse such transactions.
- **Moratorium**: A legal protection offered to the corporate debtor during the CIRP. It temporarily halts legal actions and debt recovery efforts by creditors, allowing for a fair and undisturbed resolution process.
- **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)**: A quasi-judicial body in India that adjudicates matters related to company law, including insolvency and bankruptcy cases.

# Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited - Insolvency FAQs

### 1. What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited?
Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited faced continuous losses from the financial year 2012-2013 onwards. Their inability to repay debts owed to secured creditors, namely the State Bank of India (SBI) and the Indian Overseas Bank (IOB), resulted in their accounts being declared as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs). Consequently, a corporate insolvency resolution process was initiated.

### 2. Who was appointed as the Resolution Professional (RP) for Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited, and what were their responsibilities?
Mr. Santosh Choraria was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) on July 1, 2019, and was later confirmed as the Resolution Professional (RP) by the Committee of Creditors (CoC). The RP's responsibilities included managing the CIRP, collecting and verifying claims, forming an opinion on preferential transactions, and ultimately presenting a resolution plan to the CoC for approval.

### 3. What was the outcome of the CIRP for Suraj Fabrics Industries Limited?
The CoC approved the resolution plan submitted by M/s. Sponge Sales (India) Private Limited with a 100% voting share. The plan included the repayment of the CIRP costs, employee dues, operational creditor dues, and a portion of the secured financial creditor dues.

### 4. What were the key features of the approved resolution plan?
The resolution plan proposed a financial arrangement to address the outstanding dues of various stakeholders. It prioritized the payment of the entire CIRP cost, followed by employee dues and operational creditor dues, and offered a structured repayment schedule for the secured financial creditors.

### 5. Did the resolution plan address the concerns of all stakeholders?
Yes, the resolution plan was designed to balance the interests of all stakeholders. It provided for the payment of insolvency resolution process costs, prioritized payments to operational creditors over financial creditors, and ensured the management of the affairs of the Corporate Debtor in a transparent and accountable manner.

### 6. What were the grounds for the application filed under sections 43 and 44 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016?
The application sought to recover approximately ₹11.83 crore from various related party entities. This was based on findings from a forensic audit report indicating that certain transactions during the look-back period were preferential in nature, potentially putting unsecured creditors in an advantageous position over secured financial creditors.

### 7. Why was the application seeking to avoid preferential transactions dismissed?
The Adjudicating Authority dismissed the application primarily because it was filed after the approval of the resolution plan. Furthermore, the Resolution Professional failed to adhere to the timeline stipulated in the CIRP Regulations for filing such applications, and the Authority noted that the RP had not provided sufficient independent justification for avoiding the said transactions, relying heavily on the Forensic Auditor’s report.

### 8. What was the significance of the moratorium imposed during the CIRP?
A moratorium was imposed upon admission of the company petition, providing a period of protection from legal actions against the company. This moratorium prohibited actions such as the initiation or continuation of legal proceedings, enforcement of security interests, and the recovery of property possessed by the corporate debtor, allowing for a stable environment for the resolution process to proceed.
