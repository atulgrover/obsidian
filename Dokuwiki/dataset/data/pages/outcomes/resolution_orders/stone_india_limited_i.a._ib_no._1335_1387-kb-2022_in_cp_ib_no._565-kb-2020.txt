# A Study Guide to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (India)

## Short Answer Questions

1. **What is the role of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in insolvency proceedings in India?**
   - The NCLT is the adjudicating authority responsible for initiating and overseeing insolvency proceedings for companies in India. It examines applications, appoints Resolution Professionals, approves resolution plans, and can order liquidation if necessary.

2. **Distinguish between a 'financial creditor' and an 'operational creditor' under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.**
   - A financial creditor's claim arises from a financial debt, like a loan or debt security, while an operational creditor's claim arises from the provision of goods or services in the ordinary course of business.

3. **What is the significance of a 'demand notice' in initiating insolvency proceedings against a corporate debtor?**
   - A demand notice is a formal document issued by a creditor to a corporate debtor, demanding payment of an undisputed debt. It is a prerequisite for initiating insolvency proceedings under the Code.

4. **Explain the concept of 'moratorium' and its implications during the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).**
   - Moratorium refers to a legal standstill imposed by the NCLT upon admission of an insolvency petition. It prohibits legal actions against the debtor, aims to provide a stable environment for the resolution process, and allows the RP to take control of the debtor's assets.

5. **What is a 'resolution plan' and what are the key elements it must contain?**
   - A resolution plan outlines the proposed measures for reviving the corporate debtor. It includes details about the debt restructuring, payment schedule for creditors, proposed business plan, and the management structure after resolution.

6. **Who is the 'Resolution Professional (RP)' and what are their primary responsibilities during CIRP?**
   - The RP is a licensed insolvency professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CIRP. Their duties include taking control of the debtor's assets, verifying claims, facilitating the CoC's decision-making, and proposing the resolution plan.

7. **What is the role of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in the insolvency resolution process?**
   - The CoC, comprising financial creditors, plays a crucial role in deciding the fate of the corporate debtor. They have the power to approve or reject resolution plans, decide on the distribution of assets, and can even opt for liquidation.

8. **Describe the process of approving a resolution plan and the NCLT's role in it.**
   - The RP presents the resolution plan to the CoC for voting. If approved by the requisite majority, it is submitted to the NCLT for final approval. The NCLT ensures the plan complies with legal provisions and is in the best interests of all stakeholders.

9. **What happens if a resolution plan is not approved within the stipulated timeframe?**
   - If a resolution plan isn't approved within the designated timeframe (usually 270 days, extendable to 365 days), the NCLT orders the liquidation of the corporate debtor.

10. **Can an unsuccessful resolution applicant challenge the approval of another resolution plan? Explain.**
    - Yes, an unsuccessful resolution applicant can challenge the approval of another plan under Section 60(5) of the Code. They must demonstrate material irregularities or grounds specified in Section 30(2) to the NCLT. Success depends on proving that the approved plan violates the Code or unfairly disadvantages them.

| **Committee of Creditors (CoC)** | A group consisting of financial creditors of the corporate debtor who have the power to approve or reject resolution plans. |

# Stone India Limited: Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process FAQ

1. **What is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**
   - The IBC is an Indian law that provides a time-bound process for resolving insolvency in corporations, partnerships, and individuals. It aims to maximize the value of assets and promote entrepreneurship, credit availability, and economic growth.

2. **What is Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**
   - CIRP is a process under the IBC that attempts to revive financially distressed companies. It involves a moratorium on debt recovery, appointment of a resolution professional (RP) to manage the company, and formation of a Committee of Creditors (CoC) to evaluate and approve a resolution plan.

3. **Who filed for CIRP against Stone India Limited and why?**
   - ICICI Bank Limited, a financial creditor, filed for CIRP against Stone India Limited due to non-repayment of outstanding debts exceeding ₹15 crores, originating from a sanctioned working capital facility.

4. **What is the role of the Resolution Professional (RP) in the CIRP?**
   - The RP takes control of the company, collects and verifies claims, conducts due diligence, and invites resolution plans from potential investors. They also manage the company during the CIRP and report to the CoC and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).

5. **What is the composition and function of the Committee of Creditors (CoC)?**
   - The CoC comprises financial creditors of the corporate debtor. It decides the future of the company by voting on the proposed resolution plans. The CoC's role is to ensure the best possible outcome for all creditors involved.

6. **What happens if the CoC approves a resolution plan?**
   - The approved resolution plan is submitted to the NCLT for final approval. If approved by the NCLT, the plan is legally binding on all stakeholders, and the CIRP ends.

7. **What are the key challenges faced in Stone India Limited's CIRP?**
   - Several challenges arose during the process, including disputes over land lease rights, claims from various creditors, and the need to secure approvals and waivers from multiple government authorities.

8. **What was the outcome of Stone India Limited's CIRP?**
   - The CoC approved a resolution plan submitted by Jupiter Wagons Limited. The NCLT further approved this plan, paving the way for the revival of Stone India Limited under new ownership and management.
