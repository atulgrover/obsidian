### Summary
These sources detail a legal dispute between **D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited** and its creditors, including homebuyers, financial institutions, and related companies. The dispute arose after the company was admitted into insolvency proceedings, and the **Resolution Professional**, tasked with managing the insolvency process, rejected several claims submitted by these creditors. The sources present the arguments of the applicant, D.S. Kulkarni and its related companies, as they attempt to have their claims admitted by the Resolution Professional and the **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)**. Ultimately, the Tribunal rejects these claims, finding that they were not submitted within the proper timeframe, were not adequately supported by documentation, and would further complicate the resolution process.

### Briefing Document: D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited Insolvency Case

#### Subject: Review of Key Themes and Facts in D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited Insolvency Case

### Sources:
- NCLT Mumbai Bench Orders and Applications related to D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited, available on the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) website.

### Main Themes:
- **Related Party Transactions and Siphoning of Funds**: Multiple sources allege that D.S. Kulkarni and related entities engaged in fraudulent practices, siphoning funds from the Corporate Debtor (DSKDL) to group companies, partnership firms, and individuals. This includes allegations of layering funds, using shell companies to collect deposits, and diverting funds for personal gain.

- **Claim Rejection and Creditor Disputes**: Numerous applications highlight disputes over claim rejection by the Resolution Professional (RP), particularly from individuals who invested in related entities and those holding only allotment letters.

- **Homebuyer Concerns**: Applications from homebuyers demonstrate anxiety regarding the receipt of completed flats and obtaining clear title, especially in cases where land ownership rests with third parties.

- **Resolution Plan Approval and Implementation**: While a Resolution Plan has been approved, applications raise concerns about its effectiveness in addressing various creditor claims, particularly those rejected by the RP.

### Key Facts and Findings:
- **CIRP Commencement**: The Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against DSKDL commenced on September 26, 2019, with Bank of Maharashtra filing the petition.

- **Claim Categories**: Claims were filed by various stakeholders, including secured financial creditors, unsecured financial creditors (including homebuyers and fixed deposit holders), and operational creditors.

- **Resolution Plan Approval**: The NCLT Mumbai Bench approved the resolution plan submitted by the consortium of Ashdan Properties Pvt. Ltd., Classic Promoters & Builders Pvt. Ltd., and Atul Builders on June 23, 2023.

- **Payment to Stakeholders**: The approved Resolution Plan provides for payment to stakeholders in eight tranches, with varying percentages of admitted claims being fulfilled for different categories.

### Important Quotes:
- **Applicant (Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha) Argument for Claim Inclusion**: "The funds which were collected from depositors/members of the Applicant in the form of Fixed Deposits by DSK Group companies, partnership firms/subsidiaries, were used to create assets of Corporate Debtor through layering in the accounts of partnership firms, Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni and Corporate Debtor.” (IA 895/2022)

- **Respondent (RP) Justification for Claim Rejection**: "As the RP of the Corporate Debtor, the Respondent submits that he is not empowered to accept any claims other than for monies advanced to the Corporate Debtor. The present Applicant seeks to be treated as a creditor of the Corporate Debtor on the basis that some of its members are.” (IA 895/2022)

- **Applicant (Santosh Digambar Honkarpe) Concerns Regarding Flat Possession**: "The Applicant submits that the RP must ensure that the flats are constructed and vacant and peaceful possession is handed over to them.” (IA 1545/2021)

- **NCLT Observation on RP's Claim Adjudication Role**: "All claims must be submitted to the resolution professional and decided by the resolution professional so that a prospective resolution applicant knows exactly what has to be paid.” (IA 722/2023 citing NCLAT judgment in Mukul Kumar Vs. M/s RPS Infrastructure Ltd.)

### Key Takeaways and Observations:
- This case underscores the complexities of resolving insolvency cases involving related party transactions and allegations of fraud. The challenges in tracing funds, establishing valid claims, and ensuring equitable treatment for various creditor groups are evident.

- Homebuyer anxieties surrounding flat completion and ownership highlight the need for clear provisions in resolution plans to address the specific concerns of this stakeholder group.

- The role of the RP in claim verification and adjudication is critical, demanding a thorough and transparent process to address potential disputes and ensure fairness.

- While the NCLT's approval of the Resolution Plan marks a significant milestone, close monitoring of its implementation will be crucial to assess its effectiveness in achieving the desired outcomes for all stakeholders.

### D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited: A Timeline of Insolvency

#### 2017:
- Criminal cases are filed against key personnel of the DSK Group, including Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni. Mrs. Kulkarni is imprisoned, and the properties of the DSK Group are attached.

#### September 26, 2019:
- The Bank of Maharashtra files a petition, and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench, admits D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited (DSKDL) into the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).
- Manoj Kumar Agarwal is appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

#### October 3, 2019:
- The Resolution Professional (RP) issues a notice rejecting claims related to group companies of DSKDL, stating that only claims directly related to DSKDL would be accepted.

#### November 18, 2020:
- The Resolution Professional files applications related to potentially preferential, undervalued, and fraudulent transactions under sections 43, 45, and 66 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). These matters are pending for hearing.

#### August 13, 2021:
- The Committee of Creditors (CoC) approves a resolution plan submitted by Ashdan Properties Pvt. Ltd., Classic Promoters & Builders Pvt. Ltd., and Atul Builders-Consortium.

#### August 21, 2021:
- The Resolution Professional receives a Performance Bank Guarantee of Rs. 25 crores.

#### March 3, 2023:
- The NCLT hears IA No. 1881/MB/C-I/2021, filed by Sunny Jagdish Shah, a flat purchaser seeking recognition of his claim based on an allotment letter. The RP opposes the claim, stating that allotment letter holders are not considered Financial Creditors unless they have a registered agreement. The NCLT dismisses the application.

#### March 31, 2023:
- The NCLT hears two applications:
  - IA No. 722/MB/C-I/2023, filed by D.S. Kulkarni and Company through Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni.
  - IA No. 721/MB/C-I/2023, filed by D.S. Kulkarni and Associates through Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni.
- Both applications seek recognition of their claims as Financial Creditors and inclusion in the Resolution Plan. The RP opposes both applications, citing late submission and lack of proper documentation. The NCLT rejects both applications.

#### June 23, 2023:
- The NCLT hears two applications:
  - IA No. 1950 of 2021, filed by the Resolution Professional for approval of the Resolution Plan.
  - IA No. 1545 of 2021, filed by Santosh Digambar Honkarpe, a homebuyer, seeking the inclusion of provisions in the Resolution Plan to secure the handover of completed flats, particularly in cases where land ownership lies with a third party (DSK Global).
- The NCLT approves the Resolution Plan as submitted by the RP.
- The NCLT dismisses IA No. 1545 of 2021, stating that it cannot impose obligations on DSK Global, a separate legal entity not undergoing insolvency proceedings.

### Cast of Characters
- **D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited (DSKDL)**: The corporate debtor undergoing the CIRP. A real estate development company facing financial distress.

- **Bank of Maharashtra**: The financial creditor that filed the petition to initiate the CIRP against DSKDL.

- **Manoj Kumar Agarwal**: The Resolution Professional appointed to oversee the CIRP of DSKDL. He manages the process, verifies claims, invites resolution plans, and ensures compliance with the IBC.

- **Ashdan Properties Pvt. Ltd., Classic Promoters & Builders Pvt. Ltd., and Atul Builders-Consortium**: The consortium that submitted the successful Resolution Plan for DSKDL, approved by the CoC and the NCLT.

- **Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha**: An association representing individuals who had invested in fixed deposit schemes of DSKDL and its group companies. Their claims were rejected as they were not directly related to the corporate debtor.

- **D.S. Kulkarni**: Former director of DSKDL and a key figure in the DSK Group. Facing criminal charges related to the financial irregularities.

- **Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni**: Authorized representative of D.S. Kulkarni and Company and D.S. Kulkarni and Associates. She filed applications seeking recognition of the firms' claims as Financial Creditors, which were ultimately rejected by the NCLT.

- **DSK Global Education and Research Ltd.**: A separate legal entity owning land on which DSKDL had development rights. Some homebuyers had entered into tripartite agreements involving DSKDL and DSK Global.

- **Santosh Digambar Honkarpe**: A homebuyer who had entered into a tripartite agreement with DSKDL and DSK Global. He filed an application seeking provisions in the Resolution Plan to secure the handover of completed flats, but his application was dismissed.

- **Sunny Jagdish Shah**: A flat purchaser who filed an application seeking recognition of his claim based on an allotment letter. His application was dismissed as allotment letter holders are not considered Financial Creditors without a registered agreement.

**Short-Answer Questions**

1. **What were the primary allegations made by Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha against D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited (DSKDL)?**  
   Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha alleged that DSKDL, along with its group companies, siphoned funds from depositors, particularly its members, under the guise of fixed deposit schemes. They claimed these funds were misappropriated to create assets for DSKDL through layered accounts involving partnership firms and other entities associated with DSKDL's promoters.

2. **How did the Resolution Professional (RP) respond to Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha's claims?**  
   The RP rejected Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha's claims, stating that the Sanstha lacked the legal standing to represent individual creditors. The RP argued that the claims did not arise from a direct contractual relationship with DSKDL and pertained to transactions with separate legal entities, not the Corporate Debtor itself.

3. **What was the primary objective of IA 895/2022 filed by Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha?**  
   The primary objective of IA 895/2022 was to compel the RP to admit the claims of Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha's members, investigate the flow of funds within the DSK Group, and initiate a consolidated CIRP for the entire group of companies.

4. **How did the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) rule on IA 895/2022?**  
   The NCLT dismissed IA 895/2022, upholding the RP's decision to reject the claims. It stated that the RP was not obligated to admit claims not reflected in DSKDL's books and that transactions with separate legal entities could not be attributed to the Corporate Debtor.

5. **Describe the Resolution Plan approved for DSKDL and highlight its key features.**  
   The approved Resolution Plan involved the acquisition of DSKDL by a consortium comprising Ashdan Properties Pvt. Ltd., Classic Promoters & Builders Pvt. Ltd., and Atul Builders. Key features included a structured repayment schedule to various creditors, including financial institutions, homebuyers, and operational creditors, alongside a management plan for DSKDL under new ownership.

6. **How did the approved Resolution Plan address the interests of various stakeholders, including financial creditors and operational creditors?**  
   The Resolution Plan addressed stakeholder interests by offering phased payments to financial creditors, provisions for homebuyers to receive their homes, and allocating funds for operational creditors, although at a lower percentage compared to financial creditors.

7. **What were the key arguments presented by Santosh Digambar Honkarpe in IA 1545/2021?**  
   Santosh Digambar Honkarpe argued that the Resolution Plan failed to protect the rights of homebuyers, particularly those with tripartite agreements involving DSKDL and DSK Global. He sought a clear mechanism for the handover of flats and assurances regarding peaceful possession.

8. **What was the NCLT's decision on IA 1545/2021 and the reasoning behind it?**  
   The NCLT dismissed IA 1545/2021, stating it lacked the jurisdiction to compel DSK Global, a separate entity not under CIRP, to comply with the Resolution Plan. The NCLT noted that the tripartite agreement and the binding clause in the Resolution Plan sufficiently addressed the homebuyers' concerns.

9. **What was the nature of the claim filed by D.S. Kulkarni and Company in IA 722/2023?**  
   D.S. Kulkarni and Company filed IA 722/2023 seeking recognition as a financial creditor of DSKDL, based on a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) and ledger statements reflecting transactions between them. They claimed a substantial amount was owed to them by DSKDL and sought classification at par with homebuyers in the Resolution Plan.

10. **What was the NCLT's rationale for dismissing the applications filed by D.S. Kulkarni and Company (IA 722/2023) and D.S. Kulkarni and Associates (IA 721/2023)?**  
    The NCLT dismissed both applications based on the grounds that claims arising from transactions before the CIRP commencement date could not be admitted after the approval of the Resolution Plan. The NCLT emphasized that allowing such late claims would disrupt the resolution process, especially since the Resolution Plan was already pending approval.

**Glossary of Key Terms**

- **Corporate Debtor:** The company undergoing the CIRP due to insolvency. In this case, it's DSKDL.
- **CIRP:** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process, the legal framework for resolving corporate insolvencies in India.
- **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to oversee the CIRP, manage the affairs of the Corporate Debtor, and facilitate the resolution process.
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee comprising financial creditors of the Corporate Debtor, with significant decision-making power in the CIRP, including voting on the approval of a Resolution Plan.
- **Resolution Plan:** A plan proposed by a resolution applicant outlining the restructuring or sale of the Corporate Debtor's assets and operations to revive the company or maximize value for stakeholders.
- **Financial Creditor:** An entity that has provided financial assistance to the Corporate Debtor, typically through loans or debt instruments.
- **Operational Creditor:** An entity that has provided goods or services to the Corporate Debtor and is owed payment for such supplies.
- **Homebuyer:** An individual who has entered into an agreement to purchase a residential unit from the Corporate Debtor and made part or full payment.
- **Corporate Veil:** A legal concept that separates the personality of a company from its shareholders and directors, shielding them from personal liability for the company's debts and obligations.
- **Related Party:** An individual or entity having a close relationship with the Corporate Debtor, such as promoters, directors, or subsidiaries.
- **NCLT:** National Company Law Tribunal, a judicial body responsible for adjudicating matters related to company law and insolvency in India.
- **MoU (Memorandum of Understanding):** A non-binding agreement outlining the broad terms of an understanding between two or more parties.
- **Ledger Statements:** Accounting records reflecting financial transactions between two parties, typically used to substantiate claims of debt.
- **Tripartite Agreement:** An agreement involving three parties, in this case, the homebuyer, DSKDL, and DSK Global.

### Background of the D.S. Kulkarni Developers Limited (DSKDL) Case
DSKDL was a real estate development company that entered into Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) in 2019 due to financial distress. The company had collected funds from individuals through fixed deposit schemes and also through various group companies, subsidiaries, and partnership firms. The company's director, Mr. D.S. Kulkarni, and his family were alleged to have siphoned off funds from these entities to DSKDL.

### Why was the claim by Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha rejected?
Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha, an association representing individuals who had invested in DSKDL and its related entities, filed a claim seeking to be recognized as creditors. However, the Resolution Professional (RP) rejected their claim because they did not have a direct contractual relationship with DSKDL. The individuals had invested in fixed deposit schemes of DSKDL and were subsequently persuaded to invest in other DSK Group companies, which were later found to be shell companies used to siphon off funds.

### Key Arguments Made by Chaitanya Sevabhavi Sanstha in Support of Their Claim
- The association argued that the funds collected from them were used to create assets of DSKDL through layering in the accounts of partnership firms, group companies, and Mrs. Hemanti Kulkarni (wife of D.S. Kulkarni).
- They highlighted the findings of the Directorate of Enforcement under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, which indicated that funds were siphoned off from depositors.

### Defense of the Resolution Professional on the Rejection of the Claim
The RP argued that they were only empowered to accept claims for monies advanced directly to DSKDL. They maintained that the individuals knowingly invested in related entities and assumed the associated risks. The RP also emphasized that they had initiated separate legal proceedings against the erstwhile directors and promoters of DSKDL for fraudulent activities.

### Outcome of the Resolution Plan for DSKDL
The Resolution Plan for DSKDL was approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). The plan involved payments to various stakeholders, including financial creditors, operational creditors, and homebuyers. While secured financial creditors received a significant portion of their dues, unsecured creditors like homebuyers and fixed deposit holders received a lower percentage.

### Concerns Raised by Homebuyers like Mr. Santosh Honkarpe
Homebuyers who had entered into tripartite agreements with DSKDL and DSK Global (the landowner) were concerned about the lack of clarity regarding the handover of their flats. They argued that the Resolution Plan should include provisions ensuring the completion of the project and the delivery of vacant possession of their flats.

### Claims of Related Entities like D.S. Kulkarni & Associates
No, claims from related entities such as D.S. Kulkarni & Associates, D.S. Kulkarni & Company, and others were rejected by the RP. The RP maintained that these entities were separate legal entities and their claims could not be entertained during the CIRP of DSKDL. The NCLT upheld the RP's decision, citing that admitting such claims would disrupt the resolution process.

### Significance of the NCLAT's Ruling in Mukul Kumar Vs. M/s RPS Infrastructure Ltd.
The NCLAT ruling in Mukul Kumar Vs. M/s RPS Infrastructure Ltd. clarified that claims must be submitted and decided upon during the CIRP process. Claims arising from transactions outside the scope of CIRP cannot be entertained once the resolution plan is approved. This ruling reinforced the RP's decision to reject claims from related entities and individuals who invested in those entities.
