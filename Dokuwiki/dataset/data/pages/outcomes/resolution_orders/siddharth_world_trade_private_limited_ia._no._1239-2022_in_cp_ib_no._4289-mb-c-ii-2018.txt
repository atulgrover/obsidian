This briefing document outlines the key developments and legal decisions during the **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)** of **M/s Siddharth World Trade Private Limited & Ors.**, initiated under the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)**. ### Timeline of Key Events:

- **06.11.2018**: **Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited** filed a petition under Section 7 of the IBC to initiate CIRP against M/s Siddharth World Trade Private Limited for a default of ₹92,01,03,572.
  
- **13.05.2021**: The **NCLT Mumbai Bench** admitted the petition, appointing **Mr. Sameer Kakar** as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) and imposing a moratorium, restricting legal actions against the Corporate Debtor.
  
- **27.09.2021**: Following Mr. Kakar's appointment as a Technical Member of the NCLT, **Mr. Shekhar Kumar Agrawal** was appointed as the new **Resolution Professional (RP)**.
  
- **23.06.2023**: The NCLT Mumbai Bench approved the resolution plan submitted by **M/s Sharad Milk and Milk Products**, marking the successful conclusion of the CIRP.

---

### Key Highlights:

- **Debt Acquisition**: The debt was originally owed to the **Bank of India** and was acquired by **Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited** via an assignment agreement on **26.03.2014**.
  
- **Expression of Interest (EoI) and Resolution Plans**: Two rounds of **EoIs** were issued. The initial round of resolution plans was deemed non-compliant, resulting in a second round of submissions.

- **Approved Resolution Plan**: The approved resolution plan by **M/s Sharad Milk and Milk Products** involved a capital infusion of ₹8 crores, payable within three months, and a **Performance Bank Guarantee** of ₹2 crores.

- **Distribution to Stakeholders**:
   - **CIRP costs** are prioritized for payment.
   - **Financial Creditors**: A significant portion of the resolution funds will go towards financial creditors.
   - **Operational Creditors**: They will receive **10%** of their admitted claim amount.

- **Liquidation Value**: The **liquidation value** of the Corporate Debtor was assessed to be higher than the resolution plan value. Despite this, the **Committee of Creditors (CoC)** opted for the resolution plan, emphasizing the benefit of maximizing the Corporate Debtor’s value as a going concern.

#### **Short Answer Questions**

1. **What is the main purpose of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)?**
   - The IBC aims to consolidate and amend laws related to the reorganization and insolvency resolution of corporate entities, partnership firms, and individuals in a time-bound manner to maximize the value of assets and promote entrepreneurship.

2. **How did Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited become a creditor to M/s Siddharth World Trade Private Limited & Ors.?**
   - Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited acquired the debt originally owed to Bank of India by M/s Siddharth World Trade Private Limited & Ors. through an Assignment Agreement dated 26.03.2014.

3. **What is the role of a Resolution Professional (RP) in a Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**
   - The RP manages the affairs of the corporate debtor during the CIRP, invites claims from creditors, facilitates the Committee of Creditors' (CoC) decision-making process, and ultimately submits the approved resolution plan to the NCLT.

4. **What happened in the initial rounds of inviting Expressions of Interest (EOI) and Resolution Plans for M/s Siddharth World Trade Private Limited & Ors.?**
   - The initial rounds failed to produce a viable resolution plan. The received plans either did not comply with the Request for Resolution Plan (RFRP) terms, were conditional, or contained discrepancies. This necessitated inviting fresh EOIs.

5. **Explain the significance of the "liquidation value" of a corporate debtor in relation to a proposed resolution plan.**
   - The liquidation value represents the estimated net amount that would be realized if the corporate debtor's assets were sold in a liquidation scenario. It serves as a benchmark to assess whether a resolution plan offers a better outcome for creditors compared to liquidation.

6. **Why is the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) limited in its ability to modify a resolution plan approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC)?**
   - The NCLT’s role is limited because the CoC, comprising the financial creditors, has the primary responsibility and commercial expertise to decide on the most suitable resolution plan. The NCLT’s power is primarily restricted to ensuring compliance with the IBC.

7. **What is the role of the Monitoring Committee formed after the approval of the resolution plan?**
   - The Monitoring Committee oversees the implementation of the resolution plan after its approval by the NCLT. It ensures that the terms of the plan are adhered to and the revival of the corporate debtor is progressing as intended.

8. **What specific actions did the NCLT take regarding the reliefs and concessions requested in Clause 9.7 of the resolution plan?**
   - The NCLT refused to grant exemption from stamp duty, tax liability, registration charges, and fees related to the plan’s implementation. However, it granted exemptions from liabilities for offenses committed before the CIRP commencement, as allowed under Section 32A of the Code.

9. **Explain the concept of a moratorium in the context of a CIRP.**
   - A moratorium is a legal freeze on legal actions against a corporate debtor during the CIRP. It provides temporary relief from creditor claims, allowing the debtor to focus on restructuring without disruption.

10. **What does the IBC specify regarding the payment of debts to operational creditors as part of a resolution plan?**
   - The resolution plan must provide for payment of operational creditors' debts in a manner specified by the IBBI, ensuring they receive an amount no less than what they would receive in liquidation or under the priority order of Section 53, whichever is higher.

#### **Glossary of Key Terms**

- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)**: Indian legislation that consolidates and amends laws related to insolvency resolution and bankruptcy proceedings.
  
- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**: A time-bound process under the IBC to revive a financially distressed company by restructuring its debts and operations.
  
- **Financial Creditor**: An entity that has provided financial credit to the corporate debtor, typically banks and financial institutions.
  
- **Operational Creditor**: An entity that has provided goods or services to the corporate debtor and is owed money for the same.
  
- **Corporate Debtor**: A company undergoing a CIRP due to its inability to repay debts.
  
- **Resolution Professional (RP)**: A licensed professional appointed to manage the CIRP, oversee the company’s affairs, and facilitate the resolution process.
  
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC)**: A committee formed by financial creditors to take decisions regarding the CIRP, including approving the resolution plan.
  
- **Resolution Plan**: A proposal outlining the restructuring of the corporate debtor’s debts, operations, and ownership to ensure its viability.
  
- **Liquidation Value**: The estimated net amount that would be realized if the corporate debtor’s assets were sold in a liquidation scenario.
  
- **Moratorium**: A legal freeze on legal actions against a corporate debtor during the CIRP, providing temporary relief from creditor claims.
  
- **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)**: A judicial body responsible for adjudicating matters related to company law, including CIRP proceedings.
  
- **Expression of Interest (EOI)**: A preliminary indication of interest from potential resolution applicants in reviving the corporate debtor.
  
- **Request for Resolution Plan (RFRP)**: A document issued to shortlisted resolution applicants outlining the requirements and guidelines for submitting a resolution plan.

The NCLT acted as the Adjudicating Authority responsible for overseeing the CIRP and ensuring compliance with the IBC. Its role included:

- Approving the initiation of the CIRP.
- Appointing the RP.
- Reviewing and approving the resolution plan submitted by the RP.
- Ensuring the resolution plan met the requirements of the IBC.

The NCLT approved the resolution plan submitted by M/s Sharad Milk and Milk Products.
