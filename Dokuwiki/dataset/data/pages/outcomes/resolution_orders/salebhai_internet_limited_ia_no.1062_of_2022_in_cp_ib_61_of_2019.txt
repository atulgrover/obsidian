# Briefing Document: BVC TradePort Pvt. Ltd. vs. Salebhai Internet Ltd.

**Case:** CP(IB) 61 of 2019 before the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Ahmedabad Bench  

## Parties:

- **Applicant/Operational Creditor:**  
  BVC TradePort Private Limited, a logistics services provider.

- **Respondent/Corporate Debtor:**  
  Salebhai Internet Limited, an e-commerce platform.

## Subject:  
Application for initiation of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under Section 9 of the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016.

## Summary of Facts:

- **Service Provision:**  
  BVC TradePort provided logistics services to Salebhai Internet for delivering products to customers.

- **Outstanding Dues:**  
  Salebhai Internet failed to pay outstanding dues despite repeated reminders and acknowledgments of debt.

- **Demand Notice:**  
  BVC TradePort sent a demand notice under Section 8 of the IBC claiming a total of **Rs. 7,00,297/-** (including principal, interest, and legal costs).

- **Dispute:**  
  Salebhai Internet disputed the claim in their reply, alleging losses due to BVC TradePort's service deficiencies and unethical business practices.

## NCLT Order of December 8, 2021:

- The NCLT admitted BVC TradePort's application and initiated CIRP against Salebhai Internet.

### Key Observations by the NCLT:

- **Acknowledgment of Debt:**  
  Salebhai Internet admitted to a debt of **Rs. 1,94,208/-** through email correspondence.

- **Delayed Response:**  
  Salebhai Internet's reply to the demand notice was delayed, exceeding the 10-day limit under Section 8 of the IBC.

- **Threshold Limit:**  
  The default amount met the threshold limit for initiating CIRP.

- **Appointment of IRP:**  
  The NCLT appointed **Mr. Krunal Ramanbhai Tanna** as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

- **Moratorium Declared:**  
  A moratorium was declared, prohibiting actions against Salebhai Internet's assets and ongoing legal proceedings.

## Subsequent Developments (IA/1062(AHM)2022):

- **Change of RP:**  
  Mr. **Vinod Tarachand Agrawal** replaced Mr. Tanna as the Resolution Professional (RP).

- **Extension of CIRP:**  
  The CIRP period was extended by 90 days.

- **Resolution Plan Approval:**  
  A resolution plan submitted by **Mr. Smeet Dineshkumar Chaudhari** was approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) with **100% voting**.

- **Payment to Stakeholders:**  
  The plan included a payment of **Rs. 22,78,000/-** to stakeholders, including the full admitted claim of the operational creditor (BVC TradePort).

## NCLT Order of June 20, 2023:

- The NCLT approved the resolution plan submitted by Mr. Chaudhari under Section 30(6) of the IBC.

### Key Findings:

- **Compliance with IBC:**  
  The NCLT noted that the resolution plan complied with the requirements of Section 30(2) and relevant IBBI Regulations.

- **Lifted Moratorium:**  
  The moratorium was lifted.

- **Direction to Resolution Applicant:**  
  The NCLT directed the resolution applicant to approach relevant authorities for seeking waivers and concessions for unpaid liabilities, referencing the legal precedent in **Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**.

## Key Takeaways:

- This case demonstrates the effectiveness of the IBC in facilitating the resolution of insolvency cases.

- The NCLT's orders highlight the importance of timely responses to demand notices and adherence to the stipulated timelines under the IBC.

- The successful resolution of this case through a plan approved by the CoC shows the potential for reviving distressed businesses and maximizing value for stakeholders.

- The NCLT's guidance on addressing pre-CIRP liabilities clarifies the legal position and provides a framework for resolution applicants to seek necessary relief from relevant authorities.

# Analysis of Salebhai Internet Limited Insolvency Proceedings

## Source 1: NCLT Ahmedabad Bench Order in CP(IB) 61 of 2019 (December 8, 2021)

### I. Case Background
- **Overview of the Case:**  
  BVC TradePort Pvt. Ltd. (Operational Creditor) filed an application under Section 9 of the IBC, 2016, seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Salebhai Internet Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) for a default of **Rs. 7,00,297/-**.
  
- **Description of Businesses:**  
  - **BVC TradePort Pvt. Ltd.:** A logistics service provider.
  - **Salebhai Internet Ltd.:** An e-commerce platform selling various goods across India.

### II. Operational Creditor's Submissions
- **Details of Service Agreement and Unpaid Invoices:**  
  The parties entered into an agreement on **August 31, 2016**, for BVC TradePort to provide logistics services for Salebhai. Salebhai failed to pay multiple invoices despite repeated reminders.
  
- **Documentation of Payment Demands and Responses:**  
  BVC TradePort sent notices and emails demanding payment. Salebhai acknowledged the debt in some instances, partially paid, and disputed certain amounts, claiming deductions for losses and damages.
  
- **Demand Notice and Salebhai's Reply:**  
  BVC TradePort issued a formal Demand Notice under Section 8 of the IBC on **July 25, 2018**. Salebhai's reply, received after the statutory **10-day period**, raised counterclaims for substantial losses allegedly caused by BVC TradePort.

### III. Corporate Debtor's Submissions
- **Arguments Regarding Outstanding Dues and Counterclaims:**  
  Salebhai claimed the actual outstanding was lower than alleged and disputed a significant portion of the debt. They attributed their losses to BVC TradePort's alleged mis-deliveries, delays, and unethical business practices.
  
- **Allegations Against BVC TradePort's Conduct:**  
  Salebhai accused BVC TradePort of spreading false information about their financial stability to their vendors and business partners, causing them significant damage.

### IV. Tribunal's Findings and Order
- **Analysis of Evidence and Determination of Admitted Debt:**  
  The Tribunal analyzed invoices, emails, and correspondence between the parties. It found Salebhai had admitted to a debt of **Rs. 1,94,208/-**, exceeding the threshold limit for initiating CIRP.
  
- **Moratorium Declaration and Appointment of Interim Resolution Professional (IRP):**  
  The Tribunal admitted the application and declared a moratorium, prohibiting actions against Salebhai's assets. **Mr. Krunal Ramanbhai Tanna** was appointed as the IRP.
  
- **Directives to the IRP and Further Proceedings:**  
  The IRP was instructed to publicly announce the CIRP, invite claims from creditors, and manage Salebhai's operations as a going concern. The Operational Creditor was directed to pay an advance for the CIRP process.

## Source 2: NCLT Ahmedabad Bench Order in IA/1062(AHM)2022 in CP(IB) 61 of 2019 (June 20, 2023)

### I. Application for Resolution Plan Approval
- **Introduction of the Application and Parties Involved:**  
  The Resolution Professional (RP), **Mr. Vinod Tarachand Agrawal**, filed an application under Section 30(6) of the IBC for approval of a resolution plan submitted by **Mr. Smeet Dineshkumar Chaudhari**.
  
- **Summary of the Resolution Applicant's Proposal:**  
  Mr. Chaudhari proposed a payment of **Rs. 22,78,000/-**, including **Rs. 7,00,000/-** for the operational creditor (BVC TradePort), CIRP costs, and a capital expenditure provision.

### II. CIRP Proceedings and Resolution Plan Selection
- **Overview of the CIRP Process and Appointment of RP:**  
  The initial IRP was replaced by Mr. Agrawal, and the CIRP period was extended by **90 days**. The RP published an invitation for resolution plans and received two proposals.
  
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC) and Their Decision:**  
  The CoC, consisting solely of BVC TradePort due to being the only creditor, approved Mr. Chaudhari's plan with **100% voting share**.

### III. Legal Framework and Resolution Plan Evaluation
- **Key Provisions of Section 30(2) and Relevant Regulations:**  
  The Tribunal outlined the requirements for a resolution plan to be approved, including provisions for CIRP costs, debt repayment priority, management of the corporate debtor, and legal compliance.
  
- **Analysis of the Resolution Plan Against Statutory Requirements:**  
  The Tribunal assessed the plan against each requirement, verifying its compliance with debt payment priorities, management provisions, and conformity to relevant laws and regulations.

### IV. Resolution Applicant's Background and Waivers
- **Description of Mr. Chaudhari's Qualifications and Financial Resources:**  
  The Tribunal noted Mr. Chaudhari's background as an engineer and director of a manpower supply company, outlining his financial capacity to implement the plan.
  
- **Summary of Waivers and Concessions Sought by the Applicant:**  
  Mr. Chaudhari sought waivers for certain statutory liabilities, contingent liabilities, and pre-CIRP dues. The Tribunal clarified that such waivers required separate applications to relevant authorities.

### V. Tribunal's Decision and Directives
- **Approval of the Resolution Plan and Extinguishment of Unclaimed Debts:**  
  The Tribunal approved the resolution plan, emphasizing that the resolution applicant must separately seek waivers from relevant authorities. Unpaid liabilities not included in the plan were deemed extinguished.
  
- **Lifting of Moratorium and Implementation Instructions:**  
  The moratorium on Salebhai's assets was lifted, and the resolution plan became effective immediately. The RP was instructed to ensure implementation according to the plan's schedule and submit records to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India.

## Glossary of Key Terms

- **IBC:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, a comprehensive law in India governing insolvency resolution and liquidation for companies and individuals.
  
- **Operational Creditor:** An entity that has provided goods or services to a company and is owed payment for those goods or services.
  
- **Corporate Debtor:** A company that is unable to pay its debts and is undergoing insolvency proceedings.
  
- **Demand Notice:** A formal notice issued by a creditor to a debtor demanding payment of outstanding dues within a specified timeframe.
  
- **CIRP:** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process, a time-bound process under the IBC aimed at reviving a distressed company.
  
- **IRP:** Interim Resolution Professional, a licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the affairs of a corporate debtor during the initial phase of CIRP.
  
- **RP:** Resolution Professional, the IRP confirmed by the CoC to continue managing the CIRP until a resolution plan is approved or liquidation is ordered.
  
- **CoC:** Committee of Creditors, a group of financial creditors who have a voting share in the decision-making process during CIRP.
  
- **Resolution Plan:** A proposal outlining how a distressed company's debts will be restructured or repaid to revive the company.
  
- **Liquidation:** The process of selling a company's assets to distribute the proceeds among creditors when a resolution plan cannot be approved.
  
- **NCLT:** National Company Law Tribunal, a specialized judicial body in India that adjudicates matters related to company law, including insolvency and bankruptcy.

# FAQ Regarding Salebhai Internet Limited Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process

1. **What is the background of the case CP(IB) 61 of 2019?**  
   This case was initiated under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 by **BVC Tradeport Private Limited** (the Operational Creditor) against **Salebhai Internet Limited** (the Corporate Debtor). BVC Tradeport, a logistics service provider, claimed outstanding dues of **Rs. 7,00,297/-** for services rendered to Salebhai, an e-commerce platform. The Corporate Debtor disputed a portion of the claim, citing losses incurred due to mis-deliveries and delays.

2. **What were the key findings of the NCLT Ahmedabad Bench in its order dated 08/12/2021?**  
   The NCLT Ahmedabad Bench established that Salebhai Internet Limited admitted to owing **Rs. 1,94,208/-** to BVC Tradeport. It acknowledged the default and initiated the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against the Corporate Debtor. The court appointed **Mr. Krunal Ramanbhai Tanna** as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

3. **What was the outcome of the CIRP for Salebhai Internet Limited?**  
   During the CIRP, Mr. **Vinod Tarachand Agrawal** replaced Mr. Krunal Tanna as the Resolution Professional (RP). The RP received two resolution plans, and the Committee of Creditors (CoC) approved the plan submitted by **Mr. Smeet Dineshkumar Chaudhari**. This plan was subsequently approved by the NCLT Ahmedabad Bench in June 2023.

4. **What were the key terms of the approved resolution plan?**  
   Mr. Chaudhari's resolution plan proposed a payment of **Rs. 22,78,000/-**, which included **Rs. 7,00,000/-** for the operational creditor (BVC Tradeport), **Rs. 13,78,306/-** for CIRP costs, and **Rs. 2,00,000/-** for capital expenditure. The resolution plan provided full payment to the operational creditor.

5. **What happened to unpaid liabilities or claims not included in the resolution plan?**  
   The NCLT clarified that all unpaid liabilities and claims not filed with the RP during the CIRP would be extinguished upon approval of the resolution plan. This decision aligns with the ruling of the Supreme Court in the case of **Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**.

6. **What reliefs and concessions did the resolution applicant seek?**  
   Mr. Chaudhari, the resolution applicant, requested waivers for various statutory and contingent liabilities, including those related to taxes, employee benefits, and operational dues accrued before the CIRP. However, the approval of the resolution plan was not contingent on these waivers.

7. **How did the NCLT address the resolution applicant's requests for reliefs and concessions?**  
   While the NCLT extinguished pre-CIRP liabilities not covered in the plan, it directed Mr. Chaudhari to approach relevant statutory authorities for specific waivers and concessions. The NCLT emphasized that these authorities should assess his requests in accordance with the law.

8. **What were the immediate consequences of the NCLT's approval of the resolution plan?**  
   Upon approving the resolution plan, the NCLT declared the resolution plan effective immediately. Consequently, the moratorium imposed on Salebhai Internet Limited was lifted. The RP was instructed to disseminate the order to all stakeholders and submit the CIRP records to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India.
