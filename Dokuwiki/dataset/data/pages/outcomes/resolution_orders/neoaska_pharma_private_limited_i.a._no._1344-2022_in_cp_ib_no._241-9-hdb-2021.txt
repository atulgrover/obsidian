===== Briefing Doc: Insolvency Case of Neoaska Pharma Pvt Ltd =====
==== Subject: Review of Key Themes and Facts in the Insolvency Case of Neoaska Pharma Pvt Ltd ====
 
==== Sources: Orders and Documents from the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) Hyderabad Bench – 1 ====
 
==== Date: 2023-10-26 ====
 
==== Summary: ====
This briefing document provides an overview of the insolvency proceedings initiated against Neoaska Pharma Pvt Ltd ("Corporate Debtor") by its operational creditor, Afita Constructions Pvt Ltd ("Operational Creditor"). The document highlights significant events, key players, and the approved resolution plan.
 
==== Main Themes: ====
  * **Initiation of Insolvency Proceedings:** The case began with the Operational Creditor filing a petition under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) due to an unpaid debt exceeding Rs. 1 crore.
  * **Appointment of Resolution Professionals:** Following the admission of the petition, the NCLT appointed Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) and subsequently a Resolution Professional (RP) to oversee the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).
  * **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** The CoC was constituted, consisting of two financial creditors holding 100% of the voting shares. They played a crucial role in evaluating and approving the resolution plan.
  * **Submission and Approval of Resolution Plan:** A single Expression of Interest (EOI) was received from Madhya Pradesh Waste Management Private Limited (MPWMPL), who subsequently submitted a resolution plan that was approved by the CoC with 100% votes.
  * **NCLT Approval and Binding Nature of the Plan:** The NCLT reviewed the resolution plan and ultimately approved it, making it binding on all stakeholders, including the Corporate Debtor, its employees, creditors, and guarantors.
 
==== Important Ideas/Facts: ====
  * **Debt Amount:** The Operational Creditor claimed a debt of Rs. 9,51,28,800/- along with interest at 18% per annum from the Corporate Debtor. (Source: CP IB No.241/9/HDB/2021 Order dated 01.04.2022)
  * **Operational Creditor’s Efforts:** The Operational Creditor had issued invoices, reminders, and a demand notice but received no response, leading them to approach the NCLT. (Source: CP IB No.241/9/HDB/2021 Order dated 01.04.2022)
  * **CoC Composition and Voting:** The CoC comprised Maximus ARC Limited (98.51% voting share) and Visakha Pharma City Limited (1.49% voting share). (Source: IA NO. 1344/2022 in CP(IB) No. 241/9/HDB/2021)
  * **Resolution Plan Highlights:** MPWMPL proposed a total plan consideration of Rs. 22.38 crore, representing a 41.3% recovery for creditors. (Source: IA NO. 1344/2022 in CP(IB) No. 241/9/HDB/2021)
    * Full payment of CIRP costs and admitted claims of financial creditors.
    * 10% payment to admitted claims of operational creditors.
    * Payment within 90 days of NCLT approval.
  * **NCLT's Limited Role:** The NCLT's role was limited to ensuring the resolution plan met the requirements of the IBC and did not interfere with the CoC's commercial decisions. (Source: IA NO. 1344/2022 in CP(IB) No. 241/9/HDB/2021)
 
==== Key Quotes: ====
  * "The IBC has not provided any role to a third party including the unsuccessful resolution applicant to intervene in an application filed under Section 31 of the Code seeking approval of the resolution plan by the Adjudicating Authority." (Source: IA (IBC) 1410, 1344/2022 & IVN.P (IBC) 7/2022 in 1344/2022 in Company Petition IB/241/2021)
  * “If the CoC had approved the Resolution Plan by the requisite percent of voting share, then as per Section 30 (6) of the Code, it is imperative for the Resolution Professional to submit the same to the Adjudicating Authority.” (Source: IA NO. 1344/2022 in CP(IB) No. 241/9/HDB/2021)
  * "This Court has consistently held that the commercial wisdom of the CoC has been given paramount status without any judicial intervention for ensuring completion of the stated processes within the timelines prescribed by the IBC." (Source: IA NO. 1344/2022 in CP(IB) No. 241/9/HDB/2021, quoting Vallal RCK vs M/s Siva Industries and Holdings Limited & Ors)
 
==== Conclusion: ====
The resolution plan's approval by the NCLT marked the completion of the CIRP for Neoaska Pharma Pvt Ltd. The case exemplifies the IBC's objective of maximizing the value of assets and providing a time-bound resolution process for insolvent companies, while respecting the commercial decisions of the CoC.

== Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016: A Study Guide ==

=== Short-Answer Questions ===

1. **What are the grounds for initiating a Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)?**
   - The CIRP can be initiated against a Corporate Debtor upon the occurrence of a 'default', defined as non-payment of a debt exceeding a specified threshold. The application can be filed by a Financial Creditor, Operational Creditor, or the Corporate Debtor itself.

2. **Describe the role of the Resolution Professional (RP) in the CIRP.**
   - The RP is appointed by the NCLT to manage the affairs of the Corporate Debtor during the CIRP. Responsibilities include collecting and verifying claims, managing assets, and facilitating the preparation and approval of the Resolution Plan.

3. **What is the significance of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in the CIRP?**
   - The CoC, comprising Financial Creditors, plays a central role in the CIRP, having the power to approve or reject a Resolution Plan by voting based on their voting share.

4. **Explain the concept of a 'Resolution Plan' under the IBC.**
   - A 'Resolution Plan' is a proposal outlining the restructuring of the Corporate Debtor's debts and operations to revive the company, which may involve debt restructuring, equity infusion, asset sale, or a combination of these measures.

5. **What are the key requirements for a Resolution Plan to be considered viable?**
   - A viable Resolution Plan must maximize the value of the Corporate Debtor, ensure fair treatment of all stakeholders, be capable of implementation within a specified timeframe, and comply with the provisions of the IBC and relevant regulations.

6. **What is the role of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in the CIRP?**
   - The NCLT acts as the adjudicating authority in IBC matters, admitting or rejecting CIRP applications, approving or rejecting Resolution Plans, and passing orders for liquidation if CIRP fails.

7. **Can an Operational Creditor submit a Resolution Plan after the CoC has already approved a plan submitted by a Prospective Resolution Applicant?**
   - No, as per NCLT rulings, an Operational Creditor cannot submit a Resolution Plan after the CoC has approved a plan from a Prospective Resolution Applicant, as this process is impermissible under the IBC.

8. **What happens to the existing equity shares of a Corporate Debtor upon approval of the Resolution Plan by the NCLT?**
   - Upon NCLT approval of the Resolution Plan, existing equity shares of the Corporate Debtor are canceled and extinguished, with the plan potentially providing for new equity shares to be issued to the Resolution Applicant.

9. **What is the purpose of a 'Monitoring Committee' in the context of a Resolution Plan?**
   - A 'Monitoring Committee' oversees the implementation of the Resolution Plan, typically including representatives of the Resolution Applicant, CoC, and RP to ensure timely execution and protection of stakeholder interests.

10. **Briefly outline the grounds on which the NCLT can reject a Resolution Plan.**
    - The NCLT can reject a Resolution Plan if it does not meet the requirements of Section 30(2) of the IBC, including non-compliance with priority of payments, lack of feasibility, and non-conformity with IBC provisions.

=== Short-Answer Key ===

1. **Grounds for CIRP Initiation:** Default in debt payment by the Corporate Debtor.
2. **Role of RP:** Manage Corporate Debtor's affairs during CIRP.
3. **Significance of CoC:** Approves or rejects Resolution Plans.
4. **Resolution Plan Concept:** Proposal for restructuring Corporate Debtor's debts and operations.
5. **Viable Resolution Plan Requirements:** Maximize value, equitable treatment, implementation capability, compliance with IBC.
6. **Role of NCLT:** Adjudicating authority for CIRP applications and Resolution Plans.
7. **Operational Creditor Submission:** Not allowed after CoC approval.
8. **Impact on Equity Shares:** Existing shares canceled; new shares may be issued.
9. **Purpose of Monitoring Committee:** Ensure timely execution of the Resolution Plan.
10. **Grounds for Rejection of Resolution Plan:** Non-compliance with Section 30(2) requirements.

=== Essay Questions ===

1. **Discuss the objectives and key features of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. How does it differ from previous insolvency regimes in India?**
   - The IBC aims to consolidate and amend laws relating to reorganization and insolvency resolution of corporate persons, partnerships, and individuals. Key features include a time-bound process for resolving insolvency, the establishment of the NCLT as the adjudicating authority, and a focus on maximization of value for all stakeholders. It differs from previous regimes by providing a single framework for insolvency and bankruptcy, reducing delays, and emphasizing the role of creditors.

2. **Analyze the roles and responsibilities of the key stakeholders involved in the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP), including the Resolution Professional, Committee of Creditors, and the National Company Law Tribunal.**
   - The RP manages the CIRP, ensuring compliance with IBC provisions and overseeing the Corporate Debtor's affairs. The CoC, consisting of Financial Creditors, makes decisions on the Resolution Plan, while the NCLT serves as the authority to admit applications, approve plans, and ensure adherence to legal standards.

3. **Critically evaluate the provisions of Section 29A of the IBC, which deals with the eligibility criteria for prospective resolution applicants. What are the implications of these provisions for the resolution process?**
   - Section 29A outlines disqualification criteria to ensure that only credible entities participate in the resolution process. It aims to enhance the integrity of the CIRP, preventing unscrupulous individuals from taking advantage of the process. However, it may limit the pool of potential resolution applicants, impacting the viability and competitiveness of submitted plans.

4. **Explain the significance of the rulings of the Supreme Court in the cases of K. Sashidhar v. Indian Overseas Bank & Others and Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Limited Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta & Ors for the interpretation and application of the IBC.**
   - These rulings clarified the role of the CoC in approving Resolution Plans and affirmed the limited scope for judicial intervention in commercial decisions. They reinforced the principle that the CoC's decision is final unless it contravenes the IBC, ensuring a streamlined process and protecting the interests of creditors.

5. **"The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, seeks to balance the interests of both debtors and creditors in the resolution process." Discuss this statement with reference to the relevant provisions of the Code and judicial pronouncements.**
   - The IBC aims to provide a framework that respects the rights of creditors while offering a mechanism for debtors to rehabilitate their businesses. Provisions like the moratorium protect the debtor's assets during the CIRP, while the emphasis on maximizing the value for creditors ensures their interests are safeguarded. Judicial pronouncements have reinforced this balance, emphasizing the need for equitable treatment in resolution efforts.

=== Glossary of Key Terms ===

* **Corporate Debtor:** A company incorporated under the Companies Act that has defaulted on its debt obligations.
* **Operational Creditor:** A person or entity providing goods or services to the Corporate Debtor and owed money for the same.
* **Financial Creditor:** A person or entity providing financial assistance (loans, debt securities) to the Corporate Debtor.
* **Default:** Non-payment of a debt beyond a specified threshold, triggering the possibility of initiating the CIRP.
* **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A time-bound process for resolving the insolvency of a Corporate Debtor, aimed at either reviving the company or liquidating its assets fairly.
* **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the affairs of the Corporate Debtor during the CIRP.
* **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee composed of Financial Creditors with the power to approve or reject the Resolution Plan.
* **Resolution Plan:** A proposal for restructuring the Corporate Debtor's debts and operations, aiming to revive the company.
* **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT):** The adjudicating authority for matters related to the IBC.
* **Liquidation:** The process of selling the Corporate Debtor's assets and distributing proceeds to creditors if CIRP fails.
* **Moratorium:** A period during the CIRP when legal proceedings against the Corporate Debtor are suspended to facilitate the resolution process.
* **Section 29A:** A provision specifying eligibility criteria for prospective resolution applicants to ensure integrity in the resolution process.
* **Section 30(2):** A provision outlining mandatory requirements for a valid Resolution Plan.
* **Section 31:** A provision dealing with the approval of the Resolution Plan by the NCLT.
* **Liquidation Value:** The estimated value of the Corporate Debtor's assets if sold in a liquidation scenario.
* **Fair Value:** The estimated intrinsic value of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern, considering its assets, liabilities, and future earnings potential.

# FAQ Regarding the Neoaska Pharma Private Limited Insolvency Case

### 1. What initiated the insolvency proceedings against Neoaska Pharma Private Limited?
An operational creditor, M/s Afita Constructions Pvt. Ltd, filed a petition under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, due to unpaid dues amounting to Rs. 9,51,28,800/-. The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Hyderabad Bench, admitted the petition on April 1st, 2022, initiating the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).

### 2. Who was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)?
The NCLT appointed Shri Gullapalli Kishore Babu as the IRP for Neoaska Pharma Private Limited.

### 3. Who were the key participants in the Committee of Creditors (CoC)?
The CoC consisted of two financial creditors: Maximus ARC Limited with 98.51% voting share and Visakha Pharma City Limited with 1.49% voting share.

### 4. Who was appointed as the Resolution Professional, and what were their responsibilities?
Dr. G.V. Narasimha Rao was appointed as the Resolution Professional. Their responsibilities included managing the CIRP, inviting Expressions of Interest (EOIs) from potential resolution applicants, evaluating resolution plans, and presenting the approved plan to the NCLT for final approval.

### 5. How many resolution plans were received, and who submitted the successful plan?
Only one resolution plan was received, submitted by Madhya Pradesh Waste Management Private Limited (MPWMPL).

### 6. What were the key financial proposals in the approved resolution plan?
The plan proposed a total payout of Rs. 22.38 crores to various stakeholders, representing a 41.3% recovery rate on admitted claims. Financial creditors were to receive 100% of their admitted claims, while operational creditors were to receive 10% of their admitted claims. The plan also provided for full payment of CIRP costs and unpaid statutory dues.

### 7. What was the role of the Monitoring Committee?
A Monitoring Committee, consisting of representatives from the resolution applicant, the CoC, and the Resolution Professional, was formed to oversee the affairs of the Corporate Debtor until the new Board of Directors was established.

### 8. What happened after the NCLT approved the resolution plan?
Upon NCLT approval, the resolution plan became binding on all stakeholders. The moratorium imposed under Section 14 of the Code ceased to have effect. The Resolution Professional was tasked with forwarding all CIRP-related records to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) and the Registrar of Companies, Hyderabad, for record-keeping and updates. All prior claims against the Corporate Debtor were extinguished, making way for the implementation of the approved resolution plan by MPWMPL.
