**Briefing Document: M/s. Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd. Insolvency Case**

**Subject:** Review of key themes and events in the insolvency case of M/s. Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor)

**Sources:** Judgments and orders from the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) and National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), accessible on the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) website.

---

**Main Themes:**

1. **Resolution Plan Controversies:**  
   The proposed resolution plan faced numerous challenges and appeals, primarily due to concerns around its feasibility and fairness to various stakeholders:
   - **Low Infusion vs. Liquidation Value:** The resolution plan, submitted by M/s. Ace Infracity Developers Private Limited (Resolution Applicant), proposed a relatively low infusion of **Rs.95 crore** compared to the liquidation value of **Rs.480.70 crore**. "[T]he ‘Resolution Plan’ involves infusion of only Rs.95 crore by the Resolution Applicant and that too over a period of two years." (Source: July 8, 2021 NCLAT Order)
   - **Farmer Compensation:** The plan initially assumed farmers would relinquish their **Rs.71.66 crore** claim in exchange for village development, deemed "amateurish" by the NCLT. This was later revised to include **100% principal payment** of farmer compensation, contingent on peaceful land possession handover by Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority (YEIDA).
   - **Homebuyer Treatment:** Concerns were raised about the fairness of the plan for homebuyers, particularly regarding the potential realization value of their plots in comparison to the liquidation value.
   - **Lease Cancellation Dispute:** YEIDA, the lessor of the land for the Corporate Debtor's project, canceled the lease deed, citing non-payment and breach of terms. This became a major obstacle in the resolution process.
   - **Moratorium Implications:** The Resolution Professional argued that the lease cancellation was in violation of the moratorium imposed under Section 14 of the IBC, prohibiting legal proceedings against the Corporate Debtor. "[T]he said cancellation order was passed in contravention of the moratorium imposed under Section 14 of IBC, 2016..." (Source: January 12, 2023, NCLT Order)
   - **Statutory Powers vs. IBC:** YEIDA argued that the cancellation was based on statutory powers granted under the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Area Development Act, 1976, and not subject to the IBC. The NCLT ultimately ruled in favor of the Resolution Professional, stating that the IBC's Section 238 had overriding effect, making the cancellation order void.

2. **Role of CoC and Judicial Intervention:**  
   The case highlighted the CoC's commercial wisdom in approving the resolution plan and the judiciary's role in ensuring adherence to the IBC framework:
   - **CoC Approval & Appeals:** The resolution plan received **62.9%** voting share approval from the CoC. However, this was challenged by various stakeholders, leading to appeals before the NCLAT and the Supreme Court.
   - **Minimal Judicial Interference:** The Supreme Court, in its order dated April 17, 2023, directed the NCLT to expedite the plan approval, emphasizing the need for minimal judicial intervention in the CoC's commercial decisions.

## Short-Answer Questions

1. **What were the primary reasons given by the Adjudicating Authority for rejecting the initial Resolution Plan for M/s. Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd.?**  
   The Adjudicating Authority rejected the Resolution Plan due to:
   - Low infusion of funds compared to the liquidation value.
   - Unrealistic assumptions that farmers would forgo their claims.
   - Non-compliance with the Code and CIRP Regulations.

2. **How did the Appellant in the case argue that the 'Resolution Plan' had been misrepresented in terms of the funds being released by the Resolution Applicant?**  
   The Appellant argued that the Rs. 95 crore presented as funds from the Resolution Applicant did not include debts owed to allottees, ex-management, operational creditors, discounts, or waived interest, significantly misrepresenting the actual financial commitment.

3. **Why was the matter remanded back to the Adjudicating Authority, and what were they instructed to do?**  
   The matter was remanded back to allow for:
   - Reconciliation of homebuyer benefits.
   - Compliance checks with the CIRP Regulations.
   - Inclusion of the Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority (YEIDA) to address farmer disputes.

4. **What specific relief was sought in IA-4490/2021?**  
   IA-4490/2021 sought to set aside the order by YEIDA canceling the lease deed for the land in question, arguing that the action violated the moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC.

5. **Explain the primary arguments made by both the Applicant/RP and the Respondent/YEIDA regarding the cancellation of the lease deed.**  
   - **Applicant/RP's Argument:** The cancellation was in contravention of the IBC's moratorium, and the lessor could not recover property during CIRP.
   - **YEIDA's Counterarguments:** The cancellation was within its statutory powers and predated the moratorium.

6. **How did the Tribunal ultimately rule on IA-4490/2021, and what were the key justifications for this decision?**  
   The Tribunal ruled in favor of the Applicant/RP, finding that YEIDA's action was not protected under Section 14 of the IBC and that the IBC superseded YEIDA's statutory powers. The lease cancellation was deemed void, but the RP was directed to pay CIRP period lease rentals.

7. **What was the purpose of IA-3385/2020, and what were the key events leading up to its filing?**  
   IA-3385/2020 aimed to secure approval for the Resolution Plan of M/s. Ace Infracity Developers Private Limited. It followed the CoC's approval of the plan and involved several legal challenges and appeals before reaching the final approval stage.

8. **Summarize the key arguments presented in favor of the Resolution Plan's approval.**  
   Arguments supporting the Resolution Plan included:
   - Compliance with the Code and CIRP Regulations.
   - Feasibility in revitalizing the project.
   - Provisions for homebuyer claims and repayments.
   - The Resolution Applicant's capability to execute the plan successfully.

9. **What were the main objections raised by YEIDA to the Resolution Plan?**  
   YEIDA objected that:
   - The plan disregarded its dues.
   - It unfairly allowed the Resolution Applicant to acquire the land for a pittance.
   - The plan allowed exploitation of public assets for profit, contrary to public interest and Section 30(2) of the Code.

10. **What was the final ruling on IA-3385/2020, and what were the significant implications of this decision for the various stakeholders?**  
    IA-3385/2020 was ultimately allowed, approving the Resolution Plan, binding all stakeholders to its terms. ## Glossary of Key Terms

- **Adjudicating Authority:** In the context of the IBC, this refers to the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), which has the power to decide on matters related to insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings.

- **Appellant:** The party who appeals a lower court's decision to a higher court. In these excerpts, the Appellant is likely the Resolution Applicant or a related party challenging the Adjudicating Authority's order.

- **CIRP:** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process. A time-bound process under the IBC for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor by attempting to revive the company or, if that fails, by liquidating its assets.

- **CIRP Regulations:** Regulations formulated by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) to provide detailed guidelines for the implementation of the CIRP process.

- **CoC:** Committee of Creditors. A body formed in a CIRP consisting of all financial creditors of the corporate debtor. The CoC has the power to approve or reject a Resolution Plan.

- **Corporate Debtor:** A company that is unable to pay its debts and is undergoing insolvency proceedings.

- **IBC:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The primary law governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings in India.

- **Liquidation Value:** The estimated value of a company's assets if they were sold in a liquidation scenario.

- **Moratorium:** A legal stay on all debt recovery actions against a corporate debtor during the CIRP. The moratorium prevents creditors from taking individual action to enforce their claims.

- **Resolution Applicant:** An entity that submits a plan to the CoC proposing to revive a corporate debtor or acquire its assets.

- **Resolution Plan:** A plan submitted by a resolution applicant outlining how they propose to resolve the insolvency of a corporate debtor.

- **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CIRP. The RP takes control of the corporate debtor's affairs, collects claims, and facilitates the resolution process.

- **YEIDA:** Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority. A statutory body responsible for the development of the Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Area. In this case, YEIDA is a stakeholder as the lessor of the land involved in the project.

# Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd. Insolvency Case: FAQ

1. **What is the background of the Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd. insolvency case?**  
   Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd., a real estate development company, faced financial distress due to its inability to complete the "Lotus City" project. This situation led to a petition filed by a financial creditor under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, which initiated the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).

2. **What were the key issues raised during the CIRP?**  
   Several issues emerged during the CIRP, including:
   - Disagreements over the valuation of the company and its assets.
   - Disputes between the company, the Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority (YEIDA), and farmers regarding land possession.
   - Concerns regarding the treatment of homebuyers' claims.
   - Challenges to the proposed resolution plan based on its compliance with the IBC and relevant regulations.

3. **Why was the initial resolution plan rejected by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)?**  
   The NCLT rejected the first resolution plan due to:
   - The low amount offered by the resolution applicant compared to the liquidation value.
   - The proposed settlement with farmers being considered unrealistic.
   - Non-compliance with specific provisions of the IBC and the CIRP Regulations.

4. **What were the key points of contention between the Resolution Professional and YEIDA?**  
   The Resolution Professional (RP) and YEIDA disagreed on:
   - The validity of YEIDA's cancellation of the lease deed for the land in question.
   - Whether YEIDA's actions were restricted by the moratorium imposed under Section 14 of the IBC.
   - The amount of outstanding dues owed to YEIDA by the Corporate Debtor.

5. **How did the NCLAT rule on the dispute between the RP and YEIDA?**  
   The NCLAT ruled in favor of the RP, determining that YEIDA's cancellation of the lease deed was void because it violated the moratorium provisions of the IBC. The NCLAT directed that lease rentals during the CIRP be considered CIRP costs, and any pre-CIRP claims by YEIDA should be treated as operational creditor claims.

6. **What were the primary concerns of the homebuyers in the "Lotus City" project?**  
   Homebuyers were concerned about:
   - Receiving the promised plots/flats.
   - The fairness of the valuation of their claims.
   - The possibility of receiving refunds if they opted out of the project.

7. **What were the key features of the revised resolution plan that was eventually approved?**  
   The approved plan addressed earlier concerns by:
   - Ensuring the development and delivery of plots to allottees.
   - Providing options for refunds on a case-by-case basis.
   - Addressing the outstanding dues of YEIDA.
   - Clarifying the treatment of various stakeholders' interests.

8. **What were the final outcomes of the case?**  
   The NCLT ultimately approved the revised resolution plan, paving the way for the revival of Three C Homes Pvt. Ltd. The plan aimed to ensure the completion of the "Lotus City" project, address homebuyers' concerns, and settle outstanding dues with creditors like YEIDA. The moratorium was lifted, and the RP was directed to hand over the company's management to the resolution applicant.
