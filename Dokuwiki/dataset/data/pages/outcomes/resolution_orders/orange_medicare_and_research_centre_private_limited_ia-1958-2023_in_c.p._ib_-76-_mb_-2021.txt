### **Short-Answer Questions**

1. **What led Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd. to file a petition to initiate CIRP against Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited?**  
   Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd. filed the petition because Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited had defaulted on repayment of term loans amounting to Rs. 20,67,75,550/- inclusive of interest as of November 30, 2020. The bank had issued notices and taken actions under the SARFAESI Act, but the Corporate Debtor failed to repay the dues.

2. **Summarize the key events that occurred after the admission of the petition by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).**  
   After the NCLT admitted the petition, a moratorium was declared, suspending all legal actions against the Corporate Debtor. An Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) was appointed, public announcements were made, and the IRP started inviting claims from creditors. The IRP also took control of the Corporate Debtor's assets and management.

3. **What is the role of a Resolution Professional (RP) in the CIRP process?**  
   The Resolution Professional (RP) manages the affairs of the Corporate Debtor during the CIRP. The RP's responsibilities include collecting information, inviting claims, verifying claims, forming the Committee of Creditors (CoC), facilitating the resolution process, and submitting the resolution plan to the NCLT for approval.

4. **Explain the concept of a moratorium in the context of CIRP.**  
   A moratorium is a legal protection granted to a Corporate Debtor undergoing CIRP. It temporarily halts all legal proceedings against the Corporate Debtor, including debt recovery actions, lawsuits, and enforcement of security interests. This allows the RP to conduct the resolution process without interference.

5. **What were the grounds on which Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited contested the CIRP petition?**  
   Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited argued that the petition was defective due to the bank's non-compliance with Section 7(3)(a) of the IBC, which requires furnishing a record of default from a credit information company. They also argued that the petition was time-barred and that the bank was guilty of fraud and siphoning funds.

6. **How did Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd. respond to the objections raised by Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited?**  
   Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd. refuted the allegations of fraud and provided evidence of the Corporate Debtor's admission of liability through multiple OTS proposals and requests for the release of personal properties of guarantors. They also furnished the record of default as requested by the NCLT.

7. **Describe the composition and functions of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in the CIRP.**  
   The CoC consists of financial creditors of the Corporate Debtor. They have the power to approve or reject the resolution plan, decide on the distribution of funds, and guide the RP during the CIRP. The CoC in this case comprised solely Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd., holding 100% voting rights.

8. **What is the significance of the 'Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited' judgment in CIRP cases?**  
   The judgment clarified that upon NCLT's approval of the resolution plan, all claims not included in the plan are extinguished. This means that creditors whose claims are not covered in the approved plan cannot pursue recovery actions against the Corporate Debtor after the resolution plan is implemented.

9. **What is the purpose of an application filed under Section 60(5) r/w 19(2) of the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, 2016?**  
   An application under Section 60(5) r/w 19(2) of the IBC is filed by the RP to seek the NCLT's intervention in obtaining cooperation and information from the suspended management, auditors, or other relevant parties. This provision enables the RP to gather necessary information and documents to effectively carry out their duties during the CIRP.

10. **What were the challenges faced by the Resolution Professional in obtaining information and documents from the suspended board members and auditors of Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited?**  
    The RP faced challenges such as delayed responses, unavailability of information, and non-cooperation from the suspended board members and auditors of Orange Medicare and Research Centre Private Limited. The RP had to resort to seeking police assistance to take control of the Corporate Debtor's assets and obtain relevant documents.

### **Glossary of Key Terms**

- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A time-bound process under the IBC for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor.
- **Financial Creditor:** An entity whose relationship with the corporate debtor is a financial debt.
- **Operational Creditor:** An entity that has provided goods or services to the corporate debtor and has not received payment.
- **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CIRP.
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee formed by the financial creditors to take decisions related to the CIRP.
- **Resolution Plan:** A plan proposed by a resolution applicant to revive the corporate debtor.
- **Liquidation:** A process of selling off the assets of the corporate debtor to repay creditors.
- **Moratorium:** A legal protection that temporarily suspends all legal actions against the corporate debtor.
- **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT):** A judicial body that adjudicates matters related to company law, including insolvency.
- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC):** The primary legislation governing insolvency and bankruptcy in India.
- **SARFAESI Act:** Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002.
- **One Time Settlement (OTS):** A negotiated agreement between a debtor and creditor for settling the debt for a reduced amount.
- **Non-Performing Asset (NPA):** A loan where the borrower has stopped making repayments.

After attempts to recover dues through notices and actions under the SARFAESI Act failed, the bank filed a petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC).

### What was the role of the Resolution Professional (RP) in the CIRP?
Mr. Udayraj Patwardhan was appointed as the RP. His responsibilities included:
- Publishing a public announcement inviting claims from creditors.
- Constituting the Committee of Creditors (CoC).
- Inviting Expressions of Interest and Resolution Plans from potential investors.
- Evaluating the submitted Resolution Plans for compliance with the IBC and feasibility.
- Presenting the approved Resolution Plan to the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) for final approval.

### How many Resolution Plans were received, and who submitted them?
Two Resolution Plans were initially received, from Mr. Jitendra Bhandari and M/s. Sunrise Industries. However, both were found non-compliant with the IBC. After revisions, only Mr. Jitendra Bhandari submitted a modified plan that met the requirements.

### What were the key financial aspects of the approved Resolution Plan?
The Resolution Plan proposed by Mr. Jitendra Bhandari included:
- Taking over the management and ownership of the company by acquiring 100% shareholding.
- A total consideration of ₹24,00,00,000/- (Rupees Twenty-four Crores only) to be paid to all stakeholders within 75 days of the effective date.
- ₹6,00,00,000/- (Rupees Six Crores only) allocated for capital expenditure (CAPEX) and working capital.
- Extinguishment of existing shares held by shareholders, with no payment made to them.
- Full satisfaction and extinguishment of all claims, debts, and dues of creditors pertaining to the period before the CIRP commencement date.

### What was the role of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in the process?
The NCLT acted as the adjudicating authority in the CIRP. It reviewed the Resolution Plan submitted by the RP and assessed its compliance with the IBC. The NCLT also considered the feasibility and viability of the plan before granting final approval, making it binding on all stakeholders.

### What happened to the claims of operational creditors under the approved Resolution Plan?
The approved Resolution Plan did not provide any payment to operational creditors. This was due to the resolution money offered (₹24 crores) being equivalent to the liquidation value, and the claims of the secured financial creditor (Saraswat Co-op Bank Ltd.) along with the CIRP costs exceeding this amount.

### What measures were taken to ensure the effective implementation of the Resolution Plan?
To ensure the effective implementation of the plan, the NCLT ordered the formation of a Monitoring Committee. This committee was tasked with supervising the plan's execution and reporting the implementation status to the NCLT periodically.

### Were there any legal challenges or disputes during the CIRP?
Yes, the RP faced challenges in obtaining cooperation from the suspended board members and statutory auditor of the Corporate Debtor. An application was filed with the NCLT seeking their cooperation in providing necessary information and documents. The NCLT granted the request and directed the parties to comply.
