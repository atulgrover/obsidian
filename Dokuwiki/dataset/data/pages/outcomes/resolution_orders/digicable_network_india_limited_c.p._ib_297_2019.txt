### Briefing Document: Digicable Network (India) Limited CIRP

#### Subject:
Review of the CIRP process for Digicable Network (India) Limited and the approved Resolution Plan.

#### Sources:
- **Order Sheet**: National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench, Court III dated 12.01.2023 (Source 1)
- **Application under Section 7**: Axis Bank Limited vs. Digicable Network (India) Limited (Source 2)
- **Application under Section 30(6) and 31**: Mr. Shamsher Bahadur Singh (Resolution Professional) vs. Digicable Network (India) Limited (Source 3)

#### 1. Background
Digicable Network (India) Limited (DNIL) faced financial difficulties leading to a default on loans from Axis Bank Limited.

#### 2. Initiation of CIRP
Axis Bank filed an application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, on December 4, 2020, to initiate the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against DNIL (Source 2). The application was admitted, and Mr. Sunil Kumar Choudhary was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

#### 3. Appointment of Resolution Professional and Claims
Mr. Anil Mehta was subsequently appointed as the IRP and later confirmed as the Resolution Professional (RP) by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) (Source 3). The RP received and admitted claims from various creditors, with secured financial creditors representing the largest portion of the admitted claims (Source 3).

#### 4. Resolution Plan Submission and Approval
After multiple rounds of inviting Expressions of Interest (EOI) and extensions for submitting Resolution Plans, the CoC ultimately approved the plan submitted by Mr. Madhav Dhir with a 100% voting share (Source 3).

#### 5. Salient Features of the Approved Resolution Plan
- **Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA)**: Mr. Madhav Dhir, a seasoned business professional with relevant experience (Source 3).
- **Cancellation of Existing Shareholding**: All existing shares in DNIL will be cancelled, with no payment to existing shareholders (Source 3).
- **Issue of Fresh Equity Shares**: The SRA will receive fresh equity shares based on the company's capital structure requirements (Source 3).
- **Monitoring Committee**: A Monitoring Committee will oversee the implementation of the plan until the Effective Date (Source 3).
- **Effective Date**: Marks the date the SRA takes over management of DNIL (Source 3).
- **Consideration Amount**: The plan proposes a total consideration of Rs. 22,937.59 Lakhs (Source 3).
- **Payment Schedule**: Includes upfront payment, deferred payment installments, and issuance of Zero-Coupon Debentures (ZCDs) to the secured creditors (Source 3).
- **Treatment of Unsecured Creditors**: No payment is proposed for the two unsecured creditors, who are related parties of the Corporate Debtor, as their claims were not admitted (Source 3).
- **Treatment of Operational Creditors**: The plan proposes a payment of Rs. 50 Lakhs towards the admitted claims of Workmen and Employees (Source 3).
- **Treatment of Statutory/Government Liabilities**: Provision of Rs. 87.59 Lakhs is made for any unpaid statutory or government liabilities (Source 3).
- **Capital Restructuring**: The plan details the restructuring of the share capital, including cancellation of existing shares and issuance of fresh equity shares (Source 3).
- **Sources of Funds**: The SRA will raise funds through equity contributions, issuance of ZCDs, and potentially debt or internal accruals (Source 3).

#### 6. Legal Considerations
The Tribunal considered relevant legal precedents, including *K. Sashidhar v. Indian Overseas Bank & Others* and *Essar Steel India Limited vs. Satish Kumar Gupta & Ors*, to ensure the plan complies with the IBC and relevant regulations (Source 3). The Tribunal clarified that the plan's approval does not waive any statutory obligations and highlighted the limitations of judicial review in CIRP cases (Source 3).

#### 7. Overall Assessment
The Resolution Plan for DNIL demonstrates a comprehensive approach to addressing the company's financial distress. It prioritizes payments to secured creditors and outlines a clear path for the SRA to revitalize the business. The plan's adherence to legal requirements and consideration of stakeholder interests suggests a positive outcome for DNIL's future.

### Glossary of Key Terms

- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A time-bound process under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, for reviving a financially distressed company or resolving its insolvency.

- **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed professional appointed to manage the affairs of the company during the CIRP and facilitate the resolution process.

- **Expression of Interest (EOI):** A formal invitation by the RP to solicit interest from potential resolution applicants who wish to submit a resolution plan for the distressed company.

- **Resolution Applicant:** An individual or entity that submits a resolution plan proposing a revival strategy for the company.

- **Secured Financial Creditor:** A creditor whose debt is backed by a specific asset (collateral) of the company.

- **Unsecured Financial Creditor:** A creditor whose debt is not secured by any specific asset of the company.

- **Operational Creditor:** A creditor who has provided goods or services to the company and is owed money for the same.

- **Liquidation Value:** The estimated amount that would be realized if the company's assets were sold piecemeal in a liquidation scenario.

- **Fair Value:** The estimated value of the company as a going concern, considering its future earnings potential and other factors.

- **Resolution Plan:** A formal proposal submitted by a resolution applicant, outlining a strategy for reviving the distressed company and repaying its creditors.

- **Dissenting Financial Creditor:** A financial creditor who votes against the proposed resolution plan.

- **Monitoring Committee:** A committee formed to oversee the implementation of the approved resolution plan.

- **Effective Date:** The date on which the resolution plan comes into effect and control of the company is transferred to the resolution applicant.

- **Avoidance Application:** A legal application filed by the RP to recover assets of the company that were unfairly transferred or disposed of before the commencement of the CIRP.

### Digicable Network (India) Limited - Resolution Plan FAQ

1. **What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for Digicable Network (India) Limited?**
   - Axis Bank Limited filed an application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, for initiating the CIRP against Digicable Network (India) Limited (DNIL) due to a default in the repayment of outstanding dues amounting to **Rs. 129,23,00,000**.

2. **Who was appointed as the Resolution Professional (RP)?**
   - Initially, Mr. Sunil Kumar Choudhary was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional. Later, Mr. Anil Mehta took over the role and was subsequently confirmed as the RP by the Committee of Creditors (CoC). Finally, Mr. Shamsher Bahadur Singh was appointed as the RP.

3. **What was the Resolution Plan approved by the CoC?**
   - The CoC approved the resolution plan submitted by Mr. Madhav Dhir with a **100% voting share**. The plan involved the cancellation of existing shareholding, the issuance of fresh equity shares to Mr. Dhir, and the establishment of a Monitoring Committee to oversee the implementation of the plan.

4. **How were the claims of different creditors addressed in the Resolution Plan?**
   - **Secured Financial Creditors:** They were to receive a total of **Rs. 20,000.00 Lakhs** through a combination of upfront payment, deferred payments over 36 months, and the issuance of Zero Coupon Debentures (ZCDs).
   - **Unsecured Financial Creditors:** The Resolution Plan proposed a **NIL payment** to the two identified Unsecured Financial Creditors as they were related parties of the Corporate Debtor and their claims were not admitted.
   - **Operational Creditors (including Workmen and Employees):** The plan allocated **Rs. 50.00 Lakhs** for the payment of workmen and employee claims. For other operational creditors, **Rs. 87.59 Lakhs** was set aside to cover liabilities listed in the balance sheet.

5. **What is the role of the Monitoring Committee?**
   - The Monitoring Committee, comprising an insolvency professional, a representative of the Resolution Applicant, and a representative of the Financial Creditors, is responsible for overseeing the operations of DNIL until the management and control are handed over to the Resolution Applicant (Mr. Madhav Dhir).

6. **What happens to pending disputes and litigations involving DNIL?**
   - Upon the approval of the Resolution Plan, all claims not included in the plan stand extinguished. No person can initiate or continue any legal proceedings related to such claims. However, claims that arose before or during the CIRP period will be settled within the amounts allocated to the relevant creditors in the Resolution Plan. Any amount recovered from litigation where DNIL is a party will be used for cash flow management and implementation of the Resolution Plan.

7. **How does the Resolution Plan address unknown or hidden liabilities?**
   - The Resolution Applicant and DNIL are not liable for any hidden or unknown liabilities related to the period before the Insolvency Commencement Date (ICD). If such liabilities surface later, they will be settled at a **NIL amount**.

8. **What is the timeline for the implementation of the Resolution Plan?**
   - The Resolution Plan outlines a detailed timeline for various events, including the upfront payment towards CIRP costs, payment to different creditors, the removal of existing directors, the appointment of new directors, and the transfer of management and control to the Resolution Applicant. The Effective Date, when the Resolution Applicant takes over the management of DNIL, is set as **90 days** from the NCLT approval date or the actual handover date, whichever is later.
