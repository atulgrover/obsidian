This briefing document reviews orders and applications related to the insolvency case of M/s. Alkas Spinning Mills Private Limited. Here's a summary of the key themes and findings:

I. Case Background (IA(IBC)/1046(CHE)/2021)
- M/s. Alkas Spinning Mills Private Limited was placed under the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) by order of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) on 05.05.2020.
- Mr. M. Suresh Kumar was appointed as the Resolution Professional (RP).
- KSV Cotton Mills Private Limited emerged as the successful resolution applicant and submitted their resolution plan.

II. Key Issues & Objections to the Resolution Plan
- **Non-compliance with CIRP Regulations:** A significant concern raised was the RP's failure to adhere to the CIRP Regulations, specifically Regulation 36A (6). This regulation mandates the rejection of Expressions of Interest (EOIs) received after the stipulated deadline. In this case, the RP entertained the successful resolution applicant's EOI despite it being submitted after the deadline.
- **Objection from Suspended Director:** Mr. A. Anand, a suspended director of the corporate debtor, filed applications challenging the resolution plan on various grounds. His primary contentions were:
  - The successful applicant's EOI was submitted after the deadline.
  - He was prevented from participating in the CoC meetings, despite his claim that the corporate debtor is an MSME, and he should have been allowed to participate as a stakeholder.
  - He sought to submit his own resolution plan.

III. NCLT Chennai Bench Findings and Orders
- **Resolution Plan Approval Set Aside:** In its order dated 02.11.2022 (IA(IBC)/1046(CHE)/2021), the NCLT Chennai Bench, after considering the submissions and observing procedural irregularities, set aside the approval of the resolution plan.
- **CIRP Extended:** The NCLT extended the CIRP period to accommodate a fresh resolution process. The court directed the RP to reissue the invitation for expressions of interest and complete the process in accordance with CIRP regulations.

IV. Subsequent Events and Final Resolution (IA(IBC)/430(CHE)/2022)
- The RP invited fresh EOIs and received a total of seven.
- Two resolution plans were submitted, one by the promoter and the other by M/s. MG Threads.
- The CoC ultimately approved the resolution plan submitted by Mr. A. Anand (suspended director) with 100% voting.

V. NCLT Approval of Final Resolution
- The NCLT Chennai bench, through its order pronounced on July 5, 2023, approved the final resolution plan.
- The court noted that the successful resolution applicant, being the promoter of the corporate debtor, possessed the necessary experience and understanding of the business to ensure its revival.
- The order details the implementation, management, and supervision of the resolution plan, including the financial settlement, payment schedule, and shareholding pattern post-resolution.

VI. Quotes from Sources
- **Regarding late EOI:** "The expression of interest received after the time specified in the invitation under clause (b) of sub-regulation (3) shall be rejected." - CIRP Regulations, 2016.
- **Regarding limited judicial review:** "Thus, it is clear that the limited judicial review available, which can in no circumstance trespass upon a business decision of the majority of the Committee of Creditors, insofar as the Adjudicating Authority is concerned, and Section 32 read with Section 61(3) of the Code, insofar as the Appellate Tribunal is concerned, the parameters of such review have been clearly laid down in K. Sashidhar (supra)." - IA(IBC)/430(CHE)/2022 Order

VII. Conclusion
This case highlights the importance of strict adherence to the CIRP regulations to ensure a fair and transparent insolvency resolution process. The initial resolution plan was set aside due to procedural lapses, emphasizing the NCLT's role in upholding the integrity of the process. The final resolution's approval underscores the court's objective of maximizing the value of the assets and reviving the corporate debtor, even if it means approving a plan from a previously suspended director.

## **Short Answer Questions**

### **What is the role of the Resolution Professional (RP) in the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**

The Resolution Professional (RP) plays a crucial role in the CIRP. They are responsible for:

* **Managing the Corporate Debtor:** The RP takes over the management of the company's affairs during the CIRP.
* **Inviting Resolution Plans:** The RP invites potential resolution applicants to submit their plans for reviving the company.
* **Evaluating Resolution Plans:** The RP evaluates the submitted plans based on their feasibility, viability, and compliance with legal requirements.
* **Reporting to the CoC:** The RP reports to the Committee of Creditors (CoC) on the progress of the CIRP and the status of the Resolution Plans.

### **Explain the significance of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in the CIRP.**

The CoC holds significant power in the CIRP. They are responsible for:

* **Approving or Rejecting Resolution Plans:** The CoC has the authority to approve or reject a Resolution Plan based on their assessment of its merits.
* **Representing Creditors' Interests:** The CoC represents the interests of the financial creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
* **Overseeing the CIRP:** The CoC monitors the progress of the CIRP and ensures that the RP is acting in the best interests of the creditors.

### **What is a Resolution Plan and what are its key components?**

A Resolution Plan is a comprehensive document outlining the proposed measures for reviving a Corporate Debtor or liquidating its assets. It typically includes:

* **Debt Repayment:** A plan for repaying the debts of the Corporate Debtor.
* **Business Operations:** A strategy for restructuring or reviving the company's business operations.
* **Future Management:** A plan for the future management of the company, including the appointment of new directors or management teams.
* **Valuation of Assets:** An assessment of the value of the Corporate Debtor's assets.

### **Describe the process of inviting and evaluating Resolution Plans under the CIRP.**

The process of inviting and evaluating Resolution Plans involves the following steps:

1. **Invitation for Expression of Interest (EOI):** The RP issues a public notice inviting interested parties to submit their EOI.
2. **Submission of Resolution Plans:** Eligible resolution applicants submit their Resolution Plans within the specified timeframe.
3. **Evaluation of Plans:** The RP and the CoC evaluate the submitted plans based on various factors, including feasibility, viability, and compliance with legal requirements.
4. **Selection of Preferred Plan:** The CoC typically selects a preferred Resolution Plan based on their assessment of the submitted plans.

### **What happens if a Resolution Plan is not approved by the CoC?**

If the CoC rejects a Resolution Plan, the NCLT may order the liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. This means that the company's assets will be sold to repay creditors, and the company will be dissolved.

### **Discuss the role of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in overseeing the CIRP.**

The NCLT plays a crucial role in overseeing the CIRP. It acts as an adjudicating authority, with the power to:

* **Approve or Reject Resolution Plans:** The NCLT has the authority to approve or reject a Resolution Plan if it finds it to be in the best interests of the Corporate Debtor and its creditors.
* **Resolve Disputes:** The NCLT can resolve disputes that arise during the CIRP, such as disputes between the RP, the CoC, or other stakeholders.
* **Ensure Compliance:** The NCLT ensures that the CIRP is conducted in accordance with the provisions of the IBC and other relevant laws.

### **What is the concept of 'timelines' in CIRP and why is it important?**

Timelines play a crucial role in the CIRP. The IBC sets out strict timelines for various stages of the process, including the submission of Resolution Plans, the approval of plans by the CoC, and the completion of the CIRP. These timelines are important because:

* **Efficiency:** Timelines help to ensure that the CIRP is conducted in a timely and efficient manner.
* **Protection of Creditors' Interests:** Adhering to timelines helps to protect the interests of creditors by preventing unnecessary delays.
* **Prevention of Value Erosion:** Delays can lead to a decline in the value of the Corporate Debtor's assets. Timelines help to minimize this risk.

### **What are some common objections raised against Resolution Plans?**

Some common objections raised against Resolution Plans include:

* **Feasibility of the Plan:** Objections may be raised regarding the feasibility of the plan, such as its ability to revive the company or repay creditors.
* **Valuation of Assets:** Objections may be raised regarding the valuation of the Corporate Debtor's assets, which can affect the proposed debt repayment plan.
* **Fairness to Stakeholders:** Objections may be raised regarding the fairness of the plan to certain stakeholders, such as employees or unsecured creditors.

### **Explain the concept of 'non-compliance' in the context of a Resolution Plan.**

Non-compliance refers to failure to adhere to the terms and conditions of the approved Resolution Plan. This can include:

* **Failure to Make Payments:** The resolution applicant may fail to make the required payments to creditors or other stakeholders.
* **Non-Adherence to Business Plans:** The resolution applicant may fail to implement the proposed business plans or restructuring measures.
* **Violation of Legal Requirements:** The resolution applicant may violate any applicable laws or regulations.

### **What is the role of the Financial Sponsor in a Resolution Plan?**

A Financial Sponsor provides financial backing to the Resolution Applicant for implementing the Resolution Plan. They typically invest in the Corporate Debtor to:

* **Revive the Company:** The Financial Sponsor may provide funds to help the company restructure its operations and become profitable.
* **Acquire Assets:** The Financial Sponsor may acquire the assets of the Corporate Debtor and continue the business under new ownership.
* **Provide Working Capital:** The Financial Sponsor may provide working capital to support the company's operations during the recovery process.
