# Briefing Document: Insolvency Case of Farmax India Ltd.

## Subject: Approval of Resolution Plan for Farmax India Ltd. under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

### Background:
- **Petition Filed**: Vasthi Trading Pvt Ltd, an operational creditor, filed a petition under **Section 9 of the IBC, 2016** against **Farmax India Ltd.** ("Corporate Debtor") for a debt of **Rs. 1,05,23,214**.
- **NCLT Admission**: The **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Hyderabad Bench**, admitted the petition on **01.07.2022** and appointed **Mr. Chinna Gurappa** as the **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**. Mr. Gurappa was subsequently confirmed as the **Resolution Professional (RP)**.

### Key Events:
- The RP invited claims from creditors, constituted the **Committee of Creditors (CoC)**, and appointed valuers to determine the fair and liquidation value of the Corporate Debtor.
- **Reliance Asset Reconstruction Company Limited** emerged as the sole financial creditor with a **100% voting share** in the CoC.
- The RP published an **Expression of Interest (EOI)** inviting **Resolution Plans**, receiving a single bid from **Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy** (“RSR/ ‘Resolution Applicant’).
- The CoC approved RSR's Resolution Plan with a **100% voting share**.

### Resolution Plan Highlights:
- **Resolution Applicant**: Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy
- **Total Resolution Amount**: Rs. **55,00,000**
- **Distribution**:
  - **CIRP Cost**: Rs. **12,00,000** (100% of admitted claim)
  - **Secured Financial Creditor**: Rs. **31,94,319** (17.50% of admitted claim)
  - **Operational Creditors**: Rs. **32,622** (0.31% of admitted claim)
  - **Government Dues**: Rs. **9,99,890** (0.31% of admitted claim)
  - **Contingencies**: Rs. **73,169**
- **Timeline**: 
  - Funds infusion within **30 days** of NCLT approval
  - Equity allotment within **90 days**
- **Monitoring Committee**: Comprises the Resolution Applicant, RP, and Financial Creditor.

### NCLT Order (22.06.2023):
- The NCLT approved the Resolution Plan, noting its compliance with **Section 30(2)** of the IBC and relevant regulations.
- The court cited key **Supreme Court** judgments emphasizing the limited scope of judicial review in IBC cases and the importance of upholding the CoC's commercial wisdom.
- The court stated, “This Court has consistently held that the commercial wisdom of the CoC has been given paramount status without any judicial intervention...” - *Vallal RCK vs M/s Siva Industries and Holdings Limited & Ors*

### Key Considerations:
- The Resolution Plan offers a significantly lower recovery for creditors compared to their admitted claims.
- The NCLT emphasized the **minimal interference principle** in its decision, highlighting the deference given to the CoC's judgment.

### Next Steps:
- Implementation of the Resolution Plan according to the agreed timeline.
- Monitoring of the Corporate Debtor's operations by the Monitoring Committee.

# Timeline of Events: Insolvency Case of Farmax India Ltd.

## 2021:
- **March 2, 2021**: Vasthi Trading Pvt Ltd issues a **Form-3 demand notice** to Farmax India Ltd for unpaid dues for supplied unbranded rawa.
- **Sometime in 2021**: Vasthi Trading Pvt Ltd files a petition under **Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)** with the **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) Hyderabad Bench** against Farmax India Ltd.

## 2022:
- **July 1, 2022**: NCLT Hyderabad Bench admits Vasthi Trading's petition. The court declares a **moratorium**, prohibiting legal actions against Farmax India Ltd and suspending debt recovery efforts. Mr. **Chinna Gurappa** is appointed as the **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**.
- **September 14, 2022**: Two **IBBI registered valuers** are appointed to assess the fair value and liquidation value of Farmax India Ltd's assets, including buildings and securities.
- **September 30, 2022**: The first **Expression of Interest (EOI)** is published in **Nava Telangana** and **Financial Express**, inviting prospective resolution applicants. No applications are received.
- **November 2, 2022**: During the **3rd meeting of the Committee of Creditors (CoC)**, it is decided to publish a revised EOI.
- **November 6, 2022**: A revised EOI is published.
- **December 1, 2022**: A provisional list of prospective resolution applicants is announced. Only one applicant, **Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy**, expresses interest and fulfills the EOI requirements, including the **EMD payment**.

## 2023:
- **January 3, 2023**: The CoC is reconstituted after accepting the claim from **Reliance Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**, the sole financial creditor.
- **January 4, 2023**: A memo is filed with the NCLT regarding the CoC reconstitution.
- **February 24, 2023**: Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy submits a **revised resolution plan** after consultations with the sole financial creditor.
- **March 3, 2023**: The revised resolution plan is presented to the CoC during its **5th meeting**.
- **March 6, 2023**: Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy agrees to increase the resolution amount, as requested by the sole CoC member.
- **March 9, 2023**: The CoC approves the resolution plan with a **100% voting share**.
- **June 22, 2023**: NCLT Hyderabad Bench approves the **Resolution Plan** submitted by Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy. The moratorium is lifted, and the resolution plan becomes binding on all stakeholders. Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy is responsible for implementing the plan, overseen by a **monitoring committee**.

## Glossary of Key Terms

- **IBC, 2016**: The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 is the primary legislation governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings in India.

- **Corporate Debtor**: The company facing financial distress and undergoing the CIRP.

- **Operational Creditor**: A creditor who has provided goods or services to the Corporate Debtor in the ordinary course of business.

- **CIRP**: The Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process is a time-bound process aimed at reviving a financially distressed company or resolving its affairs through liquidation.

- **Resolution Professional**: An insolvency professional appointed to manage the CIRP, acting as an intermediary between the Corporate Debtor and the creditors.

- **CoC**: The Committee of Creditors comprises financial creditors who have decision-making power over the CIRP, including approval of the Resolution Plan.

- **Resolution Plan**: A plan proposed by a resolution applicant outlining the measures to revive the Corporate Debtor or to realize its assets for distribution to creditors.

- **NCLT**: The National Company Law Tribunal is the adjudicating authority for matters related to the IBC, 2016.

- **Moratorium**: A legal protection granted to the Corporate Debtor during the CIRP, prohibiting legal actions against it and allowing for a stable resolution process.

- **Liquidation**: A process where the assets of the Corporate Debtor are sold to repay creditors in a specific order of priority when revival is not feasible.

# Farmax India Ltd. Insolvency FAQ

### What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Farmax India Ltd.?
Vasthi Trading Pvt Ltd., an operational creditor, filed a petition under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016 against Farmax India Ltd. for a defaulted payment of **Rs. 1,05,23,214**. This petition was admitted by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Hyderabad Bench-1, on **July 1, 2022**, initiating the CIRP against Farmax India Ltd.

### Who is the Resolution Professional (RP) appointed for the case?
**Mr. Chinna Gurappa** was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional and later confirmed as the Resolution Professional for Farmax India Ltd.'s insolvency process.

### Who are the creditors involved in the CIRP?
The primary creditor in this case is **Reliance Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**, holding **100%** of the voting share in the Committee of Creditors (CoC). Other creditors include operational creditors and government entities with claims for dues.

### What was the fair value and liquidation value of Farmax India Ltd.?
Independent valuers assessed the **fair value** of Farmax India Ltd. at **Rs. 42,65,865**, while its **liquidation value** was determined to be **Rs. 36,62,704**. The liquidation value represents **85.86%** of the fair market value.

### Was a resolution plan submitted and approved?
Yes, a resolution plan submitted by **Mr. Reddy Srinivasa Reddy** was approved by the CoC with a **100%** voting share on **March 9, 2023**. The resolution plan outlines the repayment structure for various creditors and the future roadmap for Farmax India Ltd.

### What is the resolution amount, and how will it be distributed among creditors?
The resolution amount is **Rs. 55,00,000**. A significant portion of the resolution amount (**74.27%**) will go to the secured financial creditor, **Reliance Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**, representing **17.50%** of their admitted claim. Operational creditors and government entities will receive a nominal **0.31%** of their claims. Additionally, **Rs. 12 lakhs** will be allocated for CIRP costs.

### What happens after the NCLT approves the resolution plan?
Upon the NCLT's approval, the resolution plan becomes binding on all stakeholders. A **monitoring committee** will oversee the plan's implementation. The moratorium imposed earlier will be lifted, and Farmax India Ltd. will operate under the terms of the approved resolution plan.

### What is the role of the NCLT in approving the resolution plan?
The NCLT's role is to ensure the resolution plan complies with the IBC and relevant regulations. The NCLT scrutinizes the plan to confirm it meets the requirements of **Section 30(2)** of the Code, particularly regarding the order of priority payments and compliance with relevant regulations. However, the NCLT's review is limited, and it cannot modify a resolution plan approved by the CoC.
