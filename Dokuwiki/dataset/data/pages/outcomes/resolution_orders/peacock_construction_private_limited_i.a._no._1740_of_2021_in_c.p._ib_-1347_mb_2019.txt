Summary

These two legal documents describe a case in the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in Mumbai, India, concerning Peacock Construction Private Limited (PCPL), a company facing financial difficulties. The first document outlines the approval of a Resolution Plan submitted by LA Kozy Builders LLP (RA), a prospective buyer of PCPL, to resolve the company's insolvency. The second document details the initial filing of a Company Petition by Good Value Financial Services Private Limited, a financial creditor of PCPL, which ultimately led to the commencement of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against PCPL. The Resolution Plan aims to revive PCPL by proposing payments to creditors and a specific investment plan, while the CIRP is a formal process to address the company's insolvency and potentially find a resolution.

### Briefing Document: Peacock Construction Private Limited Insolvency Case

**Case:** Good Value Financial Services Private Limited vs. Peacock Construction Private Limited

**Court:** National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench

**Relevant Legislation:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)

---

### Summary:
This briefing document reviews two key orders in the insolvency case of Peacock Construction Private Limited (Corporate Debtor). The first order (dated 31.12.2019) details the admission of the insolvency petition filed by Good Value Financial Services Private Limited (Financial Creditor), while the second order (dated 09.06.2023) approves the resolution plan submitted by LA Kozy Builders LLP (Resolution Applicant).

---

### Key Themes and Ideas:

1. **Nature of Debt and Default:**
   - The Financial Creditor claimed a debt of ₹5,35,00,000/- along with interest at 18% compounding quarterly, totaling ₹28,62,55,961/- as of the date of default (13.04.2018).
   - The Corporate Debtor contested the interest calculation and claimed the amount was an advance for acquiring floor space index, not a loan.
   - The Tribunal acknowledged the principal amount as a financial debt, leaving the interest calculation for the Resolution Professional to determine.
   - **Quote:** *"Therefore, under the fitness of circumstances we hereby hold that the principal amount in question is the Financial Debt, however, the question of quantum of interest is to be verified and ascertained by the Resolution Professional on the basis of the verification of the accounts of both the parties."* (Order dated 31.12.2019)

2. **Initiation of CIRP:**
   - Finding the debt and default established, the Tribunal admitted the petition and initiated the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against the Corporate Debtor.
   - The order imposed a moratorium on legal proceedings against the Corporate Debtor and appointed Mr. Arun Kapoor as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

3. **Resolution Plan Highlights:**
   - The approved Resolution Plan involved a total investment of ₹35,29,520/- by the Resolution Applicant.
   - The Fair Value of the Corporate Debtor was estimated at ₹70.19 Lakhs, while the Liquidation Value was ₹7.18 Lakhs, and the admitted claim value was ₹7,12,64,982/-.
   - The plan prioritized payment of CIRP costs in full (₹28,16,871/-) followed by a 1% payout on admitted claims to Financial Creditors, related party creditors, and any future claims from Operational Creditors, Statutory authorities, or Workmen and Employees.
   - A Monitoring Committee was established for plan implementation and supervision.
   - **Quote:** *"Regarding payment to all creditors, the SRA will pay 1% of the admitted claim amount over and above the CIRP costs."* (Order dated 09.06.2023)

---

### Important Facts:
- The CIRP faced delays due to the nationwide lockdown imposed in response to the pandemic.
- Four Prospective Resolution Applicants submitted Expressions of Interest.
- No claims were received from Secured Financial Creditors or Operational Creditors.
- The Resolution Plan included a provision for extinguishing claims not included in the plan, as per the Supreme Court judgment in *Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited*.

---

### Conclusion:
This case demonstrates the application of the IBC in resolving corporate insolvency. The Tribunal's orders showcase the process of admitting a petition, appointing an IRP, evaluating a resolution plan, and ultimately approving a plan that balances creditor recovery with the revival of the Corporate Debtor. The 1% payout to creditors highlights the challenges of maximizing value in situations where the liquidation value is significantly lower than the admitted claims.

### Glossary of Key Terms

- **CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process):** A time-bound process under the IBC to revive a financially distressed company or to ensure its orderly liquidation.
  
- **NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal):** A quasi-judicial body in India adjudicating matters relating to companies and insolvency.
  
- **IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016):** The primary legislation governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings in India.
  
- **Financial Creditor:** An entity that has provided financial debt to the Corporate Debtor.
  
- **Operational Creditor:** An entity that has provided goods or services to the Corporate Debtor.
  
- **Resolution Applicant:** An entity that submits a resolution plan to the NCLT, proposing a restructuring or revival strategy for the Corporate Debtor.
  
- **Resolution Professional:** A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to oversee the CIRP process.
  
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A body comprising financial creditors that votes on the approval of the resolution plan.
  
- **Moratorium:** A temporary standstill period imposed by the NCLT that restricts legal actions against the Corporate Debtor.
  
- **Liquidation Value:** The estimated value of the Corporate Debtor's assets if sold in a liquidation scenario.
  
- **Fair Value:** The estimated intrinsic value of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern.
  
- **Section 32A of the IBC:** A provision granting immunity to the Resolution Applicant from offenses committed by the Corporate Debtor before the commencement of the CIRP.

### Peacock Construction Private Limited FAQ

**1. What is the purpose of the order issued on June 9, 2023, by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai?**  
The order approves the Resolution Plan submitted by LA Kozy Builders LLP (the Resolution Applicant) for the revival of Peacock Construction Private Limited (the Corporate Debtor) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The plan was previously approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) with a 75.35% majority vote.

---

**2. What is the financial status of Peacock Construction Private Limited?**  
The Corporate Debtor's Fair Value is estimated at Rs. 70.19 Lakhs, while the Liquidation Value is Rs. 7.18 Lakhs. The admitted claims against the company amount to Rs. 7.12 Crores, which exceeds the liquidation value. Currently, Peacock Construction has no liquid assets.

---

**3. What does the Resolution Plan propose for the payment of creditors?**  
The Resolution Plan proposes a total investment of Rs. 35,29,520 by the Resolution Applicant. This includes full payment of the CIRP costs (Rs. 28,16,871), 1% of the admitted claim amount to unsecured financial creditors (Rs. 7,09,999), and 1% to the related party unsecured financial creditor (Rs. 2,650). Dissenting financial creditors will receive payment as per Section 30(2) read with Section 53(1) of the Code.

---

**4. How will the Resolution Plan address potential claims from Operational Creditors, Statutory dues, and Workmen/Employee dues?**  
While no claims were received from these categories during the CIRP period, the Resolution Plan outlines that any admitted claims after the plan's approval will be paid 1% of the admitted amount over and above the CIRP costs.

---

**5. What is the timeline for the implementation of the Resolution Plan?**  
The Resolution Plan states that all proposed payments will be made within 30 days from the date of the NCLT's approval.

---

**6. What role will the Monitoring Committee play in the implementation of the Resolution Plan?**  
A Monitoring Committee, consisting of nominees from the Resolution Applicant, the CoC, and the Resolution Professional, will oversee and supervise the implementation of the Resolution Plan. This committee will function with similar powers and responsibilities as the Resolution Professional under the Code.

---

**7. What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Peacock Construction Private Limited?**  
The CIRP was initiated based on a petition filed by Good Value Financial Services Private Limited, a financial creditor, under Section 7 of the IBC, 2016. The creditor alleged a default in loan repayment exceeding Rs. 1 lakh, which the NCLT confirmed in its order dated December 31, 2019.

---

**8. What was the dispute regarding the debt owed by Peacock Construction?**  
The Corporate Debtor argued that the amount of Rs. 5,35,00,000 received from Good Value Financial Services was not a loan but an advance for acquiring floor space index. They also contested the calculation of interest and claimed that the creditor's claim was barred by the law of limitations. However, the NCLT acknowledged the principal amount as a financial debt and appointed an Interim Resolution Professional to verify the interest calculation.
