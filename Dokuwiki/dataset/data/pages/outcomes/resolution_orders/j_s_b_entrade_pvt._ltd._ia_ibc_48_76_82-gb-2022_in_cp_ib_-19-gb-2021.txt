### Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: A Case Study Review

#### Short Answer Questions

1. **What are the key facts presented in the case of Mukut Chandra Deka v. J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd.?**
   - The case involves Mr. Mukut Chandra Deka (Financial Creditor) filing a petition under Section 7 of the IBC against JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) for failing to repay a debt of **Rs. 1,69,75,000**. The Corporate Debtor admitted to facing financial difficulties due to industry stagnation and the impact of the **COVID-19 pandemic**.

2. **What is the role of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) in insolvency proceedings under the IBC?**
   - The NCLT serves as the Adjudicating Authority for insolvency cases under the IBC. It examines petitions, admits cases into **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**, appoints **Interim Resolution Professionals (IRPs)**, approves resolution plans, and can order liquidation as a last resort.

3. **Explain the concept of 'moratorium' and its significance in the CIRP process.**
   - A moratorium is a legal stay that comes into effect upon the commencement of CIRP. It prohibits legal actions against the Corporate Debtor, asset transfers, and debt recovery attempts, creating a breathing space for the company to restructure and revive.

4. **What are the primary responsibilities of the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) in the initial stages of CIRP?**
   - The IRP's primary responsibilities include:
     - Taking control of the Corporate Debtor's management.
     - Verifying claims.
     - Collecting financial information.
     - Preparing an information memorandum.
     - Inviting prospective resolution applicants.
     - Managing the day-to-day affairs of the company.

5. **What are the key differences between 'Fair Value' and 'Liquidation Value' as mentioned in the case?**
   - **Fair Value** represents the estimated market value of the Corporate Debtor's assets as a going concern, while **Liquidation Value** refers to the estimated value that could be realized if the assets were sold piecemeal in a liquidation scenario.

6. **What were the main reasons cited by JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd. for its inability to repay its debts?**
   - JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd. attributed its inability to repay debts to financial difficulties stemming from a stagnation in the edible oil sector, exacerbated by the global economic slowdown due to the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent lockdowns.

7. **Describe the role of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in the CIRP process.**
   - The CoC comprises the financial creditors of the Corporate Debtor. It plays a crucial role by deciding on the approval of the resolution plan, the appointment of the Resolution Professional, and overseeing the CIRP process. It holds the majority voting power in key decisions.

8. **What were the concerns raised by the NCLT regarding the initial resolution plan submitted by NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited?**
   - The NCLT expressed concerns that the initial resolution plan did not adequately address the transfer of land owned by the Corporate Debtor, which was crucial for its operations. It directed the CoC to renegotiate terms to ensure the plan's feasibility and avoid liquidation.

9. **How did the CoC and the Resolution Applicant address the NCLT's concerns regarding the land transfer issue?**
   - The CoC and the Resolution Applicant addressed the land transfer concern by agreeing to transfer the land through the **SARFAESI Act** at a later stage. The resolution plan was approved with the understanding that the land would be leased to the Corporate Debtor for continued operations, ensuring a smooth transition and plan implementation.

10. **What is the significance of Section 66 of the IBC, and how was it relevant to the case?**
    - Section 66 of the IBC deals with fraudulent trading. In the case, the Resolution Professional filed an application under Section 66 alleging that the Corporate Debtor's management engaged in fraudulent activities to defraud creditors. This application, though pending, adds another layer of complexity to the case.

### Glossary of Key Terms

- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)**: Comprehensive legislation in India governing insolvency resolution and bankruptcy proceedings for companies, partnerships, and individuals.
  
- **Corporate Debtor (CD)**: A company facing financial distress and unable to repay its debts.
  
- **Financial Creditor (FC)**: An entity that has provided financial assistance to the CD, typically banks or financial institutions.
  
- **Operational Creditor (OC)**: An entity that has provided goods or services to the CD and is owed payment.
  
- **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**: A time-bound process under the IBC aimed at reviving a financially distressed company.
  
- **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)**: The Adjudicating Authority responsible for overseeing insolvency proceedings under the IBC.
  
- **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**: A licensed professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CD's affairs during the initial stages of CIRP.
  
- **Resolution Professional (RP)**: A licensed professional appointed by the Committee of Creditors to manage the CD's affairs and facilitate the resolution process.
  
- **Committee of Creditors (CoC)**: A committee consisting of the financial creditors of the CD, responsible for making key decisions during CIRP.
  
- **Resolution Plan**: A plan proposed by a potential investor or existing management to restructure the CD's debts and operations to revive the company.
  
- **Liquidation Value**: The estimated value of the CD's assets if they were sold piecemeal in a liquidation scenario.
  
- **Fair Value**: The estimated market value of the CD's assets as a going concern.
  
- **Moratorium**: A legal stay that comes into effect upon the commencement of CIRP, preventing legal actions against the CD and providing breathing space for restructuring.
  
- **Section 66 of the IBC**: Deals with fraudulent trading, allowing the RP to take action against those who have defrauded the CD or its creditors.
  
- **SARFAESI Act**: The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002, a law that allows secured creditors to recover debts by enforcing security interests without court intervention.

### Briefing Document: Case of Mukut Chandra Deka Vs. JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd.

**Case**: CP. (IB) No. 19/GB/2021 before the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Guwahati Bench

---

#### Subject: Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd.

---

### Main Themes

1. **Default on Financial Debt**:
   - The case revolves around **JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd.** (Corporate Debtor) defaulting on a financial debt of **Rs. 1,69,75,000.00** owed to **Mukut Chandra Deka** (Financial Creditor).

2. **Initiation of CIRP**:
   - The Financial Creditor filed a petition under **Section 7** of the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)** seeking initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Debtor.

3. **Appointment of Resolution Professional**:
   - The NCLT admitted the petition, declared a **moratorium**, and appointed an **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**, later followed by a **Resolution Professional (RP)** to oversee the CIRP.

4. **Resolution Plan Approval**:
   - The RP facilitated the submission of a **Resolution Plan** by **NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited** (Resolution Applicant). The plan was approved by the **Committee of Creditors (CoC)** and subsequently ratified by the NCLT.

---

### Important Ideas and Facts

#### Debt Details:
- The Corporate Debtor took a loan of **Rs. 1,40,00,000.00** from the Financial Creditor.
- The loan agreement stipulated an **interest rate of 1.25% per month**.
- The repayment was due on or before **31.07.2021**.
- The Corporate Debtor failed to make any repayments, leading to the default.

#### CIRP Process:
- The IRP made a **public announcement** inviting claims from creditors.
- A **CoC** was constituted, consisting solely of **Punjab National Bank** as the secured financial creditor. Mr. Deka, despite being a financial creditor, was a related party and thus excluded from voting rights.
- Two **valuation reports** were obtained to determine the **fair value** and **liquidation value** of the Corporate Debtor.
- An **Information Memorandum** and **Request for Resolution Plan** were prepared and circulated to potential resolution applicants.
- Only one resolution plan was received, from NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited.

#### Resolution Plan:
- The plan involved a resolution amount of **Rs. 8.40 crores**, exceeding the liquidation value of **Rs. 8,16,90,936.11**.
- The plan addressed the concerns raised by the NCLT regarding:
  - Transfer of land.
  - Transactions under **Sections 43, 45, 50, and 66** of the IBC.
  - Guarantees, subsidy receivable, and members of the monitoring committee.
- It was agreed that the land would be transferred through the **SARFAESI Act** at a later date.
- The CoC retained the right to pursue personal guarantees independently.

---

### Key Quotes
- **Regarding the admission of the petition**: "Considering the materials made available by the Applicant, arguments of both parties and the facts mentioned hereinabove, this Adjudicating Authority is satisfied that... the present IB Petition is admitted..." (Order dated **10.12.2021**)

- **On the objective of the IBC**: "One of the prime objectives of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 is to quickly find out a viable Insolvency Resolution Plan for the Corporate Debtor..." (Order dated **10.12.2021**)

- **Regarding the approval of the resolution plan**: "On perusal of the Resolution Plan, we are of the considered view that the Resolution Plan meets the requirement of **Section 31** r/w **Section 30(2)** of the Code. Therefore, the present application IA (IBC) No. 82/GB/2022 is allowed and the Resolution Plan is approved..." (Order dated **20.01.2023**)

---

### Pending Issues
- An application under **Section 66** of the IBC, alleging fraudulent transactions by the Corporate Debtor, is pending before the NCLT.

---

### Conclusion
The NCLT's approval of the Resolution Plan marks a successful outcome of the CIRP for JSB Entrade Pvt. Ltd. The plan is expected to revive the company as a going concern under the new ownership of **NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited**. However, the pending application under Section 66 of the IBC may have further implications for the stakeholders involved.

### J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd. Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process FAQ

---

**What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd.?**  
Mr. Mukut Chandra Deka, a financial creditor (FC), filed a petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, against J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd. (the Corporate Debtor or CD) for failing to repay a financial debt of **Rs. 1,69,75,000.00**. The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Guwahati Bench, admitted the petition and initiated the CIRP.

---

**What happened during the CIRP?**  
An Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) was appointed to manage the CD's affairs during the CIRP. The IRP verified claims, constituted a Committee of Creditors (CoC), and invited prospective resolution applicants to submit their plans.

---

**Who submitted a resolution plan and what did it entail?**  
**NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited**, the sole resolution applicant (RA), submitted a plan that was ultimately approved by the CoC. The plan offered a resolution amount of **Rs. 8.40 crores**, exceeding the liquidation value of the CD, which was determined to be **Rs. 8,16,90,936.11**.

---

**What were the key challenges in approving the resolution plan?**  
There were a few challenges, including:

- **Transfer of land**: Initial discussions focused on transferring land owned by the CD to the RA. However, both parties agreed to defer the transfer and proceed with the resolution plan, allowing the RA to lease the land for continued operations.
  
- **Pending transactions under various sections of the IBC**: The resolution professional filed an application under Section 66 of the IBC, alleging fraudulent transactions by the CD. This application was pending adjudication and required further consideration.

---

**Did the NCLT approve the resolution plan?**  
Yes, the NCLT, Guwahati Bench, approved the resolution plan submitted by NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited, considering it met the requirements of the IBC.

---

**What were the conditions for approving the resolution plan?**  
The NCLT imposed several conditions, including:

- No automatic waiver of legal proceedings against the CD.
- Continuation of personal guarantees provided by the CD's stakeholders.
- Formation of a Monitoring Committee, chaired by the resolution professional, to oversee the plan's implementation.

---

**What happened to the pending application under Section 66 of the IBC?**  
The NCLT decided to hear and dispose of the application separately, meaning the RA's acquisition of the CD wouldn't be hindered by the pending litigation.

---

**What is the current status of J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd.?**  
Following the NCLT's approval of the resolution plan, the moratorium on the CD was lifted, and the resolution professional was discharged from their duties. J S B Entrade Pvt. Ltd. is now under the management of NK Power and Infrastructure Private Limited, aiming to revive the company as a going concern.
