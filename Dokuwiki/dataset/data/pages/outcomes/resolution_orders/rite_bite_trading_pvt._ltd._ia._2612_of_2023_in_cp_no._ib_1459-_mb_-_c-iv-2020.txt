===== Briefing Doc: Rite Bite Trading Private Limited Insolvency Case =====
==== Subject: Review of NCLT Mumbai Bench Order on the Approval of Resolution Plan for Rite Bite Trading Private Limited ====

==== Sources: ====
  - Order delivered on 04.07.2023 in IA No. 2612 of 2023 IN CP No. (IB) 1459/ (MB)/ C-IV/ 2020
  - Order Dated: 02.06.2022 in CP (IB) No.1459/MB-IV/2020

==== Summary: ====
This briefing doc analyzes the key events and decisions related to the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) for Rite Bite Trading Private Limited (Corporate Debtor), initiated by National Steel and Agro Industries Limited (Operational Creditor) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC).

==== Background: ====
  - Rite Bite Trading Private Limited defaulted on payments to National Steel and Agro Industries Limited for agricultural products purchased between May and August 2018.
  - The total outstanding debt amounted to Rs. 78,61,05,760/-.
  - National Steel and Agro Industries Limited filed an application under Section 9 of the IBC to initiate CIRP on 30.12.2020.
  - The NCLT Mumbai Bench admitted the application on 02.06.2022 and appointed Mr. Navin Khandelwal as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP).

==== Key Highlights of the CIRP: ====
  - The IRP invited claims from creditors and received expressions of interest from three prospective resolution applicants.
  - Two resolution plans were submitted and put to vote before the Committee of Creditors (CoC).
  - The CoC approved the resolution plan submitted by Anjali Entrepreneurs Private Limited with 100% votes.
  - The approved resolution plan proposed a payout of Rs. 110 Lakhs over three months from the date of NCLT approval.

==== Important Considerations and Decisions: ====
  - **Treatment of Receivables:** The NCLT initially remitted the plan back to the CoC, seeking clarification on the treatment of the Corporate Debtor's receivables amounting to Rs. 51.83 Crores. The Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) clarified that the receivables were largely considered irrecoverable, with a provision for doubtful debts already made in the financial statements. They agreed that any recovery would be used as working capital for the Corporate Debtor.
  
  - **NCLT Approval:** Based on the SRA's clarification, the NCLT approved the resolution plan on 04.07.2023. The order extinguished all claims not included in the plan, per the Supreme Court's judgment in Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited.

==== Key Quotes: ====
  - **Order dated 02.06.2022:** "The Corporate Debtor has nowhere disputed in terms of the goods received and the invoices raised by the Operational Creditor."
  - **Order dated 04.07.2023:** "Moreover by paying an amount much above than the estimated liquidation value by them of the corporate debtor and providing more than mandatory payments to the creditors, the RA believes that in the amount of surplus above estimated liquidation value by them it has adequately considered the receivables as well."

==== Conclusion: ====
The NCLT Mumbai Bench's approval of the resolution plan marks the successful conclusion of the CIRP for Rite Bite Trading Private Limited. The plan ensures a higher payout to creditors than liquidation and paves the way for the Corporate Debtor's revival under new ownership. The case highlights the IBC's effectiveness in facilitating timely resolution and maximizing value for stakeholders in insolvency situations.

==== 28 August 2022 ====

  * Two Resolution Plans are submitted.

  * The submitted Resolution Plans are reviewed by the CoC.

==== 22-24 November 2022 ====

==== 24 November 2022 ====
  * Anjali Entrepreneurs Private Limited's Resolution Plan is approved with 100% votes, making them the Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA).
  * Anjali Entrepreneurs furnishes the Performance Security.

==== 30 March 2023 ====
  * NCLT remits the plan back to the RP for clarification on the treatment of receivables.

==== 12 April 2023 ====

==== 19 April 2023 ====

==== 04 July 2023 ====
  * The NCLT approves the Resolution Plan of Anjali Entrepreneurs.
  * The CIRP process concludes, and the moratorium is lifted.
  * A Monitoring Committee is established to oversee the implementation of the resolution plan.

===== Key Term Glossary ======

  * **CIRP:** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process. A time-bound process under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor.

  * **NCLT:** National Company Law Tribunal. The adjudicating authority that decides on matters relating to corporate insolvency resolution and liquidation.

  * **Operational Creditor:** A person or entity that has provided goods or services to the corporate debtor and has not received payment for the same.

  * **Financial Creditor:** A person or entity that has provided financial assistance to the corporate debtor, such as banks, financial institutions, and debenture holders.

  * **IRP:** Interim Resolution Professional. A professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the affairs of the corporate debtor during the initial stages of the CIRP.

  * **RP:** Resolution Professional. A professional who takes over the management of the corporate debtor upon admission of the CIRP application and oversees the entire resolution process.

  * **CoC:** Committee of Creditors. A committee comprising financial creditors of the corporate debtor who have a say in the approval or rejection of the resolution plan.

  * **Resolution Plan:** A plan submitted by a resolution applicant outlining the proposed measures for reviving the insolvent company.

  * **Moratorium:** A legal stay that prevents any legal action against the corporate debtor during the CIRP period, providing time for the resolution process.

  * **Liquidation:** The process of winding up the affairs of a corporate debtor when the resolution plan fails or is not approved.

====== National Steel And Agro Industries Limited vs. Rite Bite Trading Private Limited FAQ ======

  * **What is the purpose of the IA No. 2612 of 2023 case?**
    - This application seeks approval for the Resolution Plan submitted by **Anjali Entrepreneurs Private Limited**, the Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA), for the Corporate Debtor, Rite Bite Trading Private Limited, under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

  * **What led to the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**
    - The CIRP was initiated by the **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)** on **June 2, 2022**, due to a petition filed by National Steel and Agro Industries Limited, an Operational Creditor, under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

  * **What was the nature of the debt owed to the Operational Creditor?**
    - Rite Bite Trading Private Limited owed National Steel and Agro Industries Limited a total of **Rs. 78,61,05,760** for the purchase of agricultural products, specifically yellow peas, which were delivered and accepted without dispute.

  * **Were there any objections to the Resolution Plan?**
    - No objections were filed against the Resolution Plan, and no applications relating to avoidance transactions were pending.

  * **What is the key issue addressed in the NCLT order dated July 4, 2023?**
    - The NCLT addressed the treatment of outstanding receivables amounting to **Rs. 51.83 Crores** in the Corporate Debtor's books. The SRA clarified that a provision for doubtful debts of **Rs. 51.64 Crores** existed against these receivables, and any recovered amounts would be used as working capital for the Corporate Debtor.

  * **What happens to the claims that are not part of the approved Resolution Plan?**
    - According to the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of **Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited**, all claims not included in the approved Resolution Plan are extinguished, and no further proceedings can be initiated or continued for such claims.

  * **What is the role of the Monitoring Committee?**
    - The Monitoring Committee, composed of members from the Financial Creditor, the Resolution Professional, and the proposed Board of Directors, is responsible for overseeing the implementation of the Resolution Plan.

  * **What are the implications of the moratorium ceasing to have effect?**
    - With the moratorium lifted, actions such as legal proceedings, asset transfers, and security enforcement against the Corporate Debtor can resume. However, the supply of essential goods and services to the Corporate Debtor must continue uninterrupted.
