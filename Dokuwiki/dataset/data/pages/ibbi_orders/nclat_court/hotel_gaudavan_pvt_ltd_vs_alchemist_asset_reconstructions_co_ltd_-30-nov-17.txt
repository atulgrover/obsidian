	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		SUDHANSU JYOTI MUKHOPADHAYA, J.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		All these appeals arise out of common 'Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process' initiated against M/s. Hotel Gaudavan Private Limited &amp; Ors ('Corporate Debtor').
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		2. The brief facts of the case are that the Respondents- M/s. Alchemist Asset Reconstruction Company Limited &amp; Anr. filed an application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as "I&amp;B Code") against M/s. Hotel Gaudavan Private Limited &amp; Ors ('Corporate Debtor').
	</p>
	<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">
		3. On 31st March, 2017, the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal), Special Bench, New Delhi admitted the application, passed order of moratorium and appointed an 'Interim Resolution Professional' with certain directions. Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 3
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		4. It appears that the 'Corporate Debtor' thereafter, along with another shareholder filed a Writ Petition before the Hon'ble High Court of Rajasthan, challenging the order passed by the Adjudicating Authority. However, the Hon'ble High Court refused to look into the merits of the order dated 31st March, 2017 and left it open to be examined by this Appellate Tribunal.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		5. Thereafter, the 'Corporate Debtor' along with another shareholder moved before the Hon'ble Supme Court in SLP(C) No.12606-12707 of 2017 against different orders passed by Adjudicating Authority which were also dismissed on 26th April, 2017. The 'Corporate Debtor' and Another thereafter pferred appeal before this Appellate Tribunal on 2nd May, 2017, which was subsequently withdrawn on 17th July, 2017.
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		6. The 'Corporate Debtor' thereafter moved before the Arbitral Tribunal and against such action the 'Insolvency Resolution Professional' moved before the Adjudicating Authority which decided the matter against the 'Corporate Debtor' on 31st May, 2017.
	</p>
	<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">
		7. In the meantime, as the Board of Directors refused to comply with the order of the Adjudicating Authority, the 'Interim Resolution Professional' filed Contempt Petition (CA No. 183(PB)/2017) before the Adjudicating Authority against the Directors, in which an adverse order Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 4 was passed on 29th June, 2017 by the Adjudicating Authority against the Directors.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		8. As noticed above, the 'Corporate Debtor' had filed an application under Section 8 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 wherein certain orders were passed against which the Appellant(s) pferred the appeal before the District Judge, Jaisalmer, who admitted the appeal, issued notice to the Respondents and passed interim orders. Against the said order, the 'Financial Creditor' moved before the Hon'ble Supme Court in Civil Appeal No. 16929 of 2017 (arising out of S.L.P. (C) No. 18195/2017) wherein following order was passed: -
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		"ORDER
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		1) Leave granted.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		2) Heard the learned Senior Counsel/Counsel appearing for the parties.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		3) The facts of the psent case disclose a very sorry state of affairs. Several proceedings had been taken and ultimately a petition filed under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 was admitted on 31.03.2017 by the National Company Law Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi. As a result, the moratorium that is imposed by Section 14 came into effect Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 5 on that date and Respondent No.3 has been appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP). A Writ Petition was filed against this order, which was admitted only to the extent of the challenge to the vires of the Insolvency Code, is pending. A Special Leave Petition against this order was dismissed on 26.04.2017. Meanwhile, despite the moratorium, a letter was issued by Respondent No. 1 to Respondent No. 2 invoking the arbitration clause between the parties and Shri Pankaj Garg, an Advocate, was appointed as Sole Arbitrator. Shri Garg entered upon the reference. In an other order dated 31.05.2017, the National Company Law Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi referred to Section 14 (1) (a) of the Insolvency Code and stated that given the moratorium that is imposed, no arbitration proceedings could go on. A notice was issued on 29.06.2017 by the National Company Law Tribunal, Principal Bench, New Delhi in C.A. No. 186 (PB) of 2017.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		4) A First Appeal was filed before the District Judge, Jaisalmer, Rajasthan under Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 6 Section 37 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and by the impugned order dated 06.07.2017, the appeal was asked to be registered and notice was issued awaiting a reply.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		5) The mandate of the new Insolvency Code is that the moment an insolvency petition is admitted, the moratorium that comes into effect under Section 14 (1) (a) expssly interdicts institution or continuation of pending suits or proceedings against Corporate Debtors.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		6) This being the case, we are surprised that an arbitration proceeding has been purported to be started after the imposition of the said moratorium and appeal under Section 37 of the Arbitration Act are being entertained.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		Therefore, we set aside the order of the District Judge dated 06.07.2017 and further state that the effect of Section 14 (1) (a) is that the arbitration that has been instituted after the aforesaid moratorium is non est in law.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion">
		7) Mr. Jayant Bhushan, learned Senior Counsel, also informs us that criminal proceeding being F.I.R. No. 0605 dated
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 7 06.08.2017 has been taken in a desperate attempt to see that the IRP does not continue with the proceedings under the Insolvency Code which are strictly time bound. We quash this proceeding.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		8) As a result, the appeal is allowed and the steps that have to be taken under the Insolvency Code will continue unimpeded by any order of any other Court."
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		9. The aforesaid fact discloses not only very sorry state of affairs and its legal action on the part of the Director(s) of the 'Corporate Debtor(s)' which has also been noticed by Hon'ble Supme Court in the order aforesaid.
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		10. In this Appellate Tribunal three appeals have been pferred. Two appeals by 'Corporate Debtor'-M/s. Hotel Gaudavan Pvt. Ltd. and another by Mr. Harendra Singh Rathore but against three different orders arising out of same Insolvency Proceedings. All the cases were listed but since the initial date either on the ground of casualty in the family of the counsel for the Appellant(s) and illness of the counsel for the Appellant(s) or any other ground, the Appellant(s) sought for adjournments since August, 2017, which were allowed.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		Company Appeals (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 134, 163 &amp; 166 of 2017 8

	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		11. On 8th November, 2017, when all the three appeals were taken up for hearing, nobody appeared for the Appellant(s). Learned counsel brought to the notice of this Appellate Tribunal the order passed by the Hon'ble Supme Court, as recorded above, which is final.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		12. In the facts and circumstances, we have no other option but to dismiss all the three appeals with cost of Rs. 25,000/- imposed on each Appellant(s) namely, Mr. Harendra Singh Rathore, Mr. Lokendra Singh Rathore, Mr. Deependra Singh Rathore and Ms. Mohan Kanwar, to be paid by Bank Draft in favour of the Registrar, National Company Law Appellate Tribunal within thirty days from the receipt of this order.
