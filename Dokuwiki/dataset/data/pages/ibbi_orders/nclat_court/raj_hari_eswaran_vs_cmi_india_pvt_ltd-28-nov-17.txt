	<h2>
		National Company Law Appellate Tribunal
	</h2>
	<h2>
		Raj Hari Eswaran vs M/S. Cmi India Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr on 28 November, 2017
	</h2>
	<p>
		1 NATIONAL COMPANY LAW APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, NEW DELHI Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017IN THE MATTER OF:Raj Hari Eswaran ...AppellantVs.M/s. CMI India Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr. ...RespondentsPresent: For Appellant: - Mr. Amit Sibal, Senior Advocate with Mr. Aditya Verma, Mr. Aditya Gupta, Mr. Avinash Amarnath, Mr. Vinay Tripathi and Mr. Ambar Doshan, Advocates. For Respondents:- Tasneem Ahmadi, Mr. Sudhir Kumar Gupta, Mr. R.k.Gupta, Ms. Shubhi Khare and Mr. Abhinav Gupta, Advocates. ORDER
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		28.11.2017- The Appellant, who is a Shareholder and the promoter of 2nd Respondent ('Corporate Debtor') has challenged the order dated 12th October, 2017 passed by the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal), Chennai, whereby and whereunder the application pferred by the 1st Respondent- M/s. CMI India Pvt. Ltd. ('Operational Creditor') under Sections 433 and 434 of the Companies Act, 1956 has been treated to be an application under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as "I&amp;B Code") read with Rule 6 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as "Adjudicating Authority Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 2 Rules, 2016"), the application has been admitted, order of Moratorium has been passed and name of 'Interim Resolution Professional' has been called for from the 'Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India' (hereinafter referred to as "Board") with further prohibitory orders in terms of 'I&amp;B Code'.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		2. The main plea taken by the learned counsel for the Appellant/shareholder that the 1st Respondent has not complied with the provisions of the 'I&amp;B Code' before treating the application under Section
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		9. No notice under sub-section (1) of Section 8 was issued in Form-3 or 4 and the application has been admitted though there is an existence of dispute.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		3. The brief facts of the case are that the 2nd Respondent-'Corporate Debtor' placed various purchase orders during the years 2012-13 and 2013-14 with the 1st Respondent 'Operational Creditor' for the supply of cables. The 1st Respondent was then known as 'General Cable Energy (India) Ltd'. The Appellant is a Shareholder and Managing Director of the 'Corporate Debtor'. According to the Appellant, as per the purchase orders the 1st Respondent 'Operational Creditor' was required to provide a warranty for 36 months, and that during 2015, the 'Corporate Debtor' came to know that it was not going to be possible for the Respondent 'Operational Creditor' to provide the warranty. According to Appellant, the 'Corporate Debtor' clearly indicated the 1st respondent that in case Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 3 warranty was not possible, so the 1st Respondent 'Operational Creditor' was free to take away the cables. However, in spite of that the 1st Respondent 'Operational Creditor' issued a legal notice on 15th September, 2016 through a lawyer calling upon the 'Corporate Debtor' to pay the outstanding sum of Rs. 7,88,54,073/-, to which the 'Corporate Debtor' replied by letter dated 17th October, 2016 denying any liability. Thereafter, the 1st Respondent 'Operational Creditor' filed a Company Petition under Sections 433 &amp; 434 of the Companies Act, 1956 before the Hon'ble High Court of Madras bearing S.R No. 46897 of 2016 claiming a sum of Rs. 7,88,54,073/- (plus interest) from the 'Corporate Debtor'.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		4. After constitution of the Tribunal, pursuant to the Notification No. G.S.R. 1119(E) dated 7th December, 2016, issued by Central Government under sub-section (1) and (2) of Section 434 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with sub-section (1) of Section 239 of the 'I&amp;B Code', the case was transferred to Adjudicating Authority, Chennai Bench and re-numbered as TCP/4/(IB)/CB/2017.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		5. On notice, the 'Corporate Debtor' appeared on 13th June, 2017 and disputed the liability. On 31st July, 2017, the 'Corporate Debtor' filed reply. The transferred application was treated to be an application under Section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code' and was admitted by impugned order dated 12th October, 2017 giving rise to the psent appeal.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 4
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		6. Learned counsel for the Appellant submitted that no notice under sub-section (1) of Section 8 was issued in Form-3 or 4 prior to treating the application as under Section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code' or before the admission of the application. It is also submitted that the application was also not filed in Form 5, as required under Section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code' read with Rule 6 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 as per which records of dues and default were required to be provided.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		7. The aforesaid stand taken by the Appellant has not been disputed by the 1st Respondent ('Operational Creditor').
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		8. Learned counsel for the Appellant has brought to our notice the Central Government's notification dated 7th December, 2016 issued from the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. By the said notification, in exercise of the powers conferred under sub-sections (1) and (2) of Section 434 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with sub-section (1) of Section 239 of the 'I&amp;B Code', the Central Government framed "The Companies (Transfer of Pending Proceedings) Rules, 2016".
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		9. Rule 5 relates to transfer of pending proceedings of winding up on the ground of inability to pay debts which are to be transferred from the Hon'ble High Court's to the respective Tribunal and reads as follows: -
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 5 "5. Transfer of pending proceedings of Winding up on the ground of inability to pay debts.- (1) All petitions relating to winding up under clause (e) of section 433 of the Act on the ground of inability to pay its debts pending before a High Court, and where the petition has not been served on the respondent as required under rule 26 of the Companies (Court) Rules, 1959 shall be transferred to the Bench of the Tribunal established under sub-section (4) of section 419 of the Act, exercising territorial jurisdiction and such petitions shall be treated as applications under sections 7, 8 or 9 of the Code, as the case may be, and dealt with in accordance with Part II of the Code:
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		Provided that the petitioner shall submit all information, other than information forming part of the records transferred in accordance with Rule 7, required for admission of the petition under sections 7, 8 or 9 of the Code, as the case may be, including details of the proposed insolvency professional to the Tribunal within Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 6 sixty days from date of this notification, failing which the petition shall abate.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		2. All cases where opinion has been forwarded by Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction, for winding up of a company to a High Court and where no appeal is pending, the proceedings for winding up initiated under the Act, pursuant to section 20 of the Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Act, 1985 shall continue to be dealt with by such High Court in accordance with the provisions of the Act."
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		10. From the aforesaid Rule 5, it is clear after transfer of the case the Applicant (Respondent herein) was required to submit all information, other than in formation forming part of the records transferred from the High Court, for admission of the petition under Sections 7, 8 or 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code', including details of the proposed 'Insolvency Professional' within sixty days, failing which, the petition shall stand abated.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		11. As per Section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code', before admission of application and its filing, a demand notice under sub-section (1) of Section 8 is required to be issued on the 'Corporate Debtor', as quoted below: -
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 7 "8. Insolvency resolution by operational creditor.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		─ (1) An operational creditor may, on the occurrence of a default, deliver a demand notice of unpaid operational debtor copy of an invoice demanding payment of the amount involved in the default to the corporate debtor in such form and manner as may be pscribed."
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		12. It is only on receipt of such notice under sub-section (1) of Section 8 of the 'I&amp; B Code', the 'Corporate Debtor' may either pay the amount or may dispute the claim in terms of sub-section (2) of Section 8 of the 'I&amp;B Code'.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		13. Clause (a) and (b) of sub-rule (1) of Rule 5 of the 'Adjudicating Authority Rules' provides the format in which the demand notice/invoice demanding payment in respect of unpaid 'Operational Debt' is to be issued by 'Operational Creditor'. As per Rule 5(1) (a) &amp; (b), the following person (s) are authorised to act on behalf of operational creditor, as apparent from the last portion of Form-3 and 4 which reads as follows: -
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		"6. The undersigned request you to unconditionally repay the unpaid operational debt (in default) in full within ten days from the receipt of this letter failing which we shall initiate a corporate insolvency resolution process in respect of [name of corporate debtor].
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		Yours sincerely, Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 8 Signature of person authorised to act on behalf of the operational creditor Name in block letters Position with or in relation to the operational creditor Address of person signing "
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		14. Form-5 is the format for filing application under section 9, as per which the following details are to be provided: -
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		"Part I- particulars of applicant Part II- particulars of corporate debtor Part III- particulars of the proposed interim resolution professional (if proposed) Part IV- particulars of operational debt Part V- particulars of operational debt [documents, records and evidence of default]
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		15. As per the instructions, the following documents are required to be attached:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		Annex I Copy of the invoice/demand notice as in Form 3 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 served on the Corporate debtor.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		Annex II Copies of all documents referred to in this application.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 9 Annex III Copy of the relevant accounts from the banks/financial institutions maintaining accounts of the operational creditor confirming that there is no payment of the relevant unpaid operational debt by the operational debtor, if available. Annex IV Affidavit in support of the application in accordance with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016. Annex VI Written communication by the proposed interim resolution professional as set out in Form 2 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016. Annex VI Proof that the specified application fee has been paid."
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		16. Admittedly, no notice was issued under sub-section (1) of Section 8 of the 'I&amp;B Code'. In terms with Rule 5, other informations were also not placed before the Adjudicating Authority.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		17. The 1st Respondent ('Operational Creditor') having failed to provide all the details as required under Form-5 as noticed above, the application under sections 433 and 434 of the Companies Act, 1956 cannot be treated to be an application under section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code' in terms of Rule 5 of Transfer Rules, 2016. For such failure to provide the Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 10 information, in view of proviso to Rule 5 of the Transfer Rules, the application under Sections 433 and 434 of the Companies Act, 1956 filed by 1st Respondent stands abated.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		18. For the reasons aforesaid, while we set aside the impugned order dated 12th October, 2017 passed by the Adjudicating Authority, Chennai in T.C.P No. 4/(IB)/CB/2017, also declare that the application pferred by the 1st Respondent under Sections 433 and 434 of the Companies Act, 1956 stood abated.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		19. However, liberty is given to the 1st Respondent to issue fresh notice under sub-section (1) of Section 8 of the 'I&amp;B Code' and on service of such notice if there is a debt and default or no dispute is raised, it will be open to the 1st Respondent to file fresh application under Section 9 of the 'I&amp;B Code' after ten days of service of such notice. In such case, the Adjudicating Authority may decide the application uninfluenced by the impugned Order dated 12th October, 2017 and the judgment passed by this Appellate Tribunal.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		20. In effect, order (s) passed by the Adjudicating Authority appointing 'Resolution Professional', declaring moratorium, freezing of account and all other order (s) passed by the Adjudicating Authority pursuant to impugned order and action, if any, taken by the 'Resolution Professional', Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 248 of 2017 11 including the advertisement, if any, published in the newspaper calling for applications and all such orders and actions are declared illegal and are set aside. The application pferred by the 1st Respondent is dismissed as abated. Learned Adjudicating Authority will now close the proceeding. The 2nd Respondent company ('Corporate Debtor') is released from all the rigour of law and is allowed to function independently through its Board of Directors from immediate effect.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		21. The Adjudicating Authority will fix the fee of 'Resolution Professional', and the Appellant will pay the fees for the period he has functioned. The appeal is allowed with aforesaid observation and direction. However, in the facts and circumstances of the case, there shall be no order as to cost.
	</p>
