<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Petitioner's Argument">Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 Reasons and Analysis:</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">36. We have heard the learned counsel for the parties, and perused the record.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">37. The controversy lies in a narrow compass. The admitted facts, as noted above, are the following:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning">(i) SML had imported capital goods between October 2005 and November 2011 against EPCG licenses/authorizations issued in its favour by the DGFT. The imports against these licenses/authorizations obliged SML to fulfil stipulated export obligations.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning">(ii) SML, concededly, failed to fulfil its export obligations within the stipulated timeframe, and hence through various communications [to which we have <a href="/doc/186684406/">referred above</a>] sought an extension of time.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning">(iii) That SML was in financial distress is evident, given the fact that a reference was registered under <a href="/doc/239087/">Section 15(1)</a> of SICA on 18.11.2014.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">Clearly, SML's net worth stood eroded on that date.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(iv) With the enforcement of the Code, in particular, Section 252 of the Code coming into force from 01.11.2016, SICA was repealed, which left SML in a state of flux, as its reference lodged with BIFR stood abated in terms of the provisions of the Eighth Schedule of the Code.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(v) SML's woes did not come to an end, as SREI i.e., a financial creditor applied <a href="/doc/1411673/">Section 7</a> of the Code. This triggered the process of forging a Resolution Plan under the Code. The IRP, thereafter, made a Public Announcement on 01.02.2017, calling upon all creditors [which included Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 13 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 operational creditors, such as the respondents before us] to submit their proof of claims.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">(vi) As noticed above, a specific e-mail dated 17.05.2017 was served on respondent no. 3 by the RP, whereby not only was the said respondent informed that CIRP was on, but also that since SML had been issued summons under Section 108 of the 1962 Act, details concerning SML should be furnished to the RP.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">(vii) Admittedly, the DRI/DGFT did not submit their proof of claims. Furthermore, respondent no.3 neither replied nor furnished any information, as required by the RP viathe e-mail dated 17.05.2017.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">38. Given this position, one would have to examine, which side of the Rubicon [i.e., the law enunciated by the Supme Court] SML's case falls on.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">39. The Supme Court in the case of <a href="/doc/30560910/">Ghanashyam Mishra &amp; Sons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd.</a> (2021) 9 SCC 657 was inter alia called upon to rule on the following:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(i) Whether the claims reflected in the Resolution Plan as approved under <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> of the Code would stand frozen, and thus bind all stakeholders?</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(ii) Whether statutory dues, owed to the central government, state government or local authority, which did not form part of the approved Resolution Plan would stand extinguished, and thereby disable the continuation of any proceedings qua claims which concerned the period arising prior to the approval of the Resolution Plan by the adjudicating authority under <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> of the Code?</p>
Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 14 of 23
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">40. Vis-a-vis these issues, the following conclusions were reached by the Supme Court.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"102.1. That once a resolution plan is duly approved by the adjudicating authority under sub-section (1) of <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a>, the claims as provided in the resolution plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on the corporate debtor and its employees, members, creditors, including the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, guarantors and other stakeholders. On the date of approval of [the] resolution plan by the adjudicating authority, all such claims, which are not a part of [the] resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no person will be entitled to initiate or continue any proceedings in respect to a claim, which is not part of the resolution plan. 102.2. The 2019 Amendment to <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> of the I&amp;B Code is clarificatory and declaratory in nature and therefore will be effective from the date on which the I&amp;B Code has come into effect.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">102.3. Consequentlyall the dues including the statutory dues owed to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, if not part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no proceedings in respect of such dues for the period prior to the date on which the adjudicating authority grants its approval under <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> could be continued."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">[Emphasis is ours]</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">41. The position of law, as articulated above, continues to hold the field.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">42. Mr Sharma has, however, placed reliance on the judgement rendered by the Supme Court in Rainbow Papers, and contended that the Resolution Plan should be set aside, on the short ground that it did not classify the Customs department as a secured creditor.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">43. Furthermore, it was stated, that being a liability payable under the 1962 Act, the Customs department would have the first charge on the property of SML, which would be an assessee under the 1962 Act. In this regard, reliance was placed on <a href="/doc/189305526/">Section 142A</a> of the 1962 Act.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">44. It is important to note, that although <a href="/doc/189305526/">Section 142A</a> of the 1962 Act commences with a non-obstante clause, it also adverts to certain enactments Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 15 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 [such as the Code] which may provide otherwise. A plain reading of <a href="/doc/189305526/">Section 142A</a> of the 1962 Act, makes that crystal clear.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">"142A. Liability under Act to be first charge.--Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any Central Act or State Act, any amount of duty, penalty, interest or any other sum payable by an assessee or any other person under this Act, shall, save as otherwise provided in <a href="/doc/1190954/">section 529A</a> of the Companies Act, 1956, the <a href="/doc/1683455/">Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and the Financial Institutions Act, 1993</a>, [the <a href="/doc/52229129/">Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and the Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002</a> and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016], be the first charge on the property of the assessee or the person, as the case may be.] "</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">45. Mr Sharma, in effect, has sought to draw a parity between <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act, which came up for consideration in the context of <a href="/doc/59978968/">Section 53</a> of the Code, and Section 142A of the 1962 Act. Pertinently, <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act does not save provisions of other statutes, in particular the Code, as noticed above. For the sake of convenience, <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act, as extracted in Rainbow Papers is set forth hereafter:-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">"48. Tax to be first charge on property.-- Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any law for the time being in force, any amount payable by a dealer or any other person on account of tax, interest or penalty for which he is liable to pay to the Government shall be a first charge on the property of such dealer, or as the case maybe, such person."</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">46. Thus, the controversy which arose for consideration before the Supme Court was, whether <a href="/doc/59978968/">Section 53</a> of the Code, which provides for a waterfall mechanism for the distribution of proceeds, obtained from the sale of liquidation of assets, would override <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">47. The broad facts, in the background of which this issue was decided by the Supme Court, were as follows-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">47.1 The appellant i.e., the statutory authority claimed that the respondent- company owed dues towards Central Sales Tax (CST) and Value Added Tax Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 16 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 (VAT). Recovery proceedings in that regard were initiated against the respondent company in and about 8.07.2016, concerning 2011-2012.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">47.2 In furtherance of the recovery proceedings, the statutory authority attached the immovable property of the respondent-company on 08.10.2018. In the interregnum, an operational creditor filed a petition under <a href="/doc/79682323/">Section 9</a> of the Code with the NCLT, which was admitted on 12.09.2017.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">47.3 On 22.09.2017, the NCLT appointed an IRP. The IRP invited claims from the creditors, as required under <a href="/doc/75829563/">Section 15</a> of the Code, by taking out a publication to that effect in the newspaper. The last date fixed for submission of claims before the IRP was 05.10.2017.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">47.4 The COC was constituted on 10.10.2017. The COC replaced the IRP with the RP, whose appointment was approved by the NCLT on 06.11.2017.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">47.5 On 22.10.2018, after the last date for submission of claims had expired, the appellant/statutory authority lodged their claim with the RP. Via communication of even date i.e., 22.10.2018, the RP informed the appellant/statutory authority, that its entire claim had been waived, which was also conveyed to the appellant/statutory authority via e-mail dated 06.11.2018.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">47.6 It is this, which led to the appellant/statutory authority first approaching the NCLT, and thereafter the NCLAT. A statutory appeal was also filed, under <a href="/doc/112093872/">Section 62</a> of the Code, with the Supme Court. Importantly, the NCLAT had confirmed the order of NCLT, as the appellant/statutory authority, according to the NCLAT, had approached not only the RP but also the NCLT, at a belated stage.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning">Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">48. It is in this context, that the Supme Court ruled as follows:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(i) First, because of the statutory charge created under <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act, the appellant/statutory authority was a secured creditor within the meaning of <a href="/doc/183127702/">Section 3(30)</a> of the Code. Furthermore, the Supme Court also ruled, that the timeline for submission of claims under the 2016 Regulations was not mandatory, but only directory.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(ii) Second, prior to the amendment carried out via notification dated 03.07.2018 [with effect from 04.07.2018] the creditor was required to submit "proof of claim" before the expiration of the last date mentioned in the public announcement. [See <a href="/doc/159347390/">Regulation 12(1)</a> read with sub-Regulation (2)]. However, with effect from 04.07.2018, the creditor is required to submit a "claim with proof" on or before the last date mentioned in the public announcement.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(iii) Third, in case the Resolution Plan did not meet the requirements of <a href="/doc/57302277/">Section 30(2)</a> of the Code, it would be invalid, and thus not binding on the Central Government, any State Government, any statutory or other authority, any financial creditor, or other creditors to whom a debt in respect of dues arising under any law for the time being in force is owed.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">(iv) Fourth, if a Resolution Plan ignores the statutory demands payable to any State Government or legal authority, altogether, the NCLT is bound to reject the Resolution Plan.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(v) Fifth, in the event the corporate debtor is unable to pay its debts, which should include statutory dues owed to the government and/or authorities and if there is no plan which contemplates dissipation of those Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 18 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 debts in a phased manner, uniform proportional reduction, it would necessarily lead to liquidation, thus, triggering the sale of assets and their distribution in the manner stipulated in <a href="/doc/59978968/">Section 53</a> of the Code.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(vi) Sixth, the COC, which might include financial institutions and other financial creditors, cannot secure their dues at the cost of statutory dues owed to any Government or Governmental Authority or for that matter, any other authority.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(vii) Seventh, NCLAT erred in holding that <a href="/doc/59978968/">Section 53</a> of the Code overrides <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act. <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT is neither contrary to nor inconsistent with <a href="/doc/59978968/">Section 53</a> or any other provisions of the Code.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(viii) Eighth, <a href="/doc/183127702/">Section 3(30)</a> of the Code defines a secured creditor to mean a creditor in favour of whom security interest is credited. Security interests of this kind can also be created by the operation of law. Therefore, the definition of a secured creditor in the Code does not exclude any government or governmental authority.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">49. It is in this context, that the decisions of the NCLAT and NCLT were held to be erroneous, and accordingly, the Resolution Plan approved by COC was set aside, with an observation that the RP may consider a fresh Resolution Plan, in light of the observations made by the Court.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">50. Clearly, as noticed above, the judgement rendered by the Supme Court is distinguishable on facts.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">50.1 In the instant case, the DRI/DGFT neither submitted a proof of claim nor responded to a specific communication via e-mail dated 17.05.2017, addressed to respondent no.3.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">50.2 In contradiction, in Rainbow Papers, a claim was lodged in the pscribed form, albeit after the last date of submission indicated in the public announcement by the IRP, but before the NCLT had rendered its decision in the matter.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">50.3 Besides this, <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act is not pari materia with Section 142A of the 1962 Act. Section 142A of the 1962 Act plainly states that any amount payable by way of duty, penalty, interest or any other sum payable by an assessee or any other person under the Act shall have the first charge on the property of the assessee or the person, as the case may be, save as otherwise provided inter alia under the Code. <a href="/doc/77957879/">Section 48</a> of the GVAT Act does not contain any such exception and/or carve out.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">51. This is a case, where despite knowledge, the statutory authorities chose not to submit their proof of claim. Mr Sharma's argument, that since it was known to SML that amounts were due, proof of claim [under the unamended Regulation i.e., <a href="/doc/159347390/">Regulation 12</a>] was not required to be filed, is difficult to accept, because if this argument were to be sustained, then whatever the assessee [in this case SML] were to state before the RP would have to be taken as the gospel truth. In a given case, the assessee could state, that nothing was due to the concerned creditor. In our view, once a Public Announcement was made, it was incumbent upon all creditors, which included the statutory creditors, to submit the proof of claim.</p>

<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">52. Therefore, the fact that extensions were sought to fulfil export obligations would not help the cause of the respondents. As a matter of fact, respondent nos. 2, 3 and 7 have, in their counter-affidavit, admitted that since the amounts due had not been crystallized, they could not respond to the Public Announcement made by the IRP.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">52.1 According to us, this argument is flawed. If this was the stand of the respondents, it could have been articulated before the RP, something which the respondents failed to do, despite knowledge of the fact that the CIRP was on.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">52.2 Pertinently, in the reply dated 10.10.2019 submitted qua the impugned show-cause notice, this very aspect was highlighted. The respondents, throughout, have chosen not to take recourse to the provisions of the Code, to agitate their point of view.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">53. Given this situation, we are of the view, that if the law, as enunciated by the Supme Court in Ghanashyam Mishra is applied, then the dues, if any owed to the respondents would have to be declared as having extinguished, and if such is the position, the adjudication of the impugned show-cause notice would be an exercise in futility.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">54. This brings us to the other submission made on behalf of Mr Sharma, which is that this Court should not entertain the instant writ petition. In this context, in our view, Mr Sharma has made contradictory submissions. Mr Sharma's first submission is, that this Court does not have the jurisdiction. His second submission is, that this Court is not a convenient forum, since except for the adjudicating authority, all other aspects concerning the matter Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 21 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 at hand arise outside the territorial jurisdiction of this Court. In our opinion, the doctrine of forum non-conveniens assumes, that although the Court has jurisdiction, in certain cases, it takes the view, that the action filed before it could be agitated conveniently before another forum. Therefore, in such an eventuality, the writ action is not entertained by the Court for the reasons of convenience, and not on the ground that it does not have the jurisdiction to try and adjudicate the action lodged before it.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">55. Lastly, Mr Sharma contends, that this Court should return the action, by applying the doctrine of forum non conveniens. Having regard to the fact, that respondent no.1 was vested with the powers to adjudicate the impugned show-cause notice via a notification dated 29.08.2019, the cause for palpable apphension qua the petitioner arose, to our minds, within the jurisdiction of this Court. Furthermore, given the fact, that this writ petition was filed in March 2021, and the objection concerning the Court's power to entertain the writ action was taken much later, it would be unfair to return the writ petition, on the ground that this Court is not a convenient forum for adjudicating the dispute arising between the parties.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Conclusion:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">56. Thus, for the foregoing reasons, we are of the view, that the impugned show-cause notice seeks to do, what is, in fact, an exercise in futility, given the law <a href="/doc/30560910/">laid down by</a> the Supme Court in Ghanashyam Mishra. The Supme Court has enunciated, in no uncertain terms, the clean slate Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ATUL JAIN Page 22 of 23 Signing Date:16.02.2023 W.P.(C) No.3119/2021 16:56:26 NEUTRAL CITATION NO : 2023/DHC/001118 principle1; it cannot be set at naught by entertaining claims that concern the period obtaining before the approval of the Resolution Plan.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">57. Accordingly, the impugned show-cause notice is quashed.</p>
