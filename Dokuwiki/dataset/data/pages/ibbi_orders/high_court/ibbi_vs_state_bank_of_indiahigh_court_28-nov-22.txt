<p title="Analysis of the law">3. The matter came to the NCLT by way of an application filed under Section 12(2) of the IBC by the Respondent No. 1 - State Bank of India (SBI) where extension was sought for the completion of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) by a further period of 90 days.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">During the course of hearing this application, the NCLT noticed that an expssion of interest was floated however, no resolution plans were filed in response to the same.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">4. This splitting of the CIRP into inviting expssion of interest and then seeking resolution plans as provided in Regulation 36A became the subject matter of the impugned order. The NCLT then held Regulation 36A1 to be ultra vires of Section 240(1) of the IBC, despite there being no specific challenge to the said Regulation. The reason given by the NCLT was that it was contrary to the speedy disposal of the Resolution Process. The operative portion of the impugned order reads as under:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">"We are further of the view that Section 25 (2) (h) added on 23.11.2017 by way of amendment does not contemplate floating of any expssion of interest. It is beyond our understanding as to how the IBBI has taken upon itself the task of framing Regulation 36A of IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons), Regulations, 2016 using the expssion 'invitation of expssion of interest' along with Form 'G'. Such an assumption of power would be beyond the competence of IBBI as the source of power to frame Regulation under the IBC is drawn from Section 240 of IBC, 2016. Section 240(1) in categorical terms provides that the IBBI may by notification make regulation consistent with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, and further subject to the Rules 1 Inserted vide Insolvency Bankruptcy Board of India (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2018 Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed W.P.(C) 10189/2018 Page 3 of 17 By:DEVANSHU JOSHI Signing Date:02.12.2022 11:53:55 2022/DHC/00524 framed by the Government under Section 239 of IBC, 2016 for carrying out the provisions of the Code. It has been repeatedly emphasised culminating in the rendered in aforesaid judgment that speed is the essence of CIR Process and inviting 'expssion of interest' would impede to the speed. In the case of <a href="/doc/181931435/">Innoventive Industries Ltd. v. ICICI Bank Ltd.</a> (2018) 1 see 407 passed by Hon'ble Supme Court has highlighted that the speed is one of the salient features of the IBC, 2016. By use of the words 'expssion of interest' the speed is retarted and time is wasted. In the psent case on 04.06.2018 'expssion of interest' was invited and last date for expssing interest to submit the resolution plan was 18.06.2018 without in fact inviting any resolution plan. Such a course is negation of the salient features highlighted by Supme Court that the speed is essence of the IBC, 2016, therefore, we have no other option except to declare Regulation 36A as ultra-vires of Section 240(1) of IBC, 2016. The IBBI is directed to frame Regulation according to its competence and the source of power as given to it by the Code. We do not say anything more on this aspect."</p>

<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">13. Heard. The Court has perused the IBC as also the judgements cited by the ld. Counsel for the Petitioner. A perusal of the IBC would show that Section 3(1) of the IBC defines `Board' as the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India established under Section 188(1) of the IBC. Establishment and constitution of the Board is stipulated in Sections 188 and 189 of the IBC and the powers are stipulated in Section 196 of the IBC. A perusal of the powers and functions of the Board shows that the overall supervision and functions under the IBC are to be carried out by the Board. Section 196(2) of the IBC also vests with the Board, the power to make model bylaws to be adopted by the insolvency professional agencies. The Board's power to issue Regulations are recognized in Section 240 of the IBC. Lastly, Section 196(1)(u) of the IBC is a broad provision which stipulates that the IBBI can perform such other functions as may be pscribed. The relevant provisions of the IBC are set out below:</p>

<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">15. A perusal of the powers of the NCLT shows that, broadly the NCLT is vested with the power of adjudicating any application or proceeding before it and adjudicating any claims and also deciding on questions of law or fact arising out of the insolvency resolution or liquidation proceedings.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">16. On first blush, it appears that Section 60(5)(c) of the IBC would include any question of law or facts. However, a closer reading of the provision would show that questions of law or facts ought to be in respect of those proceedings which are pending before the NCLT and they ought to arise out of or in relation to the resolution or liquidation proceedings. This in the opinion of the Court cannot include the power to declare a Regulation itself as being ultra vires.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">17. Moreover, the rules and regulations which are framed by the Central Government or the IBBI are to be placed before Parliament in terms of Section 241 of the IBC. The same is extracted below:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">"Section 241 of the IBC, 2016</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">241. Every rule and every regulation made under this Code shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed W.P.(C) 10189/2018 Page 11 of 17 By:DEVANSHU JOSHI Signing Date:02.12.2022 11:53:55 2022/DHC/00524 sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or regulation or both Houses agree that the rule or regulation should not be made, the rule or regulation shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without pjudice to the validity of anything pviously done under that rule or regulation."</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">18. A perusal of the judgment in M/s <a href="/doc/72943087/">Mohan Gems &amp; Jewels Pvt. Ltd.</a> (Supra) clearly shows that the NCLAT is of the view that the need for judicial intervention or innovation from the NCLT &amp; NCLAT should be kept at its bare minimum and should not disturb the foundational principles of the IBC. The relevant part of the said judgement is as follows:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">" 26. It is a well settled proposition that the legality of propriety of any Regulation/ Notification / Rules / Act cannot be looked into by NCLT or NCLAT. The Tribunal can only ascertain whether the procedures provided for under the Code / <a href="/doc/172276913/">Companies Act, 2013</a> are being followed or not. The Adjudicating Authority cannot go beyond this.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">27. In <a href="/doc/58636887/">'Arun Kumar Jagatramka' Vs. 'Jindal Steel Power Ltd. &amp; Anr.' Reported</a> in Civil Appeal No. 9664 of 2019, the Hon'ble Apex Court while discussing the issue, 'whether in a liquidation proceeding under the Code, a person ineligible under Section 29 A of the Code, is permitted to propose a scheme for revival under <a href="/doc/43953242/">Section 230</a> of the Companies Act, 2013, has noted in the epilogue that 'the need for judicial intervention or innovation from the NCLT &amp; Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed W.P.(C) 10189/2018 Page 12 of 17 By:DEVANSHU JOSHI Signing Date:02.12.2022 11:53:55 2022/DHC/00524 NCLAT should be kept as its bare minimum and should not disturb the foundational principles of the IBC'.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">28. Keeping in view the scope and spirit of the Code, read with <a href="/doc/40064954/">Section 54</a> of the Code, Regulation 39C of CIRP Regulations, Regulations 32 (e) &amp; (f), 32A and 45 (3) of the Liquidation Process Regulations, we are of the view that the sale of the 'Corporate Debtor Company' was carried out by the liquidator in accordance with the Regulations and we are constrained to observe that the Adjudicating Authority, has, apart from travelling beyond its jurisdiction in making observation regarding the power and functions of framing of Regulations by IBBI, has also not appciated the ratio <a href="/doc/58636887/">laid down by</a> the Hon'ble Supme Court in a catena of Judgements that the Liquidation of the Company is to be seen only as a last resort and every attempt should be made to revive the Company and to continue it as a 'going concern'."</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">19. Insofar as the psent case is concerned, there was no challenge to Regulation 36A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016 before the NCLT. Therefore, in an application seeking extension of time to complete the CIRP process, the NCLT has gone ahead and declared the Regulation 36A as ultra vires.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"83. <p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">Suffice it to state that, in the light of our analysis of the 2003 Act, e hereinabove, the words 'orders, instructions or directions' in <a href="/doc/191638842/">Section 121</a> of the 2003 Act cannot confer power of judicial review under <a href="/doc/191638842/">Section 121</a> to the Tribunal, which, therefore, cannot go into the validity of the impugned 2006 Regulations, as rightly held in the impugned judgment."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">119. The summary of the findings of the Constitution Bench are contained in para 92, which is reproduced below:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"92. (i) In the hierarchy of regulatory powers and functions under the 2003 Act, <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a>, which deals with making of regulations by the Central Commission, under the authority of subordinate legislation, is wider than <a href="/doc/126531796/">Section 79(1)</a> of the 2003 Act, which enumerates the regulatory functions of the Central Commission, in specified areas, to be discharged by orders (decisions).</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(ii) A regulation under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a>, as a part of regulatory framework, intervenes and even overrides the existing contracts between the regulated entities inasmuch as it casts a statutory obligation on the regulated entities to align their existing and future contracts with the said regulations.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(iii) A regulations under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a> is made under Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed W.P.(C) 10189/2018 Page 14 of 17 By:DEVANSHU JOSHI Signing Date:02.12.2022 11:53:55 2022/DHC/00524 the authority of delegated legislation and consequently its validity can be tested only in judicial review proceedings before the courts and not by way of appeal before the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity under <a href="/doc/143397127/">Section 111</a> of the said Act.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(iv) <a href="/doc/191638842/">Section 121</a> of the 2003 Act does not confer power of judicial review on the Appellate Tribunal. The words 'orders', instructions' or 'directions' in <a href="/doc/191638842/">Section 121</a> do not confer power of judicial review on the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity. In this judgment, we do not wish to analyse the English authorities as we find from those authorities that in certain cases in England the power of judicial review is expssly conferred on the tribunals constituted under the Act. In the psent 2003 Act, the power of judicial review of the validity of the regulations made under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a> is not conferred on the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(v) If a dispute arises in adjudication on interptation of a regulation made under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a>, an appeal would certainly lie before the Appellate Tribunal under <a href="/doc/143397127/">Section 111</a>, however, no appeal to the Appellate Tribunal shall lie on the validity of a regulation made under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a>.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(vi) Applying the principle of "generality versus enumeration. it would be open to the Central Commission to make a regulation on any residuary item under <a href="/doc/197818980/">Section 178(1)</a> read with <a href="/doc/17966862/">Section 178(2)(ze)</a>.Accordingly, we hold that CERC was empowered to cap the trading margin under the authority of delegated legislation under <a href="/doc/97083846/">Section 178</a> vide the impugned Notification dated 23-1-2006.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(vii) <a href="/doc/191638842/">Section 121</a>, as amended by the <a href="/doc/2077/">Electricity (Amendment) Act</a> 57 of 2003, came into force with effect from 27-1-2004. Consequently, there is no merit in the contention advanced that the said section has not yet been brought into force."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">In our view, even though in para 94 of the judgment the Constitution Bench clarified that the judgment will not Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed W.P.(C) 10189/2018 Page 15 of 17 By:DEVANSHU JOSHI Signing Date:02.12.2022 11:53:55 2022/DHC/00524 govern the cases under the <a href="/doc/1171314/">TRAI Act</a>, the ratio of that judgment is clearly attracted in these cases.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">124. In the result, the question framed by the Court is answered in the following terms: in exercise of the power vested in it under <a href="/doc/13831203/">Section 14 (b)</a> of the TRAI Act, TDSAT does not have the jurisdiction to entertain the challenge to the regulations framed by TRAI under <a href="/doc/114051/">Section 36</a> of the TRAI Act."</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">21. In the psent case, a conjoint reading of the provisions of the IBC clearly shows that the NCLT is the adjudicating authority under the IBC. Under Section 60(5) the categories of cases which can be adjudicated have been clearly enumerated. The jurisdiction to deal with the validity and legality of the Regulations framed under the IBC is not conferred upon the NCLT. The NCLT being a creature of the IBC, cannot assume to itself the power of declaring any provisions of the IBC or the Regulations as illegal or ultra vires. This is the clear view even of the NCLAT in M/s <a href="/doc/72943087/">Mohan Gems &amp; Jewels Pvt. Ltd.</a> (supra).</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">22. In view of the aforementioned discussion, since Regulation 36A has been amended and passed in accordance with law by the IBBI, the NCLT did not have the power to declare the same as being ultra vires merely on the ground that the two stage process provided in it i.e., of inviting an expssion of interest first and then the financial bids, would be contrary to the speedier resolution of the Insolvency Resolution Process.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">23. The impugned order dated 5th September, 2018 passed by the NCLT to the extent it holds Regulation 36A as ultra vires is accordingly set aside. It is however made clear that, before this Court, no challenge has been raised on merits to the validity or legality of Regulation 36A.</p>
