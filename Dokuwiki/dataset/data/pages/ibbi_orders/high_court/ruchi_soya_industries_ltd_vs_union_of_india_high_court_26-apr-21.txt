<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">In the psent writ petition, the petitioner has challenged the reassessment of the Bill of Entry No. 2606926 dated 15.9.2015. It is the case of the petitioner that the amendment to Serial No. 55 to Notification No. 12/2012-Customs dated 17.3.2012vide Notification No. 46/2015- Customs dated 17.9.2015 which increased the rate of duty from 7.5% to 12.5% cannot be said to have come into force on the date of assessment on 17.3.2012 as per the <a href="/doc/412480/">Section 25</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 as it stood on the date.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 2 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">2. The petitioner had filed the above Bill of Entry in advance to clear the consignment of crude palm oil of edible grade in bulk. In the Bill of Entry, the petitioner had proposed to pay Basic Customs Duty (BCD) at 7.5% as per Serial No.55 to Notification No. 12/2012-Customs dated 16.3.2012 as it stood on 15.09.2015.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">3. However,after the import and at the time when the said Bill of Entry was taken up for assessment, Serial No.55 to Notification No.12/2012-Customs dated 17.3.2012 was amendedvide Notification No.46/2015-Customs dated 17.9.2015. Serial No.55 to Notification No.12/2012-Customs dated 17.3.2012 increased the rate of Basic Customs Duty ( BCD) to 12.5% from 7.5%.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">4. It is the contention of the petitioner that as per <a href="/doc/112813819/">Section 25 (4)</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 as it stood them every notification issued under <a href="/doc/753761/">Section 25(1)</a> or (2<a href="/doc/1059693/">A) of the Customs Act, 1962</a> comes into force on the date of its issue by the Central Government for publication in the Official ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 3 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 Gazette and also when it is published and offered for sale on the date of its issue by the Directorate of Publicity And Public Relations of the Board, New Delhi.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">5. <p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">6. The copy of record for that day is enclosed for further information</p>
<p title="Issue">2. The Printed Gazette was dispatched from the Government of India Press, Mayapuri, New Delhi for sale on 21.9.2015&rdquo;.</p>

<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">20. I have considered the arguments advanced by the learned counsel for the petitioner and the learned counsel for the respondent customs Department. I have also perused the notification and the documents filed along with the typeset of papers. I have also perused the affidavit filed in support of the psent writ petition.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">21. Facts are not in dispute. The petitioner has an alternate remedy to file an appeal against the assessment before an Appellate ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 10 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 Commissioner under <a href="/doc/911167/">Section 128</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 against the reassessment in the impugned Bill of Entry.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">22. Considering the fact that the writ petition has been admitted in the year 2015, I do not see any point in relegating the petitioner to work out the remedy before the Commissioner of Customs (Appeals) at this distant point of time straight away without examining the case on merits. The petitioner has also persuaded this court that a final decision may be given on merits as well.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">23. I have considered the decision in <a href="/doc/55947807/">Union of India versus Param Industries Ltd</a> 2015 (321) ELT 192 (SC) cited by the learned counsel. It was rendered in the context of the pvailing practice and the provisions of the <a href="/doc/1059693/">Customs Act, 1962</a> as it stood then. At that point of time, hosting of the notification through the website of theCentral Board of Excise and Customs had not evolved as it was in 2015.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 11 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">24. Though, <a href="/doc/112813819/">section 25 (4)</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 read similarly as it stood when the dispute arose in 2001 in <a href="/doc/55947807/">Union of India versus Param Industries Ltd</a> 2015 (321) ELT 192 (SC), there was a marked difference in the practice of dissemination of statutory information in 2015.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">25. The use of the information technology had changed by leaps and bound since 2001. By 2015, all informations were available at the click of the button of the computer in the website of the Central Board of Excise and Customs which were also physically published in the official Gazette.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">26. The answers obtained under the <a href="/doc/671631/">RTI Act</a> also do not dispute that the fact that the amended notification had been published in the Gazette on 17.9.2015.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">27. In this case, not only the notification was posted in the website of the Central Board of Excise and Customs on 17.9.2015but ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 12 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 was also published in the official Gazette of Government of India on 17.9.2015. Therefore, the petitioner cannot complain that it was unaware of the change in the rate of duty merely because the sale of official Gazette was purportedly made only on 21.9.2015.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">28. After all , the publication of any information in the official Gazette not only signifies its authenticity but also its dissemination to the public. The practice of purchasing printed copies of Gazette publication has been done away over a period of time as the information were made available to the citizens in the official website of the Central Board of Excise and Customs.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">29. Thus, the second limb of <a href="/doc/112813819/">section 25 (4)</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 requiring publication and offer for sale on the date of receipt issued by the Directorate of Publicity and Public Relation of the Board, New Delhi had been rendered vestigial over a period of time having no useful purpose in the light of the publication of such information in the website.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 13 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">30. Perhaps taking note of the above provisions of the <a href="/doc/1965344/">Information Technology Act, 2000</a> and the advancement in the use of information technology in the dissemination of information by the Central Board of Excise and Customs, the Union Parliament has also amended <a href="/doc/112813819/">section 25(4)</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 vide <a href="/doc/75703920/">Finance Act, 2016</a> to read as under:-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">&ldquo; (4) Every Notification issued under sub- section (1) or under sub- section (2A), unless otherwise provided, come into force on the date of its issue by the central government for publication in the Official Gazette.&rdquo;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">31. In fact, <a href="/doc/492974/">section 4</a> of the Information Technology Act, 2000 also makes it clear that where any law provides that information or other matter shall be in writing or in the typewritten or printed form, then, notwithstanding anything contained in such law, such requirement shall be deemed to have been satisfied if such information are matters-</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">&ldquo; (a) rendered or made available in an electronic form;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">and</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(b) accessible so as to be usable for the subsequent reference.&rdquo; ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 14 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">32. The above two conditions have been satisfied and therefore the argument based on the decision of the Hon'ble Supme Court in <a href="/doc/55947807/">Union of India versus Param Industries Ltd</a> 2015 (321) ELT 192 (SC) the facts of the case are to be rejected.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">33. In fact, the decision of the Honourable Supme Court in <a href="/doc/55947807/">Union of India versus Param Industries Ltd</a> 2015 (321) ELT 192 (SC) has not examined the issue from the perspective of <a href="/doc/492974/">section 4</a> of the Information Technology Act, 2000. If such information was brought to the notice of the Honourable Supme Court, the Honourable Supme Court would have certainly given different verdict.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">34. By 2015, an assessee was no longer required to wait to buy the printed copy of the official Gazette from the bazzar or the official says counter of the Government to find out the change in the rate of duty tax after the information were hosted in the official website.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">35. In fact, a reading of unamended <a href="/doc/112813819/">section 25(4)</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 would also indicate that every notification issued under sub-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 15 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 section (1) or sub- section (2A) shall unless otherwise provided, come into force on the date of the issue by the Central Government for publication in the official Gazette.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">36. Sub-clause (4) of <a href="/doc/412480/">Section 25</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 as it stood prior to its amendment in 2016 merely enjoined the Central Government also to offer it for sale by the Directorate of Publicity and Public Relations of the Board, New Delhi simultaneously. The said requirement would have suited before the intensive of the information technology in the dissemination of the information.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">37. In my view, in 2015, the necessity offering for sale such publication as in the second limb of Sub-clause (4) of <a href="/doc/412480/">section 25</a> of the Customs Act, 1962 had become redundant and the Parliament has rightly taken note of the same and deleted it,though somewhat belatedly.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">38. Before the Calcutta High Court, the Union of India had also filed an affidavit stating that the notification was also published and ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 16 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 offered for sale nonetheless. The court has also taken the view that if the Union&rsquo;s assertion of copies of notification being put up for sale on September 17, 2015 is disbelief, at the highest, would amount to non-</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">compliance of clause (b) which would have no effect on when the notification came into force.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">39. Therefore, I am not inclined to take a different stand in this writ petition. Therefore, the psent writ petition has to fail based on the arguments advanced that the amending notification came inot force only on 21.9.2015.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">40. In the result, I hold that the amended notification came into on the date of its publication in the official Gazette on 17.9.2015 and its publication in the website of the Central Board of Indirect Taxes on the said date.</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">41. I shall now proceed to examine the 2nd argument of the learned counsel for the petitioner regarding extinguishing of the rights of ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 17 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 the respondent Customs Department to Duty in view of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy proceedings initiated against the petitioner.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">42. It is the case of the petitioner that the respondent Customs Department has lost all its rights over the differential duty demanded in view of the corporate resolution plan approved by the National Company Law Board, Mumbai under the provisions of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">43. Question to be answered is, whether the &ldquo;customs duty&rdquo; payable under the provisions of the <a href="/doc/1059693/">Customs Act, 1962</a> and the <a href="/doc/442204/">Customs Tariff Act, 1975</a> is &ldquo;an operational debt&rdquo; of the petitioner within the meaning of <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5 (21)</a> of the IBC Code, 2016 and whether the respondent Customs Department is an &ldquo;operational creditor&rdquo; within the meaning of <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5 (20)</a> of the IBC Code, 2016?</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">44. At the outset, I would like to emphasize that such proceedings initiated at behest of a Financial creditor or an operating ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 18 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 creditor should come in the legitimate way of the Department collecting the taxes due from the petitioner.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">45. It should be remembered that Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 was enacted with a view to provide a speedy mechanism for resolving bankruptcy and insolvency of such person. It is being implemented in a phased manner. The provisions of the Companies Act, 1956 which contained provisions for winding up has been re-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">grafted into the IBC, 206 with modification.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">46. Under the scheme of the IBC, 2016, any &ldquo;operational creditor &ldquo; or a &ldquo;financial creditor&rdquo; to whom a corporate debtor owes any amount above Rupees One Lakh and above is entitled to file an application for corporate insolvency resolution proceeding against such debtor under <a href="/doc/545058/">Section 9(2)</a> of the IBC, 2016 read with Rule 6 in Form 5 before the NCLT with a fee of Rs.2,000/- accompanied with documents and records as are required under <a href="/doc/545058/">Section 9(3)</a> and under <a href="/doc/176697533/">Regulation 7(2)</a>.</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 19 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">47. If Corporate Resolution Plan filed by Corporate Applicant is approved by the jurisdictional Company Law Board, the creditors are bound by it.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">48. The expssion &ldquo;operational creditor&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/1070542/">section 5 (20)</a> and &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/1070542/">section 5 (21)</a> is defined as follows:-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law"><a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5(20)</a> of the IBC Code, <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5 (21)</a> of the 2016 IBC Code, 2016 Operational creditor Operational debt means a means a person to whom an claim in respect of the operational debt is owned provision of goods or and includes any person to services including whom such debt has been employment or a debt in legally assigned or respect of the payment of transferred; dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">49. An &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5 (21)</a> of the IBC Code, 2016.&ldquo;Operational debt&rdquo; includes both a &ldquo;claim&rdquo; and/or a &ldquo;debt&rdquo;.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">These, definitions intertwine and interlock with each other. These ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 20 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 expssions have been defined in <a href="/doc/169382/">section 3(6)</a> and <a href="/doc/169382/">3(11)</a> of IBC, 2016 as follows:-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law"><a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3(6)</a> of IBC, 2016 <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3(11)</a> of IBC, 2016 &ldquo;claim&rdquo; means-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">a) a right to payment, whether &ldquo;debt&rdquo; means a liability or or not such right is reduced to obligation in respect of the judgment, fixed, disputed, claim which is due from any undisputed, legal, equitable or person and includes a secure or unsecured; financial debt and</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">b) right or remedy for breach operational debt.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">of contract under any law for the time being in force, if such breach gives rise to a right to payment, whether or not such right it is reduced to judgment, fixed, mature, and mature, disputed, undisputed, secured or unsecured.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">50. The expssion &ldquo;Debt&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3(11)</a> means a liability or obligation in respect of the &ldquo;claim&rdquo; which is due from any person and includes a &ldquo;financial debt&rdquo; and an &ldquo;operational debt.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 21 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">51. The expssion &ldquo;claim&rdquo; as defined in <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3(11)</a> of the IBC, 2016, means-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">&ldquo; a) a right to payment, whether or not such right is reduced to judgment, fixed, disputed, undisputed, legal, equitable or secure or unsecured;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">b) right or remedy for breach of contract under any law for the time being in force, if such breach gives rise to a right to payment, whether or not such right is reduced to judgment, fixed, mature, and mature, disputed undisputed, secure or unsecured.&rdquo;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">52. A &ldquo;claim&rdquo; is a reward for the provision of goods or service or employment. Therefore, &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; cannot include a &ldquo;tax&ldquo;or &ldquo;duty&rdquo; under an enactment.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">53. The expssion &ldquo;claim&rdquo; means a right to payment, whether or not such right is reduced to judgment, fixed, disputed, undisputed, legal, equitable or secure or unsecured or a right or remedy for breach of contract under any law for the time being in force, if such breach gives right to payment, whether or not such right is reduced in judgment, fixed, mature, disputed, undisputed, secured or unsecured.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 22 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">54. For a &ldquo;claim&rdquo; to be an &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo;, it should be in respect of the provision of &ldquo;goods&rdquo; or &ldquo;service&rdquo; including &ldquo;employment&rdquo; as is evident from reading first part of the definition of &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5(11)</a> of IBC,2016.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">55. <p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">86. As discussed hereinabove, one of the principal objects of I&amp;B Code is, providing for revival of the Corporate Debtor and to make it a going concern. I&amp;B Code is a complete Code in itself. Upon admission of petition under <a href="/doc/1411673/">Section 7</a>, there are various important duties and functions entrusted to RP and CoC. RP is required to issue a publication inviting claims from all the stakeholders. He is required to collate the said information and submit necessary details in the information memorandum. The resolution applicants submit their plans on the basis of the details provided in the information memorandum. The resolution plans undergo deep scrutiny by RP as well as CoC. In the negotiations that may be held between CoC and the resolution applicant, various modifications may be made so as to ensure, that while paying part of the dues of financial creditors as well as operational creditors and other stakeholders, the Corporate Debtor is revived and is made an on-going concern. After CoC approves the plan, the Adjudicating Authority is required to arrive at a subjective satisfaction, that the plan conforms to the requirements as are provided in sub-section (2) of <a href="/doc/1035111/">Section 30</a> of the I&amp;B Code. Only thereafter, the Adjudicating Authority can grant its approval to the plan. It is at this stage, that the plan becomes binding on Corporate Debtor, its employees, members, creditors, guarantors and other stakeholders involved in the resolution Plan. The legislative intent behind this is, to freeze all the claims so that the resolution applicant starts on a clean slate and is not flung with any surprise claims. If that is permitted, the very calculations on the basis of which the resolution applicant submits its plans, would go haywire and the plan would be unworkable.</p>

<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">88. There is another reason, which persuades us to take the said view. Sub-section (10) of <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3</a> of the I&amp;B Code defines &ldquo;creditor&rdquo; thus:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">&ldquo;(10) &ldquo;creditor&rdquo; means any person to whom a debt is owed and includes a financial creditor, an operational creditor, a secured creditor, an unsecured creditor and a decree-holder;&rdquo;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">89. Sub-sections (20) and (21) of <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5</a> of the I&amp;B Code define &ldquo;operational creditor&rdquo; and &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; respectively as such:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(20) &ldquo;operational creditor&rdquo; means a person to whom an operational debt is owed and includes any person to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred; (21) &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; means a claim in respect of the provision of goods or services including employment or a debt in respect of the payment of dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority;</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">90. &ldquo;Creditor&rdquo; therefore has been defined to mean &lsquo;any person to whom a debt is owed and includes a financial creditor, an operational creditor, a secured creditor, an unsecured creditor and a decree-holder&rsquo;.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">&ldquo;Operational creditor&rdquo; has been defined to mean a person to whom an operational debt is owed and ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 38 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 includes any person to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">&ldquo;Operational debt&rdquo; has been defined to mean a claim in respect of the provision of goods or services including employment or a debt in respect of the payment of dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">91. It is a cardinal principle of law, that a statute has to be read as a whole. Harmonious construction of sub-section (10) of <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3</a> of the I&amp;B Code read with sub-sections (20) and (21) of <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5</a> thereof would reveal, that even a claim in respect of dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority would come within the ambit of &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo;. The Central Government, any State Government or any local authority to whom an operational debt is owed would come within the ambit of &lsquo;operational creditor&rsquo; as defined under sub-section (20) of <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5</a> of the I&amp;B Code. Consequently, a person to whom a debt is owed would be covered by the definition of &lsquo;creditor&rsquo; as defined under sub-section (10) of <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3</a> of the I&amp;B Code. As such, even without the 2019 amendment, the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority to whom a debt is owed, including the statutory dues, would be covered by the term &lsquo;creditor&rsquo; and in any case, by the term &lsquo;other stakeholders&rsquo; as provided in sub-section (1) of <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> of the I&amp;B Code.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">94. Therefore, in our considered view, the aforesaid provisions leave no manner of doubt to hold, that the 2019 amendment is declaratory and clarificatory in nature. We also hold, that even if 2019 amendment was not effected, still in light of the view taken by us, the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority 35 2018 SCC OnLine Cal. 142would be bound by the resolution plan, once it is approved by the Adjudicating Authority (i.e. NCLT). <p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">95. In the result, we answer the questions framed by us as under:</p>
<p title="Conclusion">(i) That once a resolution plan is duly approved by the Adjudicating Authority under sub-section (1) of <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a>, the claims as provided in the resolution plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on the Corporate Debtor and its employees, members, creditors, including the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, guarantors and other stakeholders. On the date of approval of resolution plan by the Adjudicating Authority, all such claims, which are not a part of resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no person will be entitled to initiate or continue any proceedings in respect to a claim, which is not part of the resolution plan;</p>
<p title="Conclusion">(ii) 2019 amendment to <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> of the I&amp;B Code is clarificatory and declaratory in nature and therefore will be effective from the date on which I&amp;B Code has come into effect;</p>
<p title="Conclusion">(iii) Consequently all the dues including the statutory dues owed to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, if not part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no proceedings in respect of such dues for the period prior to the date on which the Adjudicating Authority grants its approval under <a href="/doc/1138069/">Section 31</a> could be continued.&rdquo;</p>
<p title="Conclusion">75. Though the definition of &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; in <a href="/doc/1070542/">Section 5(21)</a> of the IBC, 2016 is not intended to include &ldquo;crown debt&rdquo; such as ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 40 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 taxes and duties payable to the Government and is distinct from the &ldquo;claim&rdquo; and &ldquo;debt&rdquo; as defined in <a href="/doc/169382/">Section 3 (6)</a> and <a href="/doc/169382/">3(11)</a> of the IBC,2016, as mentioned above in the beginning of the discussion on the second part of this order, this Court is bound by the interptation placed in <a href="/doc/30560910/">the above decision</a> of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supme Court in <a href="/doc/30560910/">Ghanashym Mishra and Sons Vs. Edelweiss Asset Construction</a>, and the reasons given therein and in the light of the amendment to the IBC, 2016 in 2019 and in the light of the clarification of the Finance Minister when the 2019 bill was put to discussion in the parliament.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">76. This Court therefore partly accepts the contention of the petitioner in so far as issue relating to extinguishment of the rights of the respondent customs department to claim the customs duty in the light of the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supme Court in <a href="/doc/30560910/">Ghanashym Mishra and Sons Vs. Edelweiss Asset Construction</a> referred to supra.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">77. The case is therefore remitted back the respondent to await clarification to be obtained by the Petitioner from the National ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 41 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 Company Law Board as to whether the Corporate Resolution Plan filed by the Corporate Applicant included the &ldquo;customs duty&rdquo; to be paid by the Petitioner on the import under the subject bill of entry.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">78. In the light of the discussion, the submission of the petitioner that the amending Notification came into force only 21.9.2015 and not on the date of its publication on 17.9.2015 is rejected.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">79. It is answered against the petitioner in the light of the provisions of the <a href="/doc/1965344/">Information Technology Act, 2000</a>, the decision of the Calcutta High Court and reasons given in this order.</p>

The petitioner shall therefore file an appropriate application before the National Company Law Board and get the issue clarified from the National Company Law Board that the indeed crown debts like the differential &ldquo; customs duty&rdquo; payable to the respondent under the subject bill of entry which is the subject matter of ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis/ Page No 42 of 46  W.P.No.31090 of 2015 the psent writ petition were treated as &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; before it by the &ldquo; corporate applicant&rdquo; .</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">81. The corporate applicant has indirectly taken over the petitioner in their &ldquo;Corporate Resolution Plan&rdquo; before the said Tribunal.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">It is for the petitioner to prove that the &ldquo;customs duty&rdquo; payable to the respondent under the subject Bill of Entry was factored by the Corporate Applicant in the Corporate Resolution Plan submitted before the National Company Law Board.</p>
