<p title="Precedent Analysis">17. Again in AIR para 9, the Court held:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">&bdquo;We, therefore, hold that normally petitions solely praying for the refund of money against the State by a writ of mandamus are not to be entertained. It is one thing to say that the High Court has no power under <a href="/doc/1712542/">Article 226</a> of the Constitution to issue a writ of mandamus for making refund of the money illegally collected. There is no good reason to deny a relief of refund to the citizens in such cases on the principles of public interest and equity in the light of the cases cited above. <p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">27. From the above discussion of ours, following legal principles emerge as to the maintainability of a writ petition:</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning">(a) In an appropriate case, a writ petition as against a State or an instrumentality of a State arising out of a contractual obligation is maintainable.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning">(b) Merely because some disputed questions of facts arise for consideration, same cannot be a ground to refuse to entertain a writ petition in all cases as a matter of rule.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning">(c) A writ petition involving a consequential relief of monetary claim is also maintainable."</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">7. Therefore, the pliminary objection taken by the respondents/revenue, with regard to maintainability cannot be sustained.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">8. We have asked Mr Singla, as to the stand of the respondents/revenue, regarding the merits of the matter.</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">14. In Ghanshyam Mishra, the Supme Court has taken this concern into account, and therefore, ruled that such-like debts/demands stand extinguished. The relevant observations made in the judgment are set forth hereafter:</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis">102.1.
