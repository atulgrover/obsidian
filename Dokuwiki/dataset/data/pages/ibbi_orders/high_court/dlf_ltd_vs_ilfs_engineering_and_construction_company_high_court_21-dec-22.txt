<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">1. The psent petition has been filed under <a href="/doc/1841764/">Section 11</a> of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 ("Act of 1996", hereinafter) seeking appointment of a sole arbitrator for the resolution of disputes between the parties under a Contract dated June 21, 2012.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">2. At the outset, I may briefly narrate the facts which have lead to the filing of the psent petition. The petitioner entered into a cost Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 1 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 sharing agreement with Haryana Urban Development Authority ("HUDA", hereinafter), for external development works for improvement of certain road networks in Gurgaon, Haryana. The respondent psented itself as a prominent infrastructure development company to the petitioner and based on its repsentation, the petitioner awarded development works to be carried out in the project to the respondent.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">3. Subsequently, the petitioner entered into a separate contract agreement dated June 21, 2012 ("contract", hereinafter) with the respondent. The term of the contract period was for 24 months and total price of the contract was ₹394,30,00,000/-. However, the said project was not completed because of various defaults on the part of the respondent, and the project timelines were extended till June 30, 2017. A major portion of the works were de-scoped, vide agreement dated May 11, 2018 as the respondent was unable to complete the work on time and further did not carry out the maintenance works; which in terms of the contract, the respondent was liable to do for five years. The maintenance work of the project is still ongoing and is being carried out through some other agencies at the risk and cost of the petitioner. Due to the delay caused by the respondent, and the subsequent de-scoping, the petitioner incurred additional expenses. The respondent also failed to adhere by the quality and safety norms set out in the agreement and the petitioner even imposed a fine on the respondent for its failure to adopt the safety measures in terms of the contract.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">4. There were several issues raised by the petitioner regarding the safety of the said culvert highlighting that it had become an accident prone area. Several correspondences were exchanged between the petitioner and the respondent wherein the petitioner time and again called upon the respondent to cure the defects in the area but the respondent has repeatedly failed to comply with the requests made by the petitioner.</p>

Thereafter, the Union of India filed an appeal before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal ("NCLAT") challenging the order passed by the NCLT, Mumbai. The NCLAT on October 15, 2018 passed an interim order staying the institution of suits and other proceedings against IL&amp;FS and its 348 Group Companies.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">6. Subsequently, the respondent issued a demand notice dated July 15, 2019 under <a href="/doc/58101662/">Section 8</a> of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 ("IBC") to the petitioner herein for an amount of ₹32,44,52,926/- along with interest and stated in the demand notice that the date of default was June 30, 2018. <p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">18. Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 9 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00
<p title="Fact">Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 which would throw into uncertainty amounts payable by a prospective resolution applicant who successfully takes over the business of the corporate debtor. All claims must be submitted to and decided by the resolution professional so that a prospective resolution applicant exactly knows what has to be paid in order that it may take them over and run the business of the corporate debtor. Therefore, the remedy sought to be availed by the petitioner in the psent petition is untenable.</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis">26. Mr. Rajiv Nayar, learned Senior Counsel appearing for the petitioner has stated that there are three main issues which arise for consideration in the psent petition which are as under:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(i) Whether the NCLAT being a statutory Tribunal over which this Court has supervisory jurisdiction can pass orders in relation to proceedings which are filed and can Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 14 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 only be filed before this Court.</p>

<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">27. It is the submission of Mr. Nayar that the moratorium given by NCLAT vide order dated October 15, 2018 and confirmed by the subsequent order dated March 12, 2020, is not a statutory moratorium under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC. In fact, the resolution of IL&amp;FS is not being conducted under the IBC at all but is being done pursuant to the provisions of <a href="/doc/165370771/">Sections 241</a> and <a href="/doc/11185865/">242</a> of the Companies Act, 2013. Therefore, the rigours of <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC are not attracted to the psent case at all and this is what distinguishes the psent case from a case where a company claims immunity from proceedings on the basis of a statutory provision i.e., <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC. That apart, even the order dated March 12, 2020 is under challenge before the Supme Court.</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">28. He has relied upon the Judgment of the Bombay High Court in the case of <a href="/doc/190581148/">Bay Capital Advisors Pvt. Ltd. v. IL&amp;FS Financial Services Ltd. &amp; Ors., Arbitration Petition (L) No.</a> 10089/2020 decided on April 9, 2021 to contend that the issue whether the directions passed by the NCLAT vide orders dated October 15, 2018 and March 12, 2020 curtail the jurisdiction of this Court under <a href="/doc/164179398/">Section 11</a> of the Act of 1996 need to be decided in light of the ratio of the said Judgment. In the said case, the learned Single Judge of the Bombay High Court has placed reliance on the Judgment of the Supme Court in <a href="/doc/405549/">Cotton Corporation of India Limited v. <p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">ANALYSIS</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">52. <p title="Court's Reasoning">i. The moratorium granted by the NCLAT is not a statutory moratorium under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC but has been passed under <a href="/doc/265827/">Sections 241</a> and <a href="/doc/1806598/">242</a> of the Companies Act. <p title="Court's Reasoning">iii. Reference under <a href="/doc/164179398/">Section 11</a> of the Act of 1996 cannot be denied unless it is ex facie apparent that the dispute is not arbitrable.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning">iv. In any case, claims arising after the cut-off date of October 18, 2018 are outside the resolution framework and denial of such claims would leave the petitioner remediless as it would have no forum to raise the claims arising after the cut-off date. v. The CMA has refused to admit or reject the claims of the petitioner on the ground that the claims are part of the psent arbitration proceedings. <p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 The interim order will continue until further orders and not be applicable to any petition under <a href="/doc/1712542/">Article 226</a> of the Constitution of India before any Hon‟ble High Court or under any jurisdiction of the Hon‟ble Supme Court."</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">56. The submission of Mr. Nayar is that the &bdquo;moratorium‟ granted by the NCLAT is not a statutory moratorium under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC and resolution of IL&amp;FS is not being conducted under the IBC, but under <a href="/doc/165370771/">Sections 241</a> and <a href="/doc/11185865/">242</a> of the Companies Act, 2013 and as such the rigours of <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC are not attracted to the psent proceedings. Suffice to state, the challenge to the order dated March 12, 2020 of the NCLAT is pending consideration before the Supme Court, and as such this Court cannot advert to the legality of the order of the NCLAT. The issues in this case need to be decided on the pmise that the NCLAT has stayed the institution or continuation of suits or any other proceedings by any party against IL&amp;FS and its 348 group companies. In fact, I find that the NCLAT in its order dated March 12, 2020 in Company Appeal (AT) No. 346/2018 has also considered this aspect in paragraphs 43 to 57 and finally held as under:-</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning">"Taking into consideration the aforesaid fact, we hold that Tribunal/ Appellate Tribunal has ample power to pass interim order in terms of <a href="/doc/1806598/">Section 242(4)</a> of the Companies Act as passed on 15th October, 2018 and requires no modification/ recall."</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">The matter being lis pendens before the Supme Court, it is not for this Court to sit in appeal and comment on the veracity of the order passed by the NCLAT, more so in a petition under <a href="/doc/164179398/">Section 11</a> of Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 31 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 the Act of 1996, when the order(s) are not under challenge.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">57. Mr. Nayar has placed reliance on the judgment of the Bombay High Court in <a href="/doc/190581148/">Bay Capital</a> (supra) wherein the Single Judge of the Bombay High Court has inter-alia held that the NCLAT cannot order the proceedings before the High Court to be interdicted. The said order was taken in appeal to the Division Bench of the Bombay High Court, which, while considering the application for an interim relief, on May 04, 2021 has in paragraph 5 stated as under:-</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">"5. The order of learned Single Judge rejecting the plea of maintainability of the Arbitration Petition (L) No. 10089 of 2020 in the impugned order is stayed. Parties would be at liberty to apply for early hearing of this Appeal after the issue raised before the Hon‟ble Supme Court arising out of the order passed by the NCLAT is decided."</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">58. It follows from the above that the Division Bench has passed the above order noting the pendency of the appeal before the Supme Court.</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">59. The restraint order of NCLAT stays all proceedings, including arbitration. Moreover, it is the case of the petitioner that it has filed claims before the CMA up to October 15, 2018, though the said claims have not been considered by the CMA on the ground of pendency of the psent proceedings.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">60. In view of the above, the plea of Mr. Nayar is unmerited. In this regard, I may refer to the judgment of a coordinate Bench of this Court in the case of <a href="/doc/117402866/">APCO-Titan (JV</a>) (supra), wherein this Court has held that the suit filed therein shall not be maintainable against a Group Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 32 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 Company of IL&amp;FS, in view of the order dated October 15, 2018 of the NCLAT. Relevant part of the judgment is reproduced as under:-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"32. It is not disputed that the above order continues to operate and apply even qua ITNL. The primary dispute and claim for recovery being against ITNL/SSTL, in view of the order dated 15th October, 2018 of the NCLAT, the psent suit would not be maintainable."</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">61. That apart, the High Court of Himachal Pradesh in the case of M/s <a href="/doc/72551342/">VIL Limited v. IL&amp;FS Transportation Networks, CARAP No.</a> 16/2018 dated November 30, 2018, while dealing with the same order dated October 15, 2018 of the NCLAT, has held as under:-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"9. Order dated 15.10.2018 has been passed by Appellate Tribunal under <a href="/doc/165370771/">Sections 241</a> read with 242 of the <a href="/doc/172276913/">Companies Act, 2013</a>, but it is similar to the order passed under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the Code providing declaration of moratorium, as prima facie the Appellate Tribunal has considered that it has wider power under <a href="/doc/165370771/">Sections 241</a> and <a href="/doc/11185865/">242</a> of the Companies Act, 2013, than the power vested under provisions of the Code.............</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">***** ***** *****</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">14..............interim order dated 15.10.2018 passed by Appellate Tribunal is not an order passed under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the Code. However, it is explicit from the interim order dated 15.10.2018 passed by the Appellate Tribunal that though moratorium has not been declared with respect to respondent Company, but the interim directions are identical to the provisions of <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the Code.........."</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">62. The issue which now arises for consideration is whether arbitration is permissible with regard to claims arising after October 15, 2018 in view of order dated October 15, 2018 read with order dated March 12, 2020 of the NCLAT. To answer this issue, it is necessary to Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 33 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 go into the effect of the said order staying the institution or continuation of suits or other proceedings by any party or person or bank or company against IL&amp;FS and its 348 Group Companies in any Court of law, tribunal, arbitration panel or arbitration authority. In this regard the plea of Mr. Mehta is that during the resolution process of a company, its creditor is obligated to necessarily lodge claims before a resolution professional, as a successful resolution applicant cannot suddenly be faced with undecided claims after the resolution plan submitted by him has been accepted. This would amount to a "hydra- head popping up" which would throw into uncertainty amounts payable by a prospective resolution applicant who successfully takes over the business of the corporate debtor. According to him, all claims must be submitted to and decided by the resolution professional so that a prospective resolution applicant exactly knows what has to be paid in order that it may take them over and run the business of the corporate debtor.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">63. The plea of Mr. Nayar is that the NCLAT, having pscribed the cut-off date of October 15, 2018 for commencing the resolution process and the CMA having invited claims only up to October 15, 2018, the claims of the petitioner arising after October 15, 2018 need to be referred to arbitration, failing which, the petitioner would be left remediless with regard to the said claims. He stated that the resolution framework of IL&amp;FS is different from the resolution process that is typically followed in respect of companies undergoing CIRP. According to him, the process followed in the psent case is for the sale of paid-up capital of the respondent held by IL&amp;FS and another Signature Not Verified Digitally Signed By:ANIL KUMAR YADAV Arb.P. 1166/2021 Page 34 of 38 Signing Date:21.12.2022 18:29:00 Neutral Citation Number:2022/DHC/005697 IL&amp;FS Group Company namely IFIL on a Swiss Challenge Method. Even the proceeds to be received from the sale of such shares is not to be distributed as per the approved resolution plan and mandate of the Committee of Creditors, but in a sui generis method. This submission is also without any merit. This I say so, because of the effect of the order of the NCLAT, which is primarily an order akin to an order of moratorium under <a href="/doc/126561274/">Section 14</a> of the IBC. The purpose and rationale behind granting a moratorium is to ensure that the assets of the corporate debtor are protected, with an intention to keep the company a going concern and to use the period to strengthen its financial position. It means, the intent of the order of the NCLAT is to protect the assets of IL&amp;FS and its group companies in order to make the resolution process effective/purposeful.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">64. Further, the order does not make any distinction between the claims before October 15, 2018 and after October 15, 2018. It restrains not just continuance of suits or proceedings already instituted, but also filing of fresh suits or proceedings. In other words, the order of stay/moratorium prohibits the initiation of any proceedings, regardless of the period to which the claims in the proceedings pertain.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">65. If this Court is to accept the plea of Mr. Nayar, then it would mean that there is no restraint for initiating proceedings with regard to claims arising after October 15, 2018, which could possibly lead to further liabilities being incurred by the company. This is clearly contrary to the intent of the order of the NCLAT. Hence, this plea is also rejected.</p>

Since, the matter is pending before the Supme Court and there is no stay of the NCLAT order, the petitioner has to await adjudication of the proceedings before the Supme Court.</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">68. Mr. Nayar has submitted that NCLAT being subordinate to this Court, this Court is not bound by the order dated October 15, 2018. The relief as sought for by Mr. Nayar, if granted, shall make the order of the NCLAT otiose, defeating the very purpose for which such an order was passed.
