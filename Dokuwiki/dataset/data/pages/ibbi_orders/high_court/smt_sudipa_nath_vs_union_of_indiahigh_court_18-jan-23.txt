<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">(7) The above tabulation of three provisions in the context of prayer would show: Page 8 of 16</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning">(a) <a href="/doc/172276913/">Under the Companies Act, 2013</a> or 1956, the application under <a href="/doc/46755674/">Section 339(1)</a> or Section 542 as the case may be would be filed only in the course of the winding up of company. However, an application under <a href="/doc/124671495/">Section 66(1)</a> of IBC can be filed during the corporate insolvency resolution process or a liquidation process.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) having been introduced laid down vide IBC, the legislature has consciously extended the application of the provisions even to the matters during corporate insolvency resolution process, instead of restricting it during the liquidation process.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">(b) In all three provisions, the common mandatory p-requisite satisfaction is that if any business of the company/corporate debtor has been either carried on</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(i) Intent to defraud creditors of the company/ corporate debtor or</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(ii) For any fraudulent purpose</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(c) <a href="/doc/1353758/">Under the Companies Act</a>, the application filed by the official liquidator or the liquidator or any creditor or contributory of the company could be entertained. However, under <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> of the IBC, only an application filed by resolution professional can be entertained by NCLT.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(d) All the three provisions are aimed at fixing the liability of persons responsible for such conduct of business of the company/corporate debtor which is with fraudulent purpose and a required mens rea.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(e) <a href="/doc/1353758/">Under the Companies Act</a>, the provision empowers the court/tribunal to hold such persons personally responsible before taking limitation Page 9 of 16 of liability for all or in of the debts or any other liability of the company and the court may direct. However, under <a href="/doc/152734/">Section 66(1)</a> of IBC, the NCLT can pass an order holding such person liable to make contribution to the assets of the corporate debtor or it may deem fit. The legislature has carefully made this distinction in <a href="/doc/152734/">Section 66(1)</a> of the IBC that this section may also apply during corporate insolvency resolution process and need not be only during liquidation. If no resolution plan is received under IBC or in any event has provided in IBC, corporate debtor would be subject to liquidation and the assets of the corporate debtor would be distributed amongst the creditors in the manner provided in IBC.</p>

<p title="Precedent Analysis">7) <a href="/doc/361841/">Section 337</a> refers to penalty for frauds by an officer of the company in which mis-management has taken place. <p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(9) In the context of <a href="/doc/1353758/">Companies Act, 1956</a> in <a href="/doc/660111/">South India Paper Mills Pvt. It will be useful to compare the provisions of <a href="/doc/1412989/">Section 542</a> with those of <a href="/doc/1030396/">Sections 543</a> and <a href="/doc/930802/">531</a>. <a href="/doc/1030396/">Section 543</a> empowers the court to assess damages against delinquent directors and others who occupy a fiduciary position in relation to a company. They are expected to act at all times in the interests of the company, eschewing fraud, underhand dealings and motives of personal aggrandisement. They also owe a duty of care. If they are found to be in breach of the duties attached to their special position, they are liable in damages under <a href="/doc/1030396/">Section 543</a>, and the court can order them to make good the loss sustained by the company as a result of their conduct. Misfeasance proceedings under the section lies for breach of any duty, even if it does not amount to a perpetration of fraud. The thrust of <a href="/doc/930802/">Section 531</a>, on the other hand, is against "fraudulent pference", i.e., parting with the assets of the company in favour of a few creditors with a view to defeating the others. The court is given power under this section to invalidate such transfers made on the eve of winding-up. The three sections are thus part of a scheme for reducing the liabilities of the company, recovering its assets and recouping its losses, if the conditions pscribed by them are found to exist on an examination of its affairs, after winding- up. While <a href="/doc/1412989/">Section 542</a> seeks to relieve the company of the liabilities incurred by fraudulent trading making those responsible for the fraud personally answerable, the purpose of <a href="/doc/930802/">Section 531</a> is to recover assets which should have belonged to the company but for fraudulent pference. Fraud is a common ingredient for both, whereas the proceedings under <a href="/doc/1030396/">Section 543</a> are designed to recoup losses sustained by a breach of duty which may fall short of fraud. <p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(13) As evident from the aforesaid pcedence <a href="/doc/1882688/">Section 339</a> or <a href="/doc/172276913/">Companies Act, 2013</a> and pari material, the provisions of <a href="/doc/1412989/">section 542</a> of Companies Act, 1956 Page 12 of 16 was aimed at conferring jurisdiction in the course of winding up of company to proceed against the persons responsible for fraudulent conduct of the business of the company. Both these provisions were aimed at making such persons personally liable for such fraudulent trading to recouping losses incurred thereby and to relief the company of the liabilities incurred by fraudulent trading. That <a href="/doc/152734/">Section 66(1)</a> also directed towards making such persons personally liable for such fraudulent trading to recouping losses incurred thereby and to provide that the NCLT can pass order holding such persons liable to make such contributions to the assets of the corporate debtor as it may deem fit. No power has been conferred on NCLT to pass such orders against other organizations/legal entities (other than corporate debtors) with whom such business was carried out against any person responsible in such other organizations/legal entities for carrying on business with corporate debtor. For the said purpose, the ratio of the judgment of the Hon'ble Supme Court in <a href="/doc/185514544/">Usha Ananthasubramanian</a> (supra) in the context of <a href="/doc/1882688/">section 339 (1)</a> one of the companies Act, 2013 as extracted above would clearly apply even in the context 66(1) of IBC. Accordingly, an application under <a href="/doc/152734/">Section 66(1)</a> by the resolution professional would not bar any civil action in accordance with law, either at the instance of resolution professional or liquidator or by the corporate debtor in its new avatar on a successful CIRP for recovery of any dues payable to the corporate debtor by such organization/legal entities. Such legal action is independent of <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a>. Similarly, any application under <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> will have no effect on legality or validity of any independent criminal action in accordance with law against Page 13 of 16 such organization/legal entities and persons responsible for conduct of their business with corporate debtor.</p>

It is clear from the language of <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> that unlike application provided under <a href="/doc/1502796/">section 43</a>, <a href="/doc/1300771/">section 45</a>, <a href="/doc/1547139/">section 50</a> and <a href="/doc/1360925/">section 47</a> or avoiding of such transaction and dehors these provisions. An application contemplated exclusively under <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> is not made for avoidance of any transaction. Even if fraudulent but to fix the liabilities of the persons reasonable for conducting the business of corporate debtor which is fraudulent or wrongful, that too an application made by resolution professional during the CIRP or a liquidation process.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">Page 15 of 16</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(18) In <a href="/doc/17372683/">Swiss Ribbons (P) Ltd. vs. Union of India</a> 2019 4 SCC 17 that the primary focus of the legislature to ensure revival and continuation of corporate debtor by protecting it from its own management and from a corporate by liquidation. Even its long title does not in any manner refer to liquidation which is only availed of as a last resort. If there is either no resolution plan or the resolution plan submitted are not upto the mark. The IBC is a beneficiary legislature which puts the corporate debtor back on its feet not being a mere recovery legislature for creditors.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(19) Therefore, in legislature wisdom and as apparent from the text of 66(1) it is clear that firstly it confers no jurisdiction but declaring any transaction as void, even if fraudulent, but confers jurisdiction on NCLT to fix the liabilities on the persons responsible for conducting business of corporate debtor which is fraudulent or wrongful. Secondly <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> contemplates an application thereunder only by the resolution professional and by none other. Thirdly <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66 (1)</a> also restricts the power of NCLT subject to being satisfy with p-requisite that any business of the corporate debtor has been carried on with intent to defraud creditors or the corporate debtors or for any fraudulent purpose and if satisfied it powers to pass an order is only against such person who are responsible for the conduct of such fraudulent business of the corporate debtor with mens rea to make them personally liable to make such contributions to the assets of the corporate debtor as it may deem fit.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(20) There is no arbitrariness, matchless manifest arbitrariness in <a href="/doc/152734/">section 66(1)</a> of IBC to entertain the instant petition to declare the said provisions as ultra vires of <a href="/doc/367586/">Article 14</a> and unconstitutional as alleged or otherwise. There is no Page 16 of 16 merit in the submission of the petitioner and the prayers made cannot be considered.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(21) With the above observation this court has no hesitation to dismiss the psent writ petition. Accordingly the writ petition stands dismissed.</p>
