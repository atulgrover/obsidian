<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">1] Rule. Rule is made returnable forthwith. With the consent of both sides, the matter is heard finally at the stage of admission.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">2] A very short question that arises for determination in this proceeding is as to whether moratorium prohibiting institution of a proceeding as provided for in Section 14 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2006 (hereinafter referred to as Code) applies even to a criminal proceeding.</p>
::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 :::
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">criwp1437-17 3 3] The facts as are necessary to be delineated are to the effect that the petitioner company instituted a complaint under <a href="/doc/1823824/">Section 138</a> of the Negotiable Instruments Act (hereinafter referred as N.I.Act for short) bearing SCC No.3197/2016 against the respondent no.2 company and the respondents 3 to 7 who are its Managing Director and Directors, in respect of a cheque for an amount of Rs.15,58,612/- issued by respondents 2 to 7 towards discharge of a civil liability. The learned Magistrate issued process under <a href="/doc/1827798/">Section 204</a> of the Code of Criminal Procedure. Being aggrieved, the respondents 2 to 7 challenged the order of issuance of process by pferring Criminal Revision No.147/2016.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">4] In the meanwhile, the respondents 2 to 7 initiated insolvency proceeding in Case No.CP/(IB No.20/BB/2017). The National Company Law Tribunal, Benguluru passed following order :</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">"That this Bench hereby prohibits the institution of suits or continuation of pending suits or proceedings against the corporate debtor including execution of any judgment, decree or order in any court of law, tribunal, arbitration panel or other authority, transferring, encumbering, alienating or disposing of by the corporate debtor any of this assets or any legal right or beneficial interest therein, any action to foreclose, recovery or enforce any security interest created by the corporate debtor in respect of its property including any action under the Securitization and Reconstruction of Financial assets and enforcement of security interest Act 2002, the recovery of any property by an owner or lessor ::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 ::: criwp1437-17 4 where such property is occupied by or in the possession of the corporate debtor."</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">In view of such an order, the respondents 2 to 7 submitted application (Exh.20) in the Criminal Revision and requested to keep the revision in abeyance/stayed till further order was passed in the insolvency proceeding.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">5] The petitioner opposed that application by its Say (Exh.23) inter alia on the ground that in view of the Division Bench judgment of this Court in Indorama Synthetics India Limited Nagpur V/s State of Maharashtra and others; 2016 (4) Mh.L.J.249 while considering a similar provision contained in Sub <a href="/doc/621484/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act it has interpted the words 'Suit or other proceeding' contained in that Section as not to include a criminal complaint filed under <a href="/doc/1823824/">Section 138</a> of the N.I.Act. The learned Additional Sessions Judge after hearing the arguments allowed the application Exh.20 filed by the respondents 2 to 7 and directed the revision to be kept in abeyance till further order was passed in the insolvency proceeding. Being aggrieved by the order, the petitioner has filed this Writ Petition under <a href="/doc/1712542/">Articles 226</a> and <a href="/doc/1331149/">227</a> of the Constitution of India.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">6] The learned advocate for the petitioner vehemently submitted that the provision contained in <a href="/doc/173961/">Section 14</a> of the Code does not specifically prohibit a criminal proceeding being ::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 ::: criwp1437-17 5 prosecuted against the company which has applied for insolvency under <a href="/doc/609790/">Section 10</a> of that Code. The National Company Law Tribunal, pursuant to this provision has prohibited institution or continuation of any proceeding, however even that order does not specifically prohibit continuation of a criminal proceeding. The decision in Indorama (supra) though interpts the provision of Sub <a href="/doc/621484/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act, the reasons and the analogy as applied therein for arriving at a conclusion that that provision does not debar continuation of a criminal proceeding clearly settles the law and can be followed even in the matter in hand.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">7] The learned advocate would point out that though the decision was cited before the learned Additional Sessions Judge and though the learned Judge has reproduced the observations therein, nothing has been said by her as to why the decision in that case does not govern the situation in the matter in hand.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">8] The learned advocate further submitted that the order passed by the National Company Law Tribunal in a proceeding under <a href="/doc/1907423/">Section 10</a> of the Code, also does not in clear terms directs any criminal proceeding to be kept in abeyance/stayed. Still the learned Additional Sessions Judge has readily held that the order prohibits continuation of the complaint under <a href="/doc/1824845/">Section 138</a> of the N.I.Act in the matter in hand and consequently even the criminal revision.</p>
::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 :::
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">criwp1437-17 6 9] Per contra, the learned advocate for the respondents 2 to 7 submitted that the decision in the case of Indorama (supra) is not applicable to the facts of the matter in hand. In that case, the provision of Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act was in question, whereas in the matter in hand, it is the provision of <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code which comes into play. The very purpose of prohibiting a proceeding to go on would be defeated if a complaint under <a href="/doc/1824845/">Section 138</a> of the N.I.Act is allowed to be prosecuted further. The National Company Law Tribunal has also passed an order invoking the provision of <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code and in the circumstances, no fault can be found in the impugned order passed by the learned Additional Sessions Judge in directing the hearing of the Criminal Revision to be kept in abeyance.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">10] True it is that in the case of Indorama (supra), the provision of Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act was in picture and was interpted by laying down that it does not debar filing/continuation of a proceeding under <a href="/doc/1824845/">Section 138</a> of the N.I.Act but it cannot be straightway applied to the matter in hand since in this case the provision contained in <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code is in question. However, in my considered view, the aim and object behind providing the bar under Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act and that of <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code are similar and therefore, though not strictly as a pcedent, the decision in the case of Indorama is not applicable to the matter in hand, the reasonings and the logic in ::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 ::: criwp1437-17 7 interpting the provision contained in Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act laid down therein can easily be pssed into service even in the matter in hand. The learned Additional Sessions Judge has apparently overlooked this aspect of the matter and without quoting any reason has refused to follow the reasonings and the logic contained in the case of Indorama.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">11] Be that as it may, since it is a matter of interptation of <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code, let us examine its wordings, which read as under :</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">"14. Moratorium.- (1) Subject to provisions of sub-sections (2) and (3), on the insolvency commencement date, the Adjudicating Authority shall by order declare moratorium for prohibiting all of the following, namely:-</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(a) the institution of suits or continuation of pending suits or proceedings against the corporate debtor including execution of any judgment, decree or order in any Court of law, tribunal, arbitration panel or other authority;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(b) transferring, encumbering, alienating or disposing of by the corporate debtor any of its assets of any legal right or beneficial interest therein;</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(c) any action to foreclose, recover or enforce any security interest created by the corporate debtor in respect of its property including any action under the <a href="/doc/52229129/">Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002</a> (54 of 2002);</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(d) the recovery of any property by an owner or lessor where such property is occupied by or in the possession of the corporate debtor.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(2) The supply of essential goods or services to the ::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 ::: criwp1437-17 8 corporate debtor as may be specified shall not be terminated or suspended or interrupted during moratorium period.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(3) The provisions of sub-section (1) shall not apply to such transactions as may be notified by the Central Government in consultation with any financial sector regulator.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(4) The order of moratorium shall have effect from the date of such order till the completion of the corporate insolvency resolution process:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">Provided that where at any time during the corporate insolvency resolution process period, if the Adjudicating Authority approves the resolution plan under sub-section (1) of <a href="/doc/1810383/">section 31</a> or passes an order for liquidation of corporate debtor under <a href="/doc/181078/">section 33</a>, the moratorium shall cease to have effect from the date of such approval or liquidation order, as the case may be. "</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">12] As can be seen from Clause (a) of Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code, once the adjudicating authority declares moratorium for prohibiting institution of suits or continuation of pending suits or proceeding against the corporate debtor including execution of any judgment, decree or order in any Court of law, Arbitration Tribunal or other authority, the whole emphasis of the arguments of the learned advocate for the respondents 2 to 7 is on the words 'proceedings', 'order' and 'in any Court of law'. It has been submitted that these words do not pcisely restrict its operation to only civil proceedings. The words are omnibus and even include a criminal proceeding including the one under <a href="/doc/1824845/">Section 138</a> of the N.I.Act and a criminal revision arising therefrom.</p>
::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 :::
<p title="Analysis of the law">criwp1437-17 9 13] As is the principle of interptation of Statutes, these words would take colour from words pceding thereto. These words will have to be interpted ejusdem generis with the words 'suits' used earlier thereto. So interpted, the word 'proceedings' used therein and even the words 'order' and 'in Court of law' will have to be interpted as a proceeding arising in the nature of a suit and orders passed in such proceedings and suits. Apart from the fact that the Legislature has not conspicuously used the words 'criminal' as an adjective to the word 'proceedings' and as an adjective to the noun 'Court of law', it must be assumed that the Legislature in its wisdom has consciously omitted to use such adjectives since it must have intended to prohibit only the suits and execution of the judgments and decrees or a proceeding of the like nature. Therefore, applying this principle of interptation, one cannot put any other interptation on this provision contained in <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code except that it only prohibits a suit or a proceeding of a like nature and does not include any criminal proceeding.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">14] In view of such interptation, which is deducible by following the line of reasonings for interpting a similar provision contained in Sub <a href="/doc/657817/">Section 1</a> of <a href="/doc/1537531/">Section 446</a> of the Companies Act in the case of Indorama (supra), the conclusion is inescapable. The criminal revision should not have been directed to be kept in abeyance by resorting to <a href="/doc/865734/">Section 14</a> of the Code. For that matter even the National Company Law Tribunal in its order in a proceeding under <a href="/doc/1907423/">Section 10</a> could not have and ::: Uploaded on - 06/08/2018 ::: Downloaded on - 07/08/2018 01:58:06 ::: criwp1437-17 10 has not specifically directed any prohibition against the continuation of a criminal proceeding. The learned Additional Sessions Judge has committed a gross illegality in directing the criminal revision to be kept in abeyance by the impugned order. The order is not sustainable in law and is liable to be quashed and set aside.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">15] The Writ Petition is allowed. The impugned order passed by the learned Additional Session Judge is quashed and set aside, meaning thereby that the learned Additional Sessions Judge shall proceed with the hearing of the criminal revision and shall decide it in accordance with law.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">16] The Rule is made absolute in above terms.</p>
