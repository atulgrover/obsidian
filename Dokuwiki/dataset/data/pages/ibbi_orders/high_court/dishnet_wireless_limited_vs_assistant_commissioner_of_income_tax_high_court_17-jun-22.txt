<p title="Precedent Analysis">138. In the foregoing paragraphs, we have held that the 2019 Amendment to <a href="/doc/677281/">Section 31</a> of the I&amp;B Code is clarificatory and declaratory in nature and therefore will have a retrospective operation. As such, when the resolution plan is approved by NCLT, the claims, which are not part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and the proceedings related thereto shall stand terminated.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Since the subject-matter of the petition are the proceedings, which relate to the claims of the respondents prior to the approval of the plan, in the light of the view taken by us, the same cannot be continued. Equally the claims, which are not part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">23. The above conclusion was arrived based on the conclusion in Paragraph No.102, wherein the questions framed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supme Court were answered as under:-</p>
<p title="Conclusion">Conclusion.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">102. In the result, we answer the questions framed by us as under:-</p>
<p title="Conclusion">102.1. That once a resolution plan is duly approved by the adjudicating authority under sub-section (1) of <a href="/doc/677281/">Section 31</a>, the claims as provided in the resolution plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on the corporate debtor and its employees, members, creditors, including the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, guarantors and other stakeholders. On the date of approval of resolution plan by the ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 16 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch adjudicating authority, all such claims, which are not a part of resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no person will be entitled to initiate or continue any proceedings in respect to a claim, which is not part of the resolution plan.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">102.2. The 2019 Amendment to <a href="/doc/677281/">Section 31</a> of the I&amp;B Code is clarificatory and declaratory in nature and therefore will be effective from the date on which the I&amp;B Code has come into effect.</p>
<p title="Conclusion">102.3. Consequently all the dues including the statutory dues owed to the Central Government, any State Government or any local authority, if not part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no proceedings in respect of such dues for the period prior to the date on which the adjudicating authority grants its approval under <a href="/doc/677281/">Section 31</a> could be continued.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">24. In <a href="/doc/30560910/">Ghanashyam Mishra &amp; Sons (P) Ltd. Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Co. Ltd.</a>, (2021) 9 SCC 657, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supme Court also held that &ldquo;The legislative intent of making the resolution plan binding on all the stakeholders after it gets the seal of approval from the adjudicating authority upon its satisfaction, that the resolution plan approved by CoC meets the requirement as referred to in sub-section (2) of <a href="/doc/454306/">Section 30</a> is that after the approval of the resolution plan, no surprise claims should be flung on the successful resolution ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 17 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch applicant. The dominant purpose is that he should start with fresh slate on the basis of the resolution plan approved.&rdquo;</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">25. In M/S.Ruchi Soya Industries Ltd. referred to supra, this had given liberty to the petitioner therein to obtain a clarification from the NCLT as to whether the plan included customs duty paid by the petitioner therein on the import under the subject Bill of Entry therein, whereas, in the psent case, the documents reveal that the income tax was not under the contemplation of NCLT.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">26. Upon admission of petitions under <a href="/doc/1849490/">Section 7</a>, there are various important duties and functions entrusted on the Resolution Professional and the Committee of Creditors (COC). The Resolution Professional is required to issue a publication inviting claims from all the stakeholders. He is required to collate information and submit necessary details in the information memorandum. The resolution applicants are required to submit their plans on the basis of the details provided in the information memorandum. The Resolution Plans undergo deep scrutiny by the Resolution Professional as well as Committee of Creditors (COC). ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 18 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">27. Negotiations may be held between Committee of Creditors (COC) and the Resolution Applicant and various modifications may be made so as to ensure that while paying part of the dues of financial creditors as well as operational creditors and other stakeholders, the corporate debtor is revived and is made an on-going concern. After Committee of Creditor (COC) approves the plan, the adjudicating authority is required to arrive at a subjective satisfaction that the plan conforms to the requirements as are provided in Sub-Section (2) to <a href="/doc/454306/">Section 30</a> of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">28. Only thereafter, the adjudicating authority can grant its approval to the plan. It is at this stage that the plan becomes binding on the corporate debtor, its employees, members, creditors, guarantors and other stakeholders involved in the resolution plan. The legislative intent behind this is to freeze all the claims so that the resolution applicant starts on a clean slate and is not flung with any surprise claims. ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 19 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">29. The Resolution Plan submitted on behalf of the petitioners by the Insolvency Resolution Professional under <a href="/doc/454306/">Section 30(6)</a> of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 on 21.05.2019 has not contemplated any concession from the Income Tax Department though Notices under <a href="/doc/1888237/">Section 148</a> of the Income Tax Act, 1961 had already been issued during March, 2018.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">30. Corporate Insolvency Resolution Plan approved under <a href="/doc/677281/">Section 31</a> of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) did not contemplate tax dues under the <a href="/doc/789969/">Income Tax Act, 1961</a>. Further, at the stage, the proceedings under 148 of the Act, 1961 had not crystallized.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">31. The objections of the respective petitioners were also not in the light of the voluntary Corporate Insolvency Resolution Proceedings initiated by the petitioners.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">32. Since the proceedings under the Code were initiated by the petitioners few days prior to the initiation of the proceedings under ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 20 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch <a href="/doc/1888237/">Section 148</a> of the Income Tax Act, 1961, it was incumbent for the petitioners to have ensured proper notice to the Income Tax Department and obtained appropriate concession in Corporate Insolvency Resolution Plan.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">33. That apart, claims of the Income Tax Department were not considered by the NCLT, Mumbai, while approving the Resolution Plan and therefore the question of abetment of such rights of the Income Tax Department cannot be countenanced.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">34. The provisions of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) cannot be interpted in a manner which is inconsistent with any other law in the time being in force.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">35. Therefore, Corporate Insolvency Resolution Plan sanctioned and approved cannot impinge on the rights of the Income Tax Department to pass any fresh Assessment Order under <a href="/doc/1888237/">Section 148</a> read with <a href="/doc/144280341/">Sections 143(3)</a> and <a href="/doc/1837761/">147</a> of the Income Tax Act, 1961. ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 21 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">36. Therefore, the proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) cannot be pssed into service to dilute the rights of the Income Tax Department under the <a href="/doc/789969/">Income Tax Act, 1961</a> to re-open the assessment under <a href="/doc/1888237/">Section 148</a> of the Income Tax Act, 1961.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">37. In my view, the Income Tax Department was not pcluded from reopening the assessment completed under <a href="/doc/144280341/">Section 143(3)</a> of the Income Tax Act,1961.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">38 Therefore, these Writ Petitions filed by these petitioners have to be dismissed. The Assessment Orders which have been passed pursuant to the interim order dated 27.12.2018 are directed to be given to the respective petitioners by the respondent, within a period of thirty days from the date of receipt of a copy of this order.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">39. If the petitioners are so aggrieved by such of those Assessment Orders, the petitioners have to work out their Appellate remedy before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) under <a href="/doc/163666071/">Section 246A</a> of the ______________ https://www.mhc.tn.gov.in/judis Page No 22 of 24  W.P.No.34668 of 2018 etc. batch <a href="/doc/789969/">Income Tax Act, 1961</a>. Since the time for filing appeal would have already expired, liberty is given to the petitioners to file such appeal before the Appellate Commissioner, within a period of thirty days from the date of communication of the Assessments Orders.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">40. These Writ Petitions are dismissed with the observations. No cost. Consequently, connected Miscellaneous Petitions are closed.</p>
