<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">16. <p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">17. Further, the definition of `Allottee', is split into three Categories, broadly</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">- `Plot', `Apartment' and `Building' and the `Purchasers' of these are covered under the term `Allottee' at Paragraph No. 153 of the decision the Hon'ble Supme Court, in Manish Kumar v. <p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">18. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant, refers to the decision of Hon'ble Supme Court in Manish Kumar's case, reported in (2021) 5 SCC, Page 1 and Spl Pg: 98, wherein, at Paragraph No. 158 of the aforesaid decision, it is observed as under:</p>
<p title="Issue">``As to what would constitute a real estate project, it must depend on the terms and conditions and scope of a particular real estate project in which allottees are a part of. These are factual matters to be considered in the facts of each case.''</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">19. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant, contends that the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', has incorrectly interpted the explanation of Section 3 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (16 of 2016), with the Section 2 (zn) of the said RERA Act, while deciding Section 7 of the `Application', the Hon'ble Supme Court in Manish Kumar's case, had although, referred to Section 2 (zn) and Section 3 of the RERA Act, 2016 (vide Paragraph 113 &amp; 114 of Manish Kumar's case), however even then, the Hon'ble Court had opined that `what would constitute a real estate project', will depend on a `fact-based enquiry', on what was offered to the `allottees', to be decided on case-to-case basis. Hence, it is contended on behalf of the Appellant that the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', had passed the `Impugned Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 11 of 67 Order', without going into the facts and circumstances of the instant case, but only relied on `RERA Registrations', secured by the `Corporate Debtor', pursuant to the `RERA Act, 2016'.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">20. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant, comes out with a plea that the `Corporate Debtor', apart from furnishing a `self serving interptation', to the provisions of the `RERA Act, 2016', and the I &amp; B Code, 2016, took the defence, of separate `RERA Registrations', which is an `incorrect' one. Indeed, the provisions of the `RERA Act, 2016', came into force, only on 01.05.2016, much after the creation of `Allotment', in favour of the `Unitholders', and therefore, the `Corporate Debtor', cannot seek shelter, under the provisions of the `RERA', specifically, Section 3 of the said Act, and in this connection, the `Appellant', places reliance upon the `Order', dated 16.06.2023 in Mr. Neerav Bhatnagar &amp; Ors. v. M/s. Sequel Buildcon Private Ltd. &amp; Anr. (vide Paragraph No. 4 of C.P. (IB) No. 555 (ND) / 2021).</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">21. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant submits that a blanket interptation by the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', in the `Impugned Order', holding that the term `Real Estate Project', will be equated with the phase-wise Registration, under `RERA', by the `Corporate Debtor', cannot be sustained and in short, the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', had committed an `error', while equating Section 2 (zn) with Section 3 of the `RERA' Act, 2016.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 12 of 67</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">22. <p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">23. <p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">24. Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 13 of 67</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">25. <p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">26. <p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">27. <p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">29. According to the Appellant, all the `107 Allottees', who were parties to the Section 7 Application of the Code, belong to the `Real Estate', the documents, such as the sanctioned Map of the Project, Agreements, entered between the `Developer' and the `Allottees', Environmental Clearance, `Marketing Brochure' and `Letters', were issued by the Respondent, were placed before the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal' and despite the said documents clearly demonstrating that a `Single Project', in question, namely Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 15 of 67 `Sushant Megapolis', and hence, `all the Applicant Allottees', belong to the same `Real Estate Project', the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', contrary to entering into a `fact based Assessment' of the Terms and Conditions of `Allotment' and `scope' of the `Real Estate Project', as mandated by the Hon'ble Supme Court in Manish Kumar's decision, through the `Impugned Order', dated 06.01.2023, had dismissed the Section 7 Application, filed under the I &amp; B Code, 2016, that the `Applicant Allottees', are not from the same `Real Estate Project', relying mainly on the numerous `RERA Regulations', obtained by the `Corporate Debtor' for different phases of the project `Sushant Megapolis'.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">30. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant by referring to the Paragraph 151 of Manish Kumar's decision of the Hon'ble Supme Court (2021) 5 SCC 1 at Spl Pg: 95, points out that the expansive definition, of real estate project, in Section 2 (zn) was noted, and it was observed by the Hon'ble Supme Court ``it will depend on what is offered by the promoter under the project. It may be real estate project, which seeks to develop a building and sale of the building. It may be a project for the construction of apartments, with the agreements, to convey the undivided interest of land also. It may be a project which envisages converting an existing building or a part into an apartment. It may be a project for merely development of land into plots and sale of the plotted land as such. It Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 16 of 67 may be also that the same person may also develop either apartments or building to be sold''.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">31. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant, contends that, even if a `factual assessment', would have been made by the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', then, the `Applicant Allottees', fail to meet the `threshold', as the `Applicant Allottees', had `purchased different facilities from the Respondent', i.e. some had purchased `plots', and others have purchased `apartments', `without pjudice to the submissions' that `no reasoned fact based Assessment', as mandated by the Hon'ble Supme Court of India in Manish Kumar's case, was made by the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal' and the `Application' was dismissed, solely, on the basis of `several RERA Registrations', procured by the Respondent.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">32. According to the Appellant, as held in the decision of Hon'ble Supme Court in Manish Kumar v. Union of India &amp; Another, reported in (2021) 5 SCC, On-Line SCC 1 (vide Paragraphs 170-173) and was relied upon by this `Tribunal', in Mist Direct Sale Private Ltd. v. Mitin Batra &amp; Ors. (vide CA AT INS No. 1478 / 2022) all that proviso to Section 7 of the I &amp; B Code, provides his `Endorsement' of Section 7 `Application', by 100 or 10% of the `Allottees', whichever is less of the same `Real Estate Project'. In fact, the proviso, nowhere imposes an `embargo on Allottees', having purchased different facilities Viz. Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 17 of 67 `Plot' or `Apartment' from coming together, to meet the `threshold', pscribed under second proviso to Section 7 of the I &amp; B Code, 2016.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">33. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant contends that, `if in case a `Project', comprises of three buildings, which are to be developed, along with the common areas, such as connecting Roads, Parks, Swimming Pools, etc., in different places, then the `Developer', in compliance with Section of the `RERA', will obtain three separate RERA Registrations', for each of the Buildings'.</p>

In fact, the `Appellant' and `other Petitioners', were aware about the requirement of separate Registration of each sub-project under `RERA'.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">93. On behalf of the Respondent / Corporate Debtor, it is pointed out that Section 5(8)(f) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, provides the definition, whereby, the purview of the `Financial Debt' is mentioned, in the teeth of definition of `Allottee' and the `Real Estate Project', and the same is as follows:</p>

<p title="Analysis of the law">Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 52 of 67 1 [Explanation. - For the purposes of this sub-clause, - (i) any amount raised from an allottee under a real estate project shall be deemed to be an amount having the commercial effect of a borrowing; and</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">(ii) the expssions, "allottee" and "real estate project" shall have the meanings respectively assigned to them in clauses (d) and (zn) of section 2 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (16 of 2016);''</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">94. At this stage, the Respondent / Corporate Debtor's side, points out Sections 2(d) &amp; 2(zn) of `RERA', 2016, defining the term `Allottee' and the `Real Estate Project', which proceeds to the following effect:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">``At this juncture, this `Tribunal' points out that the Section 2 (d) of the `Real Estate (Regulation &amp; Development) Act, 2016, defines `Allottee', in relation to a `Real Estate Project', means the person to whom a plot, apartment or buildings, as the case may be, has been allotted, sold (whether as freehold or leasehold) or otherwise transferred by the promoter, and includes the person who subsequently acquires the said allotment through sale, transfer or otherwise but does not include a person to whom such plot, apartment or building, as the case may be, is given on rent.'' (zn) ``real estate project' means the development of a building or a building consisting of apartments, or converting an existing building or a part thereof into apartments, or the development of land into plots or apartment, as the case may be, for the purpose of selling all or some of the said apartments or plots or building, as the case may be, and includes the common areas, the development works, all improvements and structures thereon, and all easement, rights and appurtenance belonging thereto;''</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">95. <p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">96. According to the Respondent / Corporate Debtor, Section 65 (1) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, discourages the use of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, as a `Recovery Mechanism', and even, Section 65 of the `Code', pscribes a `penalty', for those who initiate the `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process' or `Liquidation', `Fraudulently' or with a `Malicious Intent'. The Appellant (Petitioner), and the other Petitioners, who initiated the `Malicious Proceedings' in CP (IB) No. 596 (PB) /2021 (on the File of the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal'), ought to be `Punished', for filing a `Malicious Petition', as per Section 65 of the I &amp; B Code, 2016.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">97. As such, the `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process', is to be initiated, such `Proceedings', ought to be limited just to the `Megapolis Township Project' of the `Corporate Debtor', and not the `Corporate Debtor', in its `Entirety', as per `Order', dated 04.02.2020 of this Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 54 of 67 `Tribunal', in `Flat Buyers Association Winter Hills v. Umang Realtech Pvt. Ltd.' (vide Comp App (AT) (INS) 926 / 2019).</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Gist of Appellant's Rejoinder:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">98. <p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">99. According to the Appellant / 1st Petitioner, the Respondent / Corporate Debtor, was not correct in taking a plea of `Force Majeure', against an `Application', filed under Section 7 of the I &amp; B Code, 2016. In fact, the Learned Counsel for the Appellant refers to the Judgment, dated 11.05.2023 of the Hon'ble Supme Court in the matter of M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank &amp; Ors., (vide Civil Appeal No. 7121 / 2022), wherein, at Paragraph 10, it is observed and held as under:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">10. ``Thus once NCLT is satisfied that the default has occurred, there is hardly a discretion left with NCLT to refuse admission of the application under Section 7. Default is defined under sub-section 12 of Section 3 of the IB Code which reads thus:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">``3. Definitions: - In this Code, unless the context otherwise requires,-</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">.. .. .. .. .. .. .. ..</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">Comp. <p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">106. <p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">112. Indeed, as per definition Section 5(8)(f) explanation (ii) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, the expssions ``allottee'' and ``real estate project'' shall have the meanings respectively assigned to them in clauses (d) and (zn) of Section 2 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, (16 of 2016).</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">113. As a matter of fact, Section 5 (8) (f) &amp; (i) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, defines `Financial Debt', meaning `A debt along with Interest, if any, which is disbursed against the consideration for the time value of Money and includes;</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">``Any amount raised under any other transaction, including any forward sale or purchase agreement, having the commercial effect of a borrowing.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">`[Explanation. - For the purposes of this sub-clause,-</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(i) Any amount raised from an allottee under a real estate project shall be deemed to be an amount having the commercial effect of a borrowing.''</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">114. Section 7 of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, provides for an initiation of `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process', by `Financial Creditor'. The two Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 62 of 67 essential features of an `Admission' of an `Application', under Section 7 of the `Code' are (a) Existence of Debt and (b) Default.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">115. In fact, an `Adjudicating Authority' / Tribunal's jurisdiction is restricted to determine, whether the `Application' is complete and whether, there is any `Debt' and `Default', as per decision in Dr. H.N. Nagaraj vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd., reported in (2018) 148 SCL 447 (NCLAT).</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">116. It is to be remembered that an `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', is not deciding a `Money Claim'. Further, an ``Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', is not a `Civil Court', to decide the aspect of `Violation of Contract', between the `Parties'.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">117. The `proceedings', under the I &amp; B Code, 2016, are not a `Litigation', to be decided by a `Court of Law'. It is not the `Property', which is at the base of the `Code'. It is the `Liquidity', which is the foundation for triggering the `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process'.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">118. No wonder, an `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', is not constituted for the `Recovery of Debt'. It is not out of place for this `Tribunal', to make a significant mention that the provisions of `RERA', are beneficial to the `Home Buyers' and are meant to protect them, from any `Deviant' / `Fraudulent Action'.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 63 of 67</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">119. A mere perusal of Section 3 (1) Explanation of the `Real Estate (Regulation &amp; Development) Act, 2016', under the caption `Prior registration of real estate project with Real Estate Regulatory Authority', points out that where the `real estate project', is to be developed in phases, `every such phases', shall be considered a `standalone real estate project', and the `Promoter', shall obtain Registration, under this Act, for `each phase', separately.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">120. In the instant case, the Respondent / Corporate Debtor, had executed, at least `Three kinds of Agreements, with the `Petitioners' (before the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal') (a) `Plot Allottee Agreement' (b) `Builtup Unit Allottee Agreement' and (c) `Apartment Allottee Agreement'.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">121. One cannot ignore an important fact, that the second proviso to Section 7(1) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, mentions that ``for `financial creditors', who are `allottees', under a `real estate project', an `application', for initiating `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process', against the `Corporate Debtor', shall be filed jointly by not less than one hundred of such `allottees', under the same real estate project or not less than ten percent of the total number of such allottees under the same real estate project, whichever is less''.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">122. Therefore, in the psent case on hand, the foremost aspect to be taken into account is the `RERA Registration' of the `Projects', of the `Corporate Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 64 of 67 Debtor', for ensuring the `initial limit' for `pssing into service' of the ingredients of Section 7 of the `Code'.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">123. It cannot be forgotten that the Respondent / Corporate Debtor's Projects were of `different character', containing terms and conditions therein. Also that, in the instant case, it is a `Township' of more than 1500 Acres with `different type of developments' (Viz. `Plot, Apartments, Builtup Industrial and Commercial', etc.) with independent, `RERA' Registrations, and no phasewise Registrations.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">124. Moreover, the psent Township, comprises of `real estate projects' of different character, Viz. `Plots', `Apartments' (named `Fairway Apartments') in this case, `LIG EWS Builtup', etc., the said `Real Estate Projects', were given different Building Sanctioned Plans, having independent terms. In fact, for the said Sanctioned Plans, being the subject matter of `RERA' Registrations, secured for the said ongoing Projects, under the `Township', different `Approval Letters', for separate `Real Estate Projects', under this Township were issued.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">125. The `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal' in the `Impugned Order', dated 06.01.2023 in CP (IB) No. 596 (PB) / 2021 at Paragraph No. 9 (v), had mentioned that the Respondent / Corporate Debtor, had submitted RERA Registration details, comprising Twenty Five Projects, with `separate RERA Comp. App (AT) (INS) No. 248 / 2023 Page 65 of 67 Registrations', and in the Company Petition, in regard to the `Nine Categories' of the Project, the breakup of the number of Applicants, was shown and in none of the Projects / Project Categories, the Applicants (Petitioners), fulfil the requirement of `Threshold Limit' of `10% or 100 persons', whichever is less.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">126. At this juncture, this `Tribunal', worth recalls and recollects the expssions `allottee' and `real estate project', as per Section 5(8)(f) Explanation (ii) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, which unerringly points out that they shall have the meanings respectively assigned to them in clauses (d) and (zn) of Section 2 of the Real Estate (Regulation &amp; Development Act), 2016 (16 of 2016).</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">127. In the psent case, the Appellant / Petitioner and other Petitioners, who filed CP (IB) No. 596 (PB) / 2021, under Section 7 of the I &amp; B Code, 2016, read with Rule 4 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016, before the `Adjudicating Authority' / `Tribunal', for initiating `Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process', against the `Respondent / Corporate Debtor', are from `different numerous projects', and they have not established their case, as `Creditors of a class', concerning any `particular project', registered with `The Real Estate (Regulation &amp; Development) Act, 2016' (16 of 2016), with a view to fulfil the requirement of `10% or 100 Allottees', as envisaged, as per Section 7 (1) of the I &amp; B Code, 2016.</p>

Accordingly, the instant `Appeal' sans merits.</p>
