<h2>Mr.Nitin Rekhan vs Hightime Marketing Pvt Ltd on 1 October, 2021</h2>
<p>NATIONAL COMPANY LAW APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, PRINCIPAL BENCH, NEW DELHI Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020IN THE MATTER OF:Mr. Nitin Rekhan .... AppellantAged 44 years,S/o S.K. Rekhan,R/0 E-4/23, Model Town-1,New Delhi.VersusHightime Marketing Private Limited .... Respondent71/5465, Reghar Pura, Karol Bagh,New Delhi -110005.PresentFor Appellant: Mr. Manish Kaushik and Mr. Mishal Johari, AdvocatesFor Respondent: None ORDER</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">(Virtual Mode) This appeal has been filed by the Appellant aggrieved by the order dated 04.12.2019 of the Adjudicating Authority (NCLT Delhi) in CP (IB) No. 1145 (PB)/2019.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">2. It has been held by the Adjudicating Authority in the application of the Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020 Page 1 of 8 Appellant under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (in short IBC) for default in payment of interest by the Respondent (Corporate Debtor), that it is not maintainable as the default in payment of interest is not covered under the definition of &bdquo;financial debt‟ and Appellant is not a &bdquo;financial creditor‟ under the definition provided in the IBC.</p>
<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">3. The Appellant has claimed that the Corporate Debtor High Time Marketing Private Limited (Respondent in the appeal) approached the Appellant and received Rs.40 lakhs on 27.10.2010 from the Appellant for issuing shares to him. The share application money was paid in an Account No. 5582000100022401 in Karnataka Bank Limited, Karol Bagh, Delhi. Since no shares could be allotted to the Appellant, the Respondent refunded the principal amount of Rs. 40 lakhs on 9.2.2018 but no interest was paid on the principal amount as is required under Rule 17 of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposit)Rules, 2014. The Appellant has claimed that the interest payable by the Respondent is Rs. 85,35,000 and the default in payment of this alleged financial debt occurred on 9.2.2018 when the principal amount was refunded without the interest.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">4. The Learned Counsel for Appellant was heard on admission on 21.9.2021. He was also given an opportunity to file written submissions within seven days, but no written submissions were filed by the due date. Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020 Page 2 of 8</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">5. The Learned Counsel for Appellant has claimed that the interest on the principal amount is a financial debt and is payable to him by the Respondent under Rule 17 of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposit) Rules, 2014 which should also be refunded. Since the Respondent has not done so, the Appellant has filed application under Section 7 of the IBC which should be admitted and heard.</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">6. In support of his claim that an interest on the principal amount is payable and there is a default, the Learned Counsel for Appellant has cited notice by the Income Tax Department dated 24.9.2018 seeking information under Section 133 (6) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 from Hightime Marketing Private Limited for assessment year AY 2011-12 (attached at page 40 of the Appeal Paperbook). He has also referred to letter no. ROC/DL/COME/2019/IITMEL/AG/109 dated 14.5.2019 to show that he has made a complaint against the Appellant regarding non-payment of interest on the principal amount. In support of his contention about he has shown bank statement (attached at page 39 of the Appeal paperbook) wherein the refund of the principal amount is included in entry dated 9.2.2018. He has also shown bank statement and income tax return copies were sent to the Corporate Debtor by the Appellant through email dated July 22, 2019 (attached at page 41 of the Appeal Paperbook) and the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor for FY 2017-18 to show that his claim is due and payable (page 111 of the Appeal Paperbook).</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020 Page 3 of 8</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">7. The Impugned Order holds that the amount deposited by the Appellant is not covered in the definition of &bdquo;financial debt‟ as provided in the IBC as there was no no time value of money attached with the deposit. The Impugned Order also holds that no financial contract has been produced by the Appellant in compliance of the provisions of the IBC or the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016, either in the original petition or additional affidavits. Therefore, the Adjudicating Authority has held in the Impugned Order that the petitioner is not covered under the expssion &bdquo;financial creditor‟ because it does not answer the description of Section 7 read with Section 5(7) and 5 (8) of the IBC.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">8. We have considered the arguments put forward by the Appellant regarding admission of the appeal. His claim is that the Respondent was liable to repay the deposit along with interest under Sections 73 and 74 of the Companies Act, 2013, and interest was to be paid at the rate of 18% as per Rule 17 of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules 2014.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">9. The notice seeking information sent by the income tax department dated 24.9.2018 (supra),notice issued by the ROC on complaint made dated 14.5.2019 (attached at pg. 51 of Appeal paperbook), bank statement and income tax return Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020 Page 4 of 8 copies sent vide email dated July 22, 2019 and the balance sheet for the year FY 2017-2018 all only go to show that the Appellant is expecting an interest on his deposit, but no financial contract or agreement has been submitted by the Appellant to show that this interest is covered under the definition of &bdquo;financial debt‟ under Section 5(8) and that the Appellant is a &bdquo;financial creditor‟ under Section 5(7) of the IBC. The definition of &bdquo;financial debt‟ and &bdquo;financial creditor‟ provided in IBC is reproduced below: -</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"5. Definitions.-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">xxxxxxxxxx 5(7). "financial creditor" means any person to whom a financial debt is owed and includes a person to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred to.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">5(8) "financial debt" means a debt along with interest, if any, which is disbursed against consideration for the time value of money and includes -</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(a money borrowed against the payment of interest;</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">Under Section 3(11) of IBC, &bdquo;debt‟ is defined as follows: -</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">"3(11) "debt" means a liability or obligation in respect of a claim which is due from any person and includes a financial debt and operational debt."</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">10. It is clear from definition given in Section 3(11) that debt is a much bigger set of financial liabilities, whereas "financial debt" is only a subset of the overall set of debt. Moreover, Section 5(8) defines &bdquo;financial debt‟ to mean a debt along with interest which is disbursed against the consideration for the time value of money, where money should necessarily be borrowed against the condition of time value of money. Under Section 5(7), &bdquo;financial creditor‟ is a person to whom &bdquo;financial debt‟ is owed or to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred to.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">11. The Appellant has nowhere been able to show any document that there Company Appeal (AT) No. 36 of 2020 Page 7 of 8 was a financial contract to borrow by the Corporate Debtor by the Respondent - Corporate Debtor, in which refund of principal amount alongwith interest was included explicitly. We are, therefore, not persuaded by the contention of the Appellant that there was a time value of money attached with the deposited which was established through a prior contract before the principal amount was deposited for purchasing of shares by the Appellant with the Respondent. Moreover, the amount deposited should be a credit to be used by the Respondent-Corporate Debtor for the growth of his enterprise. The Appellant has not given any document or argument to show that the amount deposited by him was to be used by the Respondent for establishing or running his business.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">12. We, therefore, do not think that the impugned order needs any intervention. The appeal, therefore, does not need consideration and is dismissed at the stage of admission. No order as to costs.</p>
