<h2>M/S Gopalpur Ports Ltd vs M/S Sri Avantika Contractors Ltd on 23 July, 2021</h2>
<p>NATIONAL COMPANY LAW APPELLATE TRIBUNAL PRINCIPAL BENCH, NEW DELHI Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020IN THE MATTER OF:M/s Gopalpur Ports Ltd.Gopalpur Port, Arjeepalli,Via Chatrapur Chatrapur Ganjam,Odisha - 761 020Through Authorised Repsentative ...AppellantVersusM/s Sri Avantika Contractor (I) Ltd.610-B, Nilgiri Block, Aditya Enclave,Ameerpet, Hyderabad,Tlangana - 500 016. ...Respondent For Appellant: Mr. Arun Kathpalia, Sr. Advocate with Mr. Rajnish Sinha, Ms. Shreya Kohli, Ms. Akshaya Ganpath and Ms. Kauser Husain, Advocates. For Respondents: Mr. Ajit Kumar Sinha, Sr. Advocate with Mr. Kumar Anurag Singh and Zain A. Khan, Advocates. ORDER</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">(Virtual Mode) 23.07.2021: This Appeal has been filed by the Appellant - Corporate Debtor against the impugned order passed by the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal), Cuttack Bench, Cuttack in CP (IB) No. 159/CTB/2019. The impugned order reads as under:</p>
<p title="Issue">"ORDER Ld. Counsels for the petitioner and respondent are psent. The matter has been part heard. Ld. Counsel for the respondent shall file profit &amp; loss account and Balance sheet of the company 2 for the year 2011-2012 to till date. Post the matter on 27.03.2020 for further hearing."</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">2. The Appellant claims that the Application under Section 9 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (for short 'IBC') has been filed although there were p- existing disputes and even a suit was pending since 2016 and when the Application under Section 9 was being heard, the psent order came to be passed. It is argued by Learned Senior Counsel for the Appellant that if document at page 410 of the Appeal is perused, it can be seen that the Operational Creditor had in February, 2020 written to various authorities including the Hon'ble Prime Minister, wherein in Para 13, it is categorically stated that although the contingent liability was existing, the same was not shown in the Annual Return and thus, the Operational Creditor knows that the Balance Sheets do not reflect such contingent liability but still the psent impugned order is passed. Argument is that if such order is passed, it is in the nature of directions for Discovery of Documents under Order XI Rule 12 of Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC in short) and in that case if Section 424 of Companies Act, 2013 is perused, it would be necessary that there should be an application and there should be order on the same and that the procedure as pscribed under Order XI Rule 12 with regard to Discovery of Documents should have been followed. It is argued that no such application was filed and that it would be necessary that the Adjudicating Authority should record reasons for passing of such orders for discovery. It is stated that the impugned order which has been passed, is not a speaking order and apart from that it is stated that the Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020 3 documents are accessible to the Operational Creditor as can be seen from the notices sent to the various authorities including Hon'ble Prime Minister. It is argued that there are documents on record showing that there are disputes pending between the parties since 2016. The Learned Senior Counsel relied on the judgment in the matter of "Assistant Commissioner vs Shukla &amp; Brothers", 2010 4 SCC 785. Referring to the said judgment, the Learned Senior Counsel for the Appellant vehemently submits that the recording of reasons is mandated by the Hon'ble Supme Court and order which is bereft of reasons should not be maintained. The Learned Senior Counsel has further referred to judgments of Hon'ble High Court of Gujarat to submit that giving reasons for orders in the nature of discovery is also necessary under the provisions of CPC.</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">3. The Learned Senior Counsel submits that Rule 11 of NCLT Rules could not be relied on where there are specific provisions to be followed.</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">4. The Learned Counsel for the Respondent - Operational Creditor referred to synopsis of the Appeal Para 15(e) to submit that the Appellant itself has accepted that the documents concerned directed to be produced are in public domain and are accessible. Learned Counsel for the Respondent submits that there was no reason for the Appellant to carry the appeal only because the Adjudicating Authority had given directions to produce the Balance Sheets. Learned Counsel further referred Para 7.19 of the Appeal to submit that the Appellant is relying on the notice dated 24th February, 2020 that the Annual Returns do not reflect the Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020 4 concerned contingent liability and so the Appellant really had no grievance to file the same.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">5. Having heard Learned Counsel for both sides and having perused the record, it needs to be mentioned that the Application under Section 9 of IBC has been pending before the Adjudicating Authority since December, 2019. Keeping in view Section 9 of the IBC, it was required to be ordered upon in 14 days with Limited Notice to the Corporate Debtor. When above interim order was passed, the matter has been carried to this Tribunal and on 10th August, 2020 relying on the judgment of 'V. Padmakumar Vs. Stressed Assets Stabilisation Fund &amp; Anr.', the appeal was sought to be entertained. This Tribunal had then granted stay to the hearing before the Adjudicating Authority. The Learned Counsel for the Respondent is submitting that subsequently judgment relied on of this Tribunal has been set aside by the Hon'ble Supme Court in 'Assets Reconstruction Company (India) Ltd. vs. Bishal Jaiswal &amp; Anr', Civil Appeal No. 323 of 2021, vide judgment dated 15th April, 2021.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">6. Sub-sections (1) and (2) of Section 424 of Companies Act, 2013, which is relevant may be reproduced:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">"(1) The Tribunal and the Appellate Tribunal shall not, while disposing of any proceeding before it or, as the case may be, an appeal before it, be bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908), but shall be guided by the principles of natural justice, and, subject to the other provisions of this Act 1[or of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (31 of Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020 5 2016)] and of any rules made thereunder, the Tribunal and the Appellate Tribunal shall have power to regulate their own procedure.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(2) The Tribunal and the Appellate Tribunal shall have, for the purposes of discharging their functions under this Act 1[or under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (31 of 2016)], the same powers as are vested in a civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (5 of 1908), while trying a suit in respect of the following matters, namely:--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(a) summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person and examining him on oath;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(b) requiring the discovery and production of documents;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(c) receiving evidence on affidavits;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(d) subject to the provisions of sections 123 and 124 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (1 of 1872), requisitioning any public record or document or a copy of such record or document from any office;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(e) issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses or documents;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(f) dismissing a repsentation for default or deciding it ex parte;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(g) setting aside any order of dismissal of any repsentation for default or any order passed by it ex parte; and</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(h) any other matter which may be pscribed."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020 6</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">7. It is clear from the above that the Adjudicating Authority which is basically dealing with the matter in summary manner, is not bound by the procedure laid down by the Code of Civil Procedure but shall be guided by the principles of natural justice and can regulate its own procedure. Even if Order XI Rule 12 of CPC may be pscribing that any party may without filing affidavit apply to the Court for Discovery of Documents, the same does not stop the Adjudicating Authority from regulating own procedure reading sub-section (2) with sub-section (1) if the Adjudicating Authority in the course of arguments finds that it needs to peruse particular documents. In the psent matter the Adjudicating Authority was not dealing with any application filed or sought by the Operational Creditor but while hearing the arguments in the matter which was on p-admission stage and not yet admitted, the Adjudicating Authority found it necessary to go through the Profit and Loss account and Balance Sheets of the Company. As such it passed the impugned order. The same can be even said to be within Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules. At interlocutory stage, while hearing the parties, the Adjudicating Authority required to peruse the documents and passed the impugned order. In this view of the matter, we do not find that there was any reason to carry the psent Appeal. The Application under Section 9 of IBC has been pending for long and against the spirit of provisions of IBC. The delay must be affecting the maximization of value of assets. The merits of the application under Section 9 have yet not been adjudicated and decided and so we are not entering in the issue whether there was a p-existing dispute.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 674 of 2020 7</p>
<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">8. The Learned Counsel for the Respondent submits that judgment in the matter of "Assistant Commissioner vs Shukla &amp; Brothers" (Supra) was in the context of regular judgment disposing matter and thus would not be helpful in the psent matter.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">9. Perusal of Para 19 of the judgment of Hon'ble Supme Court shows that Orders of Court must reflect what weighed with the Court in granting or declining relief claimed by the Applicant. These observations are binding. However, in the psent matter the Adjudicating Authority was not granting or declining relief. It is only an order at interlocutory stage of hearing the matter, where the Adjudicating Authority having power to regulate its own procedure gave a direction to produce particular documents. The impugned order, as reproduced above, speaks for itself of the mind of the Adjudicating Authority. It is apparent that for hearing the matter, the Adjudicating Authority needed a particular document which is even otherwise admittedly a public document. If that was so, there was no good reason to file the psent appeal and to raise a dispute as has been raised. It has only prolonged the IBC proceeding against spirit of IBC.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">10. We do not find any substance in the Appeal. The Appeal is dismissed. Interim orders in Appeal stand withdrawn.</p>
