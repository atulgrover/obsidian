<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">1. The Appeal has been filed by Mr. Anshul Vashistha, who is the Director of the Suspended Board of Directors of the Corporate Debtor- M/s. Saturn Prefab India Pvt. Ltd., (Respondent No.2) under Section 61 of the 'Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016' (for short 'Code') against the impugned order dated 13.03.2020 passed by 'Adjudicating Authority' ('National Company law Tribunal, Indore Bench at Ahmedabad') in CP (IB) No. 374 /9/NCLT/AHM/ 2018. The Operational Creditor is M/s. Jayhind Steel Traders (Respondent No.1).</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">2. The Adjudicating Authority has initiated 'Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process' (for short 'CIRP') against the Corporate Debtor in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Code and has appointed Mr. Gopal Krishana Saraswat, 'Interim Resolution Professional' with a direction to follow the provisions of Section 13, 14 and other relevant provisions of the Code.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">3. Initiation of CIRP is based on the following findings:-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue">a) Existence of operational debt is above Rs. One Lac;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue">b) Debt is due;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue">c) Default has occurred on 30.11.2016;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue">d) Petition has been filed within the limitation period as the date of default is 30.11.2016 and the petition has been filed on 11.07.2018.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue">e) Existence of any dispute, prior to the notice issued by the Operational Creditor is not found.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">(Para 17 of the impugned order dated 13.03.2020).</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">f) Defaulted amount is Rs. 1,81,61,422.00 as per respective invoices enclosed with the Petition.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">g) Date of invoice is between April 2016 to March 2018.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">h) Date of first default is 30.11.2016.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">4. The Corporate Debtor/Respondent No.2 is engaged in the business of manufacturing of p-fabricated shed, industrial roofing sheet, p-engineering building etc., for which it requires various material produced by the Respondent at various sites.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">5. The Operational Creditor/Respondent No.1 is engaged in the business of supply of Angel, Channel, TMP, GP/CR/GC Sheet.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">6. The Respondent No.1 used to supply material to Respondent No.2 for completing its various projects at various sites and it was a normal business practice to seek delivery of required material at project site itself. It was also usual practice between the parties to ask its clients to make direct payment to the Respondent No.1 suppliers.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">The Operational Creditor vide email dated 05.02.2018 has sent an email to the Corporate Debtor asking for payment of outstanding bills and has even asked for PDC Cheque for all bills. The Respondent No.1/Corporate Debtor has responded to the same on 29.03.2018 to the Operational Creditor "Your ledger is not matching with our ledger and TC also not came with the vehicle. <p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 4 The Appellant has also submitted that the outstanding amount of Rs.1,81,61,422/- is incorrect. <p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">10. The IRP has issued public announcement on 18.06.2020 and has constituted Committee of Creditors (for short 'CoC') on 08.07.2020.</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">11. We have heard parties. It is not in dispute that both the operational creditor and the corporate debtor were maintaining Running Account.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">12. It is also observed that the operational creditor is making all attempts to realize the payment and conveniently used I &amp; B Code, 2016 for faster realization.</p>

<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">14. On going through the various submissions made by the parties, a clarity is emerging that there are multiple date of default, there is an existence of dispute on balance dues, the actual amount due needs to be reconciled and reflected.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">15. The Operational Creditor is claiming that the Debt due is more than Rs. 1 lac. If, it may also be due for payment as it will be becoming the job of IRP to reconcile and get the disputed amount segregated and the claim can be counted provided the application meets the criteria of Section 8 &amp; 9 of the Code. The Object of the Code is not recovery of money but to bring out of insolvency and maximization of value of assets of the Corporate Debtor. It is also very much clear that if there is a dispute as per relevant provisions of the Code, it is incumbent on the Adjudicating Authority to reject the petition/application as per the provisions of the Section 9 of the Code. It is also very much clear in this case that there is a dispute of the Debt and dispute resolution mechanism is also provided in the purchase order. Since the I&amp;B Code, 2016 debars the application of the Code for recovery of money as well as if there is a dispute then also petition/application requires to be rejected.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">16. The Hon'ble Supme Court has already held in Mobilox Innovations Pvt. Ltd., Vs. Kirusa Software pvt. Ltd. [2018] 1 SCC 353, that IBC is not intended to be a substitute for recovery forum. It is also laid down that wherever there is existence of real dispute, the IBC provisions cannot be invoked. Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 9</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">17. For better and fuller appciation of the psent subject matter in issue, it is useful for this Tribunal to make a pertinent reference to Section 8 &amp; 9 of the Code which provides mechanism for Operational Creditor, which runs as under:</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law">Section 8 - Insolvency Resolution by Operational Creditor:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law">8. (1) An operational creditor may, on the occurrence of a default, deliver a demand notice of unpaid operational debtor copy of an invoice demanding payment of the amount involved in the default to the corporate debtor in such form and manner as may be pscribed.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(2) The corporate debtor shall, within a period of ten days of the receipt of the demand notice or copy of the invoice mentioned in sub-section (1) bring to the notice of the operational creditor--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(a) existence of a dispute, if any, and record of the pendency of the suit or arbitration proceedings filed before the receipt of such notice or invoice in relation to such dispute;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(b) the repayment of unpaid operational debt--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(i) by sending an attested copy of the record of electronic transfer of the unpaid amount from the bank account of the corporate debtor; or</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(ii) by sending an attested copy of record that the operational creditor has encashed a cheque issued by the corporate debtor. Explanation.--For the purposes of this section, a "demand notice"</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">means a notice served by an operational creditor to the corporate Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 10 debtor demanding repayment of the operational debt in respect of which the default has occurred.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">Section 9-application for initiation of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process by Operational Creditor:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">Section 9 -. (1) After the expiry of the period of ten days from the date of delivery of the notice or invoice demanding payment under sub-section (1) of section 8, if the operational creditor does not receive payment from the corporate debtor or notice of the dispute under sub-section (2) of section 8, the operational creditor may file an application before the Adjudicating Authority for initiating a corporate insolvency resolution process.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(2) The application under sub-section (1) shall be filed in such form and manner and accompanied with such fee as may be pscribed.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(3) The operational creditor shall, along with the application furnish--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(a) a copy of the invoice demanding payment or demand notice delivered by the operational creditor to the corporate debtor;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(b) an affidavit to the effect that there is no notice given by the corporate debtor relating to a dispute of the unpaid operational debt;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(c) a copy of the certificate from the financial institutions maintaining accounts of the operational creditor confirming that there is no payment of an unpaid operational debt by the corporate debtor; and Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 11</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(d) such other information as may be specified.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(4) An operational creditor initiating a corporate insolvency resolution process under this section, may propose a resolution professional to act as an interim resolution professional. (5) The Adjudicating Authority shall, within fourteen days of the receipt of the application under sub-section (2), by an order--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(i) admit the application and communicate such decision to the operational creditor and the corporate debtor if,--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(a) the application made under sub-section (2) is complete;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(b) there is no repayment of the unpaid operational debt;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(c) the invoice or notice for payment to the corporate debtor has been delivered by the operational creditor;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(d) no notice of dispute has been received by the operational creditor or there is no record of dispute in the information utility;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">and</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(e) there is no disciplinary proceeding pending against any resolution professional proposed under sub-section (4), if any</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(ii) reject the application and communicate such decision to the operational creditor and the corporate debtor, if--</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(a) the application made under sub-section (2) is incomplete;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(b) there has been repayment of the unpaid operational debt;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(c) the creditor has not delivered the invoice or notice for payment to the corporate debtor;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(d) notice of dispute has been received by the operational creditor or there is a record of dispute in the information utility; or Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 12</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(e) any disciplinary proceeding is pending against any proposed resolution professional:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">Provided that Adjudicating Authority, shall before rejecting an application under subclause (a) of clause (ii) give a notice to the applicant to rectify the defect in his application within seven days of the date of receipt of such notice from the adjudicating Authority. (6) The corporate insolvency resolution process shall commence from the date of admission of the application under sub-section (5) of this section.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">18. Since Hon'ble Apex Court has clearly laid down the mechanism to be operated by Operational Creditor in terms of Section 8 &amp; 9 of the Code, it is very clear that the undisputed debt is sine qua non of initiating CIRP as also the debt should be due and payable. Since, the order of Adjudicating Authority in the psent case, as enumerated above, does not meet the above criteria and hence the appeal needs to be allowed. We are not passing any comment on the merit of the dispute between the parties and the parties are free to approach appropriate forum for recovery or dispute resolution.</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">19. In a case of running account where accounts are yet to be reconciled and settled, an email like 05.02.2018 sent before Section 8 demand notice dated 13.06.2018 asking Operational Creditor to take back the rejected material reflect p-existing dispute in such case Adjudicating Authority cannot sit down to settle the account and calculate the Debt dues.</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">20. In view of the above the appeal is allowed. We set aside the impugned order dated 13.03.2020 passed by the Adjudicating Authority ('National Company Law Tribunal, Indore bench at Ahmedabad') and consequently order passed by the Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 656 of 2020 13 Adjudicating Authority appointing 'Interim Resolution Professional', declaring moratorium, freezing of accounts including consequential actions taken by the 'Interim Resolution Professional' like publishing in newspapers, constitution of committee of creditors etc., are declared illegal and set aside. The Corporate Debtor is released from all the rigour of law and is allowed to function independently through its Board of Directors from immediate effect. The Corporate Debtor will, in the first instance, bear CIRP costs so far incurred by IRP/RP &amp; then entitled to recover it from Operational Creditor. No order as to costs.</p>
