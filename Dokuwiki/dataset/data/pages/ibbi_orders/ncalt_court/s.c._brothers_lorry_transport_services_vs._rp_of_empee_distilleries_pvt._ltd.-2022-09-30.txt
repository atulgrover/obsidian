<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(e) This `Tribunal'[ also would like to consider its own earlier Judgment in case of Santanu T. Ray vs Tata Capital Financial Services in CA(AT)No. 951 of 2021 on the same issue:</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 289 of 2022 16 of 23 "Para 12(vii) The Regulation 12(2) contemplates a scenario where claim is not filed within the time stipulated in the public announcement. It is in this scenario this regulation permits filing of the claim on or before 90th day of insolvency commencement date. Prior to amendment, the extended term to file claim was till the approval or resolution plan by the committee of creditors. The said time limit was curtailed and restricted to 90 days from the insolvency commencement date, vide the said amendment of 2018. Therefore, regulation fixes the outer limit for filing of the claim if it is filed after delay. This in fact curtails the power to further condone the delay or to further extend the time. If the delay in the psent case is condoned then the same will nullify the amendment made to Regulation 12(2)."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(f) In the instant `Appeal', before this Tribunal, the 'Adjudicating Authority' approved the 'Resolution Plan' on 20.01.2020, and has taken umbrage of the ratio laid down by the Hon'ble Supme Court of India in "Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited - (2021) 9 SCC 657". The Hon'ble Supme Court of India had made the observations on the "Clean Slate Theory" and stated that "if additional liabilities are allowed to be imposed on the Successful Resolution Applicant after the approval of the plan, the entire plan would become unworkable". The Hon'ble Supme Court had mentioned that such surprise `Debts' cannot be put upon the `Resolution Applicant', which were not laid down in its `Resolution Plan'. If that is Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 289 of 2022 17 of 23 allowed, the very calculations on which the `Resolution Applicants' rely to submit their `Resolution Plans', would go awry.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(g) The Hon'ble Supme Court of India had given emphasis that the 'Successful Resolution Applicant' should start with a `clean slate', on the basis of its `Approved Plan'</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(h) This `Tribunal' also notices that the amended provision as contained in Section 31 of I &amp; B Code, 2016 makes the 'Successful Resolution Plan' binding on all concerned, including the Government'. It is held in the judicial pronouncement that this particular Amendment to be `clarificatory in nature' and hence, retroactive / retrospective in operation. Therefore, any Claim, even if it pertains to a date, prior to the effective date of this Amendment, would not be entertained after the 'Resolution Plan' is so approved.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis">(i) In view of facts of the case as discussed above, the provisions in the I &amp; B Code, 2016 and cited Judgments of the Hon'ble Supme Court of India, this `Tribunal' upholds the decision of the 'Resolution Professional' adjudicated by the 'Adjudicating Authority' to reject the `Claims' of the 'Appellant' on the basis that claims are `unsupported' and `documentary evidence' furnished by the 'Appellant' were not proper and do not find any error in the 'Impugned Order' on this issue.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">Issue No. (II) Whether the relief sought by the 'Appellant' for the payment of Rs. 18,30,894/- towards claim from the `Contingency Fund' admissible?</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 289 of 2022 18 of 23</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">(a) `Contingency Fund' means a provision created in a 'Resolution Plan' to avoid `inevitable losses of creditors'. It is made to include `Liabilities' arising which are `uncertain' and `inevitable in nature' or pending under `verification'.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(b) The I &amp; B Code 2016, provides for collective Resolution Mechanism and is a Time Bound 'Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process'. No wonder, `Speed' is the essence of I &amp; B Code 2016. The Code 2016 is enacted for the sole purpose of reviving the 'Corporate Debtor' and help it, to get back on its Feet, and was not meant for recovery enforcement purpose/procedure. Maintaining a `Claim' does not necessitate or carry any nexus between the commission of a `Default' and the submission of a `Claim'. A Creditor can exercise his right to payment or to remedy for the matured, unmatured, secured, unsecured, Disputed or Undisputed Debts.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(c) When the 'Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process' is initiated, the claims of `multiple Creditors' of different nature are invited, assessed and verified by the 'Resolution Professional' and then, are included in the 'Resolution Plan'. In I &amp; B Code, 2016 to recover the `Debt' from the 'Corporate Debtor', it is necessary for a `Claimant' to be a 'Creditor'. While definite, identified or ascertained Claims can be easily accounted for, the problem lies when the Claims which are either inevitable or contingent ones.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">(d) Regulation 14 of 'Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India' (Insolvency Resolution Process for the 'Corporate Person') Regulations (IBBI) 2016 Chapter-IV- Proof of Claims, provides for determination of claim which are not pcise and contingent and it is evident from the language of the code that it mandates the 'Resolution Professional' to make best possible Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 289 of 2022 19 of 23 estimate and create a provision for the abovesaid `Contingency Fund' in the 'Resolution Plan'.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">"Regulation 14- Determination of amount of claim.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">14. (1) Where the amount claimed by a creditor is not pcise due to any contingency or other reason, the interim resolution professional or the resolution professional, as the case may be, shall make the best estimate of the amount of the claim based on the information available with him.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(2) The interim resolution professional or the resolution professional, as the case may be, shall revise the amounts of claims admitted, including the estimates of claims made under sub-regulation (1), as soon as may be practicable, when he comes across additional information warranting such revision."</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">[emphasis supplied]</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">(e) The 'Resolution Plan' is a `Rehabilitation Plan' for the 'Corporate Debtor' going in `Insolvency'. It is ppared, based on the 'Information Memorandum', provided by the 'Resolution Professional', targeting - `Legal, Financial, Management and Technical strategies', in order to bring the 'Corporate Debtor' back on its feet at the earliest point of time. As per Regulation 37 (mentioned hereunder), it empowers the 'Resolution Professional' to be `Flexible' in ascertaining and including `Claims of Creditors' and it is not limited only to the Clauses contained therein.</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(b) sale of all or part of the assets whether subject to any security interest or not;</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(d) satisfaction or modification of any security interest;</p>

<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(f) This `Tribunal' has noticed the averments that the `Contingency Fund' was maintained for a period of `6 months' from the Date of Approval of the 'Resolution Plan'. The 'Appellant' could not substantiate his `Claims', by furnishing the necessary supporting documents in respect of the Claims, so made.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">(g) This `Tribunal' notices that:-</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion">&bull; The 'Resolution Plan' as approved by the 'Adjudicating Authority' was successfully implemented;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">&bull; The 'Successful Resolution Applicant' had taken over the management;</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">&bull; The 'Monitoring Committee' had ceased to `Exist'.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">(h) This `Tribunal' pertinently points out that the `Contingency Fund' is for the specific purpose to cater for `Claims' which `were not Determined and settled finally', at the time of the 'Resolution Plan'. There is a stipulated time frame as provided in the 'Resolution Plan' and the `Fund' after meeting out the requirements of the `Claims', ceased to exist. By no stretch of imagination, it can be inferred that any `Claim can be entertained' after the `Resolution Plan', was fully implemented and the new management of the 'Successful Resolution Applicant' had taken over.</p>
<p style="padding-left: 30px;" title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">(i) This `Tribunal' upholds the decision of the 'Adjudicating Authority' to be a correct and proper one, on this issue.</p>

Consequently, the `Appeal' is devoid of merits and it fails.</p>
