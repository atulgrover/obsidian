	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		2. Section 61 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as the 'I&amp;B Code') enables an aggrieved person to pfer an Appeal against any order passed by the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal), reads as follows: -
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		"61. Appeals and Appellate Authority.--(1) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained under the Companies Act 2013, any person aggrieved by the order of the Adjudicating Authority under this part may pfer an appeal to the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(2) Every appeal under sub-section (1) shall be filed within thirty days before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal:
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		Provided that the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal may allow an appeal to be filed after the expiry of the said period of thirty days if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing the appeal but such period shall not exceed fifteen days.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(3) An appeal against an order approving a resolution plan under section 31 may be filed on the following grounds, namely:--
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(i) the approved resolution plan is in contravention of the provisions of any law for the time being in force;
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(ii) there has been material irregularity in exercise of the powers by the resolution Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1254 of 2019 Page 2 of 3 professional during the corporate insolvency resolution period;
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(iii) the debts owed to operational creditors of the corporate debtor have not been provided for in the resolution plan in the manner specified by the Board;
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(iv) the insolvency resolution process costs have not been provided for repayment in priority to all other debts; or
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(v) the resolution plan does not comply with any other criteria specified by the Board."
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		(4) An appeal against a liquidation order passed under section 33 may be filed on grounds of material irregularity or fraud committed in relation to such a liquidation order."
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		3. In the psent case, as no order is under challenge, the psent Appeal is not maintainable. The Appellant - 'Committee of Creditors' should remember that it is not a 'Writ Court' for making a prayer for a 'direction' to any of the party, which can be pferred only before the Adjudicating Authority and not before the Appellate Tribunal. The Appeal is accordingly dismissed, being not maintainable.
	</p>
