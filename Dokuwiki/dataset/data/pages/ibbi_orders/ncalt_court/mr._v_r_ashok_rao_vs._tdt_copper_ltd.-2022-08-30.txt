	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		This larger bench has been constituted to answer following two questions referred by a three-member bench of this Tribunal by its order dated 12.08.2022:
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		(a) Whether the law laid down by this Tribunal in "Mr. Jitendra Virmani Vs. MRO-TEK Realty Ltd. &amp; Ors" and three Member Bench Judgement in "Arul Muthu Kumaara Samy Vs. Registrar of Companies" that when the defect in appeal is cured and the Appeal is refiled before the Appellate Tribunal beyond seven days, the date of re-psentation of the Appeal shall be treated as a fresh Appeal, lays down correct law?
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		(b) Whether the limitation pscribed for filing an Appeal before this Appellate Tribunal under Section 61 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 or Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013 shall also govern the period under which a defect in the Appeal is to be cured and this Appellate Tribunal shall have no jurisdiction to condone the delay in refiling/re-psentation if it is beyond the limitation pscribed in Section 61 of the IBC or Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		2. The question of refiling delay is involved in all these Appeals. We have heard learned counsel for the parties appearing in the above appeals Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -4- on the two questions as noted above. For answering the questions, it shall be sufficient to notice the facts pertaining to refiling delay in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780 of 2022.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		3. The Appeal has been filed against the Order dated 11.03.2022 passed by the National Company Law Tribunal, New Delhi Bench-V in CP (IB) No. 1093/(ND)/2022 by which application filed under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as 'Code') by the Financial Creditor has been dismissed. The Appeal was psented in the office of this Tribunal on 13.04.2022. After scrutiny of the memo of the appeal defects were intimated to the Appellant on 19.04.2022. The Appellant refiled the memo of appeal on 08.06.2022. There being refiling delay of 43 days in appeal, the Registrar by office note dated 12.07.2022 placed the matter before the Court under the heading 'For Admission (Fresh Case) with defects'. A three-member bench heard the counsel for the parties on the question of refiling delay and expssed its doubt on two earlier judgments delivered by this Tribunal "Mr. Jitendra Virmani Vs. MRO-TEK Realty Ltd. &amp; Ors., (2017) SCC Online NCLAT 7" and "Arul Muthu Kumaara Samy Vs. Register of Companies, (2020) SCC OnLine NCLAT 671", resulting into reference to larger bench on the two questions, as noted above.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		4. Similarly, in all other three appeals which have been heard alongwith Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780 of 2022, there is refiling delay. 	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		5. Learned counsel for the Appellant in support of their 'Application for Condonation of Refiling Delay' submits that as per Rule 26 of NCLAT Rules, 2016, the Registrar is fully empowered to pass appropriate orders if there is failure in rectifying the defects within the 7 days. The scheme of Rule 26 does not indicate that if the defects are not removed within seven days, the re-psentation of the appeal after removal of defects beyond 7 days shall be treated as fresh appeal. The first psentation of the appeal is the date which is to be reckoned for the purpose of computation of limitation in filing of appeal and the re-psentation of the appeal has no bearing on the question of computation of limitation. The judgments of this Tribunal in "Mr. Jitendra Virmani Vs. MRO-TEK Realty Ltd. &amp; Ors" and "Arul Muthu Kumaara Samy Vs. Register of Companies" holding that when the defects in the appeal is cured after 7 days and the appeal is re- psented, the appeal shall be treated as a fresh appeal does not lay down correct law. Learned counsel for the Appellant further submits that filing of appeal and re-psentation of the appeal with the Registry are two different concepts which have two different consequences. Under the Code or Rules, no limitation has been provided for re-psentation (refiling). The period of limitation provided under Section 61 of the Code and Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013 are applicable with regard to filing of the appeal and the said limitation cannot be applied for the purpose of re-psentation (refiling) of the appeal. When the Legislature and Rule making Authority Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -6- have not provided any limitation for re-psentation/refiling, no period of limitation can be imported on the basis of limitation which is provided for filing of an appeal.
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		6. Learned counsel for the Respondent refuting the submissions of learned counsel for the Appellant contends that the defects in appeal has to be cured within the period of 7 days as provided under Rule 26 Sub-rule (2) and if the defects are not cured within 7 days and appeal was re- psented thereafter it should be treated as fresh filing and from the date of fresh filing, the limitation for filing appeal has to be computed. It is submitted that any delay in re-psentation of the appeal, if it is beyond the limitation pscribed for filing an appeal under Section 61 of the Code or Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013 shall also be applicable on the re-psentation of the appeal which has been re-psented beyond 7 days provided for removal of defects. In event, defects are not cured within the time fixed for the same, the Registrar is obliged to decline to register the appeal and after such rejection to register the appeal, Appellant has to file appeal afresh and with regard to such appeal limitation has to be computed from the said fresh filing. The limitation pscribed for filing an appeal under Section 61 and Section 421 is a limitation pscribed by statute and there shall be no power to condone the delay on re-psentation which is beyond the limitation pscribed for filing of appeal under Section 61 of the Code and Section 421 of the Companies Act, 2013. Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -7-
	</p>

	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		8. The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal Rules, 2016 (NCLAT Rules) has been framed under Section 469 of the Companies Act, 2013 for carrying out the provision of the Companies Act, 2013. Part III of the Rules deals with 'Institution of Appeals - Procedure'. Rule 22 deals with 'Presentation of Appeal'. Rule 22 is as follows:
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		"22. Presentation of appeal.- (1) Every appeal shall be psented in Form NCLAT-1 in triplicate by the appellant or petitioner or applicant or respondent, as the case may be, in person or by his duly authorised repsentative duly appointed in this behalf in the pscribed form with stipulated fee at the filing counter and non-compliance of this may constitute a valid ground to refuse to entertain the same.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(2) Every appeal shall be accompanied by a certified copy of the impugned order.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(3) All documents filed in the Appellate Tribunal shall be accompanied by an index in triplicate containing their details and the amount of fee paid thereon.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(4) Sufficient number of copies of the appeal or petition or application shall also be filed for service on the opposite party as pscribed.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law">
		(5) In the pending matters, all other applications shall be psented after serving copies thereof in advance on the opposite side or his advocate or authorised repsentative.
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -8- (6) The processing fee pscribed by the rules, with required number of envelopes of sufficient size and notice forms as pscribed shall be filled along with memorandum of appeal."
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		9. Rule 26 deals with 'Endorsement and Scrutiny of Petition or Appeal or Document', which rule has come up for consideration and interptation in the psent appeal. Rule 26 is as follows:
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		"26. Endorsement and scrutiny of petition or appeal or document.-(1) The person in charge of the filing-counter shall immediately on receipt of appeal or document affix the date and stamp of the Appellate Tribunal thereon and also on the additional copies of the index and return the acknowledgement to the party and he shall also affix his initials on the stamp affixed on the first page of the copies and enter the particulars of all such documents in the register after daily filing and assign a diary number which shall be entered below the date stamp and thereafter cause it to be sent for scrutiny.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		(2) If, on scrutiny, the appeal or document is found to be defective, such document shall, after notice to the party, be returned for compliance and if there is a failure to comply within seven days from the date of return, the same shall be placed before the Registrar who may pass appropriate orders.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		(3) The Registrar may for sufficient cause return the said document for rectification or amendment to the party filing the same, and for this purpose may allow to the party concerned such reasonable time as he may consider necessary or extend the time for compliance.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -9- (4) Where the party fails to take any step for the removal of the defect within the time fixed for the same, the Registrar may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, decline to register the appeal or pleading or document."
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		10. Sub-rule (2) of Rule 26 contemplates that if, on scrutiny, the appeal or document is found to be defective, such document shall, after notice to the party, be returned for compliance and if there is any failure to comply within seven days from the date of return, the same shall be placed before the Registrar who may pass appropriate orders. Sub-rule (3) empowers the Registrar to return the document for rectification or amendment, to the party and for which purpose he may allow to the party such reasonable time as he may consider necessary or extend the time for compliance. The power under Rule 26(3) is to be exercised after scrutiny under Sub-rule (2) when a defect is pointed out to the Appellant and there is failure to comply within 7 days from the date of return. Sub-rule (2) and (3) does not indicate that any penal consequences have been provided for removal of defects after 7 days. When specific power is there under Sub-rule (3) of Rule 26 to extend the time for compliance, the period of 7 days cannot be said to be mandatory period. The scheme of Rule 26 also does not indicate that when the appeal is re-psented after removal of the defects after 7 days, the appeal is to be treated as an appeal filed afresh.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">

	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		16. The judgment of this Tribunal in 'Jitendra Virmani' in Para 17, as noted above, has observed that if defects are not removed within 7 days and defects are removed after 7 days, the appeal is treated to be a fresh appeal. The judgment does not elaborate as to what is the basis for coming to the conclusion that when the defects are removed after 7 days and appeal is re-psented thereafter, the appeal would be treated as fresh appeal. The above conclusion is contrary to the scheme of Rule 26, as noticed above. The second three-member bench of this Tribunal in "Arul Muthu Kumaara Samy Vs. Register of Companies" (supra) has relied on judgment of 'Jitendra Virmani' and no separate reasoning has been given as to why appeal shall be treated as fresh appeal if it is filed beyond 7 days period pscribed for removal of defects.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		17. The filing of appeal (psentation) and refiling (re-psentation) are two different concepts and have been separately dealt with in the Rules. Rule 22 deals with the psentation of the appeal whereas Rule 26 envisages the re-psentation of the appeal after removal of the defects notified to the Appellant. Section 61(2) of the Code provides that appeal Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -15- under Section 61(1) shall be filed within 30 days before the NCLAT. The expssion 'filing' as occurring in Section 61(2) is for filing of the appeal/psentation of the appeal.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		18. Hon'ble Supme Court in "Indian Statistical Institute vs. M/s Associated Builders &amp; Ors, (1978) 1 SCC 483" laid down that condonation of delay as pscribed in an appeal is different from petition for excusing the delay in re-psentation. In Para 11 following has been laid down:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		"11. In a recent judgment of this Court delivered on August 3, 1977 in Mahant Bikram Dass v. Financial Commissioner and Ors.,(1) it is pointed out that the petition under section 5 of the Limitation Act seeking to condone the delay in pferring an appeal is different from a petition for excusing the delay in re-psentation."
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		19. Division Bench of Delhi High Court in "S.R. Kulkarni vs. Birla VXL Ltd., (1998) 3 RCR (Civil) 436" had also laid down that question of condonation of delay in refiling has to be considered from a different angle and view point as compared to consideration of condonation of delay in initial filing. Following observations have been made by the Delhi High Court in Para 7 of the judgment:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		"7. Notwithstanding which of the aforesaid Rules are applicable, the question of condensation of delay in refiling of an application has to be considered from a different angle and viewpoint as compared to consideration of condensation of delay in initial filing. The delay in refiling is not Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -16- subject to the rigorous tests which are usually applied in excusing the delay in a petition filed under Section 5 of the Limitation Act (See Indian Statistical Institute Vs. M/s. Associated Builders and others AIR 1978 Supme Court 335)."
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		20. The judgment of Hon'ble Supme Court which need to be noticed is the judgment reported in "(2017) 11 SCC 234, Northern Railway vs. Pioneer Publicity Corporation Pvt. Ltd." which was a case where learned single judge of Delhi High Court has refused to condone delay of 65 days in refiling the objections under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. Deputy Registrar of the Delhi High Court granted 7 days' time on 23.01.2013 to remove the defects in the objections which was not done. The Appellant refiled the matter on 21.03.2013 much beyond the 7 days' time as allowed by Deputy Registrar. The High Court refused to condone the delay, against which matter was taken before the Hon'ble Supme Court. A contention was raised before the Supme Court that refiling beyond the period pscribed under Section 34(3) is not permissible. The said submission was rejected and the Hon'ble Supme Courte laid down that Section 34(3) has no application in refiling the petition which only applies to the initial filing. It is useful to extract the entire judgment of the Hon'ble Supme Court:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		"ORDER
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		1. Leave granted. The appellant is aggrieved by the decision of the Delhi High Court dated 1.9.2015 in FAO (OS)NO.436 OF 2015 refusing to condone a period of 65 days in re-filing the Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -17- objections under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 (for short 'Act'). The award was delivered on 29.10.2012. Admittedly, the objections under Section 34 were filed within the time stipulated under Section 34 of the Act. However, since there was objections, time was granted on 23.1.2013 by the Deputy Registrar of the High Court to remove the objections within a period of 7 days. This was not done.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		2. Eventually, the appellant re-filed the matter on 21.3.2013. the explanation given by the appellant is that the amount of court fees to the extent of Rs.8,94,000/- was to be arranged and that took some time. The appellant is the Northern Railway and while it is difficult to condone such inefficiency which seems to be a persistent reality with the organisation, such as the Northern Railway, that took time in arranging even the small things.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		3. Mr. Amarjeet Singh Chandiok, learned senior counsel appearing for the respondent submitted that Section 34(3) of the Act bars re- filing beyond the period stipulated therein. The said sub-Section reads as follows:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		"34(3) An application for setting aside may not be made after three months have elapsed from the date on which the party making that application had received the arbitral award or, if a request had been made under Section 33, from the date on which that request had been disposed of by the arbitral tribunal.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		Provided that if the Court is satisfied that the applicant was pvented by sufficient cause from making the application within the said Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -18- period of three months it may entertain the application within a further period of thirty days, but not thereafter."
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		4. We find that said section has no application in re-filing the petition but only applies to the initial filing of the objections under Section 34 of the Act. It was submitted on behalf of the respondent that Rule 5(3) of the Delhi High Court Rules states that if the memorandum of appeal is filed and particular time is granted by the Deputy Registrar, it shall be considered as fresh institution. If this Rule is strictly applied in this case, it would mean that any re-filing beyond 7 days would be a fresh institution. However, it is a matter of record that 5 extensions were given beyond 7 days.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		Undoubtedly, at the end of the extensions, it would amount to re-filing.
	</p>
	<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">
		5. We are not inclined to accept this contention particularly since the petitioner has offered an explanation for the delay for the period after the extensions.
	</p>

Thus, in the event a party fails to take expeditious steps to initiate an action within the time as specified, then the courts are proscribed from entertaining such action at the instance of such a party. The rationale of pscribing time limits within which recourse to legal remedies can be taken has been explained by the Supme Court in the case of Bharat Barrel and Drum Mfg. Co. Ltd. v. The principle which forms the basis of this rule is expssed in the maximum vigilantibus, non dermientibus, jura Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 780, 823, 913 &amp; 914 of 2022 -29- subveniunt (the laws give help to those who are watchful and not to those who sleep). 	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		17. It cannot be, thus, assumed that the party has given up his rights to avail legal remedies.
