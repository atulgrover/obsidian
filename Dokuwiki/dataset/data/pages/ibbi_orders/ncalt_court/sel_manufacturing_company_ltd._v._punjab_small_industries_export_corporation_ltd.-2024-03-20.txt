<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">12. We have gone through the submissions of the appellant and the respondent including the judgments relied upon by them. The appellant has mainly relied upon the 'clean slate principle' enunciated in the judgments of Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Pvt. Ltd. v. Edelweiss Assets Reconstruction Company Limited and Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Limited Through Authorised Signatory vs. Satish Kumar Gupta &amp; Ors. cited supra by the appellant. It is appellant's contention that under the said principle, the new management cannot be saddled with any unexpected claims and should be allowed to commence and restart the business on a 'clean slate'.</p>
<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">The said principle is coded in Section 31(1) of the IBC, 2016 which states that an approved resolution plan is binding on all stakeholders including Corporate Debtor, its employees, Members, Creditors, including Central &amp; State Government or any local authority or Guarantors. The cases cited by the appellant are in support of the said 'clean slate principle'.</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">13. The issue for consideration in this case is whether in the factual matrix of this case, the successful resolution applicant can be granted ownership of leasehold rights over the subject plot without payment of dues to the respondent. <p title="Precedent Analysis">19. Before deciding the issue in hand, we refer to the following judgments on this issue:</p>
<p title="Precedent Analysis">19.1 Hon'ble Supme Court in the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai vs. Abhilash Lal and Ors. <p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">43. The scope of this provision has been the subject matter of debate in several judgments of this Court. In Employees Organization v.
