	<h2>
		Bank Of Baroda (Erstwhile Vijaya Bank) vs Suchi Paper Mills Ltd. on 18 March, 2024
	</h2>
	<p>
		CA 8190/2024 IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION Civil Appeal No 8190 of 2024 Bank of Baroda (Erstwhile Vijaya Bank) and Others Appellants Versus Suchi Paper Mills Ltd and Others Respondents ORDER
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		1 By its order dated 16 October 2023, the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal1 has set aside the order of the National Company Law Tribunal 2 dated 1 April 2020 approving the Resolution Plan. The proceedings have been remanded without touching upon the merits of the case.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		1 &ldquo;NCLAT&rdquo; 2 &ldquo;NCLT&rdquo; Signature Not Verified 1 Digitally signed by CHETAN KUMAR Date: 2024.03.19 17:13:20 IST Reason:  CA 8190/2024 2 The rationale for the impugned order is that an order approving the Resolution Plan was passed by a Single Member of the NCLT in violation of the provisions of Section 419 (3) of the Companies Act 2013. That apart, the NCLAT has also observed that none of the circumstances which are required to be borne in mind under Section 30(2) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 have been considered by the Single Member of the NCLT.
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		3 During the course of the hearing, Mr Gaurav Agarwal, senior counsel appeared on behalf of the appellant. Mr R Venkataramani, Attorney General for India appears on behalf of the fourth respondent (Kendriya Bhandar, who is the successful Resolution Applicant).
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		4 It has been submitted that substantial steps were taken in pursuance of the order of approval and the dues of the financial creditors were also settled.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		These are matters which, it is needles to add, would be considered by the NCLT when the proceedings appear before it on remand. We say no more on that aspect.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		5 Since the impugned order of the NCLAT is by way of an order of remand, we are not entertaining the appeal at the psent stage keeping open all the rights and contentions of the parties to be urged before the NCLT. The parties would be at liberty to approach the NCLT at an early date so that orders can be passed in accordance with the above directions.
	</p>
