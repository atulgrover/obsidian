		Supreme Court of India

	<h2>
		Council Of The Institute Of Chartered ... vs Shri Gurvinder Singh on 16 November, 2018
	</h2>
	<h3>
		Equivalent citations: AIRONLINE 2018 SC 993, (2018) 4 ESC 756, 2019 (11) SCC 785, (2019) 133 ALL LR 762, (2019) 195 ALLINDCAS 228, (2019) 1 CAL HN 89, (2019) 1 SCALE 43, (2019) 1 SCT 141, (2019) 1 WLC(SC)CVL 344

	<h3>
		Author: R.F. Nariman
	</h3>
	<h3>
		Bench: Navin Sinha, Rohinton Fali Nariman
	</h3>
	<p>
		 1 REPORTABLE IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION CIVIL APPEAL NO. 11034 OF 2018 (Arising out of SLP (C) No. 19564/2017) COUNCIL OF THE INSTITUTE OF CHARTERED ACCOUNTANTS OF INDIA Appellant(s) VERSUS SHRI GURVINDER SINGH &amp; ANR. Respondent(s) J U D G M E N T
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		R.F. Nariman, J.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		 1) Leave granted. 2) The psent appeal arises out of a complaint dated
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		16.03.2005 against Gurvinder Singh - Respondent No.1, who is a Chartered Accountant, relating to sale of 100 shares in 1999, which were transferred to the Chartered Accountant&rsquo;s own name.
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		3) What has been pleaded before us is that the matter has ultimately been settled between the Complainant and the Chartered Accountant, despite which the Disciplinary Committee took up the case and ultimately found that the conduct of the Respondent No.1-Chartered Accountant was derogatory in nature and highly unbecoming and held him guilty of &lsquo;Other Misconduct&rsquo; under Section 22 read with Signature Not Verified Digitally signed by R NATARAJAN Date: 2018.11.22 16:58:21 IST Reason: Section 21 of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 (hereinafter referred to as &lsquo;the Act&rsquo;).  2
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		4) The Council of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, therefore, made its recommendation to the High Court to remove the aforesaid Chartered Accountant for a period of six months from the rolls. The High Court, by the impugned judgment dated 16.08.2016, after setting out Sections 21 and 22 of the Act, arrived at the conclusion that:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		&ldquo;14. In the instant case the respondent was acting as an inpidual in his dealings with the complainant which were purely commercial. While selling the shares held by him the respondent was not acting as a Chartered Accountant. He was not discharging any function in relation to his practice as a Chartered Accountant.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		15. The Reference is accordingly answered by declaring the law as above and not inflicting any penalty upon the respondent.&rdquo;
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		5) We are afraid that the High Court has not correctly appciated Section 21(3) of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949 which states as follows:-
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		&ldquo;(3) Where the Director (Discipline) is of the opinion that a member is guilty of any professional or other misconduct mentioned in the First Schedule, he shall place the matter before the Board of Discipline and where the  3 Director (Discipline) is of the opinion that a member is guilty of any professional or other misconduct mentioned in the Second Schedule or in both the Schedules, he shall place the matter before the Disciplinary Committee.&rdquo; Schedule-I Part-IV reads as follows:-
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		&ldquo;Other Misconduct in Relation to Members of the Institute Generally A member of the Institute, whether in practice or not, shall be deemed to be guilty of other misconduct, if he-
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		(1) is held guilty by any civil or criminal court for an offence which is punishable with imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months;
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		(2) in the opinion of the Council, brings disrepute to the profession or the Institute as a result of his action whether or not related to his professional work.&rdquo;
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		6) The Disciplinary Committee has, on facts, found the Chartered Accountant guilty of a practice which was not in the Chartered Accountant&rsquo;s professional capacity.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		This, it was entitled to do under Schedule I Part-IV sub- clause(2) if, in the opinion of the Council, such act brings disrepute to the profession whether or not related to his professional work.
	</p>
	 4
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		7) This being the case, it is clear that the impugned judgment is incorrect and must, therefore, be set aside. We thus remand the matter to the High Court to be decided afresh leaving all contentions open to both parties.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		8) The appeal is disposed of accordingly.
	</p>
