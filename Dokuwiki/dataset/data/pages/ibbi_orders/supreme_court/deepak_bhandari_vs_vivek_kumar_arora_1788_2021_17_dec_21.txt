<h2>
	Deepak Bhandari vs Vivek Kumar Arora on 17 December, 2021
</h2>
<h3>
	Bench: K.M. Joseph, Pamidighantam Sri Narasimha
</h3>
<p>
	IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION CIVIL APPEAL NO. 1788 OF 2021 DEEPAK BHANDARI Appellant(s) VERSUS VIVEK KUMAR ARORA &amp; ORS. Respondent(s) O R D E R
</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">
	When Interlocutory Application No. 163646 of 2021 came up for hearing, by consent, we have also heard the learned counsel for the parties in the civil appeal.
</p>
<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
	This appeal is filed against the order passed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) dismissing the appeal carried by the appellant against the order passed by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) affirming the rejection of expssion of interest by the Resolution Professional.
</p>
<p title="Court's Reasoning" data-structure="CDiscource">
	Two grounds were found against the acceptability of the expssion of interest. In the first place, it is found that the expssion of interest would be in the teeth of the prohibition indicated in Section 29A(a) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The attempt to seek shelter Signature Not Verifiedunder Section 240A on the score that the Corporate Debtor Digitally signed by Nidhi Ahuja Date: 2021.12.18 13:57:04 IST Reason: was a MSME also did not find favour.
</p>
<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
	After hearing the learned counsel for the appellant, 1 CIVIL APPEAL NO. 1788 OF 2021 we are convinced at any rate that the expssion of interest given by the appellant will attract the wrath of Section 29A(a). In the light of this, we need not even go into the other question which is what was dealt with by the NCLAT viz., that the case of the appellant that the Corporate Debtor was a MSME could not be established.
</p>
<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
	The appeal is dismissed.
</p>
