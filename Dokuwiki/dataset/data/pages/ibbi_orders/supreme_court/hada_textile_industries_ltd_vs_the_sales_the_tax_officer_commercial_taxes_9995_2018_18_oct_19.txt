	<h2>
		M/S. Hada Textile Industries Ltd. vs The Sales Tax Officer Commercial Taxes on 18 October, 2019
	</h2>
	<h3>
		Bench: Uday Umesh Lalit, Aniruddha Bose
	</h3>
	<p>
		 1 IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION CIVIL APPEAL NO. 9995/2018 M/S. HADA TEXTILE INDUSTRIES LTD. Appellant(s) VERSUS THE SALES TAX OFFICER COMMERCIAL TAXES &amp; ORS. Respondent(s) O R D E R
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		1. This statutory appeal under Section 62 of IBC 2016 1 arises out of the final judgment and order dated 18.09.2018 passed by the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (hereinafter referred to as &ldquo;the NCLAT&rdquo;), New Delhi in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No.325 of 2017 by.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		2. The facts in brief, leading to the filing of the psent appeal are as under:-
	</p>
	<p title="Fact">
		A] By declaration dated 10.08.2001, the BFIR2 had declared the appellant to be a sick company under the provisions of SICA 3 and appointed IFCI as the Operating Agency, whereafter a scheme for rehabilitation was formed. The scheme, however, expired sometime in the year 2013 by which time, the net worth of the appellant had not 1 Signature Not Verified Digitally signed by INDU MARWAH 15:45:15 IST Reason: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 Date: 2019.10.26 2Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		3 The Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Act, 1985  2 yet turned positive. Therefore, a modified plan was submitted by the appellant which was under consideration when the Repeal Act4 came into force.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		B] Thereafter, the appellant filed C.P.No.171/2017 before the National Company Law Tribunal, Kolkata (hereinafter referred to as &ldquo;the NCLT&rdquo;] seeking extension of the scheme sanctioned by the BIFR. This application came to be dismissed by the NCLT, Kolkata on 13.4.2017.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		C] On or about 24.05.2017, Removal of Difficulties Order was passed by the Central Government clarifying that sanctioned schemes currently under implementation would be deemed to be resolution plans under IBC 2016. Another application, therefore, came to be filed by the appellant being C.P. No.406/KB/2017 before the NCLT, Kolkata seeking extension of the period of the scheme.
	</p>
	<p title="Fact" data-structure="Facts">
		D] This application was dismissed by the NCLT, Kolkata vide order dated 13.11.2017 which order was confirmed in appeal by the NCLAT by its order dated 18.9.2018.
	</p>
	<p title="Issue" data-structure="Issue">
		E] While dismissing the application, the NCLT, Kolkata in its order dated 13.11.2017 had directed:
	</p>
	<p title="Issue">
		_________________________________________________________ &ldquo;20. Since the scheme has already been approved and it is 4 Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Repeal Act, 2003  3 also on record that till date, the company could not turn its net worth into positive within the period of sanctioned scheme, therefore, it will be psumed that corporate applicant has violated the term of the sanctioned scheme.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		Thus, liquidation proceedings shall follow, in accordance with the provisions of I&amp;B Code, 2016.&rdquo; The aforesaid direction also came to be confirmed by the NCLAT while dismissing the appeal.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		3. While issuing notice in the psent appeal, this Court had passed the following order on 1.10.2018:
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		&ldquo;Mr. Shyam Divan, learned senior counsel appearing for the appellant, on instructions, submits that the appellant is willing to withdraw the original application [CP (IB) No.406/KB/2017] filed before the National Company Law Tribunal, Kolkata.&rdquo;
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		4. Thereafter, the respondents have appeared and parties have exchanged their pleadings.
	</p>
	<p title="Petitioner's Argument" data-structure="PetArg">
		5. Mr. Shyam Divan, learned senior counsel, submits that the appellant had gone before the NCLT seeking extension of the Scheme that was already in existence and the Scheme had worked for a while; however, the net worth of the appellant having not turned positive, the appellant had gone to the NCLT seeking extension of time; and it was never the intent of the appellant to liquidate itself nor was any such plea or prayer made before NCLT. In the circumstances, the directions as aforesaid passed by the NCLT, Kolkata and confirmed by the NCLAT were without any reason. He, therefore, contends that the appellant be permitted to withdraw the  4 application itself.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis" data-structure="Precedent">
		As regards the psent status of the appellant, it is submitted in the written submissions as under:-
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		&ldquo;(i) The net-worth of the Appellant has turned positive in the financial year ending on 31 March 2018.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		(ii) In terms of the Sanctioned Scheme under Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Act, 1985 (&ldquo;SICA&rdquo;), SS-04, the Appellant has paid and cleared the following amounts to its financial creditors: (a) Industrial Investment Bank of India &ndash; Rs. 70.94 lacs; (b) Rs.72.25 lacs; (c) Industrial Development Bank of India &ndash; Rs.177.35 lacs. The Appellant has accordingly received the no-dues certificate from them.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		(iii) In addition, United Bank of Indi and UCO Bank have also issued their no-dues certificate to the Appellant, Refer No-dues certificate of UCO Bank at Page 462 of the Appeal.
	</p>
	<p title="Precedent Analysis">
		(iv) The Appellant has also received a no-dues certificate from West Bengal State Electricity Distribution Company, in terms of the Sanctioned Scheme.&rdquo;
	</p>
	<p title="Respondent's Argument" data-structure="RespArg">
		6. The learned counsel appearing for the respondents have no objection, if the appellant is permitted to withdraw the original application. However, their apphension is that there are certain proceedings which are psently pending for recovery of dues and their interest in that behalf is required to be secured.
	</p>
	<p title="Analysis of the law" data-structure="Section">
		7. It goes without saying that even if, the application is allowed to be withdrawn, the rights and remedies available to any of the persons, be it a creditor or any other entity including a corporation or any statutory authority, in respect of any statutory dues or otherwise, such rights and remedies will not in any way be  5 affected by the withdrawal of the original application.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		8. We have, therefore, no difficulty in accepting the prayer made by the appellant. Accordingly, we allow the appellant to withdraw CP No.406/KB/2017. Consequently, the orders passed by the NCLT and the NCLAT stand set aside.
	</p>
	<p title="Conclusion" data-structure="Conclusion">
		9. This appeal is allowed in aforesaid terms. No costs.
	</p>
