	<h1>
		Corporate Debtor under new management cannot be asked to make any payments towards tax demands raised by Tax Authorities for the period prior to CIRP, however, Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) cannot escape liability of EPF dues even if these pertain to the period prior to the commencement of CIRP &ndash; Rocksolid Infra Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Income Tax Officer and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 20, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Hyderabad Bench holds that: (i) Under the scheme of IBC, no liability can be fastened on the CD after the commencement of CIRP. All Claimants including the Government Authorities can lodge their claim during the CIRP, and will receive their dues as per the Resolution Plan approved under the provisions of IBC. Moreover, under Section 238 the provisions of IBC have an overriding effect, notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other Law. (ii) Whatever demands have been raised by the Tax Authorities for the period prior to CIRP stand extinguished once the Resolution Plan is approved. The CD under the current management cannot be asked to make any payments towards such demands. (iii) The Employee Provident Fund (EPF) dues however stand on different footing. The SRA cannot escape the liability of EPF dues even if these pertain to the period prior to the commencement of CIRP. The present application against Respondent No.2 is therefore dismissed.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The issue in the present case is about whether liabilities of the CD prior to the date of commencement of CIRP can be imposed on the Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) after the Resolution Plan has been approved and implemented. This question has been answered in the negative by various Courts including Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, most notably in the case of
							<em>
								 Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Ltd. Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta &amp; Ors.
							</em>
							 reported at
							<em>
								 (2019) 07 SC
							</em>
							 and later again, in the matter of
							<em>
								 Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Pvt Ltd. v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd. &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2021) 54 SC.
							</em>
						</li>
						<li>
							Clearly under the scheme of IBC, no liability can be fastened on the CD after the commencement of CIRP. All Claimants including the Government Authorities can lodge their claim during the CIRP, and will receive their dues as per the Resolution Plan approved under the provisions of IBC. Moreover, under Section 238 the provisions of IBC have an overriding effect, notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other Law.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, whatever demands have been raised by the Tax Authorities for the period prior to CIRP stand extinguished once the Resolution Plan is approved. The CD under the current management cannot be asked to make any payments towards such demands.
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, Respondents No.1 and 4 are directed to take necessary steps and eliminate the Tax or any other demands raised on the CD that pertains to the period prior to the&nbsp;commencement of CIRP. The Application is therefore allowed in&nbsp;respect of Respondent No.1 and 4.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Employee Provident Fund (EPF) dues however stand on different footing. In this regard it is useful to reproduce from the order of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
							<em>
								 SK Constructions Vs. EPF Organisation &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2023) 471 NCLAT.
							</em>
							 Facts in the present case are similar to the one decided by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT. In an earlier case of
							<em>
								 Jet Aircraft Maintenance Engineers Welfare Association&nbsp;(2022) 861 NCLAT
							</em>
							 .&nbsp;The judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT have also been upheld by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court
						</li>
						<li>
							Consequently therefore, the SRA cannot escape the liability of EPF dues even if these pertain to the period prior to the commencement of CIRP. The present application against Respondent No.2 is therefore dismissed.
						</li>
						<li>
							Application against Respondent No.3 has already been dismissed through the docket order dated 17.08.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							In these consequences, this Application is partly allowed.
							<strong>
								 (p15-23)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
