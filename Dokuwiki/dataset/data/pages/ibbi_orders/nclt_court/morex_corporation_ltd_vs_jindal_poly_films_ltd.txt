	<h1>
		When delivery of goods for export could not be met by Corporate Debtor due to ban imposed by Govt. of India on export, any claim arising on account of such dispute, CIRP initiation petition u/s 9 of IBC is not maintainable &ndash; Morex Corporation Ltd. v. Jindal Poly Films Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Allahabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 22, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Allahabad Bench held that when the terms of delivery for export as per the contract could not be met by the Corporate Debtor due to the reason beyond its control in view of the ban imposed by the Govt of India on export of such goods and the Corporate Debtor being always ready for delivery of contracted goods for local sale during ban and for export after ban was lifted but the Operational Creditor not agreeing with such offer of the Operational Creditor even after being fully aware that goods were manufactured within specified time period but could not be delivered due to reason beyond the control of the Corporate Debtor and hence, any claim arising on account of such disputed course of action by the Operational Creditor would not be fit to be covered as maintainable in terms of the provision of the p 9 of the Code.
	</p>
</div>

	<p>
		<strong>
			 Brief about the decision:
		</strong>
	</p>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Facts of the case
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			The instant application is filed on 29.09.2020 by M/s Morex Corporation Ltd. (Applicant/Operational Creditor) under Section 9 of the IBC against M/s Jindal Poly Filma Ltd. (Respondent/Corporate Debtor). The prayer made therein is to initiate CIRP against the Respondent/Corporate Debtor due to default in payment of total outstanding Operational Debt of Rs.3,42,60,000/- of which principal amount is Rs. 3,00,00,000/, interest amount is Rs. 33,60,000/ and Air freight charges are Rs. 9,00,000/.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Applicant M/s Morex Corporation Ltd. is based in Hong&nbsp;Kong, which is into the business of import and export.
		</li>
		<li>
			A purchase order was placed with&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;The Applicant states that as agreed with the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor, the Order should get ready on or before 15.03.2020&nbsp;and the Corporate Debtor assured timely delivery of the goods.
		</li>
		<li>
			On a prerequisite that the entire amount has to be paid in&nbsp;advance.
		</li>
		<li>
			the&nbsp;Operational Creditor released the advance payment of&nbsp;Rs.3,00,00,000/-.
		</li>
		<li>
			Finally Order for 50000Kgs of non-woven fabric was placed&nbsp;by the Operational Creditor vide a Purchase Order dated&nbsp;12.03.2020 specifying the terms of delivery as being on or&nbsp;before 23.03.2020.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Applicant states that vide their email dated 18.03.2020,&nbsp;they reminded the Corporate Debtor that the cargo had to be&nbsp;handed over by or before 23.03.2020 as per the Contract. The&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, however, responded saying that the&nbsp;production has been delayed due to some production issues&nbsp;and the contracted goods can be dispatched only by 24th or&nbsp;25th March, 2020.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Applicant immediately raised objection and clearly stated&nbsp;that the Applicant will suffer huge losses, if the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor fails to deliver the contracted goods on 23.03.2020&nbsp;and the delivery has to be happened on 23.03.2020 as per&nbsp;the Original Contract and no other date can be agreed upon&nbsp;for delivery.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Corporate Debtor&nbsp;ultimately failed to fulfill its commitment of producing and&nbsp;dispatching the contracted goods on time i.e. 23.03.2020.&nbsp;The Applicant states that due to COVID-19 pandemic, the&nbsp;Government of India declared a nationwide lockdown from&nbsp;25.03.2020 and even then, the Corporate Debtor did not&nbsp;update on anything to the Applicant.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Applicant&nbsp;asked for&nbsp;refund of the advance amount paid by it vide an email dated&nbsp;01.04.2020.
		</li>
		<li>
			A Legal Demand Notice&nbsp;demanding payment under p 8 of the Insolvency and&nbsp;Bankruptcy Code, 2016 was served upon the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Goods were manufactured by the Corporate Debtor and it could not be delivered for export on the contracted date due to the ban imposed by the Govt of India at the relevant time but the Corporate Debtor was always ready to deliver the goods for local sale during the period of ban as well as for export after the ban was lifted.
		</li>
		<li>
			Therefore, unilateral cancellation of the contract by the Operational Creditor by email dated 01.04 2020 and demanding the refund of advance money has certainly given rise to a dispute between both parties due to there being no default on the part of the Corporate Debtor in meeting the terms of the contract and the Corporate Debtor having not been given any notice or not being consulted before termination of the contract. Therefore, while replying to the demand notice u/s 8, the Corporate Debtor in its reply dated 20.05.2020 has disputed the termination of the Contract terming such cancellation as illegal. Such dispute between both parties on termination of contract as well as dispute on meeting the terms of the contract as regards to completing the manufacturing of contracted goods in our opinion would fall under the category of pre-existing disputes between parties, especially when the terms of delivery for export as per the contract could not be met by the Corporate Debtor due to the reason beyond its control in view of the ban imposed by the Govt of India on export of such goods and the Corporate Debtor being always ready for delivery of contracted goods for local sale during ban and for export after ban was lifted but the Operational Creditor not agreeing with such offer of the Operational Creditor even after being fully aware&nbsp;that goods were manufactured within specified time period but&nbsp;could not be delivered due to reason beyond the control of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and hence, any claim arising on account of such&nbsp;disputed course of action by the Operational Creditor would not be&nbsp;fit to be covered as maintainable in terms of the provision of the&nbsp;p 9 of the Code.
			<strong>
				 (p9)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			It is also worth to note that the Corporate Debtor i.e. M/s Jindal Poly Film Ltd is a listed company of B.C. Jindal Group and is one of a largest manufacturer in packaging industry and a profit earning company widely traded on NSE and BSE. There is no evidence produced by the Operational Creditor to show that this is not a solvent company except raising doubts about its solvency as its claim for refund of advances could not be paid due to the reason of the same being disputed as it has already been discussed in details.
			<strong>
				 (p10)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In view of our findings as discussed in aforesaid paras, we&nbsp;find that the Operational Debt of Rs. 3,42,60,000/- shown in Part&nbsp;IV of the present application u/s 9 claimed to be arising on account&nbsp;of the advance money paid is not being refunded after failure of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor in supplying the material as per contract, is a&nbsp;disputed claim in which dispute has arisen on 01.04.2020 because&nbsp;of unilateral cancellation of contract by the Operational Creditor&nbsp;consequent to a dispute with the Corporate Debtor over the&nbsp;fulfilment of the terms of the contract hence, such dispute is found&nbsp;to have arisen much before the demand notice u/s 8 was issued&nbsp;on 15.05.2020, and accordingly is a pre-existing dispute for which&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor intimated in the reply submitted in response&nbsp;to the demand notice sent u/s 8, and therefore such application&nbsp;u/s 9 is liable to be rejected as per the provision of p&nbsp;9(5)(ii)(d). Therefore, we dismiss the present petition/application&nbsp;filed under Section 9 due to being a disputed operational debt of&nbsp;the nature of pre-existing dispute.
			<strong>
				 (p11)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			As the present application filed under p 9 is not found maintainable on the issue of pre-existing dispute, the second issue has not been adjudicated upon by us relating to application being barred under p 10A as this issue was not raised under pleadings and only taken up in the written submission and also, the date of default mentioned in Part IV of the application as 24.03.2020 is before the date of 25.03.2020 when the provision of p 10A has become applicable. The date of default to be taken as 01.04.2020 being the date of cancellation of contract, as contended by the Corporate Debtor, has not been considered by us keeping in view the fact that the cancellation of contract on 01.04.2020 has been disputed by the Corporate Debtor in order to show the existence of a pre-existing dispute in relation to the operational debt claimed in the application and the same has already been adjudicated upon by us hence, date on which dispute has arisen cannot be taken as date of default when a particular date has already been taken by the Operation Creditor in demand notice u/s 8 and the application u/s 9 on which default by the Corporate Debtor in meeting the terms of contract i.e. 24.03.2020 as claimed by the Operational Creditor, is occurred.
			<strong>
				 (p11)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			Accordingly, the petition CP (IB) No.12/ALD/2021 filed by the Operational Creditor u/s 9 of the Insolvency &amp; Bankruptcy Code, 2016 is hereby
			<strong>
				 dismissed
			</strong>
			 .
			<strong>
				 (p12)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
