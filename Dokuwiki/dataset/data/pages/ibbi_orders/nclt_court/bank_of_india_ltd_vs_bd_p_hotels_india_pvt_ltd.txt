	<h1>
		Whether insufficiently stamped loan/ mortgage agreements can have a bearing on the adjudication of an application u/s 7 of the IBC in case the claim can otherwise be substantiated? &ndash; Bank of India Ltd. Vs. BD &amp; P Hotels (India) Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 4, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that: (i) The default arises when the debt due is not paid and the subsequent events do not give rise to any fresh date of default. The subsequent acknowledgement acknowledges the liability and does not cause any default having come into existence. (ii) The Corporate Debtor has challenged the maintainability of this Petition on the ground of insufficiency of stamp duty paid on loan / mortgaged documents in view of decision of M/s. N.N. Global Mercantile Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Indo Unique Flame Ltd. &amp; Others (2023) 56 SC. (iii) The Tribunal, while adjudicating upon an application for admission into Resolution Process filed by a creditor, is mandated to ascertain the existence of the debt, and any default in payment of such debt. Ascertainment of existence of debt and default is not necessarily to be evidenced by a loan agreement or ancillary agreement or mortgage agreement. Instead, it can be proved by other means, as provided in Section 7(3) of the IBC prescribing record of default recorded with the information utility or such other records or evidence such may be specified. (iv) It shall be pertinent to note that the stamp duty even if paid by the lender, is recoverable from the borrower i.e. the Corporate Debtor in the present case. This Bench is of the considered view that interest of the state shall be protected in case IRP/RP is directed to notify the collector of stamps to file its claim for a deficiency in a stamp duty in case there is an objection raised by the Corporate Debtor.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 286 NCLTCase Name:Bank of India Ltd. Vs. BD &amp; P Hotels (India) Pvt. Ltd.Financial Creditor:Bank of India Ltd.Corporate Debtor:BD &amp; P Hotels (India) Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:CP (IB) No.226/MB-IV/2022Judgment Date:23-Jun-23Court/Bench:NCLT Mumbai BenchMember (Judicial):Shri Kishore VemulapalliMember (Technical):Mr. Prabhat Kumar
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This application was filed on 18.02.2022 by M/S. Bank of India, the Financial Creditor/Applicant, under p 7 of Insolvency &amp; Bankruptcy Code, 2016 for initiating CIRP in the matter BD &amp; P Hotels (India) Pvt. Ltd., the Corporate Debtor.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 a. Date of default issue
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The date of default is stated as 01.02.2017 in part IV of the Form 1 forming part of the present petition. The Corporate Debtor argues that fresh date of default comes into existence when the outstanding debt is said to have been acknowledged i.e. 08.3.2021 by the Corporate Debtor and the said date falls within the period prescribed in p 10A of the IBC barring filing of any application u/s 7, 9, &amp; 10 under the IBC.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The said argument is ex-facie illegal and not in accordance with stated position in numerous decisions of higher courts, wherein it has been consistently held that the default arises when the debt due is not paid and the subsequent events do not give rise to any fresh date of default. The subsequent acknowledgement acknowledges the liability and does not cause any default having come into existence.
							<strong>
								 (p6.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor has acknowledged the said debt in its audited financial statement for the year ended on 31.03.2020 and 31.03.2021, which contains the corresponding amounts for the preceding year i.e. 31.03.2019 &amp; 31.03.2020 also. The said financial statements clearly show that the Corporate Debtor owes debt to the applicant bank, as succinctly explained in para 6.1.3 above, and such acknowledgement extends the period of limitation.
							<strong>
								 (p6.5.2)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the case of
							<em>
								 Dena Bank Vs. C. Shivakumar Reddy and Anr&nbsp;(2021) 69 SC
							</em>
							 , the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court held that acknowledgement of debt in the audited financial statements is a valid acknowledgement of debt u/s 18 of the Limitation Act, thus the period of limitation further gets extended.
							<strong>
								 (p6.5.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 b. Whether insufficiently stamped loan agreements and
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 mortgage agreements can have a bearing on the adjudication of an
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 application u/s 7 of the Code in case the claim can otherwise be
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 substantiated
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Corporate Debtor has challenged the maintainability of this Petition on the ground of insufficiency of stamp duty paid on loan / mortgaged documents in view of decision of
						<em>
							 M/s. N.N. Global Mercantile Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Indo Unique Flame Ltd. &amp; Others&nbsp;(2023) 56 SC
						</em>
						 . The Hon&rsquo;ble Court in
						<em>
							 N. N. Global (supra)
						</em>
						 held that an instrument, which is not stamped or insufficiently stamped in accordance with the Stamps Act, is not an enforceable instrument, hence is a void contract in terms of provisions of Contract Act. Accordingly, such instrument can not be taken in evidence by the Court.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Bench finds that the law laid down in
							<em>
								 N N Global
							</em>
							 makes the contract, which is unstamped or insufficiently stamped, void, and the said decision further lays down the principle that such contract gets revived upon payment of duty with penalty under the Stamp Act. It follows therefrom that&nbsp;an instrument, insufficiently stamped or unstamped, is&nbsp;eclipsed by shadow of insufficiency of stamp duty paid on such instrument and cannot be taken in evidence; and once such shadow gets removed, the contract revives and becomes an enforceable instrument.&nbsp;The law laid down in N N Global requires the Court or Public Authority to impound such instrument, in case it is unstamped or is found insufficiently stamped prima-facie and further mandates the Court or Public Authority to ensure that the Stamp Duty is paid in accordance with the applicable Stamp Act before allowing enforcement of rights arising from such instrument.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.1-6.9.2)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The plea of insufficiency of stamp duty raised by the Corporate Debtor, to dislodge the present petition, to be contrary to its own admission while seeking disbursement of the Term Loan under these very agreements and subsequent acknowledgements, OTS proposals and its financial statements. This Bench feels that the dislodgement of the position, which remained legal for sufficiently long time as per Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s actions, would only benefit the Corporate Debtor at the cost of Financial Creditor, more so when the amount of deficient stamp paper was recoverable from the Corporate Debtor itself.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The IBC was enacted to facilitate the revival of the debtor by a resolution process, and such resolution process, as contained in the IBC, does not stipulate that the enforcement of such rights in the resolution process.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Tribunal, while adjudicating upon an application for admission into Resolution Process filed by a creditor, is mandated to ascertain the existence of the debt, and any default in payment of such debt. Ascertainment of existence of debt and default is not necessarily to be evidenced by a loan agreement or ancillary agreement or mortgage agreement. Instead, it can be proved by other means, as provided in Section 7(3) of the IBC prescribing record of default recorded with the information utility or such other records or evidence such may be specified. Further, Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 prescribed Form-1 under Chapter-II and IV of Part-II of the Code for making an Application under 7 of the Code by Financial Creditor. Part-V of this Application requires details of financial debt document records and evidence of default to be stated by the Applicant.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.5)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, this Bench of the view that, even if the contention of the Corporate Debtor is accepted that the Supplemental Mortgage Deed, Term Loan Agreement and other ancillary agreement cannot be taken in evidence to establish the existence of debt and default in payment thereof, this Bench finds that the fact of debt and default stands proved otherwise by other evidences, as is held in case of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, Chennai Bench decision in the case of
							<em>
								 Ashique Ponnamparambath vs. The Federal Bank Limited&nbsp;(2021) 336 NCLAT
							</em>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It shall be pertinent to note that the stamp duty even if paid by the lender, is recoverable from the borrower i.e. the Corporate Debtor in the present case. This Bench is of the considered view that interest of the state shall be protected in case IRP/RP is directed to notify the collector of stamps to file its claim for a deficiency in a stamp duty in case there is an objection raised by the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;In view of above discussions, this Bench does not find any merit in the arguments of the Corporate Debtor on this issue.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9.8-6.9.9)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 c. Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority concluded that since, there exist a Financial Debt of more than Rs.1.00 Crore; the Corporate Debtor has default in repayment thereof on 01.02.2017; the Petition is within Limitation period in view of various acknowledgement extending the period of limitation; and the Petition&nbsp;is complete and has been filed under the proper form, this Bench finds&nbsp;that present Petition deserves to be
						<strong>
							 admitted
						</strong>
						 .
						<strong>
							 (p7)
						</strong>
					</p>
