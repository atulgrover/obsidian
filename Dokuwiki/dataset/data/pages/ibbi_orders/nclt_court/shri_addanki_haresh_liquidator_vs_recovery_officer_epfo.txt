	<h1>
		The dues of workmen/employees from Provident Fund do not come within the meaning of &lsquo;Liquidation Estate&rsquo; for the purpose of distribution of assets u/s 53 of IBC and the damages levied by EPFO u/s 14B of the EPF &amp; MP Act, 1952 which are dues of Government and will be paid in order of priority u/s 53 of IBC, 2016 &ndash; Shri Addanki Haresh Liquidator of Right Engineers and Equipments India Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Recovery Officer, Employees Provident Fund Organisation &ndash; NCLT Bengaluru Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 27, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment on treatment of claim/demand and penalty raised by EPFO, NCLT Bengaluru Bench held that: (i) The demand/claim raised prior to the moratorium will not form part of liquidation estate and should be paid in priority; secondly, the claim/demand and penalty raised during moratorium period is not allowable under the provisions of the IBC; and the penal damages claimed under Section14B of EPF &amp; MP Act, 1952 which is prior to the moratorium period will be treated under Section 53 of IBC, 2016. (ii) The dues of workmen or employees from the provident fund do not come within the meaning of &lsquo;liquidation estate&rsquo; for the purpose of distribution of assets under Section 53 of the Code. Accordingly, it is to be paid in priority over other dues. (iii) The damages levied by EPFO under Section 14B of the EPF &amp; MP Act 1952 which are dues of Government and will be paid in order of priority under Section 53 of IBC, 2016.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Background the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						This application is filed by the Liquidator of Right Engineers and Equipments India Pvt. Ltd. under Section 35(1)(n) r/w Section 53 and 60(5)(c) of IBC, 2016
						<em>
							 interalia
						</em>
						 seeking to pass an order:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Whether the amounts claimed and payable by the Respondent PF Organisation under Section 7A and 7Q of EPF and MP Act, 1952 are the sums due to workmen and employees from the provident fund and to be considered in priority over other dues and
						</li>
						<li>
							Whether the amounts towards the damages claimed by the Respondent PF Organisation under Section 14B of EPF &amp; MP Act 1952 are dues to the Central Government and to be treated u/s 53(1)(e)(i) of the IBC 2016.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The decision the Adjudicating Authority is summarised in the following points:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							A plain reading of the Section 36(4) of IBC makes it clear that the Liquidator is empowered to include only those assets as part of liquidation estate which are defined under the provisions of sub-p (4) of Section 36 of the Code. Therefore, the Applicant/Liquidator cannot include the sums due to any workmen or employee from the provident fund, the pension fund and the gratuity fund as part of liquidation estate of the corporate debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							However, the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court in a recent decision dated 07.02.2023, the matter of
							<em>
								 State Bank of India vs. Moser Baer Karamachari Union &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2023) 14 SC
							</em>
							 , has upheld the order passed by the NCLT, Principal Bench in
							<em>
								 Alchemist Asset Reconstruction Co. Ltd. vs. Moser Baer India Ltd.&nbsp;(2019) 818 NCLT
							</em>
							 whereunder the NCLT vide order dt. 19.03.2019 held that the &lsquo;Provident Fund Dues&rsquo;, &lsquo;Pension Fund Dues&rsquo; and &lsquo;Gratuity Fund dues&rsquo; cannot be part of Section 53 of the Code. In so far as the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the decision of
							<em>
								 Mr. Savan Godiawala&nbsp;(2020) 191 NCLAT
							</em>
							 the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex court observed that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT has passed an order in that case due to ignorance of its earlier order dated 19.08.2019, in the case of
							<em>
								 State Bank of India vs. Moser Baer karamachari Union &amp; Anr.(supra)
							</em>
							 which has been affirmed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court. Accordingly, the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
							<em>
								 Mr. Savan Godiawala (supra)
							</em>
							 does not survive.
							<strong>
								 (p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is stated that following the decision in I.A No. 264 of 2021, the&nbsp;Liquidator has already admitted the claim submitted by the EPFO. However, the total admitted secured claims of the Corporate Debtor is around Rs. 17.44 Cr and the total realised amount from the sale of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor is around Rs. 11.42 Cr., therefore there is a huge hair&nbsp;cut of 6.50 Cr., in the amount payable to the Secured Creditors and when the Secured Creditors are losing 34% of secured amount, it is not justified to pay the damages and penalties to the Respondent Organisation in priority over the amounts payable to the Secured Creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In this connection, it is relevant to refer the earlier orders passed by this Tribunal on 05.01.2023 in the matter of
							<em>
								 Shri Vijay P Lulla, Liquidator of M/s. Bhuvana Infra Projects Private Limited v/s Axis Bank Limited and Anr.&nbsp;(2023) 14 NCLT
							</em>
							 and also in the case of E
							<em>
								 mployees Provident Fund Organisation vs Falcon Tyres Limited in I.A No.516 &amp; 404 of 2022 in C.P (IB) No. 14/BB/2017 dated 02.06.2023
							</em>
							 . In these orders, reliance was placed on the orders passed by Co-ordinate Bench NCLT, Kochi in I.A(IBC)/321/KOB/2022 in IBA/21/KOB/2019 dated 02.12.2022 in the matter of
							<em>
								 Regional Provident Fund CommissionerII vs. Jasin Jose, IRP
							</em>
							 and order passed by NCLT, Mumbai Bench-II on 29.07.2022 in the matter of KSS Petron Vineet K Chaudhary vs. The Regional PF Commissioner
							<em>
								 (2022) 678 NCLT
							</em>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Following the ratio of the above referred orders, it is clear that:
							</strong>
							<ul>
								<li>
									<strong>
										 The&nbsp;demand/claim raised prior to the moratorium will not form part of liquidation estate and should be paid in priority;
									</strong>
								</li>
								<li>
									<strong>
										 Secondly, the claim/demand and penalty raised during moratorium period is not allowable under the provisions of the IBC; and
									</strong>
								</li>
								<li>
									<strong>
										 The penal damages claimed under Section 14B of EPF &amp; MP Act, 1952 which is prior to the moratorium period will be treated under Section 53 of IBC, 2016.(p16)
									</strong>
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the facts and circumstances of the law and considering the provisions of the code, we are of the opinion that the dues of workmen or employees from the provident fund do not come within the meaning of &lsquo;liquidation estate&rsquo; for the purpose of distribution of assets under Section 53 of the Code. Accordingly, it is to be paid in priority over other dues. Therefore the Liquidator/Applicant is directed to distribute the amount of Rs.20,39,614/- towards the dues owned by Corporate Debtor to the&nbsp;Employees Provident Fund Organisation in priority and the damages levied&nbsp;by Respondent Organisation for sum of Rs. 9,08,117/- under Section 14B of the EPF &amp; MP Act 1952 which are dues of Government and will be paid in order of priority under Section 53 of IBC, 2016.&nbsp;In the result, I.A No. 232 of 2022 in CP (IB) No. 320/BB/2019 is&nbsp;disposed.
							<strong>
								 (p17-18)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
