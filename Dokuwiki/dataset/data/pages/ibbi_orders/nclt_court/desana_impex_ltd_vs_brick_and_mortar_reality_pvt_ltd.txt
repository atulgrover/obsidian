	<h1>
		An explicit written agreement of loan is a mandatory instrument for the Financial Creditor, being a NBFC &ndash; Desana Impex Ltd. Vs. Brick and Mortar Reality Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		November 30, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In Section 7 CIRP application, NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) The Financial Creditor an NBFC registered with the Reserve Bank of India and is bound by the Master Circular issued by the RBI. (ii) Referred its own judgment in Promtoers &amp; Fincon Pvt. Ltd. (2022) 219 NCLT and held that it is mandatory on the part of Financial Creditor, being a NBFCs to keep the terms and conditions recorded in writing. (iii) An explicit written agreement of loan is a mandatory instrument for the Financial Creditor, being a NBFC, to substantiate the nature of transactions between the lender and borrower. (iv) Judgment in Narendra Kumar Agarwal (2021) 25 NCLAT will not come to rescue to the Financial Creditor. (v) Dismissed the Company Petition.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 912 NCLTCase Name:Desana Impex Ltd. Vs. Brick and Mortar Reality Pvt. Ltd.Financial Creditor:Desana Impex Ltd.Corporate Debtor:Brick and Mortar Reality Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:Company Petition (IB) No. 342/KB/2022Judgment Date:21-Nov-23Court/Bench:NCLT Kolkata BenchMember (Judicial):Ms.&nbsp;Bidisha BanerjeeMember (Technical):Shri Arvind Devanathan
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This application has been moved by Desana Impex Limited (Financial Creditor or FC) under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) to initiate Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) against Brick and Mortar Reality Private Limited (Corporate Debtor or CD).
						</li>
						<li>
							There was no written agreement between the parties.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Financial Creditor an NBFC registered with the Reserve Bank of India and is bound by the Master Circular &ldquo;
							<em>
								 Master Circular-Fair Practices Code for NBFCs, dated July 01, 2015
							</em>
							 &rdquo; issued by the RBI.
							<strong>
								 (p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, we are fortified in our views by the decision passed by this Adjudicating Authority in the matter of Narendra
							<em>
								 Promtoers &amp; Fincon Pvt. Ltd. v. Vinline
							</em>
							<em>
								 Engineering Pvt. Ltd.
							</em>
							 reported in
							<em>
								 (2022) 219 NCLT
							</em>
							 that the RBI&rsquo;s circulars have statutory force, and this is well recognised in law. Hence, it is mandatory on the part of Financial Creditor, being a NBFCs to keep the terms and conditions recorded in writing.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							An explicit written agreement of loan is a mandatory instrument for the Financial Creditor, being a NBFC, to substantiate the nature of transactions between the lender and borrower. In the present case, the Financial Creditor has not been able to produce any loan agreement with the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the absence of any loan agreement, we are unable to determine whether the amount advanced was a financial debt and if yes, the rate of interest is 12% or the tenure of the loan agreement to determine the date of default.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the absence of signed documents, there is none to show that the said amount was to be repaid after a particular period. Therefore, the Financial Creditor is unable to clearly prove that the said loan amount is to be payable by the respondents/Corporate Debtor on a particular date.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The case law referred by the Ld. Counsel for the applicant passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
							<em>
								 Narendra Kumar Agarwal v. Monotrone Leasing Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 25 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , in our view, will not come to rescue to the Financial Creditor. In that case, even though the contract was oral, the respondent therein was able to demonstrate with money receipt, bank statement, notice issued under p 138 of the Negotiable Instrument Act, 1881, by the respondent therein and the response of the applicant agreeing to pay with interest, etc. (as laid down in para 16 of
							<em>
								 Narendra Kumar Agarwal (Supra)
							</em>
							 to prove beyond reasonable doubt that what was advanced was only a &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo;.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the given case even going by the entries made in the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor which showed that the amount due to the applicant herein is shown under the heading &ldquo;Other Liabilities&rdquo; and not as a loan. The e-mails alleging confirmation claimed to have been received by the Financial Creditor from the Corporate Debtor cannot be relied upon as Ld. Counsel for the respondent is able to demonstrate that e-mails were sent by a person who is no way connected with the Corporate Debtor and the same cannot be treated as confirmation of debt.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And the Adjudicating Authority concluded that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Financial Creditor has not been able to demonstrate categorically that what was advanced was a financial debt. Therefore, in the absence of a written agreement, we are unable to ascertain the loan terms, date of repayment, and rate of interest, if any agreed upon. In the absence of a proper confirmation by the Corporate Debtor acknowledging the debt as &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo;, we are unable to conclude the amount said to have been advanced as financial debt. We find that in view of the facts and circumstances mentioned above, the application filed by the Financial Creditor does not merit admission under p 7 of the IBC and consequently, we dismiss it as not maintainable.
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of the view above, this Company Petition being C.P. (IB) No. 342/KB/2022 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p19-20)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
				</div>
