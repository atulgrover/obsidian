	<h1>
		A Preference Shareholder is not a Financial Creditor unless Cumulatively Redeemable Preference Shares (CRPS) become due for redemption &ndash; EPC Constructions India Ltd. Through its Liquidator &ndash; Abhijit Guhathakurtha Vs. Matix Fertiliser and Chemicals Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		September 14, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) A Preference Shareholder is a Member and Shareholder of a company who is also entitled to enjoy voting rights in every resolution placed before the company. (ii) The non-redemption of preference shares does not result in preference shareholders becoming creditors or the carrying value of preference shares and dividends becoming a debt. Therefore, a Preference Shareholder cannot step into the shoes of a creditor of the Company unless their Preference Shares become redeemable in above terms. (iii) If payment against CRPS is not due, no liability can be said to arise. (iv) The necessary corollary would be that unless Cumulatively Redeemable Preference Shares (CRPS) is payable, nonpayment against CRPS cannot be termed as a default. (v) The non-redemption of preference shares does not result in preference shareholders becoming creditors or the carrying value of preference shares and dividends becoming a debt. (vi) a Preference Shareholder is not a Financial Creditor unless the Preference Shares (CRPS) become due for redemption.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 598 NCLTCase Name:EPC Constructions India Ltd. Vs. Matix Fertiliser and Chemicals Ltd.Financial Creditor:EPC Constructions India Ltd. Through Its Liquidator &ndash; Abhijit GuhathakurthaCorporate Debtor:Matix Fertiliser and Chemicals Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:Company Petition (I.B.) 					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This instant Petition is filed under Section 7 of the IBC by EPC Constructions India Limited through Its Liquidator, Mr. Abhijit Guhathakurtha (Financial Creditor/Petitioner) against M/s. Matix Fertiliser and Chemicals Ltd. (Corporate Debtor/Respondent), whereby, the Financial Creditor seeks to initiate an CIRP against the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Financial Creditor claimed that the default amount of Rs. 310 Crore is due and payable by the Corporate Debtor (Matix) to the Financial Creditor (EPC) falls within the definition of &ldquo;Debt&rdquo; as defined under Section 3(11) of the Code. The petitioner is a preference shareholder of the Respondent having 25 Crores shares and the preference shareholder has all the rights of an equity shareholder and in addition thereto has certain preference rights to share in profits available for dividend and for return of capital in priority to that of an equity shareholder.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue before the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The issues that have cropped up for determination are as follows:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							<ul>
								<li>
									(i) Whether a Preference Shareholder is a Creditor of a Company.
								</li>
								<li>
									(ii) Whether an application under Section 7 of the Code filed by a Preference Shareholder is maintainable.
								</li>
								<li>
									(iii) Whether Cumulatively Redeemable Preference Shares (CRPS) was in the nature of an investment or a financial debt having commercial effect of borrowing.
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						It is evident that &lsquo;Preference Shares&rsquo; are not defined in the Code. Therefore, one must then look into its definition and meaning assigned to preference shares under the Companies Act. Companies Act is a complete code enacted to consolidate and amend the law relating to companies and the legislature was conscious of this fact in drafting the Code. It is accordingly that Section 3(37) of the Code, the definition and relevant provisions of the Companies Act must be looked into in order to determine whether a preference share is an instrument &ldquo;
						<em>
							 having the commercial effect of borrowing
						</em>
						 &rdquo; as contended by the Petitioner.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Whether a Preference Shareholder is a Creditor of a Company
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 (a) A Preference Shareholder is a Member and Shareholder of a
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 company?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Petitioner who was issued and allotted 25 crores CRPS having face value of INR 10/- each is thus a member/shareholder of the Respondent and the CRPS or the preference shares within the meaning of Section 43 of the Companies Act, 2013 form part of the share capital of the Respondent.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Thus, a Preference Shareholder is a Member and Shareholder of a
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 company who is also entitled to enjoy voting rights in every resolution
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 placed before the company.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 (b) Redemption of the Preference Share in case the issuing company is not making profits
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Further, the Section 55 of the Companies Act envisages that Preference Shares can only be redeemed out of &ndash; (a) the profits of the company which would otherwise be available for dividend; or (b) the proceeds of a fresh issue of shares made for the purpose of such redemption. Section 55 of the Companies Act, 2013 defines the
							<em>
								 &ldquo;Issue and redemption of preference shares
							</em>
							 &rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							Thus, Section 55 is explicit that if the issuing company is not making profits which are available for dividend or has not raised any equity investments specifically for the purpose of redemption of preference shares, then the preference shares cannot be redeemed. Thus, shareholders cannot receive any payment before the debt of the company is fully discharged, unless preference shareholders are paid out of dividend/ profits of the company and thus,
							<strong>
								 the non-redemption of preference shares does not result in preference shareholders becoming creditors or the carrying value of preference shares and dividends becoming a debt.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Therefore, a Preference
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Shareholder cannot step into the shoes of a creditor of the Company
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 unless their Preference Shares become redeemable in above terms.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 (c) Preference Shares verses Equity Shares
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							a) As evident from the foregoing enumeration, &lsquo;Preference shares&rsquo; also referred to as preferred stock are company stock which get priority over equity shares in terms of repayment of dividends. The Preference Share Holders are the first to receive pay out in case the Company decides to pay its investor any dividends. The proviso to Section 43 (iii) (a) of the Companies Act, 2013 is reproduced verbatim as: &ldquo;
							<em>
								 that in respect of dividends, in addition to the preferential rights to the amounts specified in sub-clause (a) of clause (ii), it has a right to participate, whether fully or to a limited extent, with capital not entitled to the preferential right aforesaid;
							</em>
							 &rdquo;
						</li>
						<li>
							b)
							<strong>
								 Preference Share Holders
							</strong>
							 thus have preference over equity shareholders to receive dividend pay-out as per Section 43 of the Companies Act, 2013 and have right to vote in case of extra ordinary event as per Section 47 (2) of the Companies Act, 2013.
						</li>
						<li>
							c) Cumulative Preference Shares are those that allow a missed dividend payment to the cumulatively added to the next one and become payable when the Company is in profits. Cumulative preference shares entitle their holder to a fixed rate of dividend, and if any dividend is not paid on the due date, the arrears remain payable and will accumulate. The preference shareholders must receive their arrears of dividend before any ordinary share dividend can be paid to equity shareholders.
						</li>
						<li>
							d)
							<strong>
								 Advantages of issuer of Cumulative Preference Shares:
							</strong>
							 Cumulative Preference Shares allow the issuer the freedom to defer their dividend pay-outs. In case of a company lacks enough dividend funds, they are not obligated to pay their investor and can defer this payment.
						</li>
						<li>
							e)
							<strong>
								 Advantages to Cumulative Preference Shareholders:
							</strong>
							 Their position as shareholder is more secured than others. These have advantage of claiming a company&rsquo;s assets first. They have right to claim dividend payments. They thus hold a priority position over other shareholders in the company. They get priority in the Liquidation Process as per Section 53 of the I&amp;B Code to get their investment back in the event of Company&rsquo;s Liquidation.
						</li>
						<li>
							f)
							<strong>
								 Cumulative Redeemable Preference Shares (CRPS) whether a equity:
							</strong>
							 Cumulative Preference Shares allow the investor to reap missed dividends. When Company declares dividends, Preference Shares give the buyer preference over common equity shareholder. Paying dividend to preference Shareholder is not compulsory unless the company is in profit. [As per Section 43 of the Companies Act, 2013]
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 It is not obligatory for a Company to pay dividend as dividend is usually a part of the profit that the company shares with its shareholders.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Thus, CRPS is in the nature of an investment and not a debt unless it becomes redeemable.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 (d) Unless the Company earns profits, no dividend is payable against CRPS
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The provisions, Section 2(55) and Section 2(84) of the Companies Act, 2013 supra indicate that even a Preference Shareholder is Member of a Company and entitled to share its &ldquo;Profit&rdquo; in the form of &ldquo;dividends&rdquo; when the Company makes profit which would otherwise be available for dividend; or proceeds of a fresh issue of shares made for the purpose of such redemption.
							<strong>
								 Thus, unless the Company earns profits, no dividend is payable against CRPS.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 (e) If payment against&nbsp;CRPS is not due, no liability can be said to arise
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Further, Section 3(11) of the I&amp;B Code defines &ldquo;debt&rdquo; as &ldquo;
							<em>
								 a liability or obligation in respect of a claim which is due from any person and includes a financial debt and operational debt.
							</em>
							 &rdquo; Thus, if payment against&nbsp;CRPS is not due, no liability can be said to arise. Further, Section 3(12)&nbsp;of the I&amp;B Code defines &ldquo;
							<em>
								 default
							</em>
							 &rdquo; as &ldquo;
							<em>
								 non-payment of debt when whole or any part or instalment of the amount of debt has become due and payable and is not repaid by the debtor or the corporate debtor, as the case may be
							</em>
							 &rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The necessary corollary would be that unless CRPS is payable, non-payment against CRPS cannot be termed as a default.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. Whether an Application under Section 7 of Code filed by a
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 Preference Shareholder is maintainable:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						We have already discerned that
						<strong>
							 the non-redemption of preference shares does not result in preference shareholders becoming creditors or the carrying value of preference shares and dividends becoming a debt
						</strong>
						 , for the purpose reliance is placed on: &ndash;
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							a. Radha Exports v/s KP Jayaram&nbsp;[2020] 19 SC
						</li>
						<li>
							b. Aditya Prakash Entertainment Pvt. Ltd. v/s Magikwand Media Pvt. Ltd. 2018 SCC Online Bom 551
						</li>
						<li>
							c. State Bank of India v/s Alstom Power Boilers Ltd. &amp; Ors. 2003 SCC OnLine Bom 321
						</li>
						<li>
							d. Hindustan Gas &amp; Industries Ltd. v/s Commissioner of Income Tax [1978 SCC OnLine Cal 410
						</li>
						<li>
							Tata Capital Financial Services&nbsp;Limited vs. Mcnally Bharat Engineering Company Limited
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Therefore, a Preference Shareholder is not a Financial Creditor
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 unless the Preference Shares (CRPS) become due for
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 redemption.(p14.17)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 C. Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Such being the factual and legal position, we are constrained to hold that no case of any &ldquo;debt&rdquo; due to the Applicant and no existence of &ldquo;default&rdquo; on the part of Respondent within the meaning ascribed to the terms &ldquo;debt&rdquo; and &ldquo;default&rdquo; in the Code, is made out. In the aforesaid backdrop, we hold that this Petition, filed under Section 7 of IBC, 2016, is not maintainable.
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, we reject the prayer and accordingly dismiss this Company petition filed by EPC (Financial Creditor, herein). Liberty is however granted to the Petitioner to seek other remedies as may be available to it under any other law.
							<strong>
								 (p15-16)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
