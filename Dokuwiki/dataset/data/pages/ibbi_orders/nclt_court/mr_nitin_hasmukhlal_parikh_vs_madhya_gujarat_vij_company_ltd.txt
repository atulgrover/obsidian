	<h1>
		The moratorium order passed by this Tribunal applies in respect of Bank Guarantees other than Performance Guarantees furnished by the Corporate Debtor in respect of its property since it comes within the meaning of &lsquo;security interest&rsquo; &ndash; Mr. Nitin Hasmukhlal Parikh Insolvency Resolution Professional of M/s. Diamond Power Transformers Ltd. Vs. Madhya Gujarat Vij Company Limited &ndash; NCLT Ahmedabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 9, 2018
	</div>
	<p>
		Section 3(31) clearly says that Performance Guarantees are not included in the Security Interest. What is covered by the order of this Authority under Section 14(1)(c) is the Security Interest. Therefore, the moratorium order passed by this Tribunal is not applicable to the Performance Guarantees given by the Corporate Debtor. Therefore, the Bankers are at to allow the 1st to encash the Bank Guarantees that are given in respect of Performance Guarantees subject to other objections, if any. If the Applicant has got any other dispute with regard to the invocation Of the Performance Guarantees, it is not within the right of the Banker not to allow encashment of Performance Guarantees unless it is shown that fraud has been played in obtaining the Bank Guarantee or in invocation of Bank Guarantee or irrevocable injustice has caused to company as laid down decision of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in State Sugar Corporation Vs. Sumac International Ltd., reported in 1997 (1) SCC 568. It is stated that an unconditional Bank Guarantee can be invoked irrespective of the disputes raised by the customer with notable exceptions such as fraud and irrevocable injustice. It is stated that the Bank giving Guarantee is bound to honour it irrespective of any dispute raised by the customers.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Moratorium on Performance Guarantees
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Section 3(31) clearly says that Performance Guarantees are&nbsp;not included in the Security Interest. What is covered by the order of&nbsp;this Authority under Section 14(1)(c) is the Security Interest. Therefore,&nbsp;the moratorium order passed by this Tribunal is not applicable to the&nbsp;Performance Guarantees given by the Corporate Debtor. Therefore, the&nbsp;Bankers are at to allow the 1st to encash the Bank&nbsp;Guarantees that are given in respect of Performance Guarantees&nbsp;subject to other objections, if any. If the Applicant has got any other&nbsp;dispute with regard to the invocation Of the Performance Guarantees,&nbsp;it is not within the right of the Banker not to allow encashment of&nbsp;Performance Guarantees unless it is shown that fraud has been played&nbsp;in obtaining the Bank Guarantee or in invocation of Bank Guarantee&nbsp;or irrevocable injustice has caused to company as laid down&nbsp;decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
						<strong>
							<em>
								 State Sugar Corporation Vs. Sumac
							</em>
							 International Ltd.
						</strong>
						 , reported in 1997 (1) SCC 568.&nbsp;It is stated that an&nbsp;unconditional Bank Guarantee can be invoked irrespective of the&nbsp;disputes raised by the customer with notable exceptions such as&nbsp;fraud and irrevocable injustice. It is stated that the Bank giving&nbsp;Guarantee is bound to honour it irrespective of any dispute raised by&nbsp;the customers.
					</p>
					<p>
						The moratorium order passed by this Tribunal applies in respect&nbsp;of Bank Guarantees other than Performance Guarantees furnished by&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor in respect of its property since it comes within&nbsp;the meaning of &lsquo;security interest&rsquo;. Therefore, Respondent no. 1 is not&nbsp;entitled to invoke Bank Guarantees other than that comes within the&nbsp;meaning of performance Guarantees, during Moratorium period.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Disconnection of Power Supply
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Without issuing&nbsp;any notice the power supply was disconnected to the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;This Tribunal, in I.A. No. 328 of 2017 in CP (IB) No. 53 of 2017 held&nbsp;that Section 14(2) of the Code prevails over Section 56 of the Electricity&nbsp;Act, 2003 during the moratorium period. In that view of the matter,&nbsp;this Adjudicating Authority directed the Dakshin Gujarat Vij Company&nbsp;Limited not to disconnect the power supply to the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;invoking Section 56 of the Electricity Act, 2003 during the moratorium&nbsp;period. This Tribunal further ordered that DGVCL is entitled to make&nbsp;a claim for electricity consumption charges to the Resolution&nbsp;Professional and the Resolution Professional shall deal with the same&nbsp;as per the provisions of the Code, Rules and Regulations. The&nbsp;said order of this Tribunal has been challenged before the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;National Company Law Appellate Tribunal. The Hon&rsquo;ble Appellate&nbsp;Tribunal, in&nbsp;[2018] 04 NCLAT&nbsp;directed&nbsp;M/ s. AGB Shipyard Limited and the Resolution Professional to make&nbsp;payment of current electricity charges to DGVCL for the month of&nbsp;payment of current electricity charges to DGVCL for the month of&nbsp;2017 within 15 days failing which it will be open to the&nbsp;Appellant to disconnect the electricity. The said Company is&nbsp;still pending before the Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal. In the case on&nbsp;hand, it appears that the power supply was disconnected to the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor after the declaration of moratorium. Section 14(2) of&nbsp;the Code says, &lsquo;the supply of essential goods or services to the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor shall not be terminated or or interrupted&nbsp;during moratorium period. The supply of is an essential goods&nbsp;and service With reference to the Corporate Debtor. &ldquo;Essential&nbsp;Supplies&rdquo; is defined under Regulation 32 of the CIRP&nbsp;Regulations, 2016.
					</p>
					<p>
						NCLAT held that therefore, following the orders passed by this Adjudicating&nbsp;Authority in IA No. 328 of 2017 in CP (1B) No. 53 of 2017 and the&nbsp;interim order dated 15.1.2018 passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Appellate&nbsp;Tribunal in reported at&nbsp;[2018] 04 NCLAT, the&nbsp;Respondent No. 1 MGVCL is hereby directed to continue to supply&nbsp;electricity to the Corporate Debtor provided Applicant has cleared all&nbsp;the electricity consumption charges as on the date of disconnection.&nbsp;The Application stands disposed of accordingly.
					</p>
