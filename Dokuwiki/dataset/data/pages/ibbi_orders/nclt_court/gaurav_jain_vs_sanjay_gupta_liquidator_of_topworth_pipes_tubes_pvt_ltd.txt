						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Applicant herein is the successful bidder in the E-auction for&nbsp;the Sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor as a going&nbsp;concern and the Respondent herein is the Liquidator of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							While the Liquidation Process Regulations recognises going&nbsp;concern sale as one of the methods of sale, however, there is&nbsp;no definition as such for &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; either in the Code or in&nbsp;the Regulations.&nbsp;It is beneficial to refer the report of the Insolvency Law&nbsp;Committee dated 26/03/2018 (Para 8.1 of the Report), wherein&nbsp;the committee examined the term &ldquo;going concern&rdquo; as below:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;The phrase &lsquo;as a going concern&rsquo; imply that the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor would be functional as it would have been prior to&nbsp;the initiation of CIRP, other than the restrictions put by the&nbsp;Code.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							One advantage of the sale of the Corporate Debtor as a &lsquo;going&nbsp;concern&rsquo; is that the Corporate Debtor is retained, it will not be&nbsp;dissolved and the Corporate Debtor will be transferred along with the assets. So, the Corporate Debtor as a legal entity&nbsp;remains as such.&nbsp;The crux of the &lsquo;going concern sale&rsquo; is that the equity&nbsp;shareholding of the Corporate Debtor is extinguished and the&nbsp;acquirer takes over the undertaking with the assets, licenses,&nbsp;entitlements etc. The undertaking includes the business of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, assets, properties and rights etc. excluding&nbsp;the liabilities. However, in this case the assets that are included&nbsp;in the E-Auction Memorandum only have to be taken over by&nbsp;the Applicant.&nbsp;The Corporate Debtor survives, only the ownership is&nbsp;transferred by the Liquidator to the purchaser. All the rights,&nbsp;titles and interest in the Corporate Debtor including the legal&nbsp;entity is transferred to the purchaser. After the sale as a &lsquo;going&nbsp;concern&rsquo;, the purchaser will be carrying on the business of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;As far as the Liquidator is concerned, when the sale&nbsp;consideration is received from the bidder / purchaser, the same&nbsp;will be distributed to the Creditors in accordance with Section&nbsp;53 of the Code. Since the amount is paid to the Creditors in&nbsp;terms of the Code, the liabilities of the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;towards the Creditors are treated as settled and the purchaser&nbsp;takes the assets free of any encumbrances or whatsoever.
						</p>
						<p>
							In the normal parlance &ldquo;going concern&rdquo; sale is transfer of assets&nbsp;along with the liabilities. However, as far as the &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; sale in liquidation is concerned, there is a clear difference that&nbsp;only assets are transferred and the liabilities of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor has to be settled in accordance with Section 53 of the&nbsp;Code and hence the purchaser of this assets takes over the&nbsp;assets without any encumbrance or charge and free from the&nbsp;action of the Creditors. This Bench feels that following are the advantages of selling the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor &ldquo;as a going concern&rdquo;:
						</p>
						<p>
							a. The entity i.e., the Corporate Debtor itself gets&nbsp;transferred;
							<br/>
							b. The equity shareholding gets transferred or&nbsp;extinguished and new shares are issued;
							<br/>
							c. The purchaser is expected to carry on the business of&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor after the sale of assets is&nbsp;confirmed;
							<br/>
							d. The existing employees will have a chance to continue&nbsp;in their employment.&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							The decision to sell the Corporate Debtor as a going concern is&nbsp;taken by the Liquidator himself or in consultation with the&nbsp;Creditors / stakeholders and the proceeds from the sale of&nbsp;assets are going to be utilised for distribution to the Creditors in&nbsp;the manner specified under Section 53 of the Code. Hence all&nbsp;the Creditors of the Corporate Debtor get discharged and the&nbsp;assets are transferred free of any encumbrances. The legal&nbsp;entity of the Corporate Debtor however survives.&nbsp;In the case of sale as a &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;will not be dissolved in terms of Section 54 of the Code. The&nbsp;assets with the attendant, claims, limitations, licenses, permits&nbsp;or business authorisations, remains in the Company. Only the&nbsp;ownership of the Company is acquired by the successful bidder&nbsp;from the Liquidator.&nbsp;It is to be noted that even though there is no specific provision&nbsp;in the Code regarding &ldquo;sale of the Company as a going&nbsp;concern&rdquo;, IBBI has formed the Liquidation Process Regulations,&nbsp;under the Code and we have to take them as guiding principles&nbsp;in dealing with the case.
						</p>
						<p>
							The learned senior counsel for the Applicant referred to Para 83&nbsp;of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court&rsquo;s judgement in the case of&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									ArcelorMittal India Private Limited V/s. Satish Kumar Gupta&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									and Others&nbsp;[2018]  31 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court&nbsp;in the said Judgment has referred to Regulation 32 of the&nbsp;Liquidation Process Regulations which states that the Liquidator&nbsp;may also sale the Corporate Debtor as a &lsquo;going concern&rsquo;. Hence&nbsp;there is no impediment for the sale of the Corporate Debtor as&nbsp;a &lsquo;going concern&rsquo;.&nbsp;As a consequence to the above discussions and on perusal of&nbsp;the pleadings and submissions made by the learned senior&nbsp;counsel for the Applicant the following order is passed:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								(a) Prayer (a) is allowed. The Applicant is permitted to&nbsp;bring in Rs. 40 crores as share capital and Rs. 150.90&nbsp;crores as unsecured debt towards payment of sale&nbsp;consideration.
							</li>
							<li>
								(b) The Applicant shall get all the rights, title and interest&nbsp;over whole and every part of the Corporate Debtor,&nbsp;including but not limited to contracts free from&nbsp;security interest, encumbrance, claim, counter claim&nbsp;or any demur. The sale consideration, when received,&nbsp;shall be distributed by the Liquidator in terms of the&nbsp;Section 53 of the Code. Prayer (b) is accordingly&nbsp;allowed.
							</li>
							<li>
								(c) The Applicant shall not be responsible for any other&nbsp;claims / liabilities / obligations etc. payable by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor as on this date to the Creditors or&nbsp;any other stakeholders including Government dues.&nbsp;All the liabilities of the Corporate Debtor as on date&nbsp;stand extinguished, as far as the Applicant is&nbsp;concerned.
							</li>
							<li>
								(d) Any proceedings pending against the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor (other than against the Erstwhile Promoters&nbsp;or former members of the management of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor) as on date with respect to its&nbsp;liabilities, enquiries, investigations, assessments,&nbsp;claims, disputes, litigations etc. will not have any&nbsp;bearing against the assets sold in this process. The&nbsp;said assets are free from any financial implications&nbsp;arising out of any pending proceedings before&nbsp;relevant authorities, if any.
							</li>
							<li>
								(e) The existing shares / share capital of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor shall stand cancelled without there being any&nbsp;payment to the Shareholders. The Liquidator in&nbsp;consultation with the Registrar of Companies (RoC)&nbsp;concerned shall take action to change the status of&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor in the records of the RoC from&nbsp;the status of &ldquo;liquidation&rdquo; to the status of &ldquo;active&rdquo;.&nbsp;The concerned RoC shall do the needful.
							</li>
							<li>
								(f) The Board of the Corporate Debtor can be reconstituted as indicated in prayer (i) and necessary&nbsp;filings may be made to the RoC concerned.
							</li>
							<li>
								(g) Prayer (j) is allowed subject to payment of renewal&nbsp;fees, if any, from this date to the licensing&nbsp;authorities.
							</li>
							<li>
								(h) The Applicant / Corporate Debtor shall have the right&nbsp;to review and terminate any contract that was&nbsp;entered into prior to the date of this order. The assets&nbsp;specified in the E-auction memorandum, on payment&nbsp;of the consideration as above will vest with the&nbsp;Applicant. Accordingly, prayers (m), (n) &amp; (o) are&nbsp;allowed.
							</li>
							<li>
								(i) The Applicant shall not be held responsible / liable for&nbsp;any of the past liabilities of the Corporate Debtor in&nbsp;inquiries, investigations, assessments, notices,&nbsp;causes of action, suits, claims, disputes, litigations,&nbsp;arbitration or other judicial, regulatory or&nbsp;administrative proceedings against or in relation to,&nbsp;or in connection with the Corporate Debtor prior to&nbsp;this date. They shall not have any effect on the&nbsp;Applicant.
							</li>
							<li>
								(j) The Applicant is entitled to get all the rights, title and&nbsp;interest whole and every part of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor.
							</li>
							<li>
								(k) Creditors of the Corporate Debtor which include&nbsp;Creditors in any form or category including&nbsp;Government Departments shall stand extinguished&nbsp;qua the Applicant.
							</li>
							<li>
								(l) The non-compliance of provisions of any laws, rules,&nbsp;regulations, directions, notifications, circulars,&nbsp;guidelines, policies, licenses, approvals, consents or&nbsp;permissions prior to the date of acquisition shall stand&nbsp;extinguished qua the Applicant.
							</li>
							<li>
								(m) All the assets specified in the E-auction memorandum&nbsp;shall continue to be the assets of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor on making the payment of the balance sale&nbsp;consideration to the Liquidator by the Applicant.
							</li>
							<li>
								(n) The Liquidator is directed to provide all support and&nbsp;assistant to the Applicant for the smooth functioning&nbsp;of the Corporate Debtor to complete the acquisition.
							</li>
							<li>
								(o) The Liquidator and Applicant shall be at liberty to take&nbsp;all the steps required to make accounting entries for&nbsp;the smooth transmission and clearing the balance&nbsp;sheet.
							</li>
							<li>
								(p) The Liquidator is directed to ensure completion of&nbsp;pending filings with the Registrar of Companies,&nbsp;Income Tax Authorities and any other Government /&nbsp;Statutory Authorities.
							</li>
							<li>
								(q) The Corporate Debtor is entitled to get the benefits of&nbsp;brought forward losses, if any, subject to permission&nbsp;of the appropriate authority if so entitled under the&nbsp;relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
							</li>
							<li>
								(r) The Corporate Debtor may apply for incentives under&nbsp;the Package Incentive Scheme, framed by the&nbsp;Government of Maharashtra, subject to eligibility and&nbsp;other norms as provided in the said Scheme.
							</li>
							<li>
								(s) As far as the prayer for considering the bid submitted&nbsp;by the Applicant as Resolution Plan under Section 79&nbsp;of Income Tax Act, 1961 is concerned, the Applicant&nbsp;may approach the authority concerned who would&nbsp;consider such request under the Income Tax Act.
							</li>
						</ul>
