	<h1>
		The threshold of Rs. 1 crore denotes the total default of the Corporate Debtor to any Financial Creditor and not necessarily only to the Applicant Allottees | For calculating the total number of allottees, only the number of allotted units in a project shall be considered, irrespective of the number of units constructed | In cases of joint allotments, wherein a single unit is allotted to more than one person, the joint allottees of that unit shall be considered to mean a single allottee &ndash; Gajraj Jain Vs. Shivgyan Developers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Jaipur Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 1, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, an application has been filed by the allottees under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules seeking impleadment of additional Financial Creditors to initiate CIRP of the Corporate Debtor. NCLT Jaipur Bench held that: (i) The threshold of Rs. 1 crore denotes the total default of the Corporate Debtor to any Financial Creditor and not necessarily only to the Applicant Allottees. Hence, the allottees can move against the promoter even without any amount being due to them, provided that they meet the numerical threshold requirement under Section 7 of the case. For calculating the total number of allottees, only the number of allotted units in a project shall be considered, irrespective of the number of units constructed. (ii) In cases of joint allotments, wherein a single unit is allotted to more than one person, the joint allottees of that unit shall be considered to mean a single allottee. (iii) In Manish Kumar Vs. Union of India (supra), the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has held that the persons to whom the flats have already been sold after completing construction, thereof, would still be allottees and would be included for computation of threshold limit. (iv) NCLT allowed the application.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 618 NCLTCase Name:Gajraj Jain &amp; Ors. Vs. Shivgyan Developers Pvt. Ltd.Corporate Debtor:Shivgyan Developers Pvt. 					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Application has been filed by the Applicants (Petitioners/Financial Creditors) under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules seeking impleadment of additional Financial Creditors to initiate CIRP of the Corporate Debtor namely M/s Shiv Gyan Developers Pvt. Ltd. under Section 7 of the IBC in accordance with the Order passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in titled as
							<em>
								 Gajraj Jain &amp; Ors. Vs. Shivgyan Developers Pvt. Ltd.
							</em>
							 dated 18.05.2022.
						</li>
						<li>
							The main petition under Section 7 of the Code was filed by the Applicants seeking initiation of CIRP against Corporate Debtor which came to be dismissed vide Order dated 11.10.2019 passed by this Authority. Thereafter, the order dated 11.10.2019 was challenged by the Applicants before the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, New Delhi in
							<em>
								 Gajraj Jain &amp; Ors. Vs. Shivgyan Developers Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2019) 418 NCLAT
							</em>
							 . The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT dismiss the said Appeal vide order dated 18.11.2019. Afterwards, the Applicants approached the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court and vide Order dated 18.05.2022, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court set aside the order dated 18.11.2019 and further allowed the Applicants to implead 10% of the homebuyers to the main application so as to comply with the requirements of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicants submit that in compliance of order of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, this application has been filed seeking impleadment of additional number of homebuyers through their authorised representativesso as to meet the threshold and form 10% of the allottees of the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s concerned project.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar Vs. Union of India&nbsp;(2021) 16 SC
							</em>
							 while upholding the constitutionality of the Amendment also threw light on the rationality and the need for the introduction of such a threshold. The court observed that the consequence of empowering a lone allottee to initiate the CIRP at his discretion, could thwart the entire real estate project thereby endangering the interests of other allottees who were not in favour of the same and might have faith in the Developer.
						</li>
						<li>
							Thus, an application under Section 7 of the IBC is to be filed jointly by 100 allottees or 1/10th of the total number of allottees in the same Real Estate Project, whichever is less.
							<strong>
								 The threshold of Rs. 1 crore denotes the total default of the Corporate Debtor to any Financial Creditor and not necessarily only to the Applicant Allottees. Hence, the allottees can move against the promoter even without any amount being due to them, provided that they meet the numerical threshold requirement under Section 7 of the case. For calculating the total number of allottees, only the number of allotted units in a project shall be considered, irrespective of the number of units constructed.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In cases of joint allotments, wherein a single unit is allotted to more than one person, the joint allottees of that unit shall be considered to mean a single allottee.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the case in hand, originally an Application under Section 7 was preferred by Gajraj Jain and two other Applicants conjointly. In view of the amendment in Section 7 and insertion of second proviso by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2020 (1) of 2020, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court directed the Applicants to meet the threshold as provided in proviso second of Section 7 (1) of the IBC. In compliance of the order passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, an Application has been preferred impleading 25 applicants holding 17 flats, along with the original allotees (who hold two flats) which comes to 16% of the allotted unit i.e., 120. At the time of filing the Application the required minimum amount of default was Rs. 1 lakh. Hence, according to the Applicant, impleading 25 allottees who are holding 17 flats with the Original Applicants meets the requirement as envisaged under proviso second of the Section 7 (1). It is contended by the learned counsel for the Respondent/ Corporate Debtor that Applicants are holding total number of 19 units out of which possession has been given to the allottees of 14 flats; who have been finally handed over the possession of the units and sale deeds have been executed in their favour, therefore, the same cannot be included in the present application as there is no cause of&nbsp;action remaining in their favour. Thereby, the Application filed by the&nbsp;applicants is not maintainable.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In
								<em>
									 Manish Kumar Vs. Union of India (supra)
								</em>
								 , the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has held that the persons to whom the flats have already been sold after completing construction, thereof, would still be allottees and would be included for computation of threshold limit.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							A co-ordinate Bench of NCLT, New Delhi, Court V in IB- 762/ND/2020, in a similar situation while following the judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court passed in
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar Vs. Union of India
							</em>
							 held that as on the date&nbsp;of filing of the present application there were 653 allottees, and as per second&nbsp;proviso to Section 7(1) of the Code, minimum 64 allottees are needed for filing maintainability of the present petition. Moreover, the issue arising out of the plea taken by the Financial Creditor that allottees of apartments of which construction is complete are not to be included for computation of the threshold limit for the allottees required for the present petition, was considered by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar Vs. Union of India
							</em>
							 . Hence, the Financial Creditor does not fulfil the threshold limit as provided under Section 7 and due to this reason, application is not maintainable.
						</li>
						<li>
							The present application has been filed by the Allottees of 19 allotted units who are more than 10% of the allottees out of total 120 allotted units in the same real estate project thereby fulfilling the requirement of proviso second of Section 7(1).
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, this application is
							<strong>
								 allowed
							</strong>
							 and amended memo of parties is taken on record. The Registry is directed to serve a copy of this order to the parties herein. List IB No. 36/7/JPR/2018 for final arguments on 11.10.2023.
							<strong>
								 (p11-17)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
