	<h1>
		Merely because Corporate Debtor did not pay Sales Tax collected from the Applicant to Sales Tax Authorities, such amount will by no means qualify as Operational Debt owed to Applicant | A mere bald claim that entries of unpaid debt and interest have been recorded in the books of account will be of no help to Operational Creditor because mere unilateral entries in one&rsquo;s books of account unsupported by any legal justification will not qualify as Operational Debt within the meaning of Section 5 (21) of IBC &ndash; Transtec Overseas Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Dheeraj Aviation Ground Equipments Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 8, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, NCLT Mumbai Bench holds that: (i) An application under Section 9 of the Code, inter alia, requires strict proof of debt and default. (ii) A mere bald claim that entries of unpaid debt and interest have been recorded in the books of account of the Applicant will be of no help to the Applicant, because mere unilateral entries in one&rsquo;s books of account unsupported by any legal justification will not qualify as &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; within the meaning of Section 5 (21) of the Code. (iii) Merely because the Corporate Debtor did not pay sales tax collected from the Applicant to the Sales Tax Authorities in 2008-09, such amount will by no means qualify as &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; owed to the Applicant because this amount is actually owed by the Corporate Debtor to the Sales Tax Authorities and does not represent the price or consideration for supply of equipment to the Applicant. (iv) It is well-established that statutory dues like income tax, VAT etc. fall within the meaning of &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; under Section 5(21) of the Code only when these are payable to the relevant statutory authority (rather than a private party) which qualifies as &ldquo;Operational Creditor&rdquo; under the Code.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Application was filed under Section 9 of the IBC by M/s. Transtec Overseas Private Limited, the Operational Creditor on 30.08.2018 for initiating CIRP in respect of M/s. Dheeraj Aviation Ground Equipments Pvt. Ltd., the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Operational Creditor had purchased aviation ground equipment from the Corporate Debtor during the financial year 2008-09 vide two invoices dated 10.07.2008 amounting to Rs.18,32,484/- including sales tax/ VAT at the rate of 12.5% amounting to Rs. 2,03,609/-. Subsequently, during the financial year 2014-15, the Corporate Debtor purchased certain equipment from the Operational Creditor for a sum of Rs. 33,54,823/- which included VAT of Rs.3,72,758/- at the applicable rate of 12.5%.
						</li>
						<li>
							As the aforesaid amounts of sales tax/ VAT aggregating to Rs.5,76,367/-&nbsp;(Rs.2,03,609/- plus Rs.3,72,758/-) were not paid to the Sales Tax&nbsp;Authorities, the Operational Creditor had received Demand Notices from the&nbsp;Sales Tax Authorities. In these circumstances, the Operational Creditor&nbsp;treated the Corporate Debtor to be in default in respect of the aforesaid&nbsp;amount pertaining to the financial years 2008-09 and 2014-15.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Operational Creditor issued a Demand Notice dated 26.06.2017 to the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor calling upon it to pay the amount in default. Since the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor failed to pay the amount owed to the Operational Creditor,&nbsp;the latter filed the present Application for initiating CIRP in respect of the&nbsp;former.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether the VAT liability of Rs. 2,03,609/- payable by the Corporate Debtor to the Sales Tax Authorities for the financial year 2008-09 partakes the character of an &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; owed by the Corporate Debtor to the Operational Creditor?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is well-settled that an application under Section 9 of the Code,
							<em>
								 inter alia
							</em>
							 , requires strict proof of debt and default. The Applicant must prove with credible evidence and materials that there is an &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; owed to him by the Corporate Debtor and that there has been a default in payment of such debt on the date on which it fell due and payable.
							<strong>
								 (p4.2)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the present case, there was no amount due and payable to the Applicant from the Corporate Debtor in respect of the transaction in the financial year 2008-09.
							<strong>
								 (p4.2)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Similarly,
							<strong>
								 a mere bald claim that entries of unpaid debt and interest have been recorded in the books of account of the Applicant will be of no help to the Applicant, because mere unilateral entries in one&rsquo;s books of account unsupported by any legal justification will not qualify as &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; within the meaning of Section 5 (21) of the Code.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 (p4.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Merely because the Corporate Debtor did not pay sales tax collected from the Applicant to the Sales Tax Authorities in 2008-09, such amount will by no means qualify as &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; owed to the Applicant
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 because this amount is actually owed by the Corporate Debtor to the Sales Tax Authorities and does not represent the price or consideration for supply of equipment to the Applicant.(p4.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							This might be considered an act of unjust enrichment on part of the Corporate Debtor at the cost of the Applicant for which the remedy lies elsewhere but certainly not under Section 9 of the Code.
							<strong>
								 It is well-established that statutory dues like income tax, VAT etc. fall within the meaning of &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; under Section 5(21) of the Code only when these are payable to the relevant statutory authority (rather than a private party) which qualifies as &ldquo;Operational Creditor&rdquo; under the Code.(p4.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Thus, it emerges that the Applicant has failed to discharge the onus of proving the existence of an &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; due and arising under the law by the Corporate Debtor within the meaning of Section 5(21) of the Code. No concrete evidence has been placed on record in order to demonstrate its status as an &ldquo;Operational Creditor&rdquo; within the meaning of Section 5(20) of the Code vis-a-vis the Corporate Debtor regarding the alleged outstanding debt claimed in the matter. The present Application is thus liable to be dismissed on this ground alone. In view of this finding, the Applicant&rsquo;s whimsical and fanciful claims for unilateral and arbitrary interest and legal charges in the financial year 208-09 also fail as these have no legs to stand.
							<strong>
								 (p4.5)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							As regards the proof of &lsquo;default&rsquo; of alleged &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo;, it is noticed from the record that the Applicant has no uniform or consistent stand with regard to the specific date on which the alleged default occurred on part of the Corporate Debtor. For example, in column (2) of Part-IV of the Application, it claims the amount to be in default &ldquo;From the date 10th February, 2017 since the Operational Debtor has admitted their liability till date not Payment (sic)&rdquo;. However, while furnishing its fresh claim for financial year 2008-09 vide Affidavit filed on 13.04.2023, it has calculated interest of Rs.5,13,095/- (rounded off) @ 18% per annum &ldquo;from the year 2008-09 till the day 31st March, 2023&rdquo; on the alleged debt amount of Rs.2,03,609/-.
							<strong>
								 (p4.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The amount claimed to be in default in the instant case representing outstanding VAT liability of the financial year 2008-09 owed by the Corporate Debtor to the Sales Tax Authorities can by no stretch of imagination be treated as an &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; owed to the Applicant within the meaning of Section 5(21) of the Code. Hence, it is found that the present Application under Section 9 filed by the Applicant fails. Since the Applicant has not been able to prove the existence of the operational debt as well as default thereof within the period of limitation, we are of the considered view that the present Application preferred under Section 9 of the Code to initiate CIRP in the matter of the Corporate Debtor deserves to be
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p4.8)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
