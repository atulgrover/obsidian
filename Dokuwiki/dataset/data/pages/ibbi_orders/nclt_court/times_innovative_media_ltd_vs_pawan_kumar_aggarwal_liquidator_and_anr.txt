<h4>
	Section 53 of IBC, 2016 does not envisage any difference between Unsecured Operational Creditors and related party Unsecured Financial Creditors &ndash; Times Innovative Media Ltd. Vs. Pawan Kumar Aggarwal (Liquidator) and Anr. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
</h4>
<div>
	April 28, 2024
</div>
<p>
	Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench held that the constitutional validity of Section 53 of IBC has been upheld in Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Union of India &amp; Ors. (2019) ibclaw.in 03 SC. Since Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 does not envisage any difference between unsecured creditors and related party unsecured Financial Creditors, it cannot be successfully argued on behalf of the Operational Creditors that the Liquidator was wrong in placing Financial Creditor (who is related party) ahead of the Operational Creditors in the waterfall mechanism under Section 53 of IBC.
</p>
<p>
	<strong>
		 Brief about the decision:
	</strong>
</p>
<p>
	<em>
		<strong>
			 Background
		</strong>
	</em>
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		In this case, the Applicant (Operational Creditor) is seeking to set aside the order dated 03.09.2021 passed by the Liquidator/Respondent No. 2 whereby he has rejected the objections dated 20.06.2019 raised by the Applicant with an observation that Financial Creditor even though is a related party, is entitled to have priority over the Operational Creditors in terms of Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016.
	</li>
	<li>
		The Applicant seeks a direction to the Liquidator to give priority to the Operational Creditors in the distribution process under Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 over the Financial Creditor which is a related party of the Corporate Debtor.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	<em>
		<strong>
			 Submission of Applicant
		</strong>
	</em>
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		The Counsel for the Applicant has relied upon in the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT, Allahabad Bench in the matter of
		<em>
			 J. R. Agro Industries P Ltd. Vs. Swadisht Oils Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2018) ibclaw.in 21 NCLT
		</em>
		 decided on 24.07.2018 whereby it was held that the unsecured debt of related party, which is intra-group debt, will be treated as an equity contribution rather than as an intra-group loan, with the consequence that the intra-group obligation will rank lower in priority than the same obligation between the unrelated parties. It was further held that an intra-group debt given by a related Company of the Corporate Debtor be classified at par with other equity shareholder and partners as provided in waterfall mechanism given in Section 53 (1) (h) of the IB Code, 2016. The Court further held that all the Operational Creditors should be treated equally without being also classified by their ageing that is without any discrimination of the period of their outstanding dues. It was further observed that a related party of the Corporate Debtor cannot misuse the provision of Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 to defraud its creditors.
	</li>
	<li>
		Ld. Counsel for the Applicant has further pointed out that the aforesaid NCLT, Allahabad Bench judgement was upheld by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in its order dated 04.12.2018, reported in&nbsp;(2018) ibclaw.in 389 NCLAT.
	</li>
	<li>
		Ld. Counsel for the Applicant has further relied upon
		<em>
			 Binani Industries Limited vs. Bank of Baroda &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2018) ibclaw.in 06 NCLAT
		</em>
		 and has also relied upon
		<em>
			<strong>
				 P
			</strong>
			 hoenix ARC Pvt. Ltd. v. Spade Financial Services Limited&nbsp;(2021) ibclaw.in 03 SC.
		</em>
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	<em>
		<strong>
			 Submission of Respondents
		</strong>
	</em>
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		Counsel for the Respondents has argued that in Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016, there is no indication whatsoever that if the unsecured Financial Creditor is a related party, it has to be treated differently than the other unsecured Financial Creditors.
	</li>
	<li>
		Ld. Counsel for the Respondents has further argued that the ultra-virus of Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 were challenged before the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court and in its judgement passed in
		<em>
			 Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) ibclaw.in 03 SC,
		</em>
		 the constitutional validity of Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 has been upheld. Therefore, a related party Financial Creditor cannot be discriminated on any ground when Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 does not tend to distinguish the related party unsecured Financial Creditors from the other unsecured Financial Creditors in any manner.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	<em>
		<strong>
			 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
		</strong>
	</em>
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		In the matter of
		<em>
			 Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) ibclaw.in 03 SC
		</em>
		 , the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has unequivocally held that the rationale for differentiating between financial debts, which are secured, and operational debts which are unsecured, creates an intelligible differentia between financial debts and operational debts which are unsecured, is directly related to the objects sought to be achieved by the IB Code. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has further held that it can be seen that unsecured debts of various kinds and so long as there is some legitimate&nbsp;interest sought to be protected, having relations to the objects sought to be&nbsp;achieved by the statute in question, Article 14 does not get infracted and,&nbsp;therefore, the challenge to Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 must also fail.
		<strong>
			 (p20)
		</strong>
	</li>
	<li>
		It&nbsp;is, thus, evident from the law laid down in the matter of
		<em>
			 Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) ibclaw.in 03 SC
		</em>
		 that
		<strong>
			 the constitutional validity of this
		</strong>
		<strong>
			 section has been upheld.
		</strong>
		<strong>
			 Since Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 does not
		</strong>
		<strong>
			 envisage any difference between unsecured debtors and related party
		</strong>
		<strong>
			 unsecured Financial Creditors
		</strong>
		 , it cannot be successfully argued on behalf of&nbsp;the Applicant/Operational Creditors that the Liquidator was wrong in&nbsp;placing Respondent No. 2 ahead of the Operational Creditors in the waterfall&nbsp;mechanism under Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016.
		<strong>
			 (p20)
		</strong>
	</li>
	<li>
		So far as the law laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Allahabad Bench, in the matter of
		<em>
			 J. R. Agro Industries P Ltd. Vs. Swadisht Oils Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2018) ibclaw.in 21 NCLT
		</em>
		 is concerned, the same cannot be applied to the facts and circumstances of the instant case. Firstly, the order was passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT, Allahabad Bench in the context of a resolution plan, which is usually approved by the CoC in its commercial wisdom which is not justiciable and secondly, since the vires of Section 53 of the IB Code, 2016 have been upheld by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court and in the absence of any specific provision in the said section, it cannot be interpreted in a way to hold that Operational Creditors can be placed ahead and above of the unsecured Financial Creditor even if it may be a related party of the Corporate Debtor.
		<strong>
			 (p21)
		</strong>
	</li>
	<li>
		There is no merit in the application and IA No. 2382/2021 is hereby
		<strong>
			 dismissed
		</strong>
		 being devoid of merit. There shall be no order as to cost.
		<strong>
			 (p22)
		</strong>
	</li>
</ul>
