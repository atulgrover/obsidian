	<h1>
		CIRP under Sec. 7 of IBC cannot be initiated on the basis of Transfer Agreement/promise for Purchase of Debentures from Financial Creditors &ndash; Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd. Vs. Ajmera Realty and Infra India Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 8, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench holds that: (i) The relation between the Financial Creditor and the Corporate Debtor under the Transfer Agreement herein was that of a transferor and transferee. The transaction was for purchase of debentures secured, redeemable and non-convertible debentures from the Financial Creditor/Transferor for a consideration. (ii) Merely provide that the Corporate Debtor was liable to pay the balance purchase price towards purchase of debentures, mere stipulation in an agreement stating that the transferor shall be considered as a Financial Creditor of Guarantor, will not rendered the outstanding amount receivable by the Petitioner herein a Financial Debt within the meaning of the Code. (iii) The transaction ex facie is of purchase of NCD&rsquo;s in lieu of Rs.31.99 Crore and lacks the basic element of disbursal against the consideration for time value of money.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Meeti Developers Pvt. Ltd. (MDPL) had issued Non- Convertible Debentures (NCDs) of Rs. 55 Crores to the predecessor of the Petitioner under a Debenture Trust Deed dated 29.11.2016. These NCDs stood assigned to the Financial Creditor (Edelweiss Asset&nbsp;Reconstruction Company Ltd./Petitioner) under an Assignment&nbsp;Agreement dated 21.05.2019.
						</li>
						<li>
							Thereafter, MDPL defaulted in the repayment of the NCDs. The Financial Creditor&nbsp;filed a Company Petition being CP No. 783 of 2020 against&nbsp;MDPL under Section 7 of the IBC.
						</li>
						<li>
							By an order dated 05.03.2021, NCLT
							<strong>
								 admitted
							</strong>
							 CP No. 783 of 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							After the order of admission,&nbsp;Ajmera Realty and Infra India Ltd. (Corporate Debtor)&nbsp;along with MDPL&nbsp;approached the Financial Creditor to purchase the NCDs from the Financial Creditor&nbsp;for a sum of Rs. 31,66,00,000/-. Accordingly, the Financial Creditor and the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor executed a Transfer Agreement&nbsp;and Financial Undertaking dated 08.07.2022.
						</li>
						<li>
							It was agreed that out of the Rs. 31,66,00,000/-, the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;would make an upfront payment of Rs. 3,26,00,000/- and the balance&nbsp;sum of Rs. 28,40,00,000/- was to be paid by 31.12.2022. On&nbsp;default, the Corporate Debtor was also liable to pay interest at 24% p.a.
						</li>
						<li>
							It was further agreed that the NCDs would not be transferred to the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor till the time the Corporate Debtor complies with the&nbsp;payment obligations under the Transfer Agreement and Financial&nbsp;Undertaking and further, on the payment of the upfront amount, the Financial Creditor&nbsp;was required to withdraw CP No. 783 of 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Petitioner complied with its obligation and withdrew CP No. 783&nbsp;of 2020. After such withdrawal, the Corporate Debtor kept on seeking&nbsp;extension of time for paying the balance amount of Rs. 28,40,00,000/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							This is a Company Petition filed under p 7 IBC&nbsp;filed by Financial Creditor&nbsp;seeking&nbsp;to initiate CIRP against&nbsp;Ajmera Realty and Infra India Ltd. (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The relation between the Financial Creditor and the Corporate Debtor under the Transfer Agreement herein was that of a transferor and transferee. The transaction was for purchase of debentures secured, redeemable and non-convertible debentures from the Financial Creditor/Transferor for the consideration of Rs.31,66,00,000/-.
							<strong>
								 (p28)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Petitioner had contended that the Corporate Debtor has&nbsp;guaranteed or indemnified the Financial Creditor herein for all&nbsp;liabilities, losses and claims not only arising out of Transfer Agreement,&nbsp;Financial Undertaking but also the Debenture Trust Deed. The&nbsp;submission of the Petitioner cannot be considered as Clause 3 of the&nbsp;Transfer Agreement and Clause 2 &amp; 3 of Financial Undertaking&nbsp;iterated hereinabove
							<strong>
								 merely provide that the Corporate Debtor was
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 liable to pay the balance purchase price towards purchase of&nbsp;debentures,
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 mere stipulation in an agreement stating that the transferor
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 shall be considered as a Financial Creditor of Guarantor, will not
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 rendered the outstanding amount receivable by the Petitioner herein a
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Financial Debt within the meaning of the Code.(p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The transaction
							<em>
								 ex facie
							</em>
							 is of purchase of NCD&rsquo;s in lieu of Rs.31.99 Crore and lacks the&nbsp;basic element of disbursal against the consideration for time value of&nbsp;money.
							<strong>
								 (p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Anuj Jain vs Axis Bank Limited&nbsp;(2020) 06 SC
							</em>
							 has settled the position of law that the element of disbursal against the consideration of time value of money has to be traced in the genesis of debt. In the present matter, the transaction was for purchase of debentures for consideration and it is evident that the element of disbursal against the consideration for time value of money is absent. It appears that Petitioner has instituted the captioned Petition for recovery of money and seeks specific performance of the terms of the agreement.
							<strong>
								 (p30)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, CP No. 877 of 2023 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p31)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
