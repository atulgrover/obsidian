	<h1>
		Substance of a transaction is important rather than its form, and accounting entries cannot determine the character and nature of a transaction &ndash; Mr. Rakesh Bothra v. Mr. Alok Kailash Saksena &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 6, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Mumbai Bench held that: (i) Substance of a transaction is important rather than its form, and accounting entries can not determine the character and nature of a transaction. (ii) The Section 5(8) of the Code includes &ldquo;receivables sold or discounted other than any receivables sold on nonrecourse basis&rdquo; under the definition of financial debt. There is no dispute that the money was disbursed to the Corporate Debtor in consideration of assignment of such invoices and assignment of such invoices was not on nonrecourse basis, which is evidenced from the principal being acknowledged and paid by the Corporate during the course of pendency of Section 7 application filed by the Applicant. (iii) The Applicant shall provide copy of ledger account with narration in its books of account to the Corporate Debtor to assist him to make necessary inquiry for verification.
	</p>
</div>

	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Facts of the case
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			This Application IA 812/2023 is filed by the Resolution Professional of Makalu Trading Ltd. i.e. Sh. Rakesh Bothra (Applicant), in the CIRP of Topworth Urja &amp; Metals Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) u/s. 60(5) of the Insolvency &amp; Bankruptcy Code, 2016 for directions to the Interim Resolution Professional (Respondent No. 1 or IRP) to accept the interest claim for Rs. 6,32,30,108/- which has been rejected by him vide letter dated 23.1.2023.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Applicant had discounted the invoices&nbsp;raised upon MSEDCL by the Corporate Debtor, and whereby the&nbsp;Applicant paid the money due from MSEDCL to the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor, and the payment was to be received from MSEDCL by the Applicant.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Corporate Debtor had paid the interest to the Applicant in the past, which is evidenced from the TDS entries appearing in form 26AS u/s 194A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, which pertains to deduction of tax at source on interest, and this fact is also evidenced from a letter dated 1.3.2016 written by the Corporate Debtor to the applicant certifying that an amount of Rs. 79,92,329/- has been paid towards interest.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Corporate Debtor had acknowledged a sum of Rs. 9,46,20,985/- as due and payable to the Applicant on 29.3.2017 when the Applicant had sought the confirmation of amount of balance of Rs. 10,48,51,970/-, and also submitted the ledger account of the Applicant as appearing in their books.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Substance of a transaction is important rather than its form, and accounting entries can not determine the character and nature of a transaction. The evidences placed before show demonstrate that the Applicant had advanced money from time to time and the same was repayable along with interest in the form of realisation from MSEDCL, and in case of default, the same was to be repaid by the Corporate Debtor, a fact which is acknowledged in the books of accounts also.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Section 5(8) of the Code includes &ldquo;
			<em>
				 receivables sold or
			</em>
			<em>
				 discounted other than any receivables sold on nonrecourse basis
			</em>
			 &rdquo; under the&nbsp;definition of financial debt. There is no dispute that the money was&nbsp;disbursed to the Corporate Debtor in consideration of assignment of&nbsp;such invoices and assignment of such invoices was not on&nbsp;nonrecourse basis, which is evidenced from the principal being&nbsp;acknowledged and paid by the Corporate during the course of&nbsp;pendency of Section 7 application filed by the Applicant.
		</li>
		<li>
			There is no agreement in place to demonstrate the rate of interest on&nbsp;such facility. Accordingly, the Respondent is directed to deduce the&nbsp;rate of interest from the ledger entries and the period upto which&nbsp;such interest was provided in the books of the Corporate Debtor. On&nbsp;this basis, he is directed to admit any interest as discernible from the&nbsp;books of account of the corporate debtor remaining remaining&nbsp;unaccounted. The Applicant shall provide copy of ledger account&nbsp;with narration in its books of account to the Corporate Debtor to&nbsp;assist him to make necessary inquiry for verification.
		</li>
		<li>
			With the aforesaid directions, disposed of IA 812/2023.
		</li>
	</ul>
