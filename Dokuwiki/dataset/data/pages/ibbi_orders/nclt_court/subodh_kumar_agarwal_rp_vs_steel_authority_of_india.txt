	<h1>
		Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) lacks jurisdiction to set aside an Arbitral Award even the award was passed after initiation of CIRP and during in course of moratorium under Section 14 of IBC &ndash; Subodh Kumar Agarwal, RP of Hindustan Controls and Equipment Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Steel Authority of India &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 27, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) Although NCLT has the power to issue an Ante Arbitration injunction, in light of the Section 430 of the Companies Act, 2013 and also has the power to refer parties to Arbitration or enforce Arbitration Award, it cannot set aside an Arbitral Award. (ii) The NCLT cannot do what the IBC consciously did not provide the power to do. (iii) The Tribunal lacks jurisdiction to set aside an Arbitral Award, although it was passed in course of moratorium under Section 14 of IBC, the IA is disposed of with liberty to the RP to initiate appropriate proceedings to challenge the said Award.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							An Arbitral Award dated 06.03.2023 was passed by sole Arbitrator in an Arbitration Case between M/s. Steel Authority of India vs. Hindustan Controls &amp; Equipment Pvt. Ltd. and Amiya Commerce &amp; Construction Co Pvt. Ltd. after initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Debtor vide order dated 05.07.2022.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant Resolution Professional of Hindustan Controls and Equipment Pvt. Ltd., the Corporate Debtor has preferred this application seeking an order be passed setting aside of an Arbitral Award dated 06.03.2023 passed by sole Arbitrator and to restrain the Respondent to take any steps in terms of Impugned Arbitral Award dated 06.03.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is the contention of the RP that the Arbitral Award was passed after initiation of CIRP and ignoring the provisions of moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC. The RP placed reliance on
							<em>
								 Alchemist Asset Reconstrution Company Ltd. v. Hotel Gaudavan Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2017] 09 SC.
							</em>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Although NCLT has the power to issue an Ante Arbitration injunction, in light of the Section 430 of the Companies Act, 2013 and also has the power to refer parties to Arbitration or enforce Arbitration Award, it cannot set aside an Arbitral Award.(p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Referring
							<em>
								 Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Ltd. v. Mr. Amit Gupta &amp; Ors&nbsp;(2021) 44 SC
							</em>
							 holds that Section 238 of the IBC stipulates that Code would override other laws including an instrument having effect by virtue of any such law, NCLT&rsquo;s jurisdiction can be invoked only on the ground that relates to Insolvency Resolution Process. NCLT cannot derive its power from the sprit and object of the IBC. The NCLT has a wide residuary jurisdiction, under Section 60(5) of the IBC to adjudicate question of law a fact arising out of Insolvency Proceedings nonetheless it is defined by the text of IBC. The NCLT cannot do what the IBC consciously did not provide the power to do.
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In as much as, the Arbitration Award has been challenged in this application, whereas this Tribunal lacks jurisdiction to set aside an Arbitral Award, although it was passed in course of moratorium under Section 14 of IBC
							</strong>
							 , the IA is disposed of with liberty to the Resolution Professional to initiate appropriate proceedings to challenge the said Award.
							<strong>
								 (p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							This application IA(IB) No. 1820/( KB) /2023is accordingly, disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
