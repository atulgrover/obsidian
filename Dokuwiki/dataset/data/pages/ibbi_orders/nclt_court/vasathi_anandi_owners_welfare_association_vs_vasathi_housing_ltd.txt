	<h1>
		Amount deposited by Homebuyers in Corpus Fund towards maintenance of constructed apartments does not qualify as Financial Debt within the meaning of Section 5(8)(f) of IBC &ndash; Vasathi Anandi Owners Welfare Association v. Vasathi Housing Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 13, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Hyderabad Bench held that: (i) In the matters of real estate projects, not every amount raised can be considered as financial debt. The amount raised should be from an &lsquo;allottee&rsquo; and in relation to a &lsquo;real estate project&rsquo;. (ii) The money @ Rs 100/sq.ft. collected towards &lsquo;corpus fund&rsquo; cannot be said to have been raised under the real estate project. This distinction arises from the fact that this fund was not intended for the development of land into plots or apartments; instead, it was &ldquo;towards the maintenance&rdquo; of the constructed apartments. (iii) A comprehensive examination of Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC, 2016, in conjunction with the aforementioned judgments, unequivocally establishes that the said amount does not meet the criteria for classification as Financial Debt.
	</p>
</div>

	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Facts of the case
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			An application was filed by the Vasathi Anandi Owners Welfare Association (VAOWA) (Financial Creditor/ FC Association) represented by President of Vasathi Anandi Owners Welfare Association under Section 7 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC, 2016) seeking to initiate CIRP against Corporate Debtor, Vasathi Housing Ltd., for a default of Rs 5,33,12,287.00.
		</li>
		<li>
			Corporate Debtor has developed a residential project &lsquo;ANANDI PROJECT&rsquo; and the Financial Creditor is a society formed by the homebuyers in that Project. Total 483 apartments were built in this project, where each buyer had paid Rs. 100/Sq. Ft. of the apartment towards Corpus Fund under agreement of sale entered between homebuyers and Corporate Debtor.
		</li>
		<li>
			The interest accrued on Corpus Fund was meant to be utilized for maintenance of &ldquo;ANANDI PROJECT&rdquo; and the Corpus Fund was meant to be held by Corporate Debtor till 31.12.2013.&nbsp;Under terms of agreement of sale it is explicitly stated that collected Corpus Fund along with accrued interest shall be transferred to society (or an institution authorized by society and developer jointly) on 31.12.2013.
		</li>
		<li>
			Corporate Debtor delivered the possession of apartments to the homebuyers but many of the amenities such as swimming pool, tennis court, shuttle services, promised under agreement of sale pertinent to &lsquo;ANANDI PROJECT&rsquo; are still pending to be executed/developed.
		</li>
		<li>
			Corporate Debtor has paid part (INR 2 crores) of Corpus fund collected from members of Financial Creditor and there are 483 members in Financial Creditor as homebuyers with respect to Sq. Ft amount collected is totalling to INR 4,76,35,700 yet the pending amount of corpus fund along with interest has not been paid.
		</li>
		<li>
			Despite acknowledgement of amount collected as Corpus fund of &lsquo;ANANDI Project&rsquo; payable to Financial Creditor with an interest @6.5%, Corporate Debtor has not taken any steps and avers that the said amount has been due from 22.01.2018.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>
			 A. Whether the amount raised for the&nbsp;corpus fund was under the real estate project as defined in RERA&nbsp;2016 and referred to in p 5(8)(f) of IBC
		</strong>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			In the matters of real estate projects, not every amount raised can be considered as financial debt. The amount raised should be from an &lsquo;allottee&rsquo; and in relation to a &lsquo;real estate project&rsquo;. For both these terms, reference has been made to clauses (d) and (zn) of RERA 2016. It is therefore necessary to consider, whether the amount raised for the&nbsp;&lsquo;corpus fund&rsquo; was under the &lsquo;real estate project&rsquo; as defined in RERA&nbsp;2016 and referred to in p 5(8)(f) of IBC.
			<strong>
				 (p40)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In this case, while the corpus fund was collected under the agreement of sale of apartments built under ANANDI PROJECT, it was only for the purpose of ensuring maintenance of these apartments out of the interest accruing thereon.
			<strong>
				 (p42)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The money @ Rs 100/sq.ft. collected towards &lsquo;corpus fund&rsquo; cannot be said to have been raised under the real estate project. This distinction arises from the fact that this fund was not intended for the development of land into plots or apartments; instead, it was &ldquo;towards the maintenance&rdquo; of the constructed apartments.
			<strong>
				 (p43)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			Since the corpus fund was not collected under any real estate project, the owners of the apartments, who are before us in the form of a registered society, cannot be treated as allottee under p 5(8)(f) of IBC, in respect of the corpus fund as the same was not in relation to the real estate project. The term allottee referred in p 5(8)(f) of IBC is also defined in p 2(d) of RERA 2016. The corpus fund collected with an aim of maintaining the project was not from an allottee under a real estate project within the meaning of Section 5(8)(f) of IBC, and therefore it does not qualify as financial debt.
			<strong>
				 (p44)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			From clause 12 of the Agreement of Sale dated 07.02.2013, it is also clear that the corpus fund being an interest free deposit towards maintenance had no stipulation for any consideration for the time value of the money so deposited, which is an essential feature of any financial debt. As held in the landmark judgment by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
			<em>
				 Anuj Jain IRP for Jaypee Infratech Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd.&nbsp;(2020) 06 SC.
			</em>
			<strong>
				 (p45)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			Further in
			<em>
				 Pioneer Urban Land Infrastructure Ltd. &amp; Anr. Vs. Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) 13 SC
			</em>
			 , the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court clarified that, in the case of allottee&rsquo;s, only those amounts disbursed with the&nbsp;primary objective of earning profit can be deemed as financial debt and&nbsp;fall under the purview of the Explanation to Section 5(8)(f).
			<strong>
				 (p46-47)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In the present case, it is acknowledged that possession has already been handed over to the allottees, and the issue pertains to the Corpus Fund, which was intended for ensuring the proper maintenance of the project. It cannot be held that the primary motivation for collecting such a corpus fund was profit-oriented.
			<strong>
				 (p48)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<strong>
			 B. Conclusion
		</strong>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Therefore, in our considered view, the amount in question is akin to a prepayment made to a service provider, with maintenance services being the relevant service in this case. A comprehensive examination of Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC, 2016, in conjunction with the aforementioned judgments, unequivocally establishes that the said amount does not meet the criteria for classification as Financial Debt.
			<strong>
				 (p49)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			A parallel perspective was endorsed by the NCLT Mumbai Bench in the case of
			<em>
				 Innova Premises Co-operative Society Limited v/s Marathon Nextgen Realty Limited
			</em>
			 , CP (IB) No.1042/MB-IV/2020.
			<strong>
				 (p50)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			As a sequel to the foregoing observations the present application is not maintainable and the Applicant shall not fall within the meaning of Financial Creditor under p 5(7) of the IBC.
			<strong>
				 (p51)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			Accordingly, in our view the present Application is
			<strong>
				 not maintainable
			</strong>
			 . We hereby dismiss the present application filed under Section 7 of the IBC, 2016.
		</li>
	</ul>
