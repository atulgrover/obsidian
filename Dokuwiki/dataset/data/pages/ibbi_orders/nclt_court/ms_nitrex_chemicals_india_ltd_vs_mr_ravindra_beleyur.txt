	<h1>
		As per Section 30(5) of the IBC, the Resolution Applicant is entitled to attend the meeting of the CoC in which the Resolution Plan of the applicant is to be considered &ndash; Ms. Nitrex Chemicals India Limited Vs. Mr. Ravindra Beleyur and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Chennai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 13, 2018
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT held that it is clear that though the resolution applicant has no voting right in the CoC; and it is the CoC to approve or reject the resolution plan, an opportunity ought to have been provided to the resolution applicant to attend the meeting of the CoC in which the Resolution Plan is to be considered, to make his representation and to express his view point on the Resolution Plan submitted to the CoC. Therefore, the application of the Resolution Applicant is allowed and the CoC is directed to consider the plan afresh submitted by the Applicant by providing it reasonable opportunity of being heard within two weeks from the date of passing this order and RP is directed to file status report within two working days thereafter. Accordingly the application stands disposed of. The order is pronounced in the open court in the presence of both the parties/counsels.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							<em>
								 Facts of the Case
							</em>
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ol>
						<li>
							Resolution applicant (Nitrex Chemicals), filed an MA against Ravindra Beleyur/the RP of Merchem seeking the following reliefs:
							<ol>
								<li>
									Reject the CoC approved resolution plan of Acme Chem Ltd.
								</li>
								<li>
									The RP be directed to submit revised figures of Nitrex Chemicals to CoC for re-consideration.
								</li>
								<li>
									RP be directed to conduct transparent competitive bidding for value maximisation.
								</li>
								<li>
									Pending disposal of the application of Nitrex, the RP be restrained to pursue the CoC approved resolution plan.
								</li>
							</ol>
						</li>
						<li>
							Nitrex was amongst the resolution applicant of Merchem/Corporate Debtor &ndash; the RP had originally fixed the CoC meeting on 11
							<sup>
								 th
							</sup>
							 September, 2018, subsequently was altered to 12
							<sup>
								 th
							</sup>
							 September, 2018, to which Nitrex sought a change &ndash; since the promoters had some religious engagements, the request wasn&rsquo;t acceded by the RP. It was alleged by Nitrex, this implies, the RP had made up his mind to proceed with the meeting to the exclusion of Nitrex, hence being imputed/alluded of ulterior motives. Nitrex sought the copies of the minutes of meeting &ndash; 12
							<sup>
								 th
							</sup>
							 September, 2018, (
							<em>
								 in which Acme was declared the successful resolution applicant
							</em>
							 ) which was refused by the RP, expressing inability.
						</li>
						<li>
							It was the case of Nitrex, that the approval of resolution plan was held in hush, against the principles of Natural Justice &amp; to favour Acme, thus must be quashed and entire procedure be started afresh. Nitrex cited the ruling of Binani Cements V/s Vijay Kumar Iyer by NCLT Kolkata &amp; NCLAT&rsquo;s decision (15
							<sup>
								 th
							</sup>
							 May 2018) in Rajputana V/s Ultratech, wherein it was clarified of the persons entitled to attend CoC meeting in terms of&nbsp;Section&nbsp;24 &amp; 30 of I &amp; B Code,
							<em>
								 interalia
							</em>
							 also includes resolution applicant&rsquo;s whose application is/are being considered under Section 30 (5) of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							Nitrex alleged foul play on the part of RP for in a short span having reduced the eligibility criterion of annual average turnover of last three years from 200 Crores to 160 crores, to accommodate Acme &ndash; though Acme fell short of even the said revised criterion yet his resolution plan was taken up for consideration &amp; was declared as successful resolution applicant, thereby corroborating favouritism &ndash; further the RP was alleged of suppressing the revised offer of Nitrex to CoC, and slashing the eligibility criterion in favour of Acme, in sum granting an unfair advantage to Acme through an opaque/discriminatory process sans the rule of natural justice to approve the resolution plan of Acme. It is also the case of Nitrex that it offered better package to operational creditors; the CoC also didn&rsquo;t record its satisfaction/endorsement as to the viability/feasibility of the resolution plan of Acme in terms of Section 30 (4) of the I &amp; B Code; and prima-facie the resolution plan of Acme was likely to attract considerable Minimum Alternate Tax Liability under Section 115 JB of the Income Tax Act and liability under Section 140 A of the Income Tax Act &ndash; on both issues, the resolution plan of Acme was silent, thereby a distorted picture was presented to the CoC, morefully the proposal submitted by RP for approval to CoC wasn&rsquo;t in accordance with Regulation 39 (2) of CIRP&nbsp;Regulations, i.e. in compliance to the requirement of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of Section 31 (1) of the I &amp; B Code, NCLT can approve the resolution plan so approved by CoC under Section 30 (4) of the Code, provided the resolution plan meets the test of Section 30 (2) of the Code. It is the case of Nitrex that the Resolution Plan seeks to secure accord to such proposals in the Resolution Plan, beyond the scope and power of NCLT.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is the counter of the RP, that the proposal submitted and approved by 100% majority was in complete accordance with the Code &ndash; furthermore the turnover criterion so far as eligibility of participating as resolution applicant was reduced, lest it grants a dominant position to NOCIL in rubber chemical industry (and also Nitrex in its MA mislead NCLT in understating the average turnover of Acme), further the RP was not in attendance of the CoC meeting on 12
							<sup>
								 th
							</sup>
							 Sept, 2018 and in any case, he is duty bound to maintain confidentiality as to the minutes of the meeting. Morefully given the&nbsp;Supreme Court&nbsp;ruling, there stands no vested right in favour of any Resolution Applicant, that his plan ought to be considered; and that in any case Nitrex was given, opportunity enough to attend the CoC meeting, which it didn&rsquo;t attend either through himself or his authorised representative (an option anyways available, since the resolution applicant was a body corporate) and in any case all proposals (including that of Nitrex) were available to CoC and basis the &lsquo;evaluation matrix&rsquo; CoC approved the resolution plan of Acme. The ratio of ruling in Rajputana Industries isn&rsquo;t applicable in the present case as in the said case the resolution applicant wasn&rsquo;t allowed to attend the CoC meeting, herein, the resolution applicant abstained from attending the meeting &ndash; thus it can&rsquo;t be the case of Nitrex to agitate violation of Section 24 of the Code. It was further the case of RP, that invocation of Section 60 (5) of the Code is of a different import qua the jurisdiction of NCLT, which concerns application or proceedings by or against the corporate debtor, as also Nitrex mislead NCLT on various other financial criterion/data etc&hellip;in any case no differential/discriminatory criterion was adopted in evaluating various resolution applications and as per the laid out evaluation matrix. In any case, the application of Nitrex isn&rsquo;t maintainable under Section 60 (5) of the Code and remedy if any is under 60 (1) of the Code before NCLAT; qua the evaluation matrix, the resolution plan by Acme secured 73 marks against 52 by Nitrex, accordingly the resolution plan submitted by Acme was put up for approval under Section 31 of the Code, and is pending for NCLT approval.
						</li>
					</ol>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Observation by the Court:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						NCLT held that from the facts and circumstances of the present case, it is worth to be noted that the Resolution Applicant had expressed his inability to attend the CoC&rsquo;s meeting scheduled on 12.09.2018 due to religious constrains and requested the RP to fix any other date after 14.09.2018 for meeting of the CoC. However, the Resolution Professional refused to re-schedule the meeting. Therefore, a reasonable opportunity of being heard is not given to the applicant and the same is in violation of the principle of natural justice. As per Section 30 (5) of the IB Code, 2016, the resolution applicant is entitled to attend the meeting of the CoC in which the resolution plan of the applicant is to be considered. In this regard reliance is placed on the Judgement passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
						<strong>
							<em>
								 ANG Industries Ltd vs Shah Brothers Ispat Pvt. Ltd.
							</em>
						</strong>
						 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 109 of 2018, wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble Appellate Tribunal while deciding the matter, relied upon the findings of Joint Parliamentary Committee and its earlier ruling in the matter of
						<strong>
							<em>
								 Rajputana Properties Pvt. Ltd. V/s. Ultra Tech Cement Ltd. &amp; Ors.
							</em>
						</strong>
						 in I.A. No. 594 of 2018 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 188 of 2018 and observed as under:
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;&hellip;&hellip;&hellip;. the Committee of Creditors while approving or rejecting one or other resolution plan should follow such procedure which is transparent. Those who will (be) watching the proceeding such as (suspended) Board of Directors or its Partners; Operational Creditors or its representatives and Resolution Applicant(s) are not mere spectator but may express their views to the Committee of Creditors for coming to conclusion in one or other way.&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Thus, it is clear that though the resolution applicant has no voting right in the CoC; and it is the CoC to approve or reject the resolution plan, an opportunity ought to have been provided to the resolution applicant to attend the meeting of the CoC in which the Resolution Plan is to be considered, to make his representation and to express his view point on the Resolution Plan submitted to the CoC. Therefore, the application of the Resolution Applicant is allowed and the CoC is directed to consider the plan afresh submitted by the Applicant by providing it reasonable opportunity of being heard within two weeks from the date of passing this order and RP is directed to file status report within two working days thereafter. Accordingly the application stands disposed of. The order is pronounced in the open court in the presence of both the parties/counsels.
					</p>
