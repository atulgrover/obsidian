		<h1>
			Once a Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority, it cannot be re-opened and add another condition in the Plan &ndash; Jose Pradeep Vs. CA, Jasin Jose &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 5, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			NCLT held that the prayer to modify the Resolution Plan incorporating mutually agreed position regarding the grace period and to follow the revised timeline based on that cannot be accepted by this Adjudicating Authority, because once a Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority, it cannot be re-opened and add another condition in the Plan. In this connection, the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble the Supreme Court in Rahul Jain v. Rave Scans Pvt. Ltd. [2019]  05 SC may be referred to, in which it was held that once a Resolution Plan is approved by the NCLT, it attains finality and cannot be disturbed. The NCLAT followed the same reasoning and conclusion in QVC Exports Pvt. Ltd. v. United Tradeco FZC [2020]  158 NCLAT and held that the Adjudicating Authority is not permitted to alter a plan under the guise of inherent powers. Orders which have attained finality cannot be reviewed under the inherent powers of the court.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Successful Resolution Applicant has filed this IA under Section 60(5) of Code read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 against South Indian Bank&nbsp;and seeking the reliefs 1. Resolution Plan be modified by incorporating the mutually agreed position regarding the grace period. 2. The Applicant be permitted to follow the revised timeline based on mutual agreement between the parties during the 9th CoC meeting or; 3. Considering the medical condition of the Applicant, grant an extension of two months to the timelines proposed under the Resolution Plan.
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT held that the prayer to modify the Resolution Plan incorporating mutually agreed position regarding the grace period and to follow the revised timeline based on that cannot be accepted by this Adjudicating Authority, because once a Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority, it cannot be re-opened and add another condition in the Plan. In this connection, the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble the Supreme Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Rahul Jain v. Rave Scans Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2019]  05 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;may be referred to, in which it was held that once a Resolution Plan is approved by the NCLT, it attains finality and cannot be disturbed. The NCLAT&nbsp;followed the same reasoning and conclusion in&nbsp;
							<em>
								QVC Exports Pvt. Ltd. v. United Tradeco FZC&nbsp;[2020]  158 NCLAT&nbsp;
							</em>
							and held that the Adjudicating Authority is not permitted to alter a plan under the guise of inherent powers.&nbsp;Orders which have attained finality cannot be reviewed&nbsp;under the inherent powers of the court.&nbsp;Only the request of the Applicant for a mutually agreed cure period for final payment as per Resolution Plan subject to maximum of 19th March 2021 was recorded.&nbsp;Since, it is clear that the Applicant failed to honour his commitments in complying with the conditions in the approved Resolution Plan, grant of any further time is not called for in this matter. In view of the aforesaid discussions, IA/37/KOB/2021 is dismissed.
							<strong>
								(p16-19)
							</strong>
						</p>
