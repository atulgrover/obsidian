	<h1>
		Whether Earnest Money Deposit(EMD) paid by Operational Creditor against tender for services would amount to an &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; within the meaning of the IBC, 2016? &ndash; Supreme Transport Organisation Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Maharashtra Airport Development Company Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		May 26, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that it is very clear from the plain reading of the definition of &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; under Section 5(21) of the Code that the EMD deposited by an Operational Creditor is not included anywhere in the above definition and therefore the amount claimed by the Petitioner being the EMD does not fall within the definition of &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo;. The learned Senior PCA appearing for the Operational Creditor&nbsp;tried to convince this Tribunal that it is an advance paid by the Operational Creditor for securing the services by Corporate Debtor and therefore would amount to an &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo;. However, this Tribunal is unable to accept the above submission in the absence of any authoritative pronouncement.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						This Company Petition is filed by Supreme Transport Organisation Pvt. Ltd. (Operational Creditor) seeking to initiate of CIRP against Maharashtra Airport Development Company Ltd. called as Corporate Debtor by invoking the provisions of Section 9 of IBC for a Resolution of Operational Debt of Rs. 7,00,000/- (Rs. 5,00,000/- against the Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) for Design &amp; Services for up-gradation of Infrastructure at Belora Airport, Amravati and Rs. 2,00,000/- against the Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) for Consultancy Services for planning, designing and development of Greenfield Airport at Chandrapur totalling to Rs. 7,00,000/-). The amount claimed in the above Company Petition is paid to the Respondent towards EMD amount for securing a tender for Consultancy Services from the Corporate Debtor.
					</p>
					<p>
						The above amount was forfeited by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor Company due to certain alleged false and misleading information and alleged fabricated documents submitted by the Operational Creditor.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						It is very clear from the plain reading of the definition of &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; under Section 5(21) of the Code that the EMD deposited by an Operational Creditor is not included anywhere in the above definition and therefore the amount claimed by the&nbsp;Petitioner being the EMD does not fall within the definition of&nbsp;&ldquo;operational debt&rdquo;. The learned Senior PCA appearing for the Operational Creditor&nbsp;tried to convince this Tribunal that it is an&nbsp;advance paid by the Operational Creditor for securing the services by&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and therefore would amount to an &ldquo;operational&nbsp;debt&rdquo;. However, this Tribunal is unable to accept the above submission&nbsp;in the absence of any authoritative pronouncement.
						<strong>
							 (p9)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The next issue is with regard to the pre-existing dispute. As stated above, the EMD amount was forfeited by the Corporate Debtor due to the alleged misinformation and alleged fabricated documents submitted by the Operational Creditor which is purely a contractual dispute that follows outside the realm of Section 9 of the Code and this Tribunal has no power to decide the above issue. Accordingly, both the above issues were answered against the Operational Creditor.
						<strong>
							 (p10)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						For the aforesaid reasons, this Tribunal did not find any merit in the above Company Petition and is liable to be dismissed on both the grounds. Accordingly, the above Company Petition is
						<strong>
							 dismissed
						</strong>
						 .
