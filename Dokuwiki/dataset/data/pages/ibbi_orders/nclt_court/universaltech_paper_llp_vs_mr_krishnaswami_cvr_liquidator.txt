	<h1>
		The primary goal of a Resolution Plan as well as the sale of a Corporate Debtor as a Going Concern remains the same i.e revival of Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s business &ndash; Universaltech Paper LLP Vs. Mr. Krishnaswami CVR, Liquidator of Kohinoor Pulp &amp; Paper Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 17, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In IA to grant the reliefs/ concessions to successful bidder, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench holds that: (i) The primary goal of a resolution plan as well as the sale of a Corporate Debtor as a going concern remains the same i.e revival of the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s business. The struggles faced by the purchaser during the sale of a Corporate Debtor as a &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; and that of the Successful Resolution Applicant are similar, if not the same. As such, comparable reliefs and concessions ought to be granted in both cases. (ii) While the successful bidder is allowed the liberty to file application before the Adjudicating Authority claiming reliefs and concessions, the said reliefs and concessions cannot be too elaborate and general and must be in consonance with the process document in the liquidation proceedings. (iii) With respect to the waivers sought in relation to guarantors, the judgment of Lalit Kumar Jain v Union of India &amp; Ors. (2021) 61 SC wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court held in para 133 that sanction of a resolution plan and finality imparted to it by p 31 does not per se operate asa discharge of the guarantor&rsquo;s liability shall apply. In view of the above position of law, the sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor &lsquo;as a going concern&rsquo; shall be binding on the Corporate Debtor and other persons, authorities etc.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The interlocutory application has been filed by the Applicant herein being the successful bidder of the Corporate Debtor under p 60(5)(c) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 to grant the reliefs/ concessions to applicant as the corporate debtor is sold as going concern in liquidation.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The primary goal of a resolution plan as well as the sale of a Corporate Debtor as a going concern remains the same i.e revival of the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s business. The struggles faced by the purchaser during the sale of a Corporate Debtor as a &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; and that of the Successful Resolution Applicant are similar, if not the same. As such, comparable reliefs and concessions ought to be granted in both cases. As such, the law laid down in
							<em>
								 Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 54 SC
							</em>
							 ought to be made applicable to cases in which the Corporate Debtor has been sold as &lsquo;going concern&rsquo;
							<strong>
								 .(p3.7)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In light of decision in
							<em>
								 Jasamrit Designers Pvt. Ltd. v. Mr. Gian Chand Narang Liquidator and Anr.&nbsp;(2023) 414 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , it becomes clear that while the&nbsp;successful bidder is allowed the liberty to file application before the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority claiming reliefs and concessions, the said reliefs&nbsp;and concessions cannot be too elaborate and general and must be in&nbsp;consonance with the process document in the liquidation proceedings.
							<strong>
								 (p3.11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							While some of the prayers sought by the Applicant in the instant matter&nbsp;come within the purview of IBC and&nbsp;the Companies Act 2013, it can be seen that scope of the other reliefs&nbsp;are too wide and non- specific or they fall under the power and&nbsp;jurisdiction of different government authorities/departments. As such,&nbsp;the same shall be dealt with the respective competent&nbsp;authorities/forums/offices, Government or Semi Government of the&nbsp;State or Central Government keeping in view the spirit of the code.&nbsp;Since, this is a &lsquo;Going concern sale&rsquo; , the Successful Bidder shall make&nbsp;necessary applications to the concerned regulatory or statutory&nbsp;authorities for renewal of business permits and supply of essential&nbsp;services, if required, and all necessary forms along with filing fees etc.&nbsp;and such authority shall also consider granting the same keeping in&nbsp;mind the objectives of the Code which is intended to resolving the&nbsp;insolvency of the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p3.12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, it is noted that the Applicant has sought that the contracts and&nbsp;arrangements except those with existing promoters/directors of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor shall remain in existence on the same terms and&nbsp;conditions. In this regard, we are of the considered opinion that without&nbsp;having a clear picture of the various contracts between the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor and third-parties, carte blanche orders ought not be passed. As such, the prayers seeking carte blanche orders relating to contractual rights are not granted by this Adjudicating Authority.
							<strong>
								 (p3.13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							With respect to the waivers sought in relation to guarantors, the judgment of
							<em>
								 Lalit Kumar Jain v Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2021) 61 SC
							</em>
							 wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court held in para 133 that sanction of a resolution plan and finality imparted to it by p 31 does not per se operate asa discharge of the guarantor&rsquo;s liability shall apply. In view of the above position of law, the sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor &lsquo;as a going concern&rsquo; shall be binding on the Corporate Debtor and other persons, authorities etc.
							<strong>
								 (p3.14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							With respect to the reliefs and waivers sought for all inquiries, litigations, investigations and proceedings shall be granted strictly as per the p 32A of the Code.
							<strong>
								 (p3.15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							With the above observations, the instant application is allowed to the extent mentioned herein above. IA (IBC) No.1055/KB/2023 shall stand disposed of accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p3.16)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
