		<h1>
			Whether the Liquidator is competent to determine and admit a specific amount of money as payable to a particular creditor, when there is pending litigation before the appropriate forum, such as the DRT/High Courts and other Judicial Authorities &ndash; Mr. Ashok Oswal, Managing Director (Suspended) Oswal Spinning and Weaving Mills Ltd. Vs. Hemanshu Jetley (Liquidator) &ndash; NCLT Chandigarh Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			June 8, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority held that the Liquidator is required to verify the claims with reference to the date of liquidation and if there is no crystallization of a debt through a decree on that date, the Code does not direct the Liquidator to wait for the same. The Applicant contented that proceedings before DRT need to be completed before the distribution under the IBC can take place, it is clarified that the general principle of construction in a circumstance where two special Acts are in conflict with each other is that, the Act made later should prevail vide the maxim &ldquo;leges posteriores priores abrogant&rdquo;. In view of Section 238 of the Code, 2016 steps taken under the Code by the authority would have precedence over other authorities in parallel proceedings. Thus, the provisions of Code, 2016 should prevail in case of any conflict. his Authority, therefore, finds no reason to keep the liquidation proceeding in abeyance, and thus, rejects the Applicant&rsquo;s prayer that the Liquidator should await the final adjudication of the claims before the Hon&rsquo;ble DRT before accepting the claims of the Respondents and subsequently disbursal of the amounts.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Liquidator(Respondent No.1) has accepted the claims of Respondent No. 2 to 5, which the applicant states to be without any legal basis. The Managing Director (Suspended) (applicant) prays:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								to quash the&nbsp;acceptance of the claim of Respondent Nos. 2 to 5 by the Liquidator;&nbsp;and
							</li>
							<li>
								direct Liquidator to await the final adjudication of the claims&nbsp;pending before the Hon&rsquo;ble Debt Recovery Tribunal(DRT) or the Hon&rsquo;ble Punjab and&nbsp;Haryana High Court, or any other authority/Court/Tribunal as the case may be,&nbsp;before any claim for payment of financial dues on behalf of Respondent Nos. 2&nbsp;to 5 can be accepted and monies accordingly disbursed;
							</li>
							<li>
								direct the Liquidator, to effectively contest the claims made by the corporate&nbsp;debtor before various Courts/Tribunals, in order to discharge his legal&nbsp;obligations under the IB Code, 2016;
							</li>
							<li>
								pending consideration of the reliefs as&nbsp;aforesaid, restrain the Liquidator from disbursing any funds to&nbsp;Respondent Nos.2 to 5; and
							</li>
							<li>
								direct the Liquidator, to effectively&nbsp;contest the claims made by the Corporate Debtor before various Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Courts/Tribunals, in order to discharge his legal obligations under the IB Code&nbsp;2016.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							The Liquidator states that the proceedings before the Hon&rsquo;ble DRT and the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Punjab and Haryana as well as proceedings initiated by Banks before different Forum are separate and distinct from the proceedings under IBC. It is reiterated that the Liquidator is not bound by the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Punjab and Haryana or the Hon&rsquo;ble DRT while admitting the claim of Kotak Bank. Further, admission of claim by the Liquidator does not amount to overruling the decision of any court, as alleged by the Applicant.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							In the present application, the issues before this Adjudicating Authority are whether the acceptance of claims of Respondent Nos. 2 to 5 by the Liquidator is not in accordance with the provisions of the Code and the Rules made there in; and whether the Liquidator should wait for the final adjudication of the claims of the Respondents pending before the learned DRT and other Judicial Authorities, before admitting the claims and disbursing the amounts.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								It is an undisputed proposition that the Liquidator is mandated to discharge his duties strictly as per the provisions of Section 38 and 39 of the Code, 2016 and Regulations 16, 18, 30 &amp; 31 of IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016 while verifying/admitting the claims. On a careful perusal of these provisions,
								<strong>
									&nbsp;it is clear that the Liquidator is required to verify the claims with reference to the date of liquidation and if there is no crystallization of a debt through a decree on that date, the Code does not direct the Liquidator to wait for the same.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;As regards the admission of the claims, we have carefully gone through the voluminous evidence including correspondences etc. produced in the course of proceedings and also the grounds on which the claims have been admitted in the cases of Respondent Nos.2 to 5 by the Liquidator with reference to. Prima facie, the Liquidator has verified the claims on the basis of Form-D submitted by the Financial Creditors along with documents, records and financial statements filed with the same. It is also noted that the Liquidator has accepted only partial amounts of the total claims made by the financial creditors and have sought legal opinion upon some of the issues before reaching a conclusion. The issues have also been discussed in the meetings of the Stakeholders Consultation Committee (SCC). The records show that he has simply verified the claims of the stakeholders based on the information provided by the claimants along with the affidavit and by considering all the evidence before him and has filed a &ldquo;List of Stakeholders&rdquo; before this Adjudicating Authority. No particular procedural mistake on the part of the Liquidator has been pointed out by the applicant during the current proceedings. It may be noted that word adjudication has been used by the Liquidator in more than one place while verifying the claim of the respondents but it seems to be a typographical or mistake through inadvertence, and the same is ignored.&nbsp;In view of the above discussion, we hold that the allegation of the applicant that the Liquidator has &ldquo;adjudicated&rdquo; the claims filed by the respondent-financial creditors and has wrongly accepted their claims is misconceived.
								<strong>
									(p15-16)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is trite law that timelines are sacrosanct in a liquidation proceedings as is apparent from the Regulation 47 laying down Model TimeLine for liquidation process. We also note that Section 53 dealing with Distribution of Assets also commences with a Non-obstante&rsquo; clause which specifically overrides any law enacted by the Parliament or any State Legislature for the time being in force and nowhere requires the Liquidator to wait for the decisions of other court before commencing with the process of distribution. Furthermore, Regulation 43 of the Liquidation Process Regulations 2016 itself provides the remedy, if any, undue benefit is given to a stakeholder in distribution.
								<strong>
									(p17-18)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								<strong>
									Now coming to the Applicant&rsquo;s contention that proceedings before Hon&rsquo;ble DRT need to be completed before the distribution under the IBC can take place, it is clarified that the general principle of construction in a circumstance where two special Acts are in conflict with each other is that, the Act made later should prevail vide the maxim &ldquo;
									<em>
										leges posteriores priores abrogant
									</em>
									&rdquo;.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;The DRT was constituted under Recovery of Debts Due to Banks &amp; Financial Institutions Act, 1993, much prior to the Code, 2016.&nbsp;
								<strong>
									In view of Section 238 of the Code, 2016 steps taken under the Code by the authority would have precedence over other authorities in parallel proceedings.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;
								<strong>
									Thus, the provisions of Code, 2016 should prevail in case of any conflict.(p19)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Furthermore, this contention of the Applicant does not hold much water in view of the a catena of judgements of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the following matter of&nbsp;
								<em>
									Bimal Kumar Manubhai Savalia vs. Bank of India and Ors
								</em>
								.&nbsp;
								<em>
									[2020]  247 NCLAT
								</em>
								. The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of&nbsp;
								<em>
									Rakesh Kumar Gupta V. Mahesh Bansal and others&nbsp;[2020]  227 NCLAT.
								</em>
								&nbsp;Furthermore, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of&nbsp;
								<em>
									Harkirat S. Bedi Vs. Oriental Bank of Commerce&nbsp;[2019]  62 NCLAT.
								</em>
								<strong>
									(p19)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								This Authority, therefore,&nbsp;
								<strong>
									finds no reason to keep the liquidation proceeding in abeyance, and thus, rejects the Applicant&rsquo;s prayer that the Liquidator should await the final adjudication of the claims before the Hon&rsquo;ble DRT before accepting the claims of the Respondents and subsequently disbursal of the amounts.(p20)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
					</div>
