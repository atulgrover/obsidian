	<h1>
		Slump sale under IBC cannot be converted into the sale as a going concern &ndash; Jindal Power Ltd. Vs. Dushyant C. Dave Liquidator- Shirpur Power Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Ahmedabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 26, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, the applicant wanted us to issue the direction to the liquidator to convert the slump sale into the sale as a going concern. NCLT held that in our considered opinion this cannot be done. The price of the corporate debtor in the sale as a going concern was fixed at Rs. 5,66,23,00,000/- as maximum and Rs. 4,33,16,59,500/- as a minimum whereas the price of slump sale of the corporate debtor was fixed at Rs. 4,77,84,50,000/- as maximum and Rs. 3,14,38,49,770/- as a minimum. The applicant accepted the corporate debtor in slump sale for a minimum price of Rs. 3,14,38,49,770/-. There is a vast difference between the sale price of the corporate debtor as a going concern and the sale price of the corporate debtor in a slump sale. If we allow the applicant&rsquo;s request then certainly the rights of members of the stakeholders' committee will affect prejudicially.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						This IA is filed by the applicant &ndash; successful bidder with request to direct the liquidator to treat the sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern and allow the applicant to have all benefits as of approved resolution plan as per ps 31 and 32A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The limited point that arises for determination is &ldquo;Whether having accepted the corporate debtor in a slump sale, the bidder can request to treat that sale as a sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern?&rdquo;.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							There is no dispute to the fact that as per the object of the IBC, 2016, the liquidator&rsquo;s first effort should be to sale the corporate debtor as a going concern. In this case, the liquidator took all possible steps to sale the corporate debtor as a going concern but he did not get any response. The applicant herein also missed that opportunity, we do not know the reasons as to why the applicant did not accept the bid of the corporate debtor as a going concern at the first point of time.
						</li>
						<li>
							Learned senior counsel Mr. Kamal Trivedi brought to our notice our order passed in&nbsp;IA No. 238 of 2022 in CP(IB) 320 of 2018&nbsp;but the facts therein were altogether different. In that case, the liquidator had undertaken both process of slump sale as well as sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern simultaneously. Two bidders accepted the bid for the same price. One had opted for the bid of slump sale and the other accepted the bid to sale as a going concern. We directed the liquidator the accept the bid of the purchaser who was ready to purchase the corporate debtor as a going concern as it is the object of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. In this case, the sale is concluded by one process i.e., slump sale of the corporate and applicant accepted the bid.
						</li>
						<li>
							Learned counsel for the liquidator also relied on the order of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in case of
							<em>
								 M/s. Visisth Services Limited Vs. S. V. Ramani and Ors.&nbsp;(2022) 33 NCLAT
							</em>
							 . The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT was dealing with the facts &ldquo;whether the bidder can put certain conditions while accepting the bid and in case the conditions are not complied with then it can cancel the sale itself claiming the EMD amount?&rdquo;. The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT held that it cannot be done. However, such facts are not involved in this proceeding.
						</li>
						<li>
							In this case, the applicant had not put any condition when he accepted the bid to purchase the corporate debtor as a slump sale. The applicant, in this case, wanted us to issue the direction to the liquidator to convert the slump sale into the sale as a going concern. In our considered opinion this cannot be done. The price of the corporate debtor in the sale as a going concern was fixed at Rs. 5,66,23,00,000/- as maximum and Rs. 4,33,16,59,500/- as a minimum whereas the price of slump sale of the corporate debtor was fixed at Rs. 4,77,84,50,000/- as maximum and Rs. 3,14,38,49,770/- as a minimum. The applicant accepted the corporate debtor in slump sale for a minimum price of Rs. 3,14,38,49,770/-. There is a vast difference between the sale price of the corporate debtor as a going concern and the sale price of the corporate debtor in a slump sale. If we allow the applicant&rsquo;s request then certainly the rights of members of the stakeholders&rsquo; committee will affect prejudicially. We cannot permit this to happen at the instance of the successful bidder. In view of this, we hold that this application is not maintainable.
							<strong>
								 (p11-14)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
