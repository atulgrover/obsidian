	<h1>
		In terms of Section 60(5)(c) of IBC 2016, Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) has jurisdiction to adjudicate on provisional order of attachment under Section 5(1) of PMLA only to the extent of examining whether protection envisaged under Sec. 32A of IBC can be extended to the properties of Corporate Debtor forming part of approved Resolution Plan till a resolution takes place or sale of liquidation asset occurs &ndash; SREI Multiple Assets Investment Trust Vs. Enforcement Directorate and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 24, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Hyderabad Bench holds that: (i) The property which formed part of the resolution plan duly approved under the provisions of IB Code, enjoys the protection enshrined in p 32A of I&amp;B Code. This Tribunal therefore, is bound to safeguard the said &lsquo;protection&rsquo; from any kind of &lsquo;onslaught&rsquo; on the properties of the corporate debtor which formed part of the approved resolution plan. Viewed from the object behind insertion of this p, we are of the firm conclusion that the said &lsquo;protection&rsquo; needs to be &lsquo;extended&rsquo; to the &lsquo;provisional order of attachment made under the provisions of PML Act, lest the very purpose of introducing p 32A by way of an amendment by the legislature gets defeated. Therefore, for the said limited extent, this Tribunal can exercise its jurisdiction as enshrined in Section 60(5)(c) of Code 2016. (ii) NCLT while keeping ourselves aloof from entering in to any finding on the supremacy between the I&amp;B Code and the PMLA, and on other aspects relating to the maintainability of the impugned provisional order of attachment, hereby holds that, in terms of p 60(5)(c) of the Code 2016, this Adjudicating Authority has the jurisdiction to adjudicate on the impugned provisional order of attachment only to the extent of examining whether the &lsquo;protection&rsquo; envisaged under Section 32A of IB Code, can be extended to the properties of the corporate debtor forming part of the approved resolution plan till a resolution takes place or sale of liquidation asset occurs. (iii) Quashing the impugned provisional order of attachment by this Tribunal, is uncalled for and unwarranted. It is for the applicant or for that matter the erstwhile Resolution Professional, to implead in the said Writ petitions, and urge for quashing the impugned provisional order of attachment.
	</p>
</div>

						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case

						<li>
							Provisional order of Attachment dated

							Thus, order dated
							<strong>
								 03.06.2019
							</strong>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether the provisional order of attachment under Section 5(1) of the PMLA, in respect of the properties of the corporate debtor covered under the approved resolution plan, would wipe of the protection available to such properties under p 32A of the Code? If so, can the SRA be allowed to defer implementation of the resolution plan till the attachment is raised/quashed by a competent court?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The impugned provisional order of attachment of the properties/assets of the corporate debtor dated 15.10.2020 has been passed after the approval of the Resolution Plan of the applicant, and the same is in respect of some of the properties which are covered under the said approved resolution plan.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Whether NCLT has jurisdiction to rule on the validity or otherwise of the
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 provisional order of attachment passed under PMLA Act
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Hon&rsquo;ble Principal Bench, NCLAT New Delhi, in the matter between
							<em>
								 The Directorate of Enforcement v. Sh Manoj Kumar Agarwal, RP&nbsp;(2021) 182 NCLAT
							</em>
							 had an occasion to deal the issue of jurisdiction of this Tribunal on matters relating to attachment of properties of the corporate debtor which formed part of the approved resolution plan, in the wake of p 32A of I&amp;B Code, which has been introduced in IBC vide Amendment Act of 2020. Since the facts of the case on hand and in re.
							<em>
								 Directorate of Enforcement, supra,
							</em>
							 are almost identical besides, like in the case on hand reliance has been placed on the ruling in
							<em>
								 Embassy Property Developments Pvt. Ltd. Vs. State of Karnataka and Ors.
							</em>
							<em>
								 [2020] 12 SC
							</em>
							 , in the above case, we profitably rely on the above ruling.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, while arriving at the above conclusion, placed reliance on the following passage from the ruling in
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar vs. Union of India
							</em>
							<em>
								 (2021) 16 SC
							</em>
							 , wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has upheld the constitutional validity of Section 32A of the I&amp;B Code, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p28)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Though there is no controversy that, NCLT is not conferred with jurisdiction to decide &lsquo;all types of claims to property of the corporate debtor&rsquo;, and that the corporate debtor &lsquo;has to exercise rights in judicial, quasi-judicial proceedings in respect of &lsquo;such&rsquo; rights, besides that the resolution professional cannot &ldquo;short-circuit&rdquo; the same and bring a claim before NCLT taking advantage of Section 60(5)(c) of IB Code.
							<strong>
								 (p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The following categorical findings by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, in re,
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar (supra),
							</em>
							 makes more clearer than crystal, that
							<strong>
								 the property which formed part of the resolution plan duly approved under the provisions of IB Code, enjoys the protection enshrined in p 32A of I&amp;B Code. This Tribunal therefore, is bound to safeguard the said &lsquo;protection&rsquo; from any kind of &lsquo;onslaught&rsquo; on the properties of the corporate debtor which formed part of the approved resolution plan.
							</strong>
							 Viewed from the object behind insertion of this p, we are of the firm conclusion that
							<strong>
								 the said &lsquo;protection&rsquo; needs to be &lsquo;extended&rsquo; to the &lsquo;provisional order of attachment made under the provisions of PML Act
							</strong>
							 , lest the very purpose of introducing p 32A by way of an amendment by the legislature gets defeated. T
							<strong>
								 herefore, for the said limited extent, this Tribunal can exercise its jurisdiction as enshrined in Section 60(5)(c) of Code 2016.(p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							NCLT therefore, while keeping ourselves aloof from entering in to any finding on the supremacy between the I&amp;B Code and the PMLA, and on other aspects relating to the maintainability of the impugned provisional order of attachment, hereby holds that,
							<strong>
								 in terms of p 60(5)(c) of the Code 2016, this Adjudicating Authority has the jurisdiction to adjudicate on the impugned provisional order of attachment only to the extent of&nbsp;examining whether the &lsquo;protection&rsquo; envisaged under Section 32A of IB Code, can be extended to the properties of the corporate debtor forming part of the approved resolution plan till a resolution takes place or sale of liquidation asset occurs.(p30)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. Present case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Here in the case on hand the applicant since is claiming immunity under p 32A of IB Code from the provisional order of attachment made by the 1st respondent in respect of the assets formed part of the Resolution Plan, this is a fit case to invoke the residuary jurisdiction under Section 60(5)(c)] of IB Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, on careful examination of the &lsquo;impact&rsquo; of the impugned provisional order of attachment on the&nbsp;implementation of the approved resolution plan by the&nbsp;Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) and taking into&nbsp;consideration the &lsquo;clean slate&rsquo; theory propounded by Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Supreme Court, besides the very object behind introducing&nbsp;p 32A by way of an amendment in I&amp;B Code, in our&nbsp;considered view, the power of this Tribunal to interfere with the&nbsp;&ldquo;impugned measure&rdquo; is well within the teeth of p 60 (5)(c)&nbsp;of IB Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, in the light of our discussion and the case law referred above, we hereby hold that,
							<strong>
								 all the assets and properties of the corporate debtor which formed part of the approved resolution plan for the resolution of the insolvency of the corporate debtor, are immune from the provisional order of attachment made vide order No.1 of 2020, ECIR No.CIR/HYZO/02/2015 dated 15.10.2020, under p 5(1) of Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002
							</strong>
							 . Consequently, we further hold that the impugned provisional order of attachment is not an impediment for the due implementation of the Resolution Plan by the applicant herein.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Point is answered accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 C. Quashing of the provisional order of attachment of PMLA
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In so far as &lsquo;quashing&rsquo; of the impugned provisional order of attachment is concerned it may be stated that, we have already made clear that we are not entering into any discussion or arrive at a finding on the maintainability,
							<em>
								 per se
							</em>
							 , of the impugned order of attachment of the properties which formed part of the approved resolution plan, and our enquiry is confined only to find whether the provisional order of attachment under Section 5(1) of the PMLA, of the properties of the corporate debtor covered under the approved resolution plan, would wipe of the protection available to such properties under p 32A of I&amp;B Code?
							<strong>
								 (p33)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Moreover, the impugned provisional order has already been stayed by Hon&rsquo;ble High Courts of Delhi and Telangana.
							<strong>
								 (p34)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, &lsquo;quashing&rsquo; the impugned provisional order of attachment by this Tribunal, is uncalled for and unwarranted. It is for the applicant or for that matter the erstwhile Resolution Professional, to implead in the said Writ petitions, and urge for quashing the impugned provisional order of attachment.
							<strong>
								 (p34)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 D. Other issue
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Insofar as the relief of restraining the 2nd respondent from insisting for payment of balance amount payable under the approved Resolution Plan by the applicant is concerned, it is to be stated that this Resolution Plan has been approved vide order dated 03.06.2019 passed in IA No.66 of 2019 and even though the Resolution Plan was required to be implemented within a period 120 days, on some pretext or the other till date the applicant had not implemented the plan. The 2nd respondent in fact filed IA No.1205 of 2022 and IA No.559 of 2023 for directions to the applicant herein, for forthwith implementation of the resolution plan, lest to permit the 2nd respondent to forfeit the performance guarantee and other deposits made by the applicant.
							<strong>
								 (p34)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accepting the applicant&rsquo;s contention, we have already held that the properties which formed part of the approved resolution plan are immune from the impugned order of attachment and further that the provisional order of attachment is not an impediment for the applicant to proceed with the Resolution Plan.
							<strong>
								 (p35)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							After dismissal the Appeals filed by the applicant before Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, the applicant chose to file the present application instead of coming forward to implement the resolution plan.
							<strong>
								 (p36)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Prior to filing the present application, the applicant filed IA No.1171 of 2020 before this Tribunal, for identical reliefs as prayed in the present application. The said application was resisted strongly by the 3rd respondent. This Tribunal after having heard the learned counsels for both sides reserved the matter for orders and at that juncture on 21.04.2022 the applicant sought leave to withdraw the same and accordingly this Tribunal allowed withdrawal of the said application and dismasted the same as withdrawn. Thus, from the above, it is quite clear that, the applicant has been indulging in dilatory tactics in implementing the approved resolution plan.
							<strong>
								 (p36)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The resolution plan of the applicant which was first approved on 13.06.2019, therefore needs to be implemented without any further delay. Therefore, considering the facts and circumstances, we grant 30 days&rsquo; time from date of receipt of copy of this order, for implementing the approved Resolution Plan by the applicant including for payment of the entire amount due and payable under the approved Resolution Plan and in default the erstwhile RP, 2nd respondent shall forthwith proceed to forfeit the performance bank guarantee and the amount so far deposited by the applicant.
							<strong>
								 (p37)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the result, this application is partly allowed to the extent&nbsp;indicated above. Rest of the reliefs are hereby rejected. No order&nbsp;as to costs.
							<strong>
								 (p38)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
