		<h1>
			There is no provision under the IBC to terminate the CIRP once it is initiated, the only way is either to complete the CIRP by approval of the resolution plan or pass the order of liquidation under 33 of the IBC &ndash; Mr. Sudhanshu Gupta, IRP of Nesa India Producer Company Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-V
		</h1>
		<div>
			February 28, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			Adjudicating Authority holds that on conjoint reading of Section 5(14) and Section 12 of the IBC, 2016, we observed that, by amendment, the period of insolvency is extended upto 330 days. In the case in hand, the CoC is not constituted even after the expiry of more than 365 days and the IRP has also not received any claim during the CIRP. Rather, the IRP has prayed for termination of the CIRP on the ground that he has not received any claim. There is no such provision under the IBC to terminate the CIRP once it is initiated, the only way is either to complete the CIRP by approval of the resolution plan or pass the order of liquidation under 33 of the IBC.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The question is if the IRP has not received any claim even by the&nbsp;applicant, on whose application, the CIRP was initiated against the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor, in that case, after the expiry of more than one year, can CIRP be&nbsp;terminated on the request Of the IRP?
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority holds that:
						</p>
						<p>
							A bare perusal of the provision of Section 23 of the Code shows that the Resolution Professional is&nbsp;authorised to conduct the entire CIRP and&nbsp;managed the operation of the Corporate Debtor, during the CIRP period.&nbsp;Therefore, at this junction, we would also&nbsp;like to refer to the definition of Insolvency Resolution Process period, defined&nbsp;under Section 5(14) of the IBC 2016.&nbsp;Insolvency Resolution Process period&nbsp;means, the period of 180 days beginning from the Insolvency&nbsp;commencement date and ending of 180 days.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								On conjoint reading of Section&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<strong>
								5(14) and Section 12 of the IBC, 2016, we observed that, by amendment, the&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<strong>
								period of insolvency is extended upto 330 days.
							</strong>
							&nbsp;Therefore, in our&nbsp;considered view, in terms of Section 5(14) read with Section 12 of the IBC,&nbsp;Insolvency Resolution Process Period means 330 days. Admittedly, the period of 330 days has expired in the case in hand.
							<strong>
								(p8-9)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							A bare perusal of the provision of Section 33 of the IBC shows that there are two circumstances&nbsp;under which a liquidation order can be passed by the Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;(a) under Section 33(1) of the IBC and (b)&nbsp;under Section 33(2) of the IBC.&nbsp;Under p 33(1), where the Adjudicating Authority before&nbsp;the expiry of the Insolvency Resolution Process or the maximum period&nbsp;permitted for completion of the CIRP under Section 12 of the fast track CIRP&nbsp;under Section 56, as the case may be, does not receive a resolution plan under&nbsp;Section 30(6) or reject the resolution plan under Section 31 for&nbsp;non-compliance of requirement specified therein, in that case, the Adjudicating&nbsp;Authority shall pass an order for liquidation under Section 33(2)&nbsp;of the IBC, the resolution professional at any time during the CIRP&nbsp;but before the confirmation of resolution plan&nbsp;intimate the Adjudicating Authority of the decision of the COC to liquidate the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, then the Adjudicating Authority shall pass an order for&nbsp;liquidation.
							<strong>
								(p10-11)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Here in the case in hand, the CoC is not constituted even&nbsp;after the expiry of more than 365 days and the IRP has also not received any&nbsp;claim during the CIRP. Rather, the IRP has prayed for termination of the CIRP&nbsp;on the ground that he has not received any claim.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								There is no such provision&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<strong>
								under the IBC to terminate the CIRP once it is initiated, the only way is either&nbsp;to complete the CIRP by approval of the resolution plan or pass the order of&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<strong>
								liquidation under 33 of the IBC.(p12)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Here in the case in hand, there is no resolution plan as there&nbsp;was no claimant and the CoC was not constituted till the date of filing the&nbsp;application. Under such circumstances, in our considered view, in terms of Section&nbsp;33(1), the Adjudicating Authority is empowered to pass the&nbsp;liquidation order after the completion of the maximum period. Since, 330 days has already been completed and no resolution plan is received even the COC is&nbsp;not constituted, therefore, in stead of termination in the CIRP on the request of the applicant, we think it proper to pass an order under Section 33(1)&nbsp;of the IBC to pass the liquidation order.
							<strong>
								(p13)
							</strong>
						</p>
