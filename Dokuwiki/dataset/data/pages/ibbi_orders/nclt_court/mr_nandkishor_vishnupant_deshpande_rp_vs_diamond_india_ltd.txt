						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The amount of Rs. 93,11,498/- which has been given to customs by Respondent No. 1 after the commencement of CIRP from the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s account is illegal and has to be remitted back into the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s account by the Respondent No. 2, i.e., Department of Central Excise and Customs. The Bench is very clear that the payment of Rs. 93,11,498/- which has been made from the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s account maintained with Respondent No. 1 falls in the category of Operational Debt as per Section 5(21) of the Code.&nbsp;The Respondent No. 1 has mentioned that he has remitted this amount from the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s account to the Customs in consonance with the DGFT notices and the Customs Act, 1962. This Bench in this connection would refer to &ldquo;Section 238&rdquo; of IBC.
						</p>
						<p>
							This Bench also finds it proper to refer to Section 248 of the Code which states&nbsp;that the Customs Act, 1962 shall be amended in the manner specified in the Fourth Schedule.&nbsp;It is also worthwhile here to refer to corresponding Section 142(a) of the Customs Act, 1962 which reads as under:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;142A. Liability under Act to be first charge
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any Central Act or State Act, any amount of duty, penalty, interest or any other sum payable by an assessee or any other person under this Act, shall, save as otherwise provided in p 529A of the Companies Act, 1956 (1 of 1956), the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and the Financial Institutions Act, 1993 (51 of 1993) and the Securitization and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and the Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (54 of 2002) and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (31 of 2016).
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							This Bench finds that there is plethora of judicial pronouncements on this matter which clearly mentions that the Government dues cannot have precedents over the secured lenders and no recovery action can be taken against the Corporate Debtor during the CIRP period subsequent to initiation of moratorium under Section 14 of the Code. A few of the relevant judgments are as follows:
						</p>
						<p>
							a.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Pr. Commissioner of Income-tax v/s Monnet Ispat and Energy Limited&nbsp;[2018]  30 SC
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							b. Madras High Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Dishnet Wireless Limited v/s Aircel Limited&nbsp;(2018)  23 HC.
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							In view of the above, it is crystal clear to this Bench that the amount of Rs. 93,11,498/- has been illegally taken out by Respondent No. 1 and has also been illegally paid to Respondent No. 2 as customs duties and that the above amount falls under the category of Operational Debt. In view of the above, the Bench directs the following:
						</p>
						<p>
							a. The Respondent No. 2 to refund the amount of Rs. 93,11,498/- (being Rs. 90,40,290/- paid towards customs duty with applicable interest and Rs. 2,71,208/- paid towards GST authorities against the GST No. of the Corporate Debtor) with in a week&rsquo;s time to the account of the Corporate Debtor as this being statutory dues pertaining to pre-CIRP period. The Respondent No. 1, i.e., Diamond India Limited to remit back the amount, refunded by Respondent No. 2, in the account of the Corporate Debtor immediately within two days of the receipt of the money.
						</p>
						<p>
							b. The Bench grants liberty to Respondent No. 2 to file its claim for the amount of Rs. 93,11,498/- as Operational Debt with the Resolution Professional of the Corporate Debtor Company within 15 days&rsquo; time from the pronouncement date of this Order.
						</p>
						<p>
							I.A. 931/2020 in C.P.2556 of 2019 is &ldquo;Allowed&rdquo; in the above-mentioned manner.
