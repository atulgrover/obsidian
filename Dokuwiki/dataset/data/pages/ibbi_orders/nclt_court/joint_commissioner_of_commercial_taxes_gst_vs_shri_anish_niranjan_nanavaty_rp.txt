	<h1>
		NCLT allows claim of Entry Tax after approval of Resolution Plan by CoC on the basis of books of accounts and records of the Corporate Debtor &ndash; Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes &amp; GST Vs. Shri Anish Niranjan Nanavaty RP &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		November 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes &amp; GST filed its claim after approval of Resolution Plan by CoC which is pending before NCLT. NCLT Mumbai Bench refers judgment in RPS Infrastructure Ltd. (2023) 102 SC and holds that: (i) There is nothing on record to suggest that the complete books of accounts or records of the Corporate Debtor were not available. On the contrary, the Corporate Debtor was a going concern at the time of commencement of CIRP. The complete records were available in the ERP based accounting software. (ii) The existence of demand would be available in the records of the Corporate Debtor. (iii) Since the plan is still not approved by this Tribunal, we consider it appropriate to condone the delay and direct the Resolution Professional to admit the claim after verification thereof.
	</p>
</div>
<div>
	<div>
		<div>
			<div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 798 NCLTCase Name:Joint Commissioner of Commercial Taxes &amp; GST Vs. Shri Anish Niranjan Nanavaty RPCorporate Debtor:Reliance Telecom Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:I.A. 3502 of 2023 In CP (IB)1386/MB-1/2017Judgment Date:01-Nov-23Court/Bench:NCLT Mumbai BenchMember (Judicial):Justice (Retd) Mr. Virendrasingh Gyansingh BishtMember (Technical):Mr. Prabhat Kumar
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In this case, the last date for submission of claim was 21.05.2019; The Resolution Plan was approved by CoC on 04.03.2020; An application for the approval of Resolution Plan in IA 873 of 2020 was filed before this Tribunal on
						<strong>
							 06.04.2020
						</strong>
						 which is pending; and the Applicant filed its claim on 16.10.2020.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Corporate Debtor after initiation of the CIRP on 15.05.2018 has been issued with various notices&nbsp;regarding assessment, yet neither the Corporate Debtor nor the&nbsp;RP have ever intimated the petitioner about the present CIRP and finally an assessment order dated came to be passed on
						<strong>
							 27.08.2020
						</strong>
						 .
					</p>
					<p>
						It is Applicant&rsquo;s contention that it came to know about the commencement of CIRP only during the process of recovery of the demanded tax during September, 2020 and that the Respondent has not made paper publication in any of the regional dailies in the State of Odisha, although the Corporate Debtor had substantial business activities in the State. Further, even though multiple show cause letters and demand notices have been served upon the Corporate Debtor through e-mail but at no point of time either the Corporate Debtor or the RP has intimated the Applicant-Petitioner about the present CIRP proceeding. Further, the lockdown situation on account of COVID-19 prevailed in the country including State of Odisha since 23/03/2020. It is only after partial relaxation of the lockdown during August, 2020 the claim was submitted. But, the respondent-RP till date has not decided on the claim of the petitioner even though more than two months have elapsed in the meantime.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observes that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The amount of claim raised by the Applicant pertains to old outstanding demands which crystallised on 28.03.2017 after litigations reaching upto the level of Apex Court and finally re-assessment was concluded vide Order dated 27.08.2020. In so far as, demand of entry tax is concerned, we feel that this demand would have been part of contingent liability in the financial statements of the Corporate Debtor for the year ended on 31.03.2018 and prior years. It is undisputed fact that the said re-assessment took place pursuant to the remand by the first appellate authority, in which there was also a demand of tax. Hence, it can not be said that the information in relation to these tax demands could not have been available in the books of accounts and records of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 RPS Infrastructure Ltd. vs. Mukul Kumar &amp; Another
							</em>
							<em>
								 (2023) 102 SC
							</em>
							 , rejected the argument of the claimants that the Corporate debtor had not apprised it about CIRP process even during the course of Arbitration Hearing or the information about claimant&rsquo;s claim could have been ascertained from the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s books of accounts.&nbsp;The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court rejected the&nbsp;contention of the Applicant relating to ascertainment of its claim from the books of accounts after taking note of the fact that &ldquo;Respondent no. 1 did what could be done to procure the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s records by even moving an application under Section 19 of the IBC. That it was not fruitful is a consequence of the Corporate Debtor not making available the material. It is thus not even known whether there was a reflection in the records on this aspect or not&rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the present case, we find that there is nothing on record to suggest that the complete books of accounts or records of the Corporate Debtor were not available. On the contrary, the Corporate Debtor was a going concern at the time of commencement of CIRP, employing manpower in every vertical. The complete records were available in the ERP based&nbsp;accounting software. The claim was filed on 16.10.2020,&nbsp;though much after the plan was approved i.e. on 4.3.2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							However, considering the fact that the existence of demand would be available in the records of the Corporate Debtor; the demand finally came to be raised pursuant to the decision of first appellate authority directing for re-determination of tax demand and since the plan is still not approved by this Tribunal, we consider it appropriate to condone the delay and direct the Resolution Professional to admit the claim after verification thereof.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of foregoing, IA 3502 of 2023 is
							<strong>
								 allowed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p5-6)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
