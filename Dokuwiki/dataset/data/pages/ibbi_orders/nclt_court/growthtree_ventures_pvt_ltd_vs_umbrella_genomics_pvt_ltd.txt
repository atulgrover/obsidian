	<h1>
		Whether issuance of Cheques amounting of Rs 1 cr. for balance payment out of advance paid for goods, due to deficient supply by Corporate Debtor in favour of Operational Creditor automatically meets the threshold under Section 4 of IBC &ndash; Growthtree Ventures Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Umbrella Genomics Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Guwahati Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 8, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, due to deficient supply and the terms of the oral agreement, the CD issued the two Cheques in favour of the Operational Creditor which was returned unpaid by Bank. Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT holds that: (i) It is not in the realm of contention that issuance of a cheque becomes a promise to pay under Section 25(3) of the Contract Act, 1872 and in accordance with Section 118 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a presumption arises to the effect that the cheque was drawn and accepted for consideration. However, these arguments do not help the OC in meeting the threshold set under IBC in this present instance. (ii) The OC has not been able to establish by placing any evidence on record to point out that amount beyond Rs. 69,18,411 is owed by the CD to the OC in relation to the same operational debt. There is no written agreement or document delineating the settlement of an amount to the tune of Rs 1,00,00,000 or any interest arrangement vis a vis the existing debt, but the pleadings of the OC only point to an oral arrangement sans any other hard and concrete foundation for its assertions and arguments, which the CD has denied. (iii) Dismisses the petition.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Facts of the case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							To supply the best quality coal within the prescribed time period, the Petitioner/Operational Creditor(OC) has made advance payments to the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s bank account from 31.08.2022. This advance payment cumulatively added up to Rs. 3,85,00,000 from 31.08.2022 till the date of 21.09.2022.
						</li>
						<li>
							Against the amount of Rs. 3,85,00,000 advanced by the OC, coal worth only&nbsp;Rs. 3,15,81,589 was supplied. Consequently, further quantity of coal was&nbsp;due to be received from the CD and it must be noted that the CD was&nbsp;holding an amount of Rs. 69,18,411 at this instance.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Parties&nbsp;made endeavours to resolve the issues. As a result, the parties entered into&nbsp;an oral agreement whereby the Corporate Debtor agreed to pay and the&nbsp;Operation Creditor agreed to receive a lump-sum sum of Rs 1,00,00,000&nbsp;towards the amount required to be refunded, with applicable interest and&nbsp;compensation payable to the OC as a result of CD in failing to meet its&nbsp;commitments, on or before 07.02.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							In order to honor the terms of the oral agreement, the CD issued the&nbsp;two Cheques in favour of the Operational Creditor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The OC presented the said&nbsp;Cheques to the Bank for encashment. However, both the said Cheques were returned unpaid by ICICI Bank on&nbsp;10.02.2023 due to the reason &ldquo;Payment stopped by drawer&rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							Being aggrieved, Operational Creditor was constrained to issue a demand&nbsp;notice dated 25.03.2023 under Section 8 of the Code&nbsp;for an amount of Rs. 1,00,00,000&nbsp;along with payment of interest at the rate of 18% per annum and&nbsp;reimbursement of the expenses incurred by the Operational Creditor,&nbsp;through speed post and the same was duly delivered to the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor on 30.03.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							This Application has been filed under Section 9 of the IBC&nbsp;2016, by the Operational Creditor (OC) or Petitioner, namely Growthtree&nbsp;Ventures Pvt. Ltd., seeking initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Debtor (CD) or Respondent, namely&nbsp;Umbrella Genomics Private Limited.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether issuance of Cheques to the tune of Rs 1,00,00,000 by the Corporate Debtor in favour of the Operational Creditor automatically meets the threshold under the IBC.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is not in the realm of contention that issuance of a cheque becomes a promise to pay under Section 25(3) of the Contract Act, 1872 and in accordance with Section 118 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a presumption arises to the effect that the cheque was drawn and accepted for consideration. However, these arguments do not help the OC in meeting the threshold set under IBC in this present instance.
							<strong>
								 (p6.8)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The OC has not been able to establish by placing any evidence on record to point out that amount beyond Rs. 69,18,411 is owed by the CD to the OC in relation to the same operational debt. There is no written agreement or document delineating the settlement of an amount to the tune of Rs 1,00,00,000 or any interest arrangement
							<em>
								 vis a vis
							</em>
							 the existing debt, but the pleadings of the OC only point to an oral arrangement sans any other hard and concrete foundation for its assertions and arguments, which the CD has denied.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, the OC failed to establish that its operational debt meets the existing threshold of Rs 1,00,00,000 under the IBC. It would also appear that the OC has alternative remedies for the return of Cheques under law and can approach the relevant authorities for the same. Hence, the position taken by the Operational Creditor is not legally tenable and the current Petition under Section 9 of the IBC cannot be admitted.
							<strong>
								 (p6.9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, with the above observations, CP (IB)/8/GB/2023 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p7)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
