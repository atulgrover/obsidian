<section>
	<h1>
		Transaction of set off receivables from wholly owned subsidiary of stepdown subsidiaries of Corporate Debtor with payable to stepdown subsidiaries in the books of Corporate Debtor does not fall in section 43 of IBC as Preferential Transactions &ndash; Dr. Mamta Binani RP Vs. Rolta Middle East FZ LLC and Others &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench observes that in this case, the Rolta Saudi Arabia is a wholly owned subsidiary of Rolta Middle East and Rolta Middle East is a stepdown subsidiary of the Corporate Debtor (Rolta India Ltd.). The receivables from Rolta Saudi Arabia which is the wholly owned subsidiary of the Rolta Middle East has been set off against the payable to Rolta Middle East. In other words, the set off has resulted into NIL impact on the considered basis qua corporate debtor which is the ultimate holding company. Accordingly, we do not find any case of preference having been given to third party by way of such adjustment because the liability and assets of subsidiary and further stepdown subsidiaries are reflected in the value of investments held by the Corporate Debtor of the asset side.
	</p>
</section>

							<strong>
								 Brief about the decision:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									 Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Present application is being filed under Section 43 and Section 44 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 on behalf of the Resolution Professional (Applicant) of Rolta India Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) for avoidance of the preferential transactions in form of adjustment of a cumulative credit balance of Rs. 44,87,14,000/- of Rolta Middle East FZ LLC (Rolta ME/Respondent No. 1) appearing in the books of the Corporate Debtor against debit balance appearing in the books of the Corporate Debtor owed by the Corporate Debtor to Rolta Saudi Arabia Ltd. (Rolta Saudi/Respondent No. 2); during the period commencing from 19.01.2021 to 19.01.2023 (Relevant Period) in preference to the other creditors of Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Respondent No. 1 &amp; 2 has stated that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								Between 2013 and 2018, Rolta Middle East made several advances to Rolta India (in United States Dollars and Euros), which amounted to Rs. 16,925 lakhs (after applying applicable conversion rates). These advances were made against provision of IT services by Rolta India to Rolta Saudi Arabia during 2018-20. Therefore, while services were rendered to Rolta Saudi Arabia by Rolta India, advance for such services had already been paid by Rolta Middle East.
							</li>
							<li>
								Accordingly, Rolta India had a debit balance of Rs. 16,925 lakhs in its books as on March 30, 2020 which continued to remain as on March 30, 2022, since the amounts were converted in 2020 and there were no further outstanding amounts in this regard. On account of services being provided to Rolta Saudi Arabia by Rolta India, Rolta India had a credit balance of Rs. 4,550.74 lakhs (amount converted to Indian Rupees) as on March 31, 2020. However, since the adjustment was made in March 2022, due to fluctuations in the rate of United States Dollar, this amount had become Rs. 4,487.15 lakhs as on March 31, 2022. To that extent, Rolta India continues to maintain a debit balance with Rolta Middle East.&nbsp;
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								One of the ingredient for bringing a transaction within section 43 is &ldquo;
								<em>
									 a transfer of property or an interest thereof of the corporate debtor for the benefit of a creditor
								</em>
								 &rdquo;.
							</li>
							<li>
								In this case, the Rolta
								<strong>
									 Saudi Arabia
								</strong>
								 is a wholly owned subsidiary of
								<strong>
									 Rolta Middle East
								</strong>
								 and Rolta Middle East is a stepdown subsidiary of the Corporate Debtor (
								<strong>
									 Rolta India Ltd.
								</strong>
								 ). The investment in the subsidiaries by the Corporate Debtor are reflected in its balance sheets and a part of resolution process of the Corporate Debtor.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the present case, the receivables from Rolta Saudi Arabia which is the wholly owned subsidiary of the Rolta Middle East has been set off against the payable to Rolta Middle East. In other words, the set off has resulted into NIL impact on the considered basis qua corporate debtor which is the ultimate holding company. Accordingly, we do not find any case of preference having been given to third party by way of such adjustment because the liability and assets of subsidiary and further stepdown subsidiaries are reflected in the value of investments held by the Corporate Debtor of the asset side.
							</li>
							<li>
								Nonetheless, it has been stated by the Respondent No. 1 and 2 that the Rolta Middle East has made certain advances to the Corporate Debtor for the services provided by the Corporate Debtor to Rolta Saudi Arabia. In other words, these accounts were required to be settled, which in any case stand settled qua the Corporate Debtor at consolidation level.
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of above, we are of the considered view that no preference can be said to have been given in terms of Section 43 of the Code in this case.
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of the foregoing, IA 3249/2023 is
								<strong>
									 dismissed
								</strong>
								 and disposed of accordingly.
								<strong>
									 (p6.2-6.3&amp;7)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
