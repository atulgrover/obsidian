						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The main issue that&nbsp;falls for consideration in the above Company Petition is whether the amounts claimed by the petitioner falls within the definition&nbsp;of &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo;? and whether the applicant can be termed as an&nbsp;Operational Creditor.
						</p>
						<p>
							It is very clear from the Section 5(20) and 5(21) an&nbsp;Operational Debt would be related only to four categories as specified in&nbsp;Section 5(21) of the Code like goods, services, employment and&nbsp;Government dues. Admittedly, the claim of Operational Creditor relates&nbsp;to non-payment of Electricity Bills and Property Taxes payable by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor to the Operational Creditor under business conducting&nbsp;agreement and does not relate to any goods or services provided by the&nbsp;Operational Creditor and therefore does not fall under the definition&nbsp;&ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; and obviously the petitioner cannot be termed as&nbsp;&ldquo;Operational Creditor&rdquo;.
						</p>
						<p>
							Had the above company petition been filed by Municipal&nbsp;Corporation which is a local authority claiming the taxes, the petition&nbsp;would be certainly maintainable before this tribunal.&nbsp;The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT vide its order dated 17.01.2020 in the matter&nbsp;of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Mr. M. Ravindranath Reddy Vs. Mr G. Kishan &amp; Ors&nbsp;[2020]  95 NCLAT&nbsp;
							</em>
							held that enhanced rent of lease hold property&nbsp;does not fall within the definition of Operational Debt in terms of Section&nbsp;5(21) of the Code.
						</p>
						<p>
							When the enhanced rent payable under a tenancy agreement by&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor itself does not amounts to an Operational Debt, the&nbsp;municipal taxes and the Electricity Bills payable by Corporate Debtor&nbsp;under a business conducting agreement/lease agreement stands on&nbsp;lesser footing and cannot be considered as Operational Debt. Therefore,&nbsp;we have no hesitation in holding that the above amounts claimed by the&nbsp;Operational Creditor does not fall within the definition of Operational&nbsp;Debt and the petitioner cannot be termed as &ldquo;Operational Creditor&rdquo;.&nbsp;The above company petition is filed on 12.03.2018. If we examine&nbsp;the above claims, all the claims prior to 12.03.2015 are barred by&nbsp;limitation, since we are dismissing the above Company Petition on the very&nbsp;nature of the claim and the locus of the applicant, we are not dealing with&nbsp;each and every contention raised by the respondents. In view of the above observations, we are of the considered opinion&nbsp;that the above Company Petition is not maintainable before this Tribunal&nbsp;and is liable to be dismissed. Accordingly, the above Company Petition is&nbsp;dismissed without costs and pending IA&rsquo;s if any also stands disposed off.&nbsp;However, this order does not preclude the petitioners from&nbsp;instituting necessary recovery proceedings against the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;for recovery of their dues in respect of the claims that are within limitation.
						</p>
