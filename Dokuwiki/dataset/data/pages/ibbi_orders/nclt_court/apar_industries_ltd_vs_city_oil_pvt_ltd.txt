	<h1>
		Mere adducing interest clause in the invoices by Operational Creditor is not enough to claim the interest amount along with the principal amount payable by Corporate Debtor &ndash; Apar Industries Ltd. Vs. City Oil Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 17, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench has held that mere adducing interest clause in the invoices by the Operational Creditor is not enough to claim the interest amount along with the principal amount payable by the Corporate Debtor. To fortify our view, rely upon the decision passed by the Learned NCLT, New Delhi Bench in the case of Rohit &amp; Company v. Twenty First Century Wire Rods Ltd. order dated 14.09.2023, reported in (2023) 627 NCLT. Further refer to the decision passed by this Adjudicating Authority to strengthen the view in the case of Mr. Sanjay Sharma, Partner of M/s. Ma Jagadamba Enterprises v. M/s. Super Iron Foundry Private Limited in Company Petition (IB) No. 314/KB/2022.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This Application has been preferred under Section 9 of the IBC by Apar Industries Ltd. (Applicant/Operational Creditor) against City Oil Pvt. Ltd. (Respondent/Corporate Debtor&rdquo;) seeking a direction to initiate CIRP in respect to Corporate Debtor herein.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observes that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is evident that the Operational Creditor has issued and delivered the invoices from 29th May 2019 to 31st July 2019 along with the transport receipt of the lorry. In the invoices referred to by the Operational Creditor herein, 18% GST has been levied along with a clause that interest will be recovered at 24% on bill not paid on due date.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Mere adducing interest clause in the invoices by the Operational Creditor is not enough to claim the interest amount along with the principal amount payable by the Corporate Debtor. To fortify our view, rely upon the decision passed by the Learned NCLT, New Delhi Bench in the case of
							<em>
								 Rohit &amp; Company v. Twenty First Century Wire Rods Ltd.
							</em>
							 order dated 14.09.2023, reported in&nbsp;(2023) 627 NCLT.&nbsp;Further refer to the decision passed by this Adjudicating&nbsp;Authority to strengthen the view in the case of Mr.
							<em>
								 Sanjay Sharma, Partner of M/s. Ma Jagadamba Enterprises v. M/s. Super Iron Foundry Private Limited in Company Petition (IB) No. 314/KB/2022 decided on November 30, 2023.
							</em>
							<strong>
								 (p18-19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Thus, if we deduct the 24% interest part from the total amount&nbsp;claimed to be in default (i.e., of Rs. 1,54,92,104/-), then the&nbsp;principal amount claimed to be in default becomes of Rs.&nbsp;1,00,15,995/-.
							<strong>
								 (p20)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, no record&nbsp;has been annexed to the application or in the rejoinder filed by the&nbsp;Operational Creditor, substantiating payment of GST to the&nbsp;Government. Hence, if we deduct the GST amount mentioned in&nbsp;the invoices at the rate of 18% from the total amount, then the net&nbsp;amount due after deducing GST, would become of Rs. 82,13,115/- which is less than the threshold financial limit as prescribed under&nbsp;Section 4 of the I&amp;B Code, 2016 to file an application under&nbsp;Section 9 of the Code.
							<strong>
								 (p21)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, The Applicant has claimed the Date of Default on&nbsp;13.07.2019 and this Application has been filed on 06.04.2022. It&nbsp;is evident that the Operational Creditor had supplied materials&nbsp;worth a sum of Rs. 1,11,75,564/- only between the period from&nbsp;29.05.2019 to 31.07.2019 and out of the aforesaid amount, the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor has made part payment of Rs. 11,46,634/- on&nbsp;various occasions and the last payment was made on 30.11.2021.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							For the purpose of determining, whether the claims are time barred or not, we find that there are serious challenges regarding the mode of payment, i.e., whether the payment is made &lsquo;invoice wise&rsquo; basis or on &lsquo;ad-hoc&rsquo; basis. Further, this is fundamental to decide whether Section 59 or Section 60 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 applies and accordingly the time-bar aspect.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the absence of specific averments to this effect, we are not able to determine whether Section 59 or Section 60 of the Contract Act, 1872 would applicable, as this is relevant to decide the point of limitation particularly when the invoices are of 2019.
							<strong>
								 (p23)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, in terms of forgoing discussions, we don&rsquo;t have any hesitation to
							<strong>
								 dismiss
							</strong>
							 the application filed by the Operational Creditor. No Costs.
							<strong>
								 (p24)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
