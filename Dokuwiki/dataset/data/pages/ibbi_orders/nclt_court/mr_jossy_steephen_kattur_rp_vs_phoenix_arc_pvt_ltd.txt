	<h1>
		Whether claims for &lsquo;loss of rent,&rsquo; &lsquo;arbitration costs,&rsquo; and &lsquo;damages&rsquo; by Home allottees would be categorized as Financial Debt under IBC &ndash; Mr Jossy Steephen Kattur RP of Kerala Chamber of Commerce and Industries Vs. Phoenix ARC Pvt. Ltd. and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 12, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kochi Bench held that: (i) Whether the liability originates from an arbitral award or a court decree, it would be classified as either financial or operational debt, contingent upon the intrinsic nature of the underlying claim as determined by the arbitral or court proceedings. (ii) The categorization of creditors under the Code depends on understanding the fundamental nature of the transaction. (iii) Considering the underlying nature of claims of 'loss of rent,' 'cost of arbitration,' and 'damages&rsquo;, we find it right to reclassify these claims from 'other creditors' to "Financial Creditors."
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Home allottees are recognized as financial creditors
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In essence, to qualify as a &ldquo;debt,&rdquo; there must exist a legal obligation concerning a &ldquo;claim&rdquo; that is currently due. In this context, a &ldquo;claim&rdquo; denotes the entitlement to payment resulting from a breach of contract. Subsequently, there&rsquo;s the concept of &ldquo;default,&rdquo; which denotes the failure to repay a debt either in full or in part when it falls due. Section 5(8)(f) of the Code elucidates that when funds are obtained from an allottee in a real estate project, having the commercial effect of borrowing, and disbursed against the consideration for the time value of money, it constitutes a financial debt. Therefore, the right of home allottees to seek repayment of the amount of money invested by them towards the apartment or flat sought to be developed by the developer amounts to a claim against the developer. The repayment of such an amount of money is the liability of the developer towards the home allottees.
							<strong>
								 (p4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is evident from judicial precedents that the Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited and Anr. v. Union of India and Ors.&nbsp;(2019) 13 SC
							</em>
							 affirmed that amounts raised from homebuyers through real estate agreements, primarily for profit, fall within the scope of &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo; as defined in Section 5(8)(f) of the Code. Consequently, such home allottees are recognized as financial creditors.
							<strong>
								 (p5-6)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. Whether claims for &lsquo;loss of rent,&rsquo; &lsquo;arbitration costs,&rsquo; and &lsquo;damages&rsquo; by home allottees would also be categorized as financial debt
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority refers the following judgments:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Anuj Jain IRP for Jaypee Infratech Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd.&nbsp;(2020) 06 SC
						</li>
						<li>
							Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company Ltd. Vs. Navrang Roadlines Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 331 HC
						</li>
						<li>
							Mukul Agarwal Vs Royale Resinex Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 257 NCLAT
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is clear from the judicial precedents that the characterization of the payment under a decree or arbitral award resulting from the adjudication of a creditor&rsquo;s claim hinges on the underlying nature of that claim.
							<strong>
								 Whether the liability originates from an arbitral award or a court decree, it would be classified as either financial or operational debt, contingent upon the intrinsic nature of the underlying claim as determined by the arbitral or court proceedings.(p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Though it is a mandatory requirement that, for a debt to be classified as financial debt, it must be disbursed with consideration for the time value of money and have a commercial effect of borrowing. It has to satisfy the root requirements requirements of financial debt, such as &lsquo;disbursement&rsquo; and &lsquo;consideration for the time value of money.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It&rsquo;s important to note that, in the context of home allottees, disbursement refers to the payment made by home allottees to the Real Estate Developer for the specific purpose of financing the real estate project, in exchange for which the home allottees are allocated their respective units. Also, it is worth noting that in the present scenario, the former Resolution Professional appropriately admitted&nbsp;the claims made towards the consideration amount paid by the&nbsp;home allottees to the Corporate Debtor, including interest, as&nbsp;&lsquo;financial debts.&rsquo; Thus, it is of no doubt that the underlying nature of&nbsp;the claim undisputedly satisfies the essential components of &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo; as outlined in p 5(8) of the code.
							<strong>
								 The categorization of creditors under the Code depends on understanding the fundamental nature of the transaction.(p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Consequently, considering the underlying nature of claims of &lsquo;loss of rent,&rsquo; &lsquo;cost of arbitration,&rsquo; and &lsquo;damages&rsquo;, we find it right to reclassify these claims from &lsquo;other creditors&rsquo; to &ldquo;Financial Creditors.&rdquo;(p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Taking into account that the arbitral award concerning M/s Schindler India Private Limited stemmed from the non-payment related to the supply and installation of machinery, thus indicating the transaction&rsquo;s underlying nature as the supply of services and non-payment, it is deemed appropriate to reclassify M/s Schindler India Private Limited&rsquo;s claims from &lsquo;other creditors&rsquo; to operational creditors.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Resolution Professional is hereby directed to reconstitute the CoC.
