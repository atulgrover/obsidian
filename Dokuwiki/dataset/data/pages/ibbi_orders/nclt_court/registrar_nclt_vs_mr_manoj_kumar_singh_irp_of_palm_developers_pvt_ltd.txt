		<h1>
			Whether Adjudicating Authority(NCLT) has the power/jurisdiction under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 to initiate Suo Motu Contempt arising out of the IBC proceeding &ndash; Registrar NCLT Vs. Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh, IRP of Palm Developers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-II
		</h1>
		<div>
			January 24, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			It was submitted by the Respondent that there is no provision of initiating Contempt under IBC proceedings since Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 does not apply to the IBC proceedings. AA holds that in the light of the judgments Mr. Manoj K. Daga Vs. ISGEC Heavy Engineering Limited and Others [2020]  266 NCLAT and Shailendra Singh Vs. Nisha Malpani and Anr. (2021)  528 NCLAT passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, it is amply clear that under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013, this Adjudicating Authority has Jurisdiction to initiate the Contempt Proceedings in the event of any violation made pursuant to the directions passed by this Adjudicating Authority under the IBC proceedings. The Respondent also has stated that the NCLT does not have any jurisdiction to take suo-motu cognizance for the Contempt of its own order. The AA holds that this Adjudicating Authority is having jurisdiction under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 to initiate Suo Motu Contempt arising out of the IBC proceeding. Hence, we are inclined to proceed ahead with merits of the present case.
		</p>
	</div>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The present proceeding is arising out of the Suo-Motu cognizance taken by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 13.07.2021 passed in the matter of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India vs. Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh, IRP, wherein a show cause notice was issued to Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh, the erstwhile IRP of M/s. Palm Developers Pvt. Ltd (Respondent
							<strong>
								)
							</strong>
							&nbsp;that as to why the Contempt proceedings shall not be initiated against him for violation of the directions given vide order dated 07.09.2020 passed by this Adjudicating Authority.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 27.01.2019 had initiated the CIRP against the Corporate Debtor and appointed Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh, IP as the IRP of the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;That during the pendency of CIRP of the Corporate Debtor, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) preferred an Application bearing no. IA-1742 of 2021 with a prayer to replace Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh as the IRP with another Insolvency Professional from the list of Insolvency&nbsp;Professionals. That the said IA was allowed by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 13.07.2021.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Respondent had filed its Reply dated 04.08.2021 to the Show Cause Notice for initiating Contempt issued by this Adjudicating Authority. The Respondent, through its reply and the submissions made during the course of hearing, has raised the issue of Jurisdiction of this Adjudicating Authority to initiate the Contempt proceedings against the Respondent Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh, IRP.&nbsp;He&nbsp;has made two-fold argument to raise the issue of Jurisdiction. On the one hand, the Respondent has stated that Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 read with Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016 cannot be invoked to initiate Contempt proceedings under the IBC, 2016 proceedings. On the other hand, it has stated that the NCLT does not have any jurisdiction to take suo-motu cognizance for the Contempt of its own order.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								A. Adjudicating Authority has jurisdiction to initiate the Contempt Proceedings in the event of any violation made pursuant to the directions passed by it under the IBC proceedings
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Respondent has placed reliance on the judgment of the Principal Bench, NCLT passed in the matter of&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Mr. K.K. Agarwal &amp; Anr. Vs. M/s. Soni Infratech Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Ors in CA. No. 2376(PB)2019 in (IB)-&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<strong>
								448(PB)/2018 dated 13.01.2020.&nbsp;
							</strong>
							In the light of the aforesaid Judgment, it was submitted by the Respondent that there is no provision of initiating Contempt under IBC proceedings since Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 does not apply to the IBC proceedings.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority holds that&nbsp;this Bench is conscious of other judgments of subsequent dates which, in our view, need to be looked into for deciding the issue of Jurisdiction. Here, it is worthwhile to refer to the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT passed in the matter of&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Mr. Manoj K. Daga Vs. ISGEC Heavy Engineering Limited and Others [2020]  266 NCLAT
							</strong>
							&nbsp;dated 12.03.2020, wherein the Suo Motu Contempt Proceeding was initiated by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT.&nbsp;Further, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in its recent Judgment passed in the matter of&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Shailendra Singh Vs. Nisha Malpani and Anr. (2021)  528 NCLAT&nbsp;
							</strong>
							dated 22.11.2021.&nbsp;In the light of the Judgments (Supra) passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, it is amply clear that under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013, this Adjudicating Authority has Jurisdiction to initiate the Contempt Proceedings in the event of any violation made pursuant to the directions passed by this Adjudicating Authority under the IBC proceedings.
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								B. Whether Adjudicating Authority has the powers to initiate Suo Motu Contempt
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Adjudicating Authority holds that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								as regards to the Suo Motu power of this Adjudicating Authority to initiate Contempt proceedings, we observe that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT has already set a precedent by initiating Suo Motu Contempt Case in the matter of&nbsp;
								<strong>
									Mr. Manoj K. Daga [2020]  266 NCLAT.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;However, we would further like to examine as to whether this Adjudicating Authority has the powers to initiate Suo Motu Contempt.
							</li>
							<li>
								From the perusal of the contents of Section 425 Companies Act, 2013, it is evidently clear that this Adjudicating Authority is having the same jurisdiction, powers and authority in respect of contempt of itself as the Hon&rsquo;ble High Courts has and may exercise, for this purpose, the powers under the provisions of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.
							</li>
							<li>
								That in this context, it is also worthwhile to examine the powers to initiate Contempt by other Tribunals. Here, we refer to Section 17 of The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, which contains the powers of Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) to initiate contempt. From the perusal of Section 17 of The Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, it is observed that the CAT is having the same jurisdiction, powers and authority as to what are vested with NCLT under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 for initiating Contempt proceedings.
							</li>
							<li>
								That here, we would like to refer to the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of&nbsp;
								<strong>
									T. Sudhakar Prasad vs Govt. Of A.P. &amp; Ors in Appeal (Civil) 5089 1998 dated 13.12.2000,
								</strong>
								&nbsp;wherein the following was held about the Suo Moto powers of the CAT to initiate Contempt. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the Judgment&nbsp;
								<strong>
									T. Sudhakar Prasad vs Govt. Of A.P. &amp; Ors in Appeal (Civil) 5089 1998 dated 13.12.2000
								</strong>
								&nbsp;has clearly held that the CAT is having the same Jurisdiction as vested in the&nbsp;High Courts and has power to initiate contempt of its own. Since the Legislature has given the similar powers to CAT under Section 17 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 and NCLT under Section 425 of the Companies Act, 2013 with regard to initiating contempt of its own, in our view, the law laid down by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
								<strong>
									T. Sudhakar Prasad case (Supra)
								</strong>
								&nbsp;shall apply to the NCLT as well.
							</li>
							<li>
								<strong>
									In sequel to the aforesaid discussion, we are of the firm view that this Adjudicating Authority is having jurisdiction under Section 425 of Companies Act, 2013 to initiate Suo Motu Contempt arising out of the IBC proceeding. Hence, we are inclined to proceed ahead with merits of the present case.
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Decision on the present case
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								As regards to the merits of the matter, this Bench observes that the Respondent in its Reply &amp; written submissions and the Ld. Counsel appearing for the Respondent has reiterated the same grounds and reasons for not convening the meeting of CoC for carrying forward the process of CIRP which were already raised and recorded in our Order dated 13.07.2021.&nbsp;Further, the Respondent has submitted the same explanations for not conducting CoC, which have already recorded in Para 7 of the Order dated 13.07.2021 of this Adjudicating Authority.
							</li>
							<li>
								Hence, we are of the view that the Respondent has not acted in accordance with the provisions of IBC, 2016 and has derailed the CIR process besides wilfully violating the directions passed by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 07.09.2020.
							</li>
							<li>
								That the procedure of initiating the Contempt proceedings is stipulated under Section 14 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971. That this Adjudicating Authority has informed the Respondent vide order dated 13.07.2021 that it is charged with the violation of the directions passed vide order dated 07.09.2020 which fulfils the ingredients of Section 14(1)(a) of Contempt of Court Acts, 1971. That this Adjudicating Authority has given adequate opportunity to the Respondent to file its reply, written submission and addressed arguments orally, which fulfils the requirement of Section 14(1)(b) (Supra) of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.&nbsp;That the Respondent at any stage either orally or in writing has not made any request for transfer of matter to other Bench in accordance with Section 14(2) of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.
							</li>
							<li>
								That the Respondent has made continuous contempt of the directions passed vide order dated 07.09.2020 from 08.09.2020 till 12.07.2021 by not conducting the meeting of CoC and not carrying forward the CIR process of the Corporate Debtor. Therefore, the contempt proceedings has been initiated within one year, which is in terms of Section 20 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.
							</li>
							<li>
								That the Respondent has not made any apology at any stage, therefore, the question of discharging the Respondent on this ground in terms of proviso to Section 12 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 does not arise.
							</li>
							<li>
								Accordingly, in view of the facts and circumstances of the case as above, we hold that the Respondent Mr. Manoj Kumar Singh IP has committed the contempt of the directions passed by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 07.09.2020. The Respondent is directed to appear through VC for hearing on punishment on 19.01.2022.
							</li>
						</ul>
