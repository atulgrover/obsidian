	<h1>
		In Telecom Insolvency, unspent balance of Prepaid Subscribers and Refundable Security Deposit of Postpaid Customers shall be treated as Operational Debt (other than Government dues) under IBC &ndash; Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Vs. Reliance Telecom Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 18, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, the Corporate Debtor (in Mobile Telephony Services industry) had received security deposits from its Post-paid Subscribers and Telephony Charges on Prepaid basis in advance from its Prepaid Subscribers, and there were certain unspent balances under such prepaid plans and refund of security deposits, which were due for refund prior to commencement of CIRP. NCLT Mumbai held that: (i) The liabilities on account of Refundable Security Deposit, and unspent balance of Prepaid Subscribers, must be duly reflecting in the books of account of the Corporate Debtor, and the only handicap pleaded by the RP is unavailability of the subscriber wise details thereof, these liabilities can not be said to have extinguish till the Resolution Plan is approved. (ii) about of security deposit balances refundable to post paid subscribers and amount of un-spent balances in prepaid plans are the money collected in excess of the rates prescribed by TRAI. (iii) Amount of Security Deposit, and unspent balance of Prepaid Subscribers shall be admitted as Operational Debt. (iv) The said amount is a nature of Operational Debt other than Government dues, as this dues are a nature of fine for non-maintenance of quality standards only.
	</p>

	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Facts of the case
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			The CIRP of Reliance Telecom Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) commenced on 15.05.2018, and was stayed for some time after its resumption on 7.5.2019 upon vacation of the stay.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Corporate Debtor is engaged in provision of Mobile Telephony Services in terms of the license granted by Department of Telecommunications (DOT), Government of India.
		</li>
		<li>
			The Resolution Plan in the case of the Corporate Debtor was approved on 04.03.2020, by the Committee of Creditors (CoC), and is pending approval in terms of p 30(2) of the Code before Adjudicating Authority.
		</li>
		<li>
			Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI), a statutorily constituted body established under Section 3 (1) under the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997 (TRAI Act) (Applicant/TRAI), in exercise of the powers conferred upon it under p 36 read with p 11(1) (b) (i) and (v) of the TRAI Act, has made the Standards of Quality of Service for Basic Telephone Service and Cellular Mobile Telephone Service Regulations, 2009 (QoS
			<br />
			 Regulations).
		</li>
		<li>
			TRAI passed the six Orders under QoS Regulations during the period from 26.12.2016 to 31.07.2018 directing the Corporate Debtor to deposit sums as financial disincentives under these Orders, which aggregates to Rs. 81,50,000/-, and the same was unpaid on the Insolvency Commencement Date. These Orders, except order dated 31.7.2018, were passed prior to Insolvency Commencement Date, and the Order dated 31.7.2018 levying financial disincentives amounting to Rs. 2,00,000/- was passed during the stay period when the CIRP was stayed.
		</li>
		<li>
			This IA 88/2020 is filed on 20.12.2019 by TRAI under Section 60(5) of the IBC seeking an order directing the Resolution Professional to ascertain the unspent balance and security deposit payable to the subscribers and make provisions for the same in the resolution plan and pass an order directing the Resolution Professional to allow for payment of statutory dues amounting to Rs. 85,10,000/- to the TRAI.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			The Corporate Debtor had received security deposits from its Post-paid Subscribers and Telephony Charges on Prepaid basis in advance from its Prepaid Subscribers, and there were certain unspent balances under such prepaid plans and refund of security deposits, which were due for refund prior to commencement of CIRP.
			<strong>
				 (p6.2)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The main contention of the Resolution Professional is that the Applicant has not filed the claim, however, it is not disputed that these liabilities in the form Security Deposit from Subscribers and Unspent Balance of Prepaid Customers are duly accounted for in the books of accounts in accordance with the accrual system and recognition of liabilities principles.
			<strong>
				 (p6.3)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The Bench refers judgment in
			<em>
				 Puneet Kaur Vs. K V Developers Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 416 NCLAT
			</em>
			 and holds that the liabilities on account of Refundable Security Deposit, and unspent balance of Prepaid Subscribers, must be duly reflecting in the books of account of the Corporate Debtor, and the only handicap pleaded by the Resolution Professional is unavailability of the subscriber wise details thereof, these liabilities can not be said to have extinguish till the Resolution Plan is approved by this Bench in terms of Section 30(2) of the Code. It is also undisputed fact that these amounts became payable/refundable on account of sudden failure or shutdown of the Network by the Corporate Debtor prior to CIRP commencement
			<strong>
				 .(p6.3-6.4)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The Bench holds that about of security deposit balances&nbsp;refundable to post paid subscribers and amount of un-spent balances in&nbsp;prepaid plans are the money collected in excess of the rates prescribed&nbsp;by TRAI. As this amount remains unpaid as on date of commencement&nbsp;of CIRP, this amount which is outstanding of the books of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor as liability in aggregate is liable to be paid into&nbsp;Telecommunication Consumers Education and Protection Fund in&nbsp;accordance with Regulation 3 of Telecommunication Consumers&nbsp;Education and Protection Fund Regulations, 2007. Accordingly,&nbsp;amount of Security Deposit, and unspent balance of Prepaid Subscribers&nbsp;shall be admitted as Operational Debt.
			<strong>
				 (p6.6)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The Bench refers
			<em>
				 State Tax Officer Vs. Rainbow Papers Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 107 SC
			</em>
			 and holds that as regards demand on account of financial disincentive levied by TRAI we of the considered view that the said amount is a nature of Operational Debt other than Government dues, as this dues are a nature of fine for non-maintenance of quality standards only.
			<strong>
				 (p6.7)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In view of the foregoing the IA is
			<strong>
				 allowed
			</strong>
			 in terms of aforesaid directions.
			<strong>
				 (p7)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
