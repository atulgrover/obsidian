		<h1>
			Copy of Valuation Report must be supplied to ex- management/Suspended Directors &ndash; Hemant Shantilal Shah s Anr. Vs. Care Office Ltd. through RP Vikash Jain &amp; Ors. &ndash; NCLT Ahmedabad Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			May 2, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority held that resolution plan is a highly confidential document but when such document is required to given to the most affected parties being erstwhile members of the Board of Directors of the debtor, then the valuation report in our view which is an important document having bearing onto the fate of corporate debtor must be supplied to ex- management. The ex-management is the appropriate concerned parties to comment on valuation of properties of corporate debtor. Resolution Professional shall provide a copy of the valuation report to the suspended management of the corporate debtor subject to an undertaking from members of the suspended management, to maintain confidentiality.
		</p>
	</div>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							It is argued by Resolution Professional that as per wording of p 24(3)(b) read with CIRP Regulation 21(3)(iii), the documents were to be supplied to members of COC. Since the suspended management is not member of the COC, but merely participant in meetings of COC, without any voting rights. This being the reasons, behind suspended management not entitled to copy of valuation report.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority referred the of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Vijay Kumar Jain Vs. Standard Chartered Bank &amp; Ors.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;
								<strong>
									[2019]  24 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							&nbsp;wherein the copy of the resolution plan was directed to be given to ex-directors and held&nbsp;
							<strong>
								that&nbsp;Resolution plan is a highly confidential document but when such document is required to given to the most affected parties being erstwhile members of the Board of Directors of the debtor, then the valuation report in our view which is an important document having bearing onto the fate of corporate debtor must be supplied to ex- management.&nbsp;
							</strong>
							It further held that:
						</p>
						<p>
							In present case it is the valuation report which is in question. Whether it can be shared by Resolution Professional with ex-directors specially when the ex-management alleges and apprehends that properties of corporate debtor are being valued and offered at throw away price.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								In our view, the ex-management is the appropriate concerned parties to comment on valuation of properties of corporate debtor.
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The corporate debtor is the body of Ex-management and they were in control and handling the same till CIRP initiation. It would be pre-judicial if they are not even allowed to view the valuation and point out the shortfalls in valuation, if any.
						</p>
						<p>
							The intent of code being maximization of value while insolvency resolution process, all concerned should given access to the document which will be crucial for deciding worth of corporate debtor which is intended to be given new lease of life.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								There is no specific provision to not to share copy of valuation report with ex-directors, we hold that in interest of justice the copy of valuation report needs to be supplied to ex- directors, as already directed by the Predecessor Bench.
							</strong>
							&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Accordingly, we direct Resolution Professional to supply copy of valuation report to the applicants within 2 days of this order.
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority is of the opinion that Resolution Professional shall provide a copy of the valuation report to the suspended management of the corporate debtor&nbsp;
							<strong>
								subject to an undertaking from members of the suspended management, to maintain confidentiality.
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The source of this power is Regulation 7(2)(h) of the Insolvency Professionals Regulations, 2016, read with paragraph 21 of the First Schedule thereto. This can be in the form of a non-disclosure agreement in which the resolution professional can be indemnified in case information is not kept strictly confidential.
							<strong>
								(p9-10)
							</strong>
						</p>
