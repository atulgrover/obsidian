	<h1>
		IBC prevails over State Financial Corporation Act, 1951 &ndash; Mr. Rajesh Lahila Vs. West Bengal Industrial Development Corporation Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 16, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench holds that: (i) Section 29(5) of the State Financial Corporation Act contains provisions which makes the Financial Corporation Corporation to be deemed owner of such concern, only for the purposes of initiating suits by or against the concern, and the State Financial Corporation shall sue and be sued in the name of the concern. This in our view is only for the purpose of initiating or defending any suit with reference to the property which has been pledged with the Financial Corporation, and do not make the State Financial Corporation the owner of such properties. (ii) In this case, the inconsistency arises between Section 18 of the Code and Section 29 of State Financial Corporation Act. While Section 18(f) of the Code, the Code enunciates that any asset recorded the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor as its assets, and it is the duty of the resolution professional to take custody of such assets of the Corporate Debtor. The explanation to the said Section 18 of Code also provides that assets owned by Corporate Debtor but not in possession of Corporate Debtor will also need to be taken custody whereas Section 29 of State Financial Corporation Act provides that if the borrower fails to owner honour the terms and conditions of the borrowings then the Corporation which has got first charge over the immovable properties of the Corporate Debtor would be entitled to retain and dispose properties pledged. (iii) Thus, this inconsistency needs to be resolved and the same is resolved by applying Section 238 of Code, which provides that IBC would prevail over any other statute in the case of such inconsistency.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor had availed loan of Rs. 20 Crore from the respondent in terms of loan agreement made between the parties on 21.08.2009
						</li>
						<li>
							The Charge was created based on the agreement in terms of clause 4 on first equitable mortgage on all immovable properties both present and future.
						</li>
						<li>
							First charge on movable properties were also created for securing the borrowings made by the Corporate Debtor from the respondent for working capital requirements.
						</li>
						<li>
							the Corporate Debtor defaulted amount payable to the respondent under the said loan agreement. As soon as the default occurred the respondent herein took possession of the factory situated at Paschim Medinipur, Kharagpur, West Bengal.
						</li>
						<li>
							On 17.02.2022, the Applicant was appointed as the Resolution Professional (RP) to manage the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor and to take custody of all the assets of the Corporate Debtor and accordingly took steps.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the said process, the RP became aware about the property of the Corporate Debtor being in physical possession of the respondent herein.
						</li>
						<li>
							The applicant met with officials of WBIDC several times and also sent emails apart from sending a formal letter on 24.01.2022 to hand over the possession of the property. As all his attempts met with no success this application has been filed.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As per Section 18(f) of Code it is the duty of the RP to take control and custody of any assets over which the Corporate Debtor has ownership rights as recorded in the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor, or with information utility or the depository of securities or any other registry that records the ownership of the assets of the Corporate Debtor including assets that may not be in possession of the Corporate Debtor at the date of commencement of CIRP.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is not in dispute that the said property which is in possession of&nbsp;WBIDC has been recorded in the balance sheet of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor as its assets though it has been pledged with the&nbsp;respondent for the purpose of securing the borrowings made by&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p20)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Reference made to Section 238 of Code, the Code prevailing&nbsp;over the relevant Sections contend in State Financial Corporation&nbsp;Act, 1951 has been examined. We find that Section 29(5) of the&nbsp;State Financial Corporation Act contains provisions which makes&nbsp;the Financial Corporation Corporation to be deemed owner of such&nbsp;concern, only for the purposes of initiating suits by or against the&nbsp;concern, and the State Financial Corporation shall sue and be&nbsp;sued in the name of the concern. This in our view is only for the&nbsp;purpose of initiating or defending any suit with reference to the&nbsp;property which has been pledged with the Financial Corporation,&nbsp;and do not make the State Financial Corporation the owner of&nbsp;such properties.
							<strong>
								 (p21)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							On the&nbsp;one hand respondent had filed the claim with reference to the&nbsp;financial debt with Resolution Professional and on the other hand&nbsp;claiming the ownership of the assets and refusing to hand over the&nbsp;same to the applicant. This contrary stand does not serve the&nbsp;purpose of the respondent.
							<strong>
								 (p24)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							No attempt has been made to transfer&nbsp;assets in favour of the corporation by executing appropriate&nbsp;documents of conveyance, after invoking p 29 of SFC Act.&nbsp;Any transfer of immovable property which has a value of more&nbsp;than hundred rupees can be made only by a registered&nbsp;instrument, as per Section 54 of Transfer of Property Act, 1882&nbsp;and therefore WBIDC cannot be treated as owner of said&nbsp;properties. They are merely mortgage at this point in time, holding&nbsp;security interest.
							<strong>
								 (p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Reliance on several other similar judgments including the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Supreme Court Judgement in
							<em>
								 Navichandra Steels Pvt. Ltd. v.
							</em>
							<em>
								 SREI Equipment Finace Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 25 SC
							</em>
							 is placed to state that Code would prevail over any&nbsp;Act, if there were any inconsistency.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In this case, the inconsistency&nbsp;arises between Section 18 of the Code and Section 29 of State&nbsp;Financial Corporation Act. While Section 18(f) of the Code, the&nbsp;Code enunciates that any asset recorded the balance sheet of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor as its assets, and it is the duty of the resolution&nbsp;professional to take custody of such assets of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor. The explanation to the said Section 18 of Code also&nbsp;provides that assets owned by Corporate Debtor but not in&nbsp;possession of Corporate Debtor will also need to be taken custody&nbsp;whereas Section 29 of State Financial Corporation Act provides&nbsp;that if the borrower fails to owner honour the terms and conditions&nbsp;of the borrowings then the Corporation which has got first charge&nbsp;over the immovable properties of the Corporate Debtor would be&nbsp;entitled to retain and dispose properties pledged. Thus, this&nbsp;inconsistency needs to be resolved and the same is resolved by&nbsp;applying Section 238 of Code, which provides that IBC would&nbsp;prevail over any other statute in the case of such inconsistency.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above finding above, we direct the WBIDC to hand&nbsp;over the possession of the property to the resolution professional&nbsp;within 15 days from the date of pronouncement of this order. This&nbsp;application, being I.A. (IB) No. 204/KB/2022 is accordingly&nbsp;allowed and disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p28)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
