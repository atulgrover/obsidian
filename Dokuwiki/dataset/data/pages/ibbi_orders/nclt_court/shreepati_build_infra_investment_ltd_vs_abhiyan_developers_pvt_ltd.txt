	<h1>
		Initially entered into an agreement for purchase of flat and subsequently advance paid was converted into a loan, cannot change the basic nature of transaction and Financial Creditor will remain an allottee within the meaning of Sec. 2(d) of the RERA Act, 2016 and is required to comply proviso to Section 7(1) of IBC to initiate CIRP &ndash; Shreepati Build Infra Investment Ltd. Vs. Abhiyan Developers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 1, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, the Financial Creditor entered into an agreement with the Corporate Debtor for purchase of flat after the payment of a sum of Rs. 3,50,00,000/-. However, as all the permissions and/or approvals were not sought properly, the Corporate Debtor was unable to commence the construction for the same. Subsequently, the said amount was converted into a loan account by the Corporate Debtor and the same was to be repaid along with the agreed interest. NCLT Mumbai Bench held that: (i) Any agreement cannot change the basic nature of transaction and the Financial Creditor over here will remain an allottee within the meaning of Section 2(d) of the RERA Act, 2016. (ii) The main Company Petition in the instant case was filed by a single allottee. The Applicant has not taken any steps to comply with the amended provisions of Section 7 of the Code, and the application is, therefore, deemed to be withdrawn before admission or the petition could have been filed jointly by not less than one hundred allottees or not less than 10% of the total number of allottees under the same real estate project.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Full text of the judgment
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The C.P.(IB) 4149/MB/2018 was filed under p 7 of the IBC, 2016 by Abhigyan Developers Pvt. Ltd., seeking to initiate CIRP against Shreepati Build Infra Investment Ltd. The Financial Creditor entered into an agreement with the Corporate Debtor for purchase of flat after the payment of a sum of Rs. 3,50,00,000/-. However, as all the permissions and/or approvals were not sought properly, the Corporate Debtor was unable to commence the construction for the same. Subsequently, the said amount was converted into a loan account by the Corporate Debtor and the same was to be repaid along with the agreed interest.
						</li>
						<li>
							However, no payment was received by the Applicant in this regard. On 31.03.2018, the Respondent informed the Applicant through letter confirming that the sum of Rs. 3,50,00,000/- received during the period of 2009-2010 is due and would be repaid back with 15% cumulative interest, compounded quarterly from the date of investment till the date of payment. However, no payment was made.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Financial Creditor filed the main Company petition on 10.08.2018 and the same was admitted by this Tribunal vide order dated 29.10.2021. Against the said order, the Corporate Debtor filed an appeal before Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, wherein, NCLAT vide order reported at&nbsp;(2022) 206 NCLAT&nbsp;has set aside the order of admission dated 29.10.2021 passed by this Tribunal and remanded back the case to this Adjudicating Authority to consider the objection of maintainability as required under third proviso of Section 7(1).
						</li>
						<li>
							The present IA is filed under p 60(5) of IBC, 2016 r/w Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016 by Shreepati Build Infra Investment Limited (Applicant) being the Respondent in the main company petition C.P.(IB) 4149/MB/2018, bringing on record an order passed by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, reported at&nbsp;(2022) 206 NCLAT.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The actual nature of transaction between the&nbsp;parties was in the capacity of builder and allottee, based on the agreement dated 15.02.2011. Further any agreement cannot change the basic nature of transaction and the Financial Creditor over here will remain an allottee within the meaning of Section 2(d) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. Therefore, it will also remain as a &ldquo;financial creditor&rdquo; who is owed a &ldquo;financial debt&rdquo; as per provisions of Section 5(7) read with Section 5(8) (f) of the IB Code 2016.
						</li>
						<li>
							However, as a result of amendment in Section 5(7) read with Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC, 2016 which came into effect on 28.12.2019, the application for initiating CIRP against the Corporate Debtor was required to be filed by not less than 100 of such allottees under the same real estate project or not less than 10% of the total number of such allottees of such real estate project, whichever is less.
						</li>
						<li>
							The third proviso gave an opportunity for persons who had already initiated corporate insolvency resolution process against a corporate debtor to comply with the said requirement within 30 days of the commencement of the said ordinance, failing which the application would be deemed to be withdrawn before its admission.
						</li>
						<li>
							The main Company Petition in the instant case was filed by a single allottee. The Applicant has not taken any steps to comply with the amended provisions of Section 7 of the Code, and the application is, therefore, deemed to be withdrawn before admission or the petition could have been filed jointly by not less than one hundred allottees or not less than 10% of the total number of allottees under the same real estate project. So, in view of the above, CP(IB)/4149/2018 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed as non-maintainable
							</strong>
							 . Accordingly, IA/739/2022 is allowed as disposed of in&nbsp;aforesaid terms.
							<strong>
								 (p5)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
