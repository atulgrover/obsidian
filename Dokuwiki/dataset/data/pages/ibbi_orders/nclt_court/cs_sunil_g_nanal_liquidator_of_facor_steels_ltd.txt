		<h1>
			From the Liquidation Regulation 33 that the Adjudicating Authority has the power to permit the private sale subject to certain conditions &ndash; CS Sunil G Nanal Liquidator of Facor Steels Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			August 21, 2020
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that it is clear from the Liquidation Regulation 33 that the Adjudicating Authority has the power to permit the private sale subject to certain conditions. In the case in hand since the secured creditors have already given their consent for private sale, this Adjudicating Authority is of the opinion that the request of the Liquidator be considered. Accordingly, the sale of the property of the Corporate Debtor by private treaty is allowed and liquidator is permitted to sell the property i.e. factory structure and sheds for ₹15,00,00,000/- excluding applicable indirect taxes, stamp duty, cess, if any. Therefore, in view of the above provisions of Section 53 of the Code the liquidator have to act as provided under Section 53 of the Code for which the specific direction is not required from this Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;In view of the provisions of Section 35 and Section 53 of the Code the liquidator is also seeking permission of this Bench to distribute the proceeds of the sale.&nbsp;However, the liquidator has to arrive at the amount available for distribution after deducting the costs, if any, as required under p 53(1)(a). Further there are no details as to amount payable to labourers in terms of p 53(1)(b)(i). Hence no directions can be given in respect of this prayer at this stage and he has to file application providing all the details preferably by serving a copy to the security interest holders over this asset.&nbsp;MA is partially allowed and disposed of.
		</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The applicant submits that he was appointed as the Liquidator of the Corporate Debtor- Facor Steels Limited by an order of this Bench dated 26.06.2018. He submits that p 35(f) subject to p 52 of the Code empowers liquidator to sell the immovable properties and actionable claims of the Corporate Debtor in liquidation through public auction or private contract. The applicant further submits that he is also acting as the Resolution Professional of another company, namely Vidarbha Iron and Steel Corporation Ltd. (VISCO). VISCO is a guarantor to the lenders of the Corporate Debtor. The said VISCO had mortgaged its land and building located in Nagpur. VISCO had leased out its land to the Corporate Debtor. The Corporate Debtor had constructed factory structure and sheds admeasuring 33,152 sq. mtrs. on the leasehold land provided by VISCO. The said factory structure and sheds are inseparable from the land without affecting its usefulness.
						</p>
						<p>
							VISCO had filed a petition under Section 10 of the Code which was admitted by this Tribunal vide its order dated 12.03.2018. Further, the resolution plan submitted by R C Plasto Tanks and Pipes Private Limited (RC Plasto), Nagpur for VISCO was approved by this Tribunal by an order dated 23.08.2019. It is submitted that the Resolution Plan proposed by the Resolution Applicant of VISCO is a consolidated proposal in respect of VISCO as well as the assets of the Corporate Debtor i.e. the factory structure and sheds. It is also submitted that the CoC of VISCO consist of the entire class of secured creditors of the Corporate Debtor, who hold the first&nbsp;
							<em>
								pari passu
							</em>
							&nbsp;charge on all the assets of the Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							It is submitted that it is difficult to segregate the assets of VISCO and the Corporate Debtor without causing any harm to its usefulness. It is submitted that if the assets of the Corporate Debtor are to be sold separately to different parties then most part of the building which is owned by the Corporate Debtor will have to be demolished which will substantially affect the realisable value. Further such demolition will also amount to violating the MIDC norms with respect to the percentage of construction and render the purchase of land unviable for the Resolution Applicant of VISCO.&nbsp;It is further submitted that the secured creditors who have security interest over the assets of the Corporate Debtor and who hold exclusively&nbsp;
							<em>
								pari passu
							</em>
							&nbsp;charge on the assets of the Corporate Debtor have agreed for the sale of assets of the Corporate Debtor to RC Plasto, the Resolution Applicant of VISCO. It is submitted that RC Plasto is willing to purchase the structure for a sum of Rs. 15 Crores, which is over and above the liquidation value of the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;It is further submitted that considering the unique situation of the Corporate Debtor, it is imperative to sell the assets of the Corporate Debtor by way of private contract to RC Plasto. Hence this application.
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								<em>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</em>
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that it is clear from the Liquidation Regulation 33 that the Adjudicating Authority has the power to permit the private sale subject to certain conditions. In the case in hand since the secured creditors have already given their consent for private sale, this Adjudicating Authority is of the opinion that the request of the Liquidator be considered. Accordingly, the sale of the property of the Corporate Debtor by private treaty is allowed and liquidator is permitted to sell the property i.e. factory structure and sheds for ₹15,00,00,000/- excluding applicable indirect taxes, stamp duty, cess, if any. Therefore, in view of the above provisions of Section 53 of the Code the liquidator have to act as provided under Section 53 of the Code for which the specific direction is not required from this Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;In view of the provisions of Section 35 and Section&nbsp;53 of the Code the liquidator is also seeking&nbsp;permission of this Bench to distribute the proceeds of the&nbsp;sale.&nbsp;However, the liquidator has to arrive at the amount&nbsp;available for distribution after deducting the costs, if any,&nbsp;as required under p 53(1)(a). Further there are no&nbsp;details as to amount payable to labourers in terms of&nbsp;p 53(1)(b)(i). Hence no directions can be given in&nbsp;respect of this prayer at this stage and he has to file&nbsp;application providing all the details preferably by serving a&nbsp;copy to the security interest holders over this asset.&nbsp;MA is partially allowed and disposed of.
