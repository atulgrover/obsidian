	<h1>
		Whether the Section 29 of State Financial Corporation Act, 1951 shall override the provisions of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 &ndash; Pankaj Tibrewal RP of Dutta Agro Mills Pvt. Ltd. Vs. West Bengal Industrial Development Corporation &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		September 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, the physical possession of Corporate Debtor has been taken over by West Bengal Industrial Development Corporation (WBIDC) (a Financial Corporation comes under the purview of Section 3 of the State Financial Corporation Act, 1951) and all the employees of Corporate Debtor have left the organization and that the physical possession of the factory cum registered office of the Corporate Debtor was under the possession of the WBIDC. NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) No authority, post framing of IBC has been cited to substantiate the view that an earlier Special Act (have SFC Act, 1951) with a non obstante clause shall prevail over the IBC which is a later one. (ii) The CD is a defaulter debtor but the WBIDC the Creditor Corporation is not the owner of the property hypothecated/mortgaged to it by the CD, it is rarely in control over the assets of the CD. (iii) It is, therefore, explicit by virtue of Section 238 of IBC, the provisions of IBC will prevail over the other Statutes. (iv) IBC, 2016 is indubitably and indisputably a Special Statute as also a later statute vis-a-vis the SFC Act of 1951, both having non obstante clause. The reach of non obstante clause of SFC Act is limited by Section 46B of the Act, whereas non obstante clause of IBC shows that it prevails in all situations. Thus, IBC as a Special statute has a non obstante clause which does not have a limited reach unlike SFC Act.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Applicant, as the Resolution Professional of the Corporate Debtor apprised the ex-management about the initiation of the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor vide email dated 07.07.2022 and requested to provide the complete details and requisite documents regarding the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							That the ex-management of the Corporate Debtor vide its email dated 09.07.2022 informed that the physical possession of Dutta Agro Mills Private Limited has been taken over by West Bengal Industrial Development Corporation (WBIDC), i.e., the Respondent herein, that all the employees of Corporate Debtor have left the organization and that the physical possession of the factory cum registered office of the Corporate Debtor was under the possession of the Respondent Corporation.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor in the year 2006 had availed a term loan of Rs.17,20,00,000 from the Respondent Corporation. As per the terms of the loan agreement, a charge was created by the Respondent upon the movable and immovable assets of the Corporate Debtor. The respondent being a Financial Corporation comes under the purview of Section 3 of the State Financial Corporation Act, 1951 (SFC) and had powers/rights to take over the management or possession or both of the Corporate Debtors by virtue of Section 29 of the SPC Act, 1951.
						</li>
						<li>
							This is an application preferred by the Applicant/Resolution Professional in the matter of M/s. Dutta Agro Mills Private Limited (Corporate Debtor), under Section 19(2) read with Section 60 (5) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 and Rule 11of the National Company Law Tribunal Rules, 2016, inter alia, seeking the following reliefs;
							<ul>
								<li>
									i. Allow the present application.
								</li>
								<li>
									ii. Direct the Respondent to assist the Applicant to complete the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor in a time bound manner.
								</li>
								<li>
									iii. Direct the Respondent to handover the possession of the movable and immovable properties/assets of the Corporate Debtor to the Applicant.
								</li>
								<li>
									iv. Pass any other Order or relief as this Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal deems fit andproper in the interest of justice.
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The issue that fell for consideration is whether the Section 29 of the State Financial Corporation Act, 1951 shall override the provisions of IBC, 2016 ignoring Section 238 in IBC.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							No authority, post framing of IBC has been cited to substantiate the view that an earlier Special Act (have SFC Act, 1951) with a non obstante clause shall prevail over the IBC which is a later one.
						</li>
						<li>
							The argument advanced by the WBIDC that there has been a change of ownership in favour of WBIDC before enactment of IBC, has no legs to stand upon. The CD is a defaulter debtor but the WBIDC the Creditor Corporation is not the owner of the property hypothecated/mortgaged to it by the CD, it is rarely in control over the assets of the CD.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is, therefore, explicit by virtue of Section 238 of IBC, the provisions of IBC will prevail over the other Statutes.
						</li>
						<li>
							IBC, 2016 is indubitably and indisputably a Special Statute as also a later statute vis-a-vis the SFC Act of 1951, both having non obstante clause. The reach of non obstante clause of SFC Act is limited by Section 46B of the Act, whereas non obstante clause of IBC shows that it prevails in all situations. Thus, IBC as a Special statute has a non obstante clause which does not have a limited reach unlike SFC Act.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of above enumerations, for all purposes, provisions of IBC, 2016 should prevail over SFC Act, 1951.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the foregoing analysis, the application being IA(IB) No. 1267/KB/ 2022 is
