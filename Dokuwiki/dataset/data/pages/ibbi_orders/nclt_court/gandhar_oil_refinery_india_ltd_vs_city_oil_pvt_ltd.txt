	<h1>
		Whether interest can be clubbed with principal debt to crossover the threshold limit of 1 Crore for filing CIRP petition under Section 9 of the IBC? &ndash; Gandhar Oil Refinery (India) Ltd. Vs. City Oil Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 30, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Operational Creditor has failed to bring forth any agreement between the parties that a default in payment would attract interest. The interest of 24% is levied only in terms of a unilateral clause in the invoice and the Corporate Debtor never agreed or promised. Without clubbing such interest with the Principal amount, threshold as per Section 4 of the Code is not met. The Adjudicating Authority held that levying of interest being neither mentioned in any agreement entered into by the parties, nor being specifically admitted by the Corporate Debtor, in absence of any promise of the Corporate Debtor to pay such interest, could not be clubbed with the principal amount due to hold the interest as a &lsquo;debt&rsquo; so as crossover the threshold amount of 1 Crore as ingrained in Section 4 of the Code by virtue of notification No. SO 1205(E) dated 24 March, 2020 of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs increasing the threshold limit from one lakh to one crore for the purpose of p 4 of the Code.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 168 NCLTCase Name:Gandhar Oil Refinery (India) Ltd. Vs. City Oil Pvt. Ltd.Operational Creditor:Gandhar Oil Refinery (India) Ltd.Corporate Debtor:City Oil Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:C.P. (IB) No. 150/KB/2021Judgment Date:19-Apr-23Court/Bench:NCLT Kolkata BenchMember (Judicial):Ms.&nbsp;Bidisha BanerjeeMember (Technical):Shri Balraj Joshi
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This is a Company Petition filed under p 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (the Code) by Gandhar Oil Refinery (India) Limited (Operational Creditor or GORIL) for initiation of CIRP against City Oil Private Limited (Corporate
						<br />
						 Debtor).
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observed that as held by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
						<em>
							 Jumbo Paper Products v. Hansraj Agrofresh Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 497 NCLAT
						</em>
						 that the threshold limit of Rs. 1 cr. will be applicable for application filed u/s 7 or u/s 9 on or after 24 March, 2020 even if default is of a date earlier than 24 March, 2020.&nbsp;Now, the prima facie issue that arises is Whether &lsquo;interest&rsquo; can be clubbed with principal debt to crossover the threshold limit of 1 Crore for filing petition under Section 9 of the Code?
					</p>
					<p>
						The Operational Creditor has failed to bring forth any agreement between the parties that a default in payment would attract interest.&nbsp;The interest of 24% is levied only in terms of a unilateral clause in the invoice and the Corporate Debtor never agreed or promised.&nbsp;Without clubbing such interest with the Principal amount, threshold as per Section 4 of the Code is not met.
						<strong>
							 (p9-11)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority referred the following decision:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Hon&rsquo;ble High Court at Karnataka in Jyothi Limited vs. Boving Fouress Limited [MANU/KA/1022/2000]
						</li>
						<li>
							Steel India v. Theme Developers Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2020] 192 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Pavan Enterprises v. Gammon India&nbsp;[2018] 46 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							SS Polymers vs. Kanodia Technoplast Limited&nbsp;[2019] 193 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Recent order, on one such situation, the co-ordinate bench of NCLT Delhi in CBRE South Asia (P) Ltd. v. United Concepts and Solutions (P) Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 02 NCLT
						</li>
						<li>
							In Mr. Prashat Agarwal v. Vikash Parasrampuria &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2022) 509 NCLAT
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And, the Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<p>
						Section 3(6)(b) is explicit that a claim is also a right to remedy for &lsquo;breach of contract&rsquo; therefore unless the &lsquo;contract&rsquo; is explicit about the liability to pay interest in default, no actionable claim can be said to exist.&nbsp;Whereas the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court at Karnataka in Jyothi Limited (Supra) in no uncertain terms as on 01 December, 2000 summarised the legal&nbsp;proportion as to claims for &lsquo;interest&rsquo; albeit in a Winding Up proceeding&nbsp;under p 433(e) of the Companies Act, 1956, as enumerated supra.
					</p>
					<p>
						Mr. Prashat Agarwal (Supra) is a subsequent judgment, where the decision of the the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court at Karnataka in Jyothi Limited (Supra) was not placed, which if placed may have influenced the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT. The decision of a superior forum, which is also prior in time, could not be brushed aside. Hence, we would be constrained to hold that levying of interest being neither mentioned in any agreement entered into by the parties, nor being specifically admitted by the Corporate Debtor, in absence of any promise of the Corporate Debtor to pay such interest, could not be clubbed with the principal amount due to hold the interest as a &lsquo;debt&rsquo; so as crossover the threshold amount of 1 Crore as ingrained in Section 4 of the Code by virtue of notification No. SO 1205(E) dated 24 March, 2020 of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs increasing the threshold limit from one lakh to one crore for the purpose of p 4 of the Code.&nbsp;In light of the above precedents C.P. (IB) No. 150/KB/2021 is dismissed.
						<strong>
							 (p18-21)
						</strong>
					</p>
