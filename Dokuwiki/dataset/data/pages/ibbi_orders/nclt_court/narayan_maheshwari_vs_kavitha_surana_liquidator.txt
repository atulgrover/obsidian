	<h1>
		An application for withdrawal cannot be filed during the Liquidation process &ndash; Narayan Maheshwari Vs. Kavitha Surana Liquidator of M/s. Shri Veerganapathi Steels (P) Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Chennai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 24, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment on withdrawal of Liquidation process, the NCLT Chennai Bench held that: (i) It is to be noted here that IBC, 2016 treats CIRP and Liquidation as two different parts. All the provisions under Chapter-II of IBC, 2016 which deals with CIRP cannot be made applicable under Chapter-III of IBC, 2016 which deals with Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. (ii) As regards the order passed by the coordinate Benches of NCLT, the same has only a persuasive value and cannot have any binding effect. (iii) Moreover, IBBI which is the Regulator of IBC, 2016 has still not proposed for withdrawal of cases during the liquidation process. Law and attended regulations are yet to be notified by the IBBI. (iv) In the absence of any express provisions either under the provisions of IBC, 2016 for withdrawal of Applications during Liquidation process or under the Regulations framed by IBBI, an Application for withdrawal cannot be filed during the Liquidation process.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>

Shri Veerganapathi Steels (P) Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA(IBC)/193(CHE)/2023 in CP/229/(IB)/2018Judgment Date:19-Jul-23Court/Bench:NCLT Chennai BenchMember (Judicial):Shri&nbsp;Sanjiv JainMember (Technical):Shri Sameer Kakar
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The CIRP against Shri Veeraganapathi Steels Pvt. Ltd (Corporate Debtor) commenced vide order dated 26.04.2018. Thereafter, liquidation of the Corporate Debtor was ordered on 12.04.2019.
						</li>
						<li>
							Present application is the erstwhile Promoter Director has been filed to direct the Liquidator to file an application for withdrawal of liquidation proceedings as against the Corporate Debtor pursuant to One Time Settlement (OTS) entered into between the 2nd Respondent (100% of voting rights) and the Promoters/Guarantors of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Section 12A of the IBC, 2016 finds place under Chapter-II of IBC, 2016 which deals with &ldquo;Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process&rdquo; of the Corporate Debtor. The Liquidation process of the Corporate Debtor is contained under Chapter-III of the IBC, 2016. It is to be noted here that IBC, 2016 treats CIRP and Liquidation as two different parts. All the provisions under Chapter-II of IBC, 2016 which deals with CIRP cannot be made applicable under Chapter-III of IBC, 2016 which deals with Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. For instance, the moratorium under Section 14 of IBC, 2016 is lifted when an order of Liquidation is passed and a fresh moratorium under Section 33(5) is ordered.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, it is significant to note here that the Regulator viz. IBBI has inserted Regulation 30A under CIRP Regulations, 2016 with effect from 04.072018, which deals with withdrawal of Application under Section 12A of IBC, 2016. The said Regulation 30A deals only with the withdrawal of Application during the CIRP and not during Liquidation. Further, there is no such amendment made in the Liquidation Process Regulation by the Regulator viz. IBBI. In the absence of any express provisions either under the provisions of IBC, 2016 for withdrawal of Applications during Liquidation process or under the Regulations framed by IBBI, an Application for withdrawal cannot be filed during the Liquidation process.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							From
							<em>
								 V. Navaneetha Krishnan Vs. Central Bank of India Coimbatore &amp; Another&nbsp;(2018) 298 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , more particularly from para 4, it is clear that the Corporate Debtor was under CIRP and the order of Liquidation was passed during the said stage. It is also noted that in the aforestated matter, the Application under Section 12A of IBC, 2016 was never filed during the Liquidation period and the suspended Director / ex-promoter of the Corporate Debtor had filed an Application under Section 230 of the Companies Act, 2013 to take over the Company by way of Scheme of Compromise.
							<strong>
								 (p21)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							However, in the present case, the Liquidation was ordered as early as on 12.04.2019. Further from the submissions made by the Liquidator, it is seen that certain immovable property owned by the Corporate Debtor was sold and the amounts realized were distributed in accordance with the provisions of IBC, 2016.
							<strong>
								 Hence the said proposition of
								<em>
									 Navaneetha Krishnan (supra)
								</em>
								 cannot be made applicable to the facts of the present case.(p21)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							At this juncture, we find it apt, to refer to the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
							<em>
								 Mr. Hemanth Meka Rao Vs. Asset Reconstruction Company (India) Ltd. (ARCIL) &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2019) 588 NCLAT
							</em>
							 in which the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the year 2019 has categorically held that no order of settlement can be passed, even though the promoter agrees to pay all the dues. Hence, the exhorted arguments de by the Learned Counsel for the Applicant are not valid in the eye of law.
							<strong>
								 (p22-23)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Binding nature of coordinate Benches:
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 As regards the order passed by the coordinate Benches of NCLT, the same has only a persuasive value and cannot have any binding effect.
							</strong>
							 Moreover, IBBI which is the Regulator of IBC, 2016 has still not proposed for withdrawal of cases during the liquidation process. Law and attended regulations are yet to be notified by the IBBI.
							<strong>
								 (p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Opportunity to Promoters to close the CIRP:
							</strong>
							 Question also arises to what extent opportunity should be given to the Promoters to close the CIRP. In Our view the promoter had enough opportunities available with them to settle the matter with the Creditors. i.e.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						a. Before filing of the application under Section 7 of IBC, 2016.
					</p>
					<p>
						b. Before Order of Admission passed under Section 7(5) of IBC, 2016.
					</p>
					<p>
						c. Before the order of Liquidation under Section 33 of IBC, 2016 is passed by this Tribunal.
						<strong>
							 (p26)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the present case, if we allow for withdrawal of Applications under Section 12A during the Liquidation process, then every suspended Directors / Promoters or ex-Promoters will wait till liquidation is ordered for the assets of the Corporate Debtor and wait for the assets to be put in for auction. Only at the last stage, the Promoter will enter into a settlement with the Financial Creditors at a throw away price. This is in stark contrast to the object of IBC, 2016. Further, as already stated, IBBI has so far not brought out the Regulations for withdrawal of Application during Liquidation proceedings and also Parliament has not modified the existing structure of IBC, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In so far as invocation of Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016 to order for 12A during Liquidation period is concerned, we find it apt to refer to the Judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
							<em>
								 Kridhan Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr. Vs. Venkatesan Sankaranarayan &amp; Anr.&nbsp;[2020] 73 NCLAT,
							</em>
							 it is manifestly made clear that the inherent powers cannot be exercised in contravention or in conflict or ignoring express provision of Law.
							<strong>
								 (p28-30)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Be that as it may, this Tribunal already in the matter of
							<em>
								 Jayashree Mohan Vs. Pathukasahasram Raghunathan Raman Liquidator M/s. RA-NI Precast Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 848 NCLT
							</em>
							 in vide its order dated 14.10.2022 has held that there is no provision under IBC 2016 to come out of the Liquidation Process once liquidation is ordered.
							<strong>
								 (p31)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Thus, in view of the reasons stated supra and also guided by the principle of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of
							<em>
								 Arun Kumar Jagatramka Vs. Jindal Steel And Power Ltd. &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2021) 46 SC
							</em>
							 , the present Application filed by the Applicant is not sustainable and liable to dismissed and accordingly stands dismissed. No order as to costs.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
