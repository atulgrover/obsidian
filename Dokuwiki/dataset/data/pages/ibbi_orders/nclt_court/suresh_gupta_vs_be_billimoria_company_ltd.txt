	<h1>
		Whether a claim in respect of a settlement agreement can be regarded as &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; within the meaning of Section 5(21) of IBC &ndash; Suresh Gupta Vs. B.E. Billimoria &amp; Company Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 30, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench holds that: (i) It is the case of the OC that out of the total 6 instalments of payment as agreed in the Settlement Agreement, the CD has not made payment even against one instalment. Hence it is found that the cause of action for the OC against the CD arose out of the Settlement Agreement and the failure of the CD to pay instalments to the OC as agreed between them. (ii) The moment the parties entered into the Settlement Agreement, the nature of the debt changed. The amount outstanding pursuant to the Settlement Agreement is a settlement amount which can only be construed as a mere debt and does not qualify to be an operational debt as defined under Section 3(11) of the IBC as it lost its character of operational debt. Hence, the claim of the OC as regards existence of operational debt due and payable by the CD fails. (iii) An application under Section 9 of the IBC cannot be admitted when there exists a cloud of suspicion, especially when the nexus between the OC and CD in their engagement or agreement is not clearly made out. The Settlement Agreement does not offer any credence in establishing the relationship between the parties. Even if it is believable, CIRP cannot be initiated on the failure of the Settlement Agreement.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As per the Settlement Agreement, the CD appointed the OC to represent and act on its behalf to satisfactorily and successfully monitor, supervise, mange, coordinate and complete the construction contract entered into between the CD and the DDA in connection with construction of CWG Site associated with CWG Games, 2010. The CD‟s Appointment Letter dated 16.11.2009 states that the OC shall be eligible for Gross Commission in respect of the profits of the projects at the rate of 17% of the final net pre-tax profits and that the commission due to the OC shall be paid at the end of the project after the completion certificate has been issued and the bills and accounts have been finalised, tallied and settled fully but the OC would be eligible for some advance on mutual agreement. It further states that the final dues are to be adjusted only on finalisation of accounts and receipt of final dues from the DDA. The OC has agreed for engagement for commission, Annexure-D of the Application is the salary slip for the month of August 2017 in which the OC is shown as an &ldquo;employee‟ of the CD in the capacity as &ldquo;Advisor‟ for its &ldquo;Site Delhi Branch‟ in the grade of &ldquo;Top Management‟. A consolidated payment of Rs. 5,10,000/- is shown to have been made to the OC by deducting Rs. 15,000/- as TDS and making other deductions. The Settlement Deed dated 09.02.2017 is shown to be entered between Mr. Suresh Gupta, the OC in his individual capacity and the Managing Director of the CD, i.e., B.E. Billimoria &amp; Co., viz., Mr. Digant L. Kapadia. However, the Acknowledgment Letter dated 09.02.2017 is on the Letter Head of &ldquo;Bebanco Northern Contracts Ltd.‟, which is shown as a subsidiary of B.E. Billimoria &amp; Co. Ltd. It is interesting to find that the said Acknowledgment Letter is signed on behalf of &bdquo;Bebanco Northern Contracts Ltd.‟(BNCL) by the same Mr. Digant Kapadia as the Director of BNCL who also executed the Settlement Agreement on behalf of the CD in the capacity of its Managing Director. It is the same Mr. Kapadia had filed the Affidavit-in-Reply for and on behalf of the CD.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, the Acknowledgment Letter attached to the Settlement Agreement is&nbsp;addressed to two entities, namely, &bdquo;RSB Projects Ltd, A-34 Okhla Industrial Area,&nbsp;Phase-I, New Delhi- 1100020‟ and &bdquo;RSB Infrastructure‟ having the same address. The&nbsp;Acknowledgment Letter is seen to have been accepted by Mr. Suresh Gupta on behalf&nbsp;of &ldquo;RSB Projects Ltd.‟ The said Acknowledgment Letter has also been accepted on&nbsp;behalf of &bdquo;RSB Infrastructure‟ and signed by yet another person on behalf of it. The&nbsp;Acknowledgment Letter bears the stamp of &bdquo;RSB Infrastructure‟, New Delhi. As per&nbsp;Annexure- F &ldquo;(Colly)&rdquo;, all the emails between Mr. Suresh Gupta are seen to have been&nbsp;sent from the ID of the OC, viz., &ldquo;gupta@rsbinfra.com‟ and the recipients &ldquo;dlk@bebanco.com‟ with copies to &ldquo;margaret@bebenco.com‟ and &ldquo;nimish@rsbinfra.com‟. All other correspondence is seen to have been made to the CD&nbsp;herein by the OC. Neither &ldquo;RSB Projects Ltd.‟ nor &ldquo;RSB Infrastructure‟ is a party to the&nbsp;dispute. Further, Annexure 1- &ldquo;Colly&rdquo; to Annexure 39 are correspondences between&nbsp;the OC in his individual capacity and the CD, i.e., B.E Billimoria &amp; Co. However,&nbsp;Bebanco Northern Contracts Ltd was never in the picture. Then the question remains&nbsp;as to the role and involvement of &bdquo;Bebanco Northern Contracts Ltd‟ in the matter.&nbsp;Neither the OC nor the CD has offered any explanation to the above discrepancy,&nbsp;either in their pleadings or during arguments. The above certainly casts shadow on the&nbsp;pleadings advanced by both the OC and the CD, which points to the fact that both the&nbsp;parties have suppressed certain material facts before us.
						</li>
						<li>
							Be that as it may, the CD has contended that one of the pre-conditions for payments to&nbsp;the OC was procurement of completion certificate from the DDA and tallying and&nbsp;finalisation of bills and accounts in the Settlement Agreement and the Appointment Letter. The OC has produced copy of the provisional completion certificate dated&nbsp;12.10.2010. The said certificate has flagged certain rectifications; however, there is&nbsp;nothing directly or indirectly connecting the performance of the OC in the said&nbsp;certificate. No dispute regarding quality of service of OC was ever raised by the CD&nbsp;before 02.07.2018, on which date the OC issued demand notice under Section 8 of the&nbsp;IBC. In its reply dated 18.07.2018, the CD stated that they were receiving various&nbsp;complaints from DDA for completion and rectification of the defective work and that the&nbsp;same had to be looked into by them on their own due to the inability of the OC to&nbsp;attend the same. Further, on a bare perusal of the Settlement Agreement, it is evident&nbsp;that it was executed to resolve the disputes between the parties.
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, it is seen that the &ldquo;dispute‟ mentioned therein is only with regard to &ldquo;payment and dues‟ and nothing else. There existed no dispute as to quality of service rendered by the OC. The decision of NCLT Mumbai in
							<em>
								 Outdoor Advertising Professionals(India) Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Graphene Media Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 78 NCLT
							</em>
							 as relied by the OC is not applicable in the present matter since not only the facts and circumstances of the aforesaid decision was totally different from the present matter but also the CD never admitted the OC‟s claims as mentioned in the aforesaid Settlement Agreement. Therefore, the defence of this moonshine preexisting dispute by the CD goes against it.
						</li>
						<li>
							Now, the cardinal question is to decide whether there existed an operational debt for the OC to invoke Section 9 of the IBC. The cause of action for the OC revolves around the Settlement Agreement dated 09.02.2017 and failure of the CD to give effect to the same. Further to this agreement, a salary slip was issued for the month of August, 2017. The OC has claimed Rs. 1,36,95,472/- as the amount of default. However, the manner in which the OC arrived at this figure in Part IV has not been explained. The Application has been filed for the failure to give effect to the terms of the Settlement Agreement. Clause 5 of the Settlement Agreement listed out 6(six) instalments of Rs. 34,23,868/- each. The question as to whether a claim in respect of a settlement agreement can be regarded as &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; within the meaning of Section 5(21) has been considered in a number of cases by the Courts and Tribunals. It has been held by the Hon‟ble NCLAT Bench Chennai in
							<em>
								 Maulik Kirtibhai Shah v United Telecom Limited
							</em>
							 ,
							<em>
								 (2023) 595 NCLAT
							</em>
							 that definition of &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; cannot be interpreted widely so as to include any agreement between parties which does not specifically pertain to the supply of goods or service. It is the case of the OC that out of the total 6 instalments of payment as agreed in the Settlement Agreement, the CD has not made payment even against one instalment. Hence it is found that the cause of action for the OC against the CD arose out of the Settlement Agreement and the failure of the CD to pay instalments to the OC as agreed between them.
						</li>
						<li>
							From the records, it is seen that pursuant to the Settlement Agreement entered between the parties, the parties had agreed to settle the outstanding operational debt at Rs. 2,05,43,208/- which is lower than alleged outstanding dues of Rs. 5, 58,61,000/-, when the second winding up notice was issued by the OC on 18.05.2015. We find that the moment the parties entered into the Settlement Agreement, the nature of the debt changed. The amount outstanding pursuant to the Settlement Agreement is a settlement amount which can only be construed as a mere debt and does not qualify to&nbsp;be an operational debt as defined under Section 3(11) of the IBC as it lost its character&nbsp;of operational debt. Hence, the claim of the OC as regards existence of operational debt&nbsp;due and payable by the CD fails.
						</li>
						<li>
							Moreover, there is a shadow of doubt as regards the engagement and agreement between the OC and the CD. We feel that both parties have suppressed number of things from the Adjudicating Authority. An application under Section 9 of the IBC cannot be admitted when there exists a cloud of suspicion, especially when the nexus between the OC and CD in their engagement or agreement is not clearly made out. The Settlement Agreement does not offer any credence in establishing the relationship between the parties. Even if it is believable, CIRP cannot be initiated on the failure of the Settlement Agreement.
							<strong>
								 Hence, we cannot admit this Application to initiate CIRP in respect of the CD.(p5)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
