		<h1>
			The Demand Notice post tracking receipt only records that &ldquo;Item Dispatched&rdquo;, this is not enough to satisfy the requirement of p 9(5)(ii)(c) of the IBC read with rule 5(2) of AAA Rules, 2016, both of which use the term &ldquo;delivered&rdquo; &ndash; Pankaj Modani Vs. Big Vision Water Tech Private Limited &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			September 23, 2020
		</div>
		<p>
			The case of the Operational Creditor is that the Operational Creditor raised invoices on the Corporate Debtor towards professional fees for providing professional compliance services for the month of October, 2018. The Corporate Debtor was granted a credit period of one month after raising the invoice on 05.10.2018. The total debt due and payable to the Operational Creditor is stated to be ₹ 1,40,682.00. The Operational Creditor had served a Demand Notice in Form 3 dated 12.04.2019 to the Corporate Debtor in terms of p 8 of the IBC. The Corporate Debtor has not replied to the Demand Notice. The Corporate Debtor has not submitted any reply in the matter. NCLT held that proof of service of Demand Notice has not been attached to the Petition. However, proof of posting in the form of Speed Post receipt No.EM779732264IN dated 12.04.2019 has been attached. The tracking receipt attached only records that &ldquo;Item Dispatched.&rdquo; This is not enough to satisfy the requirement of p 9(5)(ii)(c) of the IBC read with rule 5(2) of AAA Rules, 2016, both of which use the term &ldquo;delivered.&rdquo; The pre-requisites of p 9 of the IBC have not been satisfied. Therefore, the present petition fails and therefore, the same is rejected in terms of p 9(5)(ii)(c) of the IBC.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision&nbsp;
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The case of the Operational Creditor is that the Operational Creditor raised invoices on the Corporate Debtor towards professional fees for providing professional compliance services for the month of October, 2018. The Corporate Debtor was granted a credit period of one month after raising the invoice on 05.10.2018.&nbsp;The total debt due and payable to the Operational Creditor is stated to be ₹ 1,40,682.00.&nbsp;The Operational Creditor had served a Demand Notice in Form 3 dated 12.04.2019 to the Corporate Debtor in terms of p 8 of the IBC. The Corporate Debtor has not replied to the Demand Notice.&nbsp;The Corporate Debtor has not submitted any reply in the matter.
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT observed from the records that:
						</p>
						<p>
							(a) There is no record of any contractual arrangement between the Operational Creditor and the Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							(b) Copy of the invoice has not been attached. What has been attached at p.49 is only a calculation sheet mentioning the principal amount of the invoice, the interest calculation. There is no record of the invoice having been served on the Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							(c) There is no record of what professional services have been rendered by the Operational Creditor.
						</p>
						<p>
							(d) Proof of service of Demand Notice has not been attached to the Petition. However, proof of posting in the form of Speed Post receipt No.EM779732264IN dated 12.04.2019 has been attached. The tracking receipt attached only records that &ldquo;
							<em>
								Item Dispatched.
							</em>
							&rdquo; This is not enough to satisfy the requirement of p 9(5)(ii)(c) of the IBC read with rule 5(2) of AAA Rules, 2016, both of which use the term&nbsp;
							<em>
								&ldquo;delivered.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The pre-requisites of p 9 of the IBC have not been satisfied. Therefore, the present petition fails and therefore, the same is rejected in terms of p 9(5)(ii)(c) of the IBC.
						</p>
