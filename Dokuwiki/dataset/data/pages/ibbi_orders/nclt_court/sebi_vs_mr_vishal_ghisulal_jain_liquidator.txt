	<h1>
		A penalty&nbsp;imposed by SEBI during the liquidation can be claimed before Liquidator and can be considered for the purpose of distribution of assets under Section 53 of IBC? &ndash; Securities and Exchange Board of India Vs. Mr. Vishal Ghisulal Jain, Liquidator of, Sterling International Enterprises Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 5, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Mumbai Bench referred the judgment in Sundaresh Bhatt (2022) 103 SC and held that: (i) IRP, RP or Liquidator as the case may be, has an obligation to ensure that the assessment of statutory dues like taxes, fine, penalty etc. is legally completed. Natural corollary to this is, if determination of the statutory dues is allowed during liquidation period then filing of the claim arising out of such determination cannot be barred under IBC otherwise it would amount to empty formality. (ii) The only restriction the belated claimant has to face is that it cannot disturb the amount already distributed as per waterfall mechanism under Section 53 of the IBC. (iii) Any creditor who files belated claim has to suffer the consequences that it is entitled to receive the amount only from the left over assets of Corporate Debtor and has no right to disturb the distribution already made. (iv) The dues arising out of Adjudication Order passed on 17.02.2022 by SEBI which is admittedly after liquidation commencement date i.e. 18.10.2021, can be claimed before Liquidator.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 624 NCLTCase Name:SEBI Vs. 					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							A Show Cause Notice dated
							<strong>
								 12.02.2020
							</strong>
							 was issued to the Corporate Debtor in terms of Rule 4(1) of the Adjudication Rules read with Section 15-I of SEBI Act.
						</li>
						<li>
							A reply letter dated 20.01.2021 was received from the&nbsp;Resolution Professional (RP) of the Corporate Debtor informing that the Corporate Debtor has been relegated to insolvency proceedings vide order dated
							<strong>
								 16.07.2018
							</strong>
							 . Vide email dated 01.12.2021, it was informed that the Company has gone into liquidation vide order dated
							<strong>
								 18.10.2021
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							The Adjudicating Officer of SEBI vide Adjudication Order dated
							<strong>
								 17.02.2022
							</strong>
							 imposed a penalty of Rs. 2,00,000/- upon the Corporate Debtor in terms of Section 15HA for violation of provisions of Section 12A(a), (b), (c) of SEBI Act read with Regulations 3(a), (b), (c), (d) and Regulations 4(1), 4(2)(a) and (g) of PFUTP Regulations.
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, SEBI submitted Form-C to&nbsp;Liquidator on
							<strong>
								 14.12.2022
							</strong>
							 claiming an amount of Rs. 2,00,000/-. However, the Liquidator vide communication dated 20.01.2023
							<strong>
								 rejected
							</strong>
							 the claim on the technical ground of
							<strong>
								 delay in submission
							</strong>
							 of the same.&nbsp;The Tribunal vide its order dated 07.02.2023 allowed the Company Appeal filed by SEBI condoning the delay with a direction to the Liquidator to decide the claim of the appellant independently.
						</li>
						<li>
							In pursuance to the aforesaid order dated 07.02.2023, the&nbsp;Liquidator assessed the Form C- Proof of Claim submitted by the Appellant.
							<strong>
								 The liquidator vide communication dated 28.02.2023 rejected the claim of the Appellant by stating that the liquidation proceeding has commenced on 18.10.2021, and as such the claim has to be filed as on that date. The Adjudication Order on the basis of which you have filed the claim dated 17.02.2022, which is after the liquidation commencement date, and is hence not admissible, as no claim existed as on 18.10.2021.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, being aggrieved by the rejection of the claim by the liquidator, the present Company Appeal has been filed by SEBI.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Question before Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The only question that falls for consideration in this Company Appeal is &ldquo;Whether the claim of SEBI amounting to Rs. 2,00,000/- which arose consequent to passing of the Adjudication Order on 17.02.2022 and is admittedly after commencement of liquidation of Corporate Debtor on 18.10.2021, can be considered for the purpose of distribution of assets under p 53 of IBC?&rdquo;.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Contention of the parties
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Appellant contended that it is a Statutory Authority and has only taken steps to determine the penalty payable by Corporate Debtor. The appellant has not enforced claim for recovery of penalty due from Corporate Debtor during the period of moratorium under Section 33(5) of the IBC.
						</li>
						<li>
							She further contended that the appellant has initiated&nbsp;adjudication proceedings and imposed a penalty of Rs. 2,00,000/- by relying on the principle laid down by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in case of
							<em>
								 Sundaresh Bhatt, Liquidator of ABG Shipyard Vs. Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs&nbsp;(2022) 103 SC
							</em>
							 and the liquidator has an obligation to ensure that the levy of penalty is legal. The appellant also placed reliance on the&nbsp;IBBI Circular No. IP/002/2018 dated 03.01.2018&nbsp;which inter alia stipulates that a Corporate Debtor undergoing liquidation process, needs to comply with provisions of the applicable laws (Act, Rules, and Regulations, Circulars, Guidelines, Orders, Direction, etc.) during such process.
						</li>
						<li>
							In response to the above contention the learned counsel appearing for the Respondent/Liquidator contended that the Judgement cited by the appellant is immaterial with regard to this case. He also replied that the claim being in nature of &lsquo;Statutory Dues&rdquo; falls within the purview of Section 53(1)(e)(i) which delineates the manner in which disbursement of asset will take effect wherein any amount due to the Central or the State Government, including the amount to be received on account of Consolidates Fund of India or Consolidated Fund of a State shall be considered for disbursement of proceeds only till the extent of &lsquo;such due arisen in whole or any part of the period of two years&rsquo; preceding the liquidation commencement date.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the case of
							<em>
								 Sundaresh Bhatt (Supra)
							</em>
							 before dealing with moratorium under Section 14 or Section 33(5) of IBC, the Supreme Court has referred to the judgment of
							<em>
								 S.V. Kondaskar v. V.M. Deshpande, (1972) 1 SCC 438
							</em>
							 which was a case of winding up under Companies Act, 1956.
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has referred to moratorium not only under Section 14 but also under Section 33(5) of IBC. In fact, the Court categorically states that the interim resolution professional, resolution profession or the liquidator as the case may be, has an obligation to ensure that the assessment of statutory dues like taxes, fine, penalty etc. is legally completed.&nbsp;Natural corollary to this is, if determination of the statutory dues&nbsp;is allowed during liquidation period then filing of the claim&nbsp;arising out of such determination cannot be barred under IBC otherwise it would amount to empty formality.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 The only restriction the belated claimant has to face is that it cannot disturb the amount already distributed as per waterfall mechanism under Section 53 of the IBC.(p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is settled law that any creditor who files belated claim has to suffer the consequences that it is entitled to receive the amount only from the left over assets of Corporate Debtor and has no right to disturb the distribution already made as held in
							<em>
								 Ganeshilal Gupta v. Bharatpur Oil Mills through Official Liquidator (1972), the Rajasthan High Court.
							</em>
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above discussion, we answer the question raised&nbsp;in this appeal in affirmative i.e.
							<strong>
								 the dues arising out of Adjudication Order passed on 17.02.2022 by SEBI which is admittedly after liquidation commencement date i.e. 18.10.2021, can be claimed before Liquidator.(p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, the above Appeal is
							<strong>
								 allowed directing the Liquidator to admit the claim of the Appellant/SEBI amounting to Rs. 2,00,000. It is hereby made clear that Liquidator shall strictly adhere to the provisions of Section 53 of the IBC.
							</strong>
							 With the above direction, the COMPANY APPEAL NO. 12/2023 is disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p14-15)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
