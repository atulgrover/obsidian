						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the Case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							It is submitted that suspended directors of the Corporate Debtor stood disqualified as per the record of MCA during the CIRP period in view of the provisions of Section 164(2)(a) of the Companies Act, 2013, for non-filing of financial statements and Annual Returns for the FY 2013-14 till date. Section 167 of the Companies Act, 2013 further states that&nbsp;
							<em>
								&ldquo;the office of a director shall become vacant in case:&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								(a) He incurs any of the disqualifications specified in p 164.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							In such a situation Section 167(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 gives the power of appointment of directors to the promoters of the company, or in their absence to the Central Government.&nbsp;Hence, this application is filed to appoint the directors on the said Board in accordance with due procedure of law.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT held that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								In my opinion, in the event of commencement of CIRP of a Corporate Debtor, the management of the Corporate Debtor is handed over to the RP as per p 17(1)(b) of the Code.
							</li>
							<li>
								In this regard there is no need of appointing any new directors on the board of the corporate debtor as the code gives mandatory power of management to the RP and no one else. Hence, the RP is authorised to do all such acts as would any director be empowered to do had there been any director on the management of the Corporate Debtor immediately prior to commencement of insolvency proceedings of the Corporate Debtor. The RP can very well file the Financial Statements and Annual Returns on behalf of the Company and comply with all the procedural formalities as laid down in the Companies Act, 2013.
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of the judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in&nbsp;
								<em>
									Steel Konnect (India) Pvt. Ltd v. Hero Fincorp Ltd.&nbsp;
									<strong>
										[2017]  42 NCLAT
									</strong>
								</em>
								, wherein it was held that&nbsp;
								<em>
									&ldquo;From clause (a) of sub-p 1 of p 17 while it is clear that the Management of affairs of the Corporate Debtor stand vested with the &lsquo;Interim Resolution Professional, such vesting is limited and restricted to the extent of power vested under sub-p 2 of p 17 which empowers the &lsquo;Interim Resolution Professional&rsquo; to act and execute in the name of &lsquo;Corporate Debtor&rsquo; all deeds, receipts and other documents, if any, to take such action in the manner and subject to such restrictions, as may be specified by the Board and have access of authority to the electronic records of &lsquo;Corporate Debtor&rsquo;, books of account, records etc.&rdquo;
								</em>
							</li>
							<li>
								Furthermore, in the case of&nbsp;
								<em>
									Punjab National Bank V. James Hotels Ltd., [CP (IB) No.15/Chd/CHD/2017],
								</em>
								&nbsp;it was held that RP is the creature of the Code and for any&nbsp;
								<strong>
									difficulty
								</strong>
								&nbsp;he has to apply to the Adjudication Authority under the code. However, in the present case, no such difficulty can be seen prima facie as pleaded by the RP. Hence, the prayers for appointment of directors etc. are all rejected.
							</li>
							<li>
								Hence, in view of the above observation and the fact that RP has ample power under the Code to perform the functions of the management of the Corporate Debtor, this Application is&nbsp;
								<strong>
									Dismissed
								</strong>
								.
							</li>
						</ul>
