		<h1>
			Constitute Project wise CoC for conducting Reverse CIRP, issue separate EOI for each project and invite Resolution Plans for each project in Real Estate Insolvency are not allowed under IBC &ndash; Mr. N. Kumar RP of M/s. Sheltrex Developers Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Tata Capital Housing Finance Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Chennai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			May 4, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			In the case, the Resolution Professional filed application to permit to constitute Project-based Committee of Creditors, for the purpose of conducting reverse CIRP and to permit to issue separate Expression of Interest for each project under control of the Corporate Debtor and to consequently invite and place before the respective Committee of Creditors, Resolution Plans for each project under control of the Corporate Debtor. The The Adjudicating Authority held that on a thorough reading of the IBC, 2016 read alongwith the regulations made thereunder envisages the insolvency of the Corporate Debtor and it can be seen that there is no concept of limited CIRP or CIRP for specific projects anywhere. It can be further seen in the Hon'ble Supreme Court judgement in Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd. Vs. Union of India [2019]  13 SC, that the IBC, 2016 is a beneficial legislation which can be triggered to put the Corporate Debtor back on its feet in the interest of unsecured creditors like allottees, so that a replaced management may carry out the real estate project as originally envisaged and deliver the flat/apartment as soon as possible or pay late fees for late delivery.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							An Application was filed under Section 60(5) of IBC, 2016 by the Resolution Professional for M/s Sheltrex Developers Pvt. Ltd (Corporate Debtor) seeking the following reliefs:
						</p>
						<p>
							a) Permit the Applicant herein to constitute Project-based Committee of Creditors, for the purpose of conducting reverse corporate insolvency resolution process, as mandated by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									Flat Buyers Association Winter Hills &ndash; 77 Gurgaon Vs. Umang Realtech Pvt. Ltd through IRP &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2020]  166 NCLAT
								</em>
							</strong>
							;
						</p>
						<p>
							b) Permit the Applicant hereinto issue separate Expression of Interest for each project under control of the Corporate Debtor and to consequently invite and place before the respective Committee of Creditors, Resolution Plans for each project under control of the Corporate Debtor;
						</p>
						<p>
							c) Pass such other order as this Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal may, in the facts and circumstances of this case, deem fit and thus render justice.&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								<em>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</em>
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that on a thorough reading of the IBC, 2016 read alongwith the regulations made thereunder envisages the insolvency of the Corporate Debtor and it can be seen that there is no concept of limited CIRP or CIRP for specific projects anywhere. It can be further seen in the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court judgement in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd. Vs. Union of India&nbsp;[2019]  13 SC
							</em>
							, that the IBC, 2016 is a beneficial legislation which can be triggered to put the Corporate Debtor back on its feet in the interest of unsecured creditors like allottees, so that a replaced management may carry out the real estate project as originally envisaged and deliver the flat/apartment as soon as possible or pay late fees for late delivery.
						</p>
						<p>
							As far as the case laws relied on by the Applicant is concerned i.e.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									Flat Buyers Association Winter Hills &ndash; 77 Gurgaon Vs. Umang Realtech Pvt. Ltd through IRP &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2020]  166 NCLAT,
								</em>
							</strong>
							&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									Rajesh Goyal Vs. Babita Gupta &amp; Ors.
								</em>
								<em>
									[2020]  273 NCLAT
								</em>
								&nbsp;
							</strong>
							and&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									Shri Bijay Pratap Singh Vs. Unimax International
								</em>
								&nbsp;
								<em>
									[2020]  165 NCLAT
								</em>
							</strong>
							, this Adjudicating Authority is of the view that, the mechanism adopted by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT is too peculiar to the facts and circumstances of the Winter Hills judgement and cannot be used as a precedent in the present scenario. Moreover, in the present case, as no promoters have put forward any funds to avoid CIRP, the process of project-wise CIRP cannot be followed here as how it was followed in the Winter Hills judgement and the decision in Rajesh Goyal.
						</p>
						<p>
							Therefore, since the reliefs sought by the Applicant are well outside the purview of the IBC, 2016 and the relevant regulations, and by reason that the view taken by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the decisions as relied on by the Applicant does not apply in the present case, this Application is not maintainable and is liable to be dismissed. Accordingly, this IA viz., stands Dismissed; however without cost.
							<strong>
								(p9-10)
							</strong>
						</p>
