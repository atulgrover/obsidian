	<h1>
		Expiry of Performance Bank Guarantee submitted by Successful Resolution Applicant not covered the Resolution Plan Implementation Schedule is non-compliance of the mandatory Regulation 36B(4A) of CIRP Regulations, 2016, NCLT rejects Resolution Plan in M/s Viceroy Hotels Ltd. &ndash; Dr. Govindarajula Venkata Narasimha Rao, RP of Viceroy Hotels Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 4, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		Bank Guarantee has been issued by Kotak Mahindra Bank Limited in favour of the ARC and the applicant therein is the Successful Resolution Applicant, for a period of six months commencing from 10.11.2022. The sum in Resolution Plan is payable within 675 days. In terms of Regulation 36B (4A) of CIRP Regulations, 2016, Performance Bank Guarantee shall cover the Plan Implementation Schedule. Therefore, Performance Bank Guarantee from the Successful Resolution Applicant being non-est, in the eye of law as on date, non-compliance of the mandatory Regulation 36B (4A) of CIRP Regulations, 2016 is apparent. The Adjudicating Authority following the ruling in re, M.K. Rajagopalan, supra, held that the resolution plan of the successful resolution applicant as submitted before us, being in breach of the statutory provision, is liable to be rejected. We accordingly reject the same.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 264 NCLTCase Name:Dr. Govindarajula Venkata Narasimha Rao, RP of Viceroy Hotels Ltd.Corporate Debtor:M/s Viceroy Hotels Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:I.A. No. 1343/2022 in CP (IB) No. 219/7/HDB/2017Judgment Date:09-Jun-23Court/Bench:NCLT Hyderabad BenchMember (Judicial):Shri Venkata Ramakrishna Badarinath NandulaMember (Technical):Shri Charan SinghCase Status:Set aside by NCLAT, reported at&nbsp;(2023) 780 NCLAT
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The IA is filed by the Resolution Professional under Section 30(6) &amp; 31 of IBC, 2016 r/w regulation 39(4) of CIRP&nbsp;Regulations, 2016 &amp; Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016, seeking approval of the resolution plan submitted by Anirudh Agro Farms Limited (Resolution Applicant) as duly approved by the CoC with 95.82% votes.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observed that:
					</p>
					<p>
						We are fully conscious of the need to keep judicial intervention or innovation from NCLT at its bare minimum and should not disturb the foundational principles of the IBC, as the law laid down in catena of rulings of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India, including in the rulings referred above, However at the same time we cannot overlook or brush aside glaring breach of compliance of a statutory mandatory provision by the successful resolution applicant when noticed.
						<strong>
							 (p23)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						A perusal of Compliance Certificate (Form-H) filed by the Resolution Professional discloses that in compliance of Request for Resolution Plan (RFRP), Bank Guarantee for Rs.16.85 crores, has been submitted by the Resolution Applicant. The said Bank Guarantee has been issued by Kotak Mahindra Bank Limited in favour of the ARC and the applicant therein is the Successful Resolution Applicant, for a period of
						<strong>
							 six months commencing from 10.11.2022
						</strong>
						 .
						<strong>
							 (p27)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						As per the Resolution Plan submitted by the CoC timeline for payment of Total Resolution Plan Amount of Rs.168.50 crores in five tranches. The above sum is payable, in all, within 675 days. Needless to say that in terms of Regulation 36B (4A) of CIRP Regulations, 2016, Performance Bank Guarantee shall cover the Plan Implementation Schedule.
						<strong>
							 (p28)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the above backdrop, having found that the Performance Bank Guarantee furnished by the Successful Resolution Applicant since does not cover the Plan Implementation Schedule, during the hearing on 09.12.2022.&nbsp;Having perused the above undertaking, we are of the considered view that the above &lsquo;clarification undertaking&rsquo; of the Successful Resolution Applicant as filed cannot be accepted or even construed as compliance of the above mandatory Regulation, for the simple reason that unilateral clarificatory undertaking cannot result in extending Bank Guarantee beyond six months from 10.11.2022.
						<strong>
							 (p30-31)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Therefore, it is not in doubt that the Performance Bank Guarantee furnished on 10.11.2022 by the Successful Resolution Applicant had expired by afflux of time specified therein, especially in the absence of any record placed before this Tribunal evidencing extension of Performance Bank Guarantee beyond the initial six months period. Therefore, &lsquo;Performance Bank Guarantee&rsquo; from the Successful Resolution Applicant being non-est, in the eye of law as on date, non-compliance of the mandatory Regulation 36B (4A) of CIRP Regulations, 2016 is apparent. In that view of the matter and following the ruling of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India, in
						<em>
							 M.K. Rajagopalan vs Dr. Pariasamy Plalani Gounder &amp; Another&nbsp;(2023) 60 SC
						</em>
						 , we&nbsp;have no alternative but to reject the Resolution Plan on the ground of non-compliance of the statutory provision. However, this order of ours does not&nbsp;preclude the Successful Resolution Applicant from pursuing remedies, if any, available to him under law in this regard.
						<strong>
							 (p32)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Therefore, under these circumstances, following the ruling in re,
						<em>
							 M.K. Rajagopalan, supra
						</em>
						 , we hare by hold that the resolution plan of the successful resolution applicant as submitted before us, being in breach of the statutory provision, is liable to be rejected. We accordingly reject the same.
						<strong>
							 (p33)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the light of rejection of Resolution Plan, as per sub-clause (1)(b) of p 33 of the I&amp;B Code, 2016, this Tribunal shall pass an order requiring the Corporate Debtor to be liquidated in the manner as laid down in Chapter-III. However, considering the facts and circumstances of the case, besides liquidation of the Corporate Debtor being the last resort, we hereby direct continuation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process of the Corporate Debtor by directing the Resolution Professional to issue fresh Form-G and complete the resolution process as expeditiously as possible, but not later than sixty days from the date of this order.&nbsp;This petition is therefore dismissed accordingly. No costs.
						<strong>
							 (p34-35)
						</strong>
					</p>
