	<h1>
		It shall not be a right in law for a Financial Creditor under Section 7 of IBC to change the date of default | Amendment of date of default in an application under Section 7 is not to be allowed for mere asking &ndash; Inakshi Sobti &amp; Ors. Vs. Starlight Systems (I) Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 8, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment on amendment in CIRP Application , Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench holds that: (i) Ordinarily, a party to the litigation is entitled to seek amendments of its pleadings in order to correct mistakes or to bring in clarity of facts and figures or to add or omit certain facts or circumstances to advance its case. (ii) An amendment of pleading cannot be allowed which takes away the valuable defence available to the opposite side. This Adjudicating Authority cannot cause irretrievable prejudice to one of the parties, especially when the amendment sought has the effect of nullifying a statutory mandate. (iii) It shall not be a right in law for an applicant under Section 7 of the IBC to change the date of default. We need to be cautious if the change of date of default cuts the very root of maintainability of the Main Application. (iv) No amendment to an application under Section 7 of the IBC nullifying a statutory mandate can be allowed by the Adjudicating Authority.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Interim Application has been filed by Smt. Inakshi Sobti for herself and representing 27 other housing unit holders, the Applicants / Financial Creditors (FCs), under p 60(5) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) for changing the date of default in the Main Application.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicants / FCs are the flat purchasers in the building, namely, Signia Pearl Co-operative Housing Society, which is an association of housing unit holders (Housing Society) and the Corporate Debtor (CD), is the developer of the housing project. The purported debt amounting to Rs. 7,65,00,000/- is in relation to a corpus fund to which the individual housing unit holders have deposited respective amounts out of this deposit with the CD. The CD was supposed to transfer the unutilised amounts to the Housing Society.
						</li>
						<li>
							Instead of filing different applications, 27 Applicants / FCs have given their consent to support the Main Application filed by Smt. Inakshi Sobti for initiating CIRP in respect of the CD as the default and amounts outstanding are identical.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Adjudicating Authority only addresses the prayers in this IA, the entire Main Application is not considered on merits.
						</li>
						<li>
							Ordinarily, a party to the litigation is entitled to seek amendments of its pleadings in order to correct mistakes or to bring in clarity of facts and&nbsp;figures or to add or omit certain facts or circumstances to advance its&nbsp;case. However, the question that this Adjudicating Authority needs to consider in the present matter is whether the FCs are attempting to correct any error or omission or bringing new facts or revised cause of action. We are also required to test whether the amendment as sought for in this IA, if allowed and incorporated in Part IV of the Main Application, would be prejudicial to the opposite side and would take away any of their legal defence.
						</li>
						<li>
							Section 10A of the IBC provides that no application for initiation of CIRP &ldquo;shall ever be filed&rdquo; for any default arising on or after 25th March, 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							The FCs have clearly stated in Part IV of the Main Application that the date of default was 05.09.2020. By the present IA, the attempt of the FCs is to shift the date of default from 05.09.2020 to 01.04.2021 by stating that the Housing Society was formed in September, 2020, and thus till the end of financial year 2020-21, the Applicants / FCs had waited for the CD to transfer the unutilised maintenance / corpus fund to the Housing Society, so the default would start from the 1st day of April, 2021.
						</li>
						<li>
							It can be inferred that the FCs are attempting to introduce a new plea that the default was one of continuing in nature and that it was in pursuance of certain &lsquo;informal&rsquo; settlement that the Applicants / FCs had waited till end of March 2020, being the end of financial year in which a Housing Society was formed. However, the Applicants / FCs have not provided any document along with the present IA to suggest that there existed such a settlement process between the parties except a mere statement that there were &ldquo;informal settlements&rdquo; with the CD.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, we are conscious of the settled proposition of
							<strong>
								 law that an amendment of pleading cannot be allowed which takes away the valuable defence available to the opposite side.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 This Adjudicating Authority cannot cause irretrievable prejudice to one of the parties, especially when the amendment sought has the effect of nullifying a statutory mandate.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							We are of the considered opinion that
							<strong>
								 it shall not be a right in law for an applicant under Section 7 of the IBC to change the date of default.
							</strong>
							 We need to be cautious if the change of date of default cuts the very root of maintainability of the Main Application.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Amendment of date of default in an application under Section 7 is not to be allowed for mere asking.
							</strong>
							 The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Dena Bank Vs. Shivakumar Reddy&nbsp;(2021) 69 SC
							</em>
							 also observed that amendment of pleading should be allowed depending on the facts and circumstances of each case. Mentioning date of default as 05.09.2020 in Part IV of the Main Application cannot be construed as mere error but a conscious admission. Allowing the amendment as prayed for in this IA would defeat the very purpose of Section 10A of the IBC and would render it redundant, amounting to abuse and misuse of the process of law.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 No amendment to an application under Section 7 of the IBC nullifying a
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 statutory mandate can be allowed by the Adjudicating Authority.
							</strong>
							 Accordingly, we are of the considered view that the above IA is only to be
							<strong>
								 dismissed.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
