	<h1>
		Whether an attachment on Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s bank account imposed by Provident Fund Authority before the initiation of CIRP can continue during Moratorium under Section 14 of IBC? &ndash; Mrs. Teena Saraswat Pandey Vs. Regional Provident Fund Commissioner &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 12, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that: (i) The lien created prior to the initiation of the CIRP cannot sustain as it will hinder the entire resolution process. (ii) The amount deducted for Provident Fund purely belongs to an employees and is not to be treated as an asset of the Corporate Debtor and cannot be touched by an IRP/RP/Liquidator as the case may be. (iii) However, it is important to note that such Provident Fund has to be an establishment fund kept separately by the company and only then this proviso will be applicable. If even wrongly and in violation of the laws of the land, the company fails to establish such Provident Fund in that event IRP/RP/Liquidator is not expected to provide for same, except under Section 53 of the Code, 2016 (iv) In this case since the Corporate Debtor had not opened a separate Bank Account for the Provident Fund, the aforesaid account has to be treated as per Section 53 of the Code, 2016.
	</p>
</div>

						</strong>
					</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The present Application is filed by the Applicant,&nbsp;Insolvency Professional of M/s S &amp; H Gears Pvt. Ltd., under p 60(5) of the IBC, 2016 read with rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 seeking directions to the Regional Provident Fund Commissioner (Respondent) to remove the lien and release the amount which is held in its custody during the CIRP.
						</li>
						<li>
							The HDFC Bank Account&nbsp;was frozen by the Respondent on 24.08.2018. However, the CIRP was commenced against the Corporate Debtor vide order dated 24.01.2020.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The issue which needs to be resolved is whether an Attachment on Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s bank account that was imposed before the initiation of CIRP, can continue during Moratorium under Section 14 of IBC?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 a. The Law
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							From the Section 14(1)(a) of IBC, it is clear that continuation of pending suits or proceedings against the Corporate Debtor including execution of any judgment, decree or order in any Court of law, Tribunal, Arbitration Panel or other Authority will temporarily cease to operate during moratorium. The purpose of the Section 14 is to ensure that no depletion of assets of the Corporate Debtor takes place during the CIRP and the Corporate Debtor is allowed to continue as a going concern in order to maximise the value for all the stakeholders.
							<strong>
								 (p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, this Bench is of the considered view that Section 14(1)(a) imposes complete embargo on any proceeding against the Corporate Debtor by any Authority till the completion of CIRP. Moratorium covers attachment of Bank accounts by any Authority including EPFO and it is required to be lifted to grant Corporate Debtor a fair chance of revival and to ensure that Resolution Plans are received.
							<strong>
								 It may also be inferred from the circumstances and intent of legislation that in the present cases, the lien created prior to the initiation of the CIRP cannot sustain as it will hinder the entire resolution process.(p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							From the Section 36(4) of Code 2016, it is evident that the that amount deducted for Provident Fund purely belongs to an employees and is not to be treated as an asset of the Corporate Debtor and cannot be touched by an Interim Resolution Professional/Resolution Professional/Liquidator as the case may be.
							<strong>
								 However, it is important to note that such Provident Fund has to be an establishment fund kept separately by the company and only then this proviso will be applicable. If even wrongly and in violation of the laws of the land, the company fails to establish such Provident Fund in that event Interim Resolution Professional/Resolution Professional/Liquidator is not expected to provide for same, except under Section 53 of the Code, 2016.(p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 b. Present case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is pertinent to note that in the present case, the Resolution Professional in the 6th CoC meeting dated 14.12.2020 had sought permission to operate the Current Account which is being maintained with HDFC Bank, Dewas Branch for receipt and payment for the CIRP period and the same was approved by the members of the CoC. However, the said bank account had been frozen by the Respondent on 24.08.2018. Therefore,
							<strong>
								 the Bench is of the considered view that since the Corporate Debtor had not opened a separate Bank Account for the Provident Fund, the aforesaid account has to be treated as per Section 53 of the Code, 2016.
							</strong>
							 It would be pertinent to mention here that so far as the EPF claims are concerned, the Resolution Professional had admitted the said claims to the tune of Rs. 1,26,12,838/- , based on the records available with the Corporate Debtor and the admitted amount of EPF is proposed to be paid in the Resolution Plan approved by the CoC in its 21st meeting held on 13.12.2021 and 14.12.2021. Therefore,&nbsp;the continuation of the lien on the bank account would not serve any&nbsp;purpose.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, the Bench is of the considered view that the Resolution Professional is in its right in seeking lifting of the lien created by the Respondent on Bank Account of the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;Accordingly, this IA is
							<strong>
								 allowed
							</strong>
							 with an order that the lien created by the Respondent on the HDFC Bank Account is hereby quashed/set aside and the Resolution Professional shall be at liberty to deal with the same in accordance with the IB Code 2016. A copy of this order shall be forwarded to the Bank concerned for necessary compliance.
							<strong>
								 (p29-30)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
