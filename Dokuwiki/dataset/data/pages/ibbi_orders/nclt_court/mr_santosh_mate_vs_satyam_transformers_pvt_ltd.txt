	<h1>
		Whether the parties under an agreement can convert an &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; into a &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo; and file Section 7 application on the basis of such agreement? &ndash; Mr. Santosh Mate (Prop. of Mahalaxmi Traders) Vs. Satyam Transformers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 24, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, filed CIRP application u/s 7 of IBC, the applicant has supplied goods and services to the Corporate Debtor and accordingly the Corporate Debtor was indebted to an amount of Rs.1,69,00,000/-. Since, the Corporate Debtor was not in a position to clear the outstanding due and payable to the applicant, both the parties have entered into a Debt Conversion Cum Agreement dated 01.04.2019 whereunder both parties agreed to convert the &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; of the applicant into &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo;. NCLT Mumbai Bench held that it is very clear from the definitions of the &lsquo;Financial Creditor&rsquo; and &lsquo;Financial Debt&rsquo; as defined under Section 5(7) and (8) that the above claim of the applicant does not fall under any of the categories of the &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo; and the Petitioner does not fall under definition of &ldquo;Financial Creditor&rdquo; within the meaning of the Code. The Counsel appearing for the applicant could not cite any authoritative pronouncement of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court or Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT to show that such a debt conversion is legally permissible under an agreement between the parties. In the absence of any such authoritative pronouncement of higher forum, this tribunal is unable to accept that the parties under an agreement can convert &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; into a &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo;. If Courts and Tribunals recognise such agreement as valid and permissible it would defeat the very object of the Code and would lead to rewriting the Code.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Company Petition is filed by Mr. Santosh Mate (Prop. of Mahalaxmi Traders), (Financial Creditor) seeking to initiate CIRP against M/s. Satyam Transformers Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) by invoking the provisions of Section 7 of IBC for resolution of an Financial Debt of Rs. 1,69,00,000/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							The applicant has supplied goods and services to the Corporate Debtor from 2014 and accordingly the Corporate Debtor was indebted to an amount of Rs.1,69,00,000/-. Since, the Corporate Debtor was not in a position to clear the outstanding &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; due and payable to the applicant, both the parties have entered into a Debt Conversion Cum Agreement dated 01.04.2019 whereunder both parties agreed to convert the &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; of the applicant into &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							Parties have executed the Loan Agreement dt. 01st April 2019 and accordingly the loan of INR 1.60 Crore was due from CD to FC.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Question before the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The important issue that arises for consideration in the present case is whether the parties under an agreement can convert an &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; into a &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo; and file Section 7 application on the basis of such agreement?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that it is very clear from the definitions of the &lsquo;Financial Creditor&rsquo; and &lsquo;Financial Debt&rsquo; as defined under Section 5(7) and (8) that the above claim of the applicant does not fall under any of the categories of the &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo; and the Petitioner does not fall under definition of &ldquo;Financial Creditor&rdquo; within the meaning of the Code. The Counsel appearing for the applicant could not cite any authoritative pronouncement of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court or Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT to show that such a debt conversion is legally permissible under an agreement between the parties. In the absence of any such authoritative&nbsp;pronouncement of higher forum, this tribunal is unable to accept that&nbsp;the parties under an agreement can convert &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo; into a &ldquo;Financial Debt&rdquo;. If Courts and Tribunals recognise such agreement as valid and permissible it would defeat the very object of the Code and would lead to rewriting the Code. The Conduct, manner coupled with the terms and conditions of the debt conversion agreement clearly raises doubts and suspicion regarding the genuineness of the agreement and therefore the collusion between the parties cannot be ruled out in this case. Therefore, the above Company Petition filed U/s 7 is not maintainable and it ought to have been filed U/s 9 of the Code.
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the aforesaid discussion this bench has no hesitation in holding that the above application filed by the applicant is nothing but a misuse and abuse of the process of the tribunal and the Code and the application has to be dismissed on that score alone. Accordingly, there are no merits in the above Company Petition and the above Company Petition is dismissed.
