		<h1>
			Income Tax Assessment Orders which pertain to the period prior to initiation of the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor, which is now led by the New Management on approval of the Resolution Plan, are unsustainable in law and without jurisdiction &ndash; Uttam Strips Ltd. Vs. Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-II
		</h1>
		<div>
			October 27, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that the CIRP against the Corporate Debtor commenced vide Order dated 09.04.2018, all the three Assessment Orders passed by the Income Tax Department on the subsequent dates (i.e., during the moratorium period), relate to the pre-CIRP period from 2012-13 to 2017-18. Hence, it is amply clear that through these Assessment Orders, the Income Tax Department has been attempting to recover their dues pertaining to the period prior to the initiation of CIRP of the Corporate Debtor. From the plain reading of the Section 32A of the Code, it can be inferred that the Corporate Debtor, on approval of the Resolution Plan by the Adjudicating Authority and on change in management, is immune from the proceedings which pertain to the pre-CIRP period.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Applicants that before initiation of CIRP of the Corporate Debtor, the Income Tax Department had issued notices under Section 143(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. It is added that the during the CIRP proceedings of the Corporate Debtor, certain notices were issued by the Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax/Respondent against the Corporate Debtor in contravention of Section 14 of the Code when the moratorium was already in place. The Income Tax Department continued to proceed against the Corporate Debtor even during the period of moratorium and based on such proceedings, it had passed three Assessment orders.&nbsp;While replying to the notice issued by Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax under p 144 and 147 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, the Resolution professional vide its letter dated 26.12.2018 had duly informed the Income Tax Department about CIRP of the Corporate debtor and imposition of moratorium in terms of Section 14 of IBC. Despite knowledge of the CIRP and moratorium in respect of the Corporate Debtor, the Income Tax Department did not file its claims in relation to any of the Assessment Years at any stage before the Resolution Professional as required under the provisions of IBC, 2016.&nbsp;Resolution Plan with respect to the Corporate Debtor was approved by this Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 06.06.2019.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that the CIRP against the Corporate Debtor commenced vide Order dated 09.04.2018, all the three Assessment Orders passed by the Income Tax Department on the subsequent dates (i.e., during the moratorium period), relate to the pre-CIRP period from 2012-13 to 2017-18. Hence, it is amply clear that through these Assessment Orders, the Income Tax Department has been attempting to recover their dues pertaining to the period prior to the initiation of CIRP of the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;From the plain reading of the Section 32A of the Code, it can be inferred that the Corporate Debtor, on approval of the Resolution Plan by the Adjudicating Authority and on change in management, is immune from the proceedings which pertain to the pre-CIRP period.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority referred to the Judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court dated 15.11.2019 in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Limited Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2019]  07 SC
							</em>
							,&nbsp;the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Karnataka in its Judgement dated 27.05.2021 passed in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Union of India &amp; Ors. Vs Ruchi Soya Industries Ltd.
							</em>
							&nbsp;
							<em>
								(2021)  12 HC
							</em>
							&nbsp;and&nbsp;Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court dated 13.04.2021 in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited versus Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited&nbsp;(2021)  54 SC.
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							It held that in view of the aforesaid settled position of law, the Assessment Orders, as referred to in the three I.A.s, passed by the Income Tax Department, which pertain to the period prior to initiation of the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor, which is now led by the New Management on approval of the Resolution Plan, are unsustainable in law and without jurisdiction.
							<strong>
								(p17-23)
							</strong>
						</p>
