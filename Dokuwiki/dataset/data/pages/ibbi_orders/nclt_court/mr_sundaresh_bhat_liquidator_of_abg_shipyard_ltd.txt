						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the Case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							CP(IB) No. 53 of 2017 was filed by Financial Creditor viz. ICICI&nbsp;Bank, under p 7 of the IBC, seeking&nbsp;initiation of CIRP&nbsp;against the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;which was admitted on 01.08.2017 by this Adjudicating Authority. Mr. Sundaresh Bhat was appointed as Interim&nbsp;Resolution Professional (hereinafter referred as &ldquo;IRP&rdquo;).&nbsp;The Committee of Creditors (hereinafter referred as &ldquo;COC&rdquo;) later on confirmed Mr. Sundaresh Bhat as Resolution&nbsp;Professional (&ldquo;RP&rdquo;) and thereafter, since no Resolution Plan was approved by the COC,&nbsp;Liquidation application was filed and Liquidation order was&nbsp;passed on 25.04.2019.
						</p>
						<p>
							The applicant had put the assets of the Corporate Debtor for sale through public auction&nbsp;by way of e-auction starting from public advertisement dated&nbsp;17 September, 2019 but has failed for four consecutive&nbsp;attempts on one or the other reasons, even after reduction of&nbsp;the reserve price of the assets of the Corporate Debtor, time&nbsp;and again, with regard to different e-auctions.&nbsp;After the failure of four e-&nbsp;auctions and during the of the fifth e-auction, the&nbsp;applicant has attempted to understand the local market&nbsp;Scenario and learnt regarding the possibility of attracting few&nbsp;buyers. However, the time frame of payment of sale&nbsp;consideration within fifteen days on conclusion of the e-&nbsp;auction and declaration of the highest bidder is considered to&nbsp;be too short especially keeping in mind the quantum of sale&nbsp;consideration and the ongoing economic slow-down due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Questions before the Tribunal
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							i. Whether the Liquidator, with respect to the assets of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor (in liquidation) of which liquidation process&nbsp;had commenced before 25 July, 2019, can be permitted to&nbsp;grant the benefit of the amendment regarding the payment of&nbsp;the balance consideration within 90 days to the highest bidder&nbsp;instead Of 15 days, as applicable to the liquidation processes&nbsp;commenced before the date of amendment?
						</p>
						<p>
							ii.&nbsp;Whether the Liquidator can be permitted to sale/dispose of&nbsp;the assets of the Corporate Debtor (in liquidation) by way of Private Sale after having failed to sell / dispose of the said&nbsp;assets by way of e-auction?
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Tribunal
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Regarding the first issue of granting of the permission to the&nbsp;liquidator for granting the benefit of the amendment to the highest&nbsp;bidder of an auction, it is important to deal with the relevant&nbsp;provisions and other material as mentioned hereinafter:
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Legal Position before the amendment on 25th July, 2019:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Relevant extract of the Clause 12 of the Schedule I to the Insolvency&nbsp;and Bankruptcy Board of India (Liquidation Process) Regulations,&nbsp;2016:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;(12) On the close of the auction, the highest bidder shall be invited&nbsp;to provide balance sale within fifteen days of the date&nbsp;when he is invited to provide the balance sale consideration. On&nbsp;payment of the full amount, the sale shall stand completed, the&nbsp;liquidator shall execute of sale or sale deed to transfer&nbsp;such assets and the assets shall be delivered to him in the manner&nbsp;specified in the terms of sale. &ldquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Legal Position on and after the amendment on 25th July, 2019:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Relevant extracts Of the Clauses 12 and 13 of the Schedule I to the&nbsp;Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (Liquidation Process)&nbsp;Regulations, 2016:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;(12) On the close of the auction, the highest bidder shall be invited&nbsp;to provide balance sale consideration within ninety days of the date&nbsp;of such demand:
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								Provided that payments made after thirty days shall attract interest&nbsp;at the rate of 12%:
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								Provided further that the sale shall be cancelled if the payment is&nbsp;not received within ninety days.
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								(13) On payment of the full amount, the sale shall stand completed,&nbsp;the liquidator shall execute certificate of sale or sale deed to&nbsp;transfer such assets and the assets shall be delivered to him in the&nbsp;manner specified in the terms of sale. &ldquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								IBBI Circular
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Relevant extract of the circular issued by the Insolvency and&nbsp;Bankruptcy Board of India bearing reference no.&nbsp;IBBI/LIQ/024/2019 dated 20 August,2019:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;2. The stakeholders have expressed a difficulty in applying the Amendment Regulations to a Liquidation process, which commenced before 25th July, 2019. It is reiterated that the&nbsp;provisions of Amendment Regulations are not applicable to the&nbsp;Liquidation process, which had commenced before into&nbsp;force of the aid Amendment Regulations and that they are&nbsp;applicable only to liquidation processes, which on or&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								after 25th July, 2019.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							It is a matter of record that the liquidation order with regard to the&nbsp;present Corporate Debtor was passed under p 33 of the IB&nbsp;Code, 2016 and the Applicant was appointed as the liquidator on&nbsp;25th April, 2019 i.e. much prior to the date of the amendment to the&nbsp;regulations relating to the liquidation process which are under&nbsp;consideration.&nbsp;As per the provisions of p 35(1)(f) of the Code, the liquidator&nbsp;is under obligation to sell / dispose Of the assets of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor (in liquidation) by way of public auction or private contract or&nbsp;in parcels as may be specified by the Board through the&nbsp;IBBI&nbsp;(Liquidation Process) Regulations,&nbsp;2016.
						</p>
						<p>
							It is observed that after the failure of four e-auctions and during the&nbsp;pendency of the fifth e-auction, it is felt by the applicant that time&nbsp;frame of payment of sale consideration within fifteen days as&nbsp;applicable to the Corporate Debtor is considered to be too short&nbsp;especially keeping in mind the quantum of sale consideration and&nbsp;the ongoing economic slow-down due to the COVID-19 pandemic.&nbsp;However, it is pertinent to mention that applicant had commenced&nbsp;the e-auction processes with effect from 17th September, 2019 and&nbsp;the COVID-19 pandemic and imposition of the lockdown thereto had&nbsp;taken effect from 25th March, 2020.
						</p>
						<p>
							It is a matter of record that Liquidation application was allowed&nbsp;much prior to the amendment and thereafter, amendment came into&nbsp;force. Hence, the amendment benefit cannot be granted to the&nbsp;Applicant retrospectively in view of the clarification made therein&nbsp;dated 26.08.2019. Therefore, only option left is private sale which is&nbsp;allowed by this order.&nbsp;Under the facts and circumstance as narrated above, we partially&nbsp;allow this application by permitting Prayer 4 only. Accordingly, the&nbsp;instant application stands disposed of.
						</p>
