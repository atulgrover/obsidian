	<h1>
		Whether the word &lsquo;or&rsquo; in CIRP Regulation 39(1A) would mean as an alternative or can the word &lsquo;or&rsquo; be read as &lsquo;and&rsquo; and whether the restriction incorporated in CIRP Regulation 39 fetters the modifications being made more than once in Resolution Plan &ndash; M/s. Consortium of Prudent ARC Ltd. &amp; M/s. Vizag Minerals &amp; Logistics Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Ravi Sankar Devarakonda RP of M/s. Meenakshi Energy Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 30, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that it is a discretion vested in the Resolution Professional which can be understood from the word &ldquo;May&rdquo; used therein. The pre condition for the Resolution Professional is that the two modes should in the first place be envisaged in the RFRP. Hence, the word &ldquo;or&rdquo; used in Regulation 39(1A) does not put a restriction on the envisaging of the said methods in the Resolution Plan. If the same is not envisaged in the Resolution Plan, the Resolution Professional would not have any opportunity of exercising the discretion given therein. Hence, when there is no such embargo and there is freedom for a Resolution Applicant to envisage both the methods in the Resolution Plan, contending that the Resolution Professional may restrict the scope of the Resolution Plan by allowing only one method would be totally against the spirit of the freedom that is given for the Resolution Applicant to envisage both the methods in the Resolution Plan. Hence, we are not inclined to read the word &ldquo;or&rdquo; as meaning that either the method under Clause `a&rsquo; or the method under Clause `b&rsquo; alone can be permitted by the Resolution Professional. Further, it held that since it is the CoC that permitted the modification, the restriction incorporated in Regulation 39 does not fetter the modifications being made more than once.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 26 NCLTCase Name:M/s. Consortium of Prudent ARC Ltd. &amp; M/s. Vizag Minerals &amp; Logistics Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Ravi Sankar Devarakonda RP of M/s.Meenakshi Energy Ltd.Corporate Debtor:M/s.Meenakshi Energy Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA No.37/2023 in CP(IB) No. 184/7/HDB/2019Judgment Date:23-Jan-23Court/Bench:NCLT Hyderabad BenchMember (Judicial):Justice Telaprolu RajaniMember (Technical):Shri Charan Singh
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This application is filed by the Applicant M/s. Consortium of Prudent ARC Ltd. against the Resolution Professional of M/s. Meenakshi Energy Ltd. and Others, seeking to restrain the Respondents from proceeding with the Challenge Process and to direct Respondent Nos.1 &amp; 2 to accept the Resolution Plan as submitted on 28.10.2022 and to restrain Respondent No.1 from considering all the Resolution Plans submitted after 28.10.2022.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether the word &ldquo;Or&rdquo; in Regulation 39 (1A) would mean as an alternative or can the word &ldquo;or&rdquo; be read as &ldquo;And&rdquo;.
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The said Regulation is extracted hereunder for ready reference:
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 &ldquo;39. Approval of resolution plan.
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						&hellip;&hellip;.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 (1A) The resolution professional may, if envisaged in the request for resolution plan-
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 (a) allow modification of the resolution plan received under sub-regulation (1), but not&nbsp;more than once; or
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 (b) use a challenge mechanism to enable resolution applicants to improve their plans.&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that
						<strong>
							 it is a discretion vested in the Resolution Professional which can be understood from the word &ldquo;
							<em>
								 May
							</em>
							 &rdquo; used therein. The pre condition for the Resolution Professional is that the two modes should in the first place be envisaged in the RFRP. Hence, the word &ldquo;or&rdquo; used in Regulation 39(1A) does not put a restriction on the envisaging of the said methods in the Resolution Plan.
						</strong>
						 If the same is not envisaged in the Resolution Plan, the Resolution Professional would not have any opportunity of exercising the discretion given therein. Hence, when there is no such embargo and there is freedom for a Resolution Applicant to envisage both the methods in the Resolution Plan, contending that the Resolution Professional may restrict the scope of the Resolution Plan by allowing only one method would be totally against the spirit of the freedom that is given for the Resolution Applicant to envisage both the methods in the Resolution Plan.
						<strong>
							 Hence, we are not inclined to read the word &ldquo;or&rdquo; as meaning that either the method under Clause `a&rsquo; or the method under Clause `b&rsquo; alone can be permitted by the Resolution Professional.
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						After having observed as above, we now would like to peruse the RFRP. It is seen that Clause 1.17 of RFRP is couched in extremely wide terms, that the CoC may negotiate with other qualified Applicants that provide the next highest bid after&nbsp;the H1 bidder or undertake simultaneous negotiations with&nbsp;various qualified Applicants and/or adopt any other process of negotiations, as they may deem appropriate for maximization of value to the stakeholders in a time bound manner (including, without limitation, open auction, Swiss challenge etc.).
					</p>
					<p>
						It further recites that The CoC may decide the successful Applicant by way of Swiss option and further, in case more than one Applicant scores equally as per evaluation matrix, the Resolution Professional / the CoC can reserve the right to conduct Swiss auction or any methodology to decide the highest qualified Applicant.
						<strong>
							 The CoC also gives to itself the power to reject all the qualified Applicants and/or Resolution Plans submitted by the qualified Applicants and thereafter call for submission of new Resolution Plans by other Prospective Applicants.
						</strong>
						 Any Member of the CoC individually or along with the other Members of the CoC shall have the right to submit the Resolution Plan on its own or instruct any of his advisors to submit the Resolution Plan for the Company. It has also given to itself the right to reject all the Resolution Plans and thereafter call for submission of new Resolution Plans from other Prospective Resolution Applicants. Hence, it can be seen that the CoC has reserved to itself ample powers and left sufficient scope with regard to the mode and method in which the Resolution Plans can be received and accepted.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 On the facts of this case, we conclude that the Regulation 39(1A) does not restrict the CoC from invoking any of the Clauses a &amp; b under Clause 1A of Regulation 39.
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						So far as permitting the modification of the Resolution plan by the Resolution professional more than once is concerned, we do not see any rationale in the said rule. When the CoC is given powers to permit modifications more than once, restricting the RP from doing so seems to be illogical, since no modifications would usually be permitted by the RP without the proposal of modification being placed before the CoC. However we take the help of Chennai bench&rsquo;s order (Reported at
						<em>
							 M.K. Rajagopalan Vs. Rajendran Shanmugam RP of Vasan Health Care Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 809 NCLT
						</em>
						 ) and hold that
						<strong>
							 since it is the CoC that permitted the modification, the restriction incorporated in Regulation 39 does not fetter the modifications being made more than once.
						</strong>
					</p>
