	<div>
		<h1>
			The United Kingdom, where the Arbitration Award has been passed, is a reciprocating territory, thus, the Award would be capable of execution in India &ndash; Agrocorp International Private (PTE) Limited Vs. National Steel and Agro Industries Limited &ndash; NCLT Mumbai
		</h1>
		<div>
			June 13, 2020
		</div>
		<p>
			On foreign award &lsquo;not binding&rsquo; upon parties in India issue, NCLT held that the United Kingdom, where the Arbitration Award has been passed in the present case, is a reciprocating territory in terms of p 44A vide notification No. 51 dated 01.03.1953 published in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary as SRO 399. Thus, the Award would be capable of execution in India, and the challenge by the Corporate Debtor on this score need to be repelled. On a pre-existing dispute between the parties since the Foreign Award has not obtained finality issue, NCLT held that The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in K. Kishan Vs M/S Vijay Nirman Company Pvt. Ltd held that pendency of challenge to an arbitral award qualifies as &lsquo;pre-existing dispute&rsquo; for the purposes of initiating corporate insolvency resolution process by the operational creditor. In the present case, there is no pending challenge to the Arbitral Award. This Bench is of the considered view that it is not possible to wait indefinitely for the Corporate Debtor to challenge the Arbitral Award, and that it has to decide the present petition on the basis of the admitted positions, that is to say, there is an Arbitral Award passed by a competent Arbitral Tribunal after the consideration of the positions of both the sides, and there is no challenge to the Arbitral Award dated 16.04.2018 in a manner known to law. Hence the same cannot be considered as a pre-existing dispute, and the objection of the Learned Counsel for the Corporate Debtor on this count is rejected.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the Case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								This is a Company Petition filed under p 9 of the&nbsp;Insolvency &amp; Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) by Agrocorp&nbsp;International Private (PTE) Limited, a Private Company&nbsp;incorporated under the Companies Act (CAP 50) of Republic of&nbsp;Singapore, seeking to initiate Corporate Insolvency Resolution&nbsp;Process (CIRP) against National Steel and Agro Industries&nbsp;Limited [CIN: L27100MH1985PLC140379] (&ldquo;the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor&rdquo;).
							</li>
							<li>
								The Corporate Debtor is a company incorporated on&nbsp;09.01.1985 under the Companies Act, 1956, as a public&nbsp;company limited by shares with the Registrar of Companies,&nbsp;Maharashtra, Mumbai. Its registered office is at No.621,&nbsp;Tulsiani Chambers, Nariman Point, Mumbai &ndash; 400021, within&nbsp;the State of Maharashtra. Therefore, this Bench has jurisdiction&nbsp;to deal with the present petition.
							</li>
							<li>
								The present petition was filed on 22.02.2019 before this&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority on the ground that the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor failed to make payment of a sum of $930,000.00 (US&nbsp;Dollars nine lakh thirty thousand only) as principal amount and&nbsp;$38,971.84 (US Dollars thirty-eight thousand nine hundred&nbsp;seventy-one and cent eighty-four only) as interest at the rate of&nbsp;4% p.a. compounded quarterly along with $9,536.00 (US Dollars nine thousand five hundred and thirty-six only) being&nbsp;cost awarded to the Operational Creditor by Arbitral Award&nbsp;arising out of a sale contract dated&nbsp;11.10.2017.&nbsp;The date of default is 19.04.2018, date on which Award of&nbsp;Arbitration was announced.
							</li>
							<li>
								The objections of the Learned Counsel for the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor can be classified as follows: &ndash;
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							(a) A foreign award is not binding upon parties in India when it&nbsp;is yet to be found to be enforceable by the competent court&nbsp;under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996;
						</p>
						<p>
							(b) There is a pre-existing dispute between the parties since the&nbsp;foreign award has not obtained finality;
						</p>
						<p>
							(c) The petition is defective in as much as the signatory to the&nbsp;petition is not authorised by the board of directors of the&nbsp;Operational Creditor.
						</p>
						<p>
							(d) Draft agreement and not a final contract between the&nbsp;parties.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									I. On foreign award &lsquo;not binding&rsquo; upon parties in India
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Corporate Debtor:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Corporate Debtor has referred to p 46 of the&nbsp;Arbitration and Conciliation Act,1996 which provides that the&nbsp;enforceability of a foreign award is a pre-condition to its&nbsp;binding nature under the Indian law.
							</li>
							<li>
								Accordingly, the reliance on the foreign award in the present&nbsp;petition by the Operational Creditor, without it being declared&nbsp;enforceable under applicable Indian law, is incorrect.&nbsp;The provisions of p 46 read with p 48 and 49 of the&nbsp;Arbitration and Conciliation Act,1996 entitles a party to have a&nbsp;foreign award declared un-enforceable and not binding in India&nbsp;and that the binding nature of a foreign award is subject to the&nbsp;court being &ldquo;satisfied that the foreign award is enforceable under this&nbsp;chapter&rdquo;. The scheme of the Arbitration and Conciliation&nbsp;Act,1996 further requires the party applying for enforcement of&nbsp;the foreign award to adduce evidence under p 47 in proof&nbsp;of the said foreign award.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Corporate Debtor has also relied on the judgment of the&nbsp;Hon&rsquo;ble Bombay High Court in the matter of&nbsp;
								<em>
									<strong>
										Noy Vallesina&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Engineering Spa v. Jindal drugs Ltd.
									</strong>
								</em>
								&nbsp;2006 5 Bom CR 155.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Operational Creditor:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Operational Creditor submits that at the time of&nbsp;enforcement of foreign judgments in India, two situations may&nbsp;arise depending on whether the foreign judgment is passed by a&nbsp;court in &ndash; (i) a reciprocating territory or; (ii) a non-reciprocating&nbsp;territory.
							</li>
							<li>
								A &lsquo;reciprocating territory&rsquo; means any country or territory&nbsp;outside India which the Central Government may, by&nbsp;notification in the Official Gazette, declare to be a reciprocating&nbsp;territory. The decree passed by a court in a reciprocating&nbsp;territory can be executed in India as if it had been passed by the&nbsp;District Court.
							</li>
							<li>
								The United Kingdom, where the Award has been passed in the&nbsp;present case, is a reciprocating territory in terms of p 44A vide notification No.51 dated 01.03.1953 published in the&nbsp;Gazette of India, Extraordinary. Thus, the Award would no&nbsp;longer be open to challenge on merits.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Operational Creditor submits that a Foreign Award is&nbsp;binding on persons between whom it was made and can be used&nbsp;by any of them by way of defence, set-off or otherwise in any&nbsp;legal proceeding in India. , in terms of p 46 of the&nbsp;Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Operational Creditor has relied on a judgment of the&nbsp;Hon&rsquo;ble National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT)&nbsp;in the matter of&nbsp;
								<em>
									<strong>
										Usha Holdings L.L.C. &amp; Anr. Vs Francorp&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Advisors Pvt. Ltd.
									</strong>
								</em>
								&nbsp;C.P. No. (IB)- 196(PB)/2017.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Adjudicating Authority:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The United Kingdom, where the Arbitration Award has been&nbsp;passed in the present case, is a reciprocating territory in terms of&nbsp;p 44A vide notification No. 51 dated 01.03.1953&nbsp;published in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary as SRO 399.&nbsp;Thus, the Award would be capable of execution in India, and&nbsp;the challenge by the Corporate Debtor on this score need to be&nbsp;repelled.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									II. On a pre-existing dispute between the parties since the Foreign Award has&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									not obtained finality
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Corporate Debtor:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								During the arbitration proceedings the Corporate Debtor filed&nbsp;their defences and submissions on 19.02.2018, 07.03.2018 and&nbsp;20.03.2018 raising inter alia the following disputes:
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							i. That there was no valid and binding contract between the&nbsp;parties;
						</p>
						<p>
							ii. That the contract being adjudicated upon was merely a draft&nbsp;contract; and
						</p>
						<p>
							iii. That the Corporate Debtor never confirmed the terms and&nbsp;conditions nor had they signed the contract.
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The petition was filed on the basis of the underlying transaction&nbsp;and hence the Corporate Debtor had raised a prior existing&nbsp;dispute vide its replies in the arbitration proceedings.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Corporate Debtor submits that the foreign award had not&nbsp;obtained finality as it could either, prefer an appeal in the&nbsp;appropriate forum in London or it can object to the&nbsp;enforcement of the award under the provisions laid under&nbsp;p 48 of the Arbitration Act.
							</li>
							<li>
								The amount due under the un-enforced foreign award does not&nbsp;fall under the definition of claim or debt under the IBC as the&nbsp;foreign award has not been enforced in India under the&nbsp;Arbitration Act, it cannot be said to be a judgment under Indian&nbsp;law and therefore there can be no right to payment.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
								<em>
									<strong>
										K. Kishan Vs M/S Vijay Nirman&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Company Pvt. Ltd
									</strong>
								</em>
								&nbsp;held that&nbsp;pendency of challenge to an arbitral award qualifies as &lsquo;pre-existing dispute&rsquo; for the purposes of initiating corporate&nbsp;insolvency resolution process by the operational creditor.&nbsp;
							</li>
							<li>
								A claim is defined to include a right to a remedy for breach of&nbsp;contract, if such breach gives rise to a right of payment, whether&nbsp;or not such a right has been reduced to a judgment. Thus, a&nbsp;claim includes a judgment, being an award passed by a court of&nbsp;competent jurisdiction.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Arbitral Award passed as per the Arbitration Rule No. 126&nbsp;of the &lsquo;GAFTA&rsquo; was initiated for the breach of sales contract,&nbsp;after considering the disputes and submissions made by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor in the Arbitral proceedings.
							</li>
							<li>
								The existence of contract was already proved during Arbitration&nbsp;proceedings and after considering those the award was decided&nbsp;by the Sole Arbitrator.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Operational Creditor:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Operational Creditor submits that if a party receives a&nbsp;binding award from a country which is signatory to the New&nbsp;York Convention or the Geneva Convention and the award&nbsp;was made in the territory which has been notified as a&nbsp;convention country by India, the award would then be&nbsp;enforceable in India. An award is &lsquo;final&rsquo; if under the laws of the&nbsp;country in which an award has been made, is no longer open to&nbsp;challenge on merits.
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Adjudicating Authority:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									K. Kishan Vs M/S Vijay&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Nirman Company Pvt. Ltd
								</strong>
							</em>
							&nbsp;held that pendency of challenge to an arbitral award qualifies as&nbsp;&lsquo;pre-existing dispute&rsquo; for the purposes of initiating corporate&nbsp;insolvency resolution process by the operational creditor. In the&nbsp;present case, there is no pending challenge to the Arbitral&nbsp;Award. This Bench is of the considered view that it is not&nbsp;possible to wait indefinitely for the Corporate Debtor to&nbsp;challenge the Arbitral Award, and that it has to decide the&nbsp;present petition on the basis of the admitted positions, that is to&nbsp;say, there is an Arbitral Award passed by a competent Arbitral&nbsp;Tribunal after the consideration of the positions of both the&nbsp;sides, and there is no challenge to the Arbitral Award dated 16.04.2018 in a manner known to law. Hence the same cannot&nbsp;be considered as a pre-existing dispute, and the objection of the&nbsp;Learned Counsel for the Corporate Debtor on this count is&nbsp;rejected.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									III. On defective petition
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Corporate Debtor submitted that the Board Resolution&nbsp;submitted by Operational Creditor&nbsp;authorises Mr. Vijaykumar Gopalan Iyengar to file the preset&nbsp;petition. However, the petition is signed by Mr. Nirav Gandhi&nbsp;as per the Power of Attorney submitted. The Corporate Debtor&nbsp;submits that in absence of specific authority in favour of Mr.&nbsp;Nirav Gandhi, the present petition is defective.&nbsp;The Corporate debtor has relied on the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT)&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Palogix&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Infrastructure Private Limited Vs. ICICI Bank Limited
								</strong>
							</em>
							─&nbsp;Company Appeal (AT) (Insol.) No. 30 of 2017 held that a&nbsp;power of attorney holder is not authorised to present an&nbsp;insolvency application under ps 7, 9 and 10 of the&nbsp;Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (&ldquo;IBC&rdquo;). It is only&nbsp;authorised representatives, duly authorised by board resolution, who are eligible to present the same. This is based on the simple&nbsp;rationale that a company being juristic person acts only through&nbsp;its board of directors, who can exercise all powers that the&nbsp;company is entitled to exercise, and it may through a resolution&nbsp;authorise any person to present an application. Further, officers&nbsp;authorised by the board cannot in turn give a power of attorney&nbsp;to any other person.
						</p>
						<p>
							Board Resolution dated 05.07.2018&nbsp;appoints Mr. Vijaykumar Gopalan Iyengar, Director&nbsp;as constituted attorney giving him the authority to appoint and&nbsp;authorise substitute as attorneys and to represent the Operational&nbsp;Creditor in various courts including the National Company Law&nbsp;Tribunal under the provisions of the IBC, 2016 of India. Further at&nbsp;pp.12-13 of the petition a Power of Attorney dated 08.11.2018&nbsp;signed by Mr. Vijaykumar Gopalan Iyengar, Director is&nbsp;annexed which states that the Operational Creditor appoints&nbsp;Mr. Nirav Dilip Gandhi as a constituted attorney to represent the&nbsp;Operational Creditor in various courts including the National&nbsp;Company Law Tribunal under the provisions of the IBC, 2016 of India&nbsp;along with Mr. Vijaykumar Gopalan Iyengar.&nbsp;Hence it is clear from the above that Mr. Nirav Dilip Gandhi is&nbsp;rightly the authorised representative for the Operational&nbsp;Creditor to initiate the corporate insolvency resolution process&nbsp;against the Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Adjudicating Authority:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Corporate Debtor submitted that the Board Resolution&nbsp;submitted by Operational Creditor (Exhibit A to the petition)
								<br/>
								authorises Mr. Vijaykumar Gopalan Iyengar to file the present&nbsp;petition. However, the petition is signed by Mr. Nirav Gandhi&nbsp;as per the Power of Attorney submitted. The Corporate Debtor&nbsp;submits that in absence of specific authority in favour of Mr.&nbsp;Nirav Gandhi, the present petition is defective.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the present case the Board of Directors of the Operational&nbsp;Creditor has, at its meeting held on 05.07.2018.
							</li>
							<li>
								In pursuance thereof, and in exercise of the powers conferred&nbsp;on Mr. Iyengar Vijaykumar Gopalan, a specific authorisation was given to Mr. Nirav Gandhi to initiate the present&nbsp;proceedings.
							</li>
							<li>
								At the outset, it is clarified that the authority of Mr. Nirav&nbsp;Gandhi to file this petition cannot be challenged given the
								<br/>
								judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in Palogix Infrastructure&nbsp;Private Limited v ICICI Bank Ltd. [Company Appeal (AT)&nbsp;(Insolvency) No. 30 of 2017], the judgment is crystal clear in&nbsp;terms of the authority to file the insolvency petition.&nbsp;
							</li>
							<li>
								Therefore, on a plain reading of this decision, it is held that Mr&nbsp;Nirav Gandhi has the proper authority to file the present&nbsp;petition, and the objection raised by the Corporate Debtor holds&nbsp;no water.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							NCLT held that therefore, the Petition made by the Operational Creditor is&nbsp;complete in all respects as required by law. It clearly shows that&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor is in default of a debt due and payable,&nbsp;and the default is more than minimum amount of one lakh&nbsp;rupees stipulated under p 4(1) of the IBC. Therefore, the&nbsp;default stands established and there is no reason to deny the&nbsp;admission of the Petition. In view of this, this Adjudicating&nbsp;Authority admits this Petition and orders initiation of CIRP&nbsp;against the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;
						</p>
