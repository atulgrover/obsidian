						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The present application is filed for the closure of the liquidation&nbsp;process.&nbsp;The e-auction is stated to be conducted on 19.11.2019&nbsp;and Shiv Shakti participated in the e-auction and successfully bid an amount of ₹18,45,86,646/- being 100% of the reserve&nbsp;price.&nbsp;Sale certificate is stated to be issued on 26.11.2019 and the&nbsp;proceeds distributed as per the provisions of Section 53 of the Code on&nbsp;26.11.2019.&nbsp;It is submitted that post distribution of funds in compliance of&nbsp;Section 53 of the Code, the applicant handed over the possession of all&nbsp;assets of K.T.C. Foods as a going concern to Shiv Shakti.
						</p>
						<p>
							Adjudicating Authority observes that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The present application is not filed for dissolution of&nbsp;K.T.C. Foods. It has been pleaded during the course of hearing that&nbsp;Regulation 45 of the Liquidation Process Regulations 2016 provides for&nbsp;closure of the liquidation process of the corporate debtor where the&nbsp;corporate debtor is sold as a going concern.&nbsp;We find that Regulation 45(3) of the Liquidation Process&nbsp;Regulations 2016 was amended by the IBBI(Liquidation Process)(Amendment) Regulations, 2019 (Liquidation&nbsp;Process Amendment Regulations 2019). The IBBI issued a&nbsp;Circular No. IBBI/LIQ/024/2019 dated 26.08.2019&nbsp;stating that the&nbsp;stakeholders have expressed a difficulty in applying the Liquidation Process&nbsp;Amendment Regulations 2019 to liquidation process, which commenced&nbsp;before 25.07.2019. It was further stated in the Circular that it is reiterated&nbsp;that the provisions of the Liquidation Process Amendment Regulations 2019&nbsp;are not applicable to the liquidation processes, which had commenced&nbsp;before coming into force of the said Liquidation Process Amendment&nbsp;Regulations 2019 and that they are applicable only to liquidation processes,&nbsp;which commenced on or after 25.07.2019.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the present case, the liquidation process commenced on&nbsp;31.05.2019 i.e. before the date of 25.07.2019. We find that before&nbsp;25.07.2019, Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Process Regulations inter alia&nbsp;provided for the Liquidator selling the corporate debtor as the going concern.&nbsp;However, there was no provision that in case a sale is made as a going&nbsp;concern, the purchaser can continue the business in the name of the&nbsp;concern which he has purchased as a going concern. This clarification is provided for in Regulation 45(3) of the Liquidation Process Regulations.&nbsp;Therefore, reading Section 54 of the Code with Regulation 32(e) of the&nbsp;Liquidation Process Regulations 2016, we consider it fit to direct closure of&nbsp;the liquidation process of K.T.C. Foods where it is being sold a going&nbsp;concern without directing dissolution of K.T.C. Foods.&nbsp;
							</li>
							<li>
								We have noted above that the then Resolution Professional filed&nbsp;application CA 510/2019 regarding identified transactions under Section 66,&nbsp;68 and 70 of the Code. The application is pending before this Bench. The&nbsp;issue involved is whether the liquidation process can be closed pending the&nbsp;avoidance application. During the course of the hearing, the learned senior&nbsp;counsel for the Liquidator has referred to Regulation 44 of the Liquidation&nbsp;Process Regulations 2016.
							</li>
							<li>
								It has also been submitted that as per Clause 9.3 of the e-auction process information document dated 05.11.2019, it is stated that &ldquo;the&nbsp;Liquidator has filed an application with Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Chandigarh Bench&nbsp;seeking directions on account of transactions identified by the auditor as per his audit report. Any amount recovered from the respondents upon Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;NCLT directions, will be distributed to the financial creditors only and will not&nbsp;form part of the assets to be taken over by the successful bidder.&rdquo; It is&nbsp;further submitted that as per Section 43, 44, 45 and 66 of the Code, the&nbsp;Liquidator or Resolution Professional has to pursue the application filed by&nbsp;the Resolution Professional/Liquidator and in the present case, the&nbsp;application is to be pursued by the Liquidator.
							</li>
							<li>
								We find that Regulation 44(1) of the Liquidation Process Regulations&nbsp;2016 (supra) is as substituted by the Liquidation Process Amendment&nbsp;Regulations 2019 dated 25.07.2019. It would not be in the interest of justice&nbsp;to keep the liquidation process of the corporate debtor ongoing, till the time&nbsp;the application for avoidance of transactions is finally decided, especially&nbsp;since KTC Foods is not being directed to be dissolved. We are, therefore, of&nbsp;the view that even though the amendment of Regulation 44(1) is through the&nbsp;Liquidation Process Amendment Regulations 2019, the same be applied in&nbsp;the present case and notwithstanding the pendency of the Application No.&nbsp;510/2019, the closure of liquidation process of the corporate debtor be&nbsp;directed. We direct accordingly. We further direct that as per the&nbsp;submissions made during the course of the hearing by the learned senior&nbsp;counsel for the Liquidator, the Liquidator diligently pursue the Application&nbsp;510/2019. We also direct that as per Clause 9.3 of the e-auction process&nbsp;information documents dated 05.11.2019, any amount recovered from the&nbsp;respondents in CA 510/2019 upon the directions of the Tribunal, be&nbsp;distributed to the financial creditors only and will not form part of the assets&nbsp;to be taken over by the successful bidder. We may clarify here that as per&nbsp;details in Form H, the financial creditor Oriental Bank of Commerce has&nbsp;been distributed only 13.17% of the amount admitted of ₹1,28,02,31,577.52.&nbsp;However, in case the amount recovered from the respondents in CA&nbsp;510/2019 is more than the amount still outstanding to Oriental Bank of&nbsp;Commerce, the amount recovered be distributed among the operational&nbsp;creditors (amount admitted ₹23,76,12,703/- and amount distributed zero).
							</li>
							<li>
								In the application, the Liquidator has prayed for approval of the&nbsp;sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern to Shiv Shakti the highest&nbsp;bidder without any liabilities including contingent liabilities and with immunity&nbsp;from existing litigation if any against the corporate debtor. Reference has&nbsp;been made to Regulation 45(3)(a) of the Liquidation Process Regulations&nbsp;2016. Reference is also made to Regulation 32A and 33(1) of the&nbsp;Liquidation Process Regulations 2016. However, none of these provisions&nbsp;provides for any approval by the Tribunal of the sale as a going concern to&nbsp;the highest bidder. Therefore, the directions sought for cannot be granted.&nbsp;On similar considerations, the prayers at &lsquo;c&rsquo; &amp; &lsquo;e&rsquo; sought for in the application&nbsp;cannot be granted. As regards prayer at &lsquo;d&rsquo;, the necessary action under the&nbsp;Companies Act, 2013 may be taken.
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of the above discussion, and in view of the provisions of&nbsp;Section 54 of the Code and Regulations 44 and 45 of the Liquidation&nbsp;Process Regulations 2016, we direct that since the corporate debtor K.T.C. Foods is sold as a going concern, the liquidation process of the corporate&nbsp;debtor K.T.C. Foods Private Limited be closed without dissolution of K.T.C&nbsp;Foods Private Limited and that Application 510/2019 for avoidance of&nbsp;transactions under Chapter III/ VI of Part II of the Code shall continue to be&nbsp;adjudicated upon by the Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;CA No. 1189/2019 is accordingly disposed. Copy of this order be&nbsp;sent to the Liquidator, Shiv Shakti and all the respondents in CA No.&nbsp;510/2019 at the latest addresses given in that CA.&nbsp;
							</li>
							<li>
								We have noted above that the application under Section 9 of the&nbsp;Code for initiation of CIRP in the case of K.T.C. Foods was filed by an&nbsp;operational creditor Ashok Kumar and Brothers. IA No. 132/2020 is filed by&nbsp;Ashok Kumar and Brothers under Section 60(5) of the Code for directions to&nbsp;Shiv Sakti to make payment to the applicant as per his claim as Shiv Shakti&nbsp;has acquired K.T.C. Foods as a going concern and also to take appropriate&nbsp;action against the Liquidator for not performing his duties as Resolution&nbsp;Professional and for wilfully disobeying the Rules and Regulations framed by&nbsp;IBBI.
							</li>
							<li>
								It has been prayed that appropriate action be taken against the&nbsp;Liquidator for not performing his duties as Resolution Professional and for&nbsp;wilfully disobeying the Rules and Regulations framed by IBBI in the interest&nbsp;of justice. The matter relates to IBBI and therefore, the application becomes&nbsp;infructuous on this ground.
							</li>
							<li>
								It is stated in the application that the applicant submitted his&nbsp;claim being an operational creditor before the Interim Resolution&nbsp;Professional alongwith an affidavit and documents required to furnish to&nbsp;prove his claim for an amount of ₹5,49,73,699/- which includes&nbsp;₹3,43,21,069/- due as principal amount and ₹2,06,52,630/- due as interest&nbsp;on the delayed payment as agreed by the corporate debtor. It is stated that&nbsp;respondent Shiv Shakti has purchased the corporate debtor as a going&nbsp;concern and therefore, Shiv Shakti has acquired all its assets and liabilities&nbsp;and when the business of the corporate debtor is being sold on going&nbsp;concern basis, then it has a presumption that sale will be with assets and liabilities. Directions have therefore, been sought against Shiv Shakti for&nbsp;making the payment of claim to the applicant.
							</li>
							<li>
								During the course of the hearing, the learned counsel for the applicant&nbsp;has relied on judgment dated 26.09.2000 of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court&nbsp;in Allahabad Bank Vs. ARC Holding Ltd. and others and CP(IB) No.&nbsp;182/KB/2017 dated 11.01.2018 of NCLT Kolkata Bench in the matter of&nbsp;Gujarat NRE Coke Limited. On a careful consideration, we find that the&nbsp;judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in Allahabad Bank Vs. ARC&nbsp;Holding Ltd. and others (supra) in a case relating to Official Liquidator&nbsp;appointed by the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court, was relied upon by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT&nbsp;Kolkata in CP (IB) No. 182/KB/2017 and it was directed that the Liquidator&nbsp;shall try to dispose of the corporate debtor company as a going concern after&nbsp;publication of notice in newspaper with the reserve price which shall be&nbsp;equal to the total debt amount including interest and maximum period&nbsp;applicable for trying the sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern will&nbsp;be only three months from the date of the order.
							</li>
							<li>
								We further find that the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata is&nbsp;with reference to Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Process Regulations 2016&nbsp;as it then stood, in which the prescribed manner of sale was that the&nbsp;Liquidator may (a) sell and asset on a stand alone basis; or (b) sell (i) the&nbsp;assets in a slump sale, (ii) a set of assets collectively, or (iii) the assets in&nbsp;parts (page 8 of the judgment). The judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata&nbsp;has reference to sale by the Liquidator of the assets in a slump sale. In the&nbsp;present case, the applicable Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Process&nbsp;Regulations 2016 inter alia provides for the Liquidator selling the assets in a&nbsp;slump sale but also provides for the Liquidator selling the corporate debtor&nbsp;as a going concern. The sale of the corporate debtor in CA 1189/2019 is&nbsp;stated to be a sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern. Therefore,&nbsp;CP (IB)/182/KB/2017 having reference to the Liquidator selling the assets in&nbsp;a slump sale would not be applicable.&nbsp;We find that in IA No. 1038/2019 in CP (IB) No.&nbsp;343/7/HDB/2018 in the matter of Bank of India Vs. Southern Online&nbsp;Biotechnologies Limited, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Hyderabad
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of the above finding, the prayer made in the application&nbsp;for directions to Respondent No. 2 to make the payment of claim to the&nbsp;applicant cannot be accepted.&nbsp;IA No. 132/2020 is rejected and accordingly disposed. Copy of&nbsp;this order be sent to the applicant as well as the respondents.&nbsp;Copies of the order be sent as directed above in CA 1189/2019&nbsp;and IA No. 132/2020.&nbsp;
							</li>
						</ul>
