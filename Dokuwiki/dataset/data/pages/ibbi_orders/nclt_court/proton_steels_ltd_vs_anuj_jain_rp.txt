	<h1>
		Treatment of interest levied as per MSMED Act, 2006 on arbitral award amount passed by Arbitral Tribunal in favour of Operational Creditor, in Resolution Plan under IBC &ndash; Proton Steels Ltd. v. Anuj Jain, RP of McNally Bharat Engineering Company Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 22, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, award is under challenged under Sec. 34 of Arbitration Act, RP has admitted the claim of award amount, however, the interest was not admitted as an application challenging the arbitral award was still pending before the Calcutta High Court. Therefore, the interest amount has been admitted as a contingent claim with a notional value of Rs.1/-. NCLT Kolkata Bench held that the amount claimed has been categorised as a contingent liability, pending the final decision in the matter of challenge. The acceptance of the principal as a contingent liability is therefore merely for the accounting purpose following the principle of Complete disclosure. By the very definition of the contingent liability, if the condition precedent for confirming the entry as an expense is fulfilled, a reverse entry is made to treat the liability as an expense, otherwise the same is reversed. Thus, making a mere accounting entry, which is not irreversible, of the principal claim as a liability does not mean the acceptance of the claim. Since the matter is receiving consideration of the Hon&rsquo;ble High court, it would be premature to make a confirmed entry towards the claim or reverse it, as the case may be.
	</p>

	<p>
		<strong>
			 Brief about the decision:
		</strong>
	</p>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Facts of the case
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			The Applicant had entered into a contract with the Corporate Debtor for the supply of steel casting, but the Corporate Debtor failed to pay the entire dues and hence the applicant became entitled to a sum of Rs.8,48,711.45/- pursuant to which the applicant filed an application before Micro, Small Enterprise Facilitation Council at Cuttack, Orissa (MSMEFC).
		</li>
		<li>
			Thereafter the matter was referred for arbitration and an award dated&nbsp;30.11.2011 was passed in favour of the applicant amounting to Rs.&nbsp;8,48,711.45/-.
		</li>
		<li>
			Again an application for modification of the said award was&nbsp;made by the Applicant wherein the applicable interest under the MSMED&nbsp;Act, 2006 on the principal sum of Rs.6,62,404.54/- was at three times the&nbsp;bank rate as notified by RBI as per the MSMED Act was to be paid from&nbsp;April 1,1997 to June 18, 2006.&nbsp;The said modification was done&nbsp;vide an order dated 13.09.2013.
		</li>
		<li>
			Corporate Debtor&nbsp;filed a writ against the said award before Orissa High Court but no order&nbsp;for stay was passed and the same was dismissed as withdrawn vide order&nbsp;dated 22.02.2023.
		</li>
		<li>
			Again on 18.07.2019, the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor filed an application under Section 34 of A &amp;&nbsp;C Act, 1996 before the Calcutta High Court to set aside the arbitral award.
		</li>
		<li>
			Before the execution proceedings pending before the DJ, Sundargarh at Orissa could be concluded, NCLT admitted the p 7 application against the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor vide order dated 29.04.2022.
		</li>
		<li>
			the applicant submitted its Proof&nbsp;of claim dated 18.05.2022 for a sum of Rs. 11,22,71,322.64/-. The RP had only admitted&nbsp;an amount of Rs.6,62,404.54/-.
		</li>
		<li>
			The applicant filed I.A 1011 of 2022 to direct the Respondent to given reasons for partially admitting the claim of the applicant but during the pendency of the said IA, the Respondent vide emails wrote to the applicant that as the amount of Rs.6,62,404.54/- was admitted in the books of accounts of&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and the interest was not admitted as an application challenging the arbitral award was still pending before the Calcutta High Court. Therefore, the interest amount has been admitted as a contingent claim with a notional value of Rs.1/-.
		</li>
		<li>
			The principal argument in the matter is that once the RP has admitted the principal claim of Rs.6,62,404.54/- which is a subject matter of an arbitral award under MSME Act, therefore, by operation of law, the applicant becomes entitled for claiming interest at a rate which is three times of the normal interest rate as also awarded in the arbitral award.
		</li>
		<li>
			It is also the contention of the applicant that the challenge to the arbitral award under p 34, is hopelessly time barred. Further for sustaining a challenge to the award given under the aegis of MSME act, the appellant is obliged to deposit 75% of the amount of the award with the court, which admittedly has not been done. Thus, when two necessary conditions for sustaining a challenge have not been fulfilled, there is no valid challenge.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Be that as it may, the factual situation as of today is that there is a challenge to the arbitral award under p 34 pending before Hon&rsquo;ble High Court. Therefore, it is evident that the award is yet to attain finality.
		</li>
		<li>
			In so far as the admittance of the principal amount under the arbitral award by RP is concerned, it is clear that the amount claimed has been categorised as a contingent liability, pending the final decision in the matter of challenge. The acceptance of the principal as a contingent liability is therefore merely for the accounting purpose following the principle of Complete disclosure. By the very definition of the contingent liability, if the condition precedent for confirming the entry as an expense is fulfilled, a reverse entry is made to treat the liability as an expense, otherwise the same is reversed.
		</li>
		<li>
			Thus, making a mere accounting entry, which is not irreversible, of the principal claim as a liability does not mean the acceptance of the claim. Since the matter is receiving consideration of the Hon&rsquo;ble High court, it would be premature to make a confirmed entry towards the claim or reverse it, as the case may be.
		</li>
		<li>
			In light of the foregoing reasons, we do not find any infirmity in the decision of the RP and therefore this IA is
			<strong>
				 rejected
			</strong>
			 .
			<strong>
				 (p8-9)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
