	<h1>
		Treatment of Assets/Fixed Deposits identified by Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) after implementation of Resolution Plan/Post approval of Resolution Plan and which were not included in Information Memorandum &ndash; SPS Steels Rolling Mills Ltd. Vs. Indian Overseas Bank and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 18, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, NCLT, Kolkata Bench approved the resolution plan and the SRA took over the management of the Corporate Debtor. Around March, 2020, the SRA came to know about certain FDRs which were issued before for commencement of CIRP lying in the name of the corporate debtor with a Bank. SRA requested that said FDRs may be liquated and the corresponding amount with commercial interest may be returned to the corporate debtor which is now under the management and control of the SRA. Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT, Kolkata Bench holds that: (i) The Corporate Debtor which is now under the control of SRA is not entitled to get the proceeds of the Fixed Deposits lying with the Bank. (ii) Bank have made full claim of their dues before Resolution Professional during CIRP without adjusting the Fixed deposits amounts lying with them and their claims were settled as per the Resolution plan. Therefore, even the Respondent Bank is not entitled to retain the balance amount and will have to refund it along with rate of Interest applicable to the FDR. (iii) Once the matter is be placed before the erstwhile members of the CoC of Corporate Debtor, and the distribution of this amount is decided by them in the CoC meeting which will include the respondent Bank as well.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This application has been preferred by the Successful Resolution Applicant of the Corporate Debtor (SRA) SPS Steels Rolling Mills Ltd.
						</li>
						<li>
							By an order dated 08.04. 2019 the NCLT, Kolkata Bench approved the resolution plan. Pursuant to the Resolution Plan, payments were made to the creditors, and the Applicant took over the management of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							Around March, 2020, the Successful Resolution Applicant came to know about certain Fixed Deposit Receipts (FDRs) which were issued before for commencement of CIRP lying in the name of the corporate debtor with the respondent Indian Overseas Bank.
						</li>
						<li>
							All such Bank guarantees have expired and hence Successful Resolution Applicant requested that said FDRs may be liquated and the corresponding amount with commercial interest may be returned to the corporate debtor which is now under the management and control of the SRA.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I.A. (IB) No. 501/KB/2022
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Successful Resolution Applicant was not provided with the details of the said FDRs by the Resolution Professional and consequently it was not in the information memorandum as &ldquo;assets&rdquo; of the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							When it was pointed out by the Bench that, had this FDR been included as assets in the information memorandum, the resolution plan value would have been higher to that extent and, consequently, the creditors of the Corporate Debtor would have been paid accordingly to which the Ld. Counsel for the Applicant agreed.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is not the case of the respondent Bank that they have not made full claim of their dues before Resolution professional to be placed before the CoC. In other words, the respondent Bank did not adjust the amount available with them in the form of Fixed Deposits against their dues while making their claim during CIRP.
							<strong>
								 (p21-22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							If the information memorandum contained the details of FDR, the resolution plan would have been enhanced and accordingly all the creditors of the Corporate Debtor would have been paid, on the basis of enhanced value.
							<strong>
								 (p23)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor which is now under the control of SRA is not entitled to get the proceeds of the Fixed Deposits lying with the Respondent Bank. In fact, whatever amount they have already received to the tune of Rs. 79,24,041/- will have to be refunded by them along with rate of Interest applicable to the FDR. Once the matter is be placed before the erstwhile members of the CoC of Corporate Debtor, and the distribution of this amount is decided by them in the CoC meeting which will include the respondent Bank as well, the amount so refunded will have to be distributed, as per the commercial wisdom of the Committee of Creditors.
							<strong>
								 (p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Respondent Bank have made full claim of their dues before Resolution Professional during CIRP without adjusting the Fixed deposits amounts lying with them and their claims were settled as per the Resolution plan. Therefore, even the Respondent Bank is not entitled to retain the balance amount and will have to refund it along with rate of Interest applicable to the FDR, once the matter is be placed before the erstwhile members of the CoC of Corporate Debtor, and the distribution of this amount is decided by them in the CoC meeting which will include the respondent Bank as well.
							<strong>
								 (p26)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above, this application being I.A. (IB) No. 501/KB /2022 filed by the applicant is dismissed with above directions.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I.A. (IB) No. 500/KB /2022
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The respondent bank has adjusted the Fixed Deposit against the dues payable by the Corporate Debtor as on 26.06.2019, i.e., after the implementation of the resolution plan of the applicant has approved by the Committee of Creditors, one of the members being the respondent bank itself.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Respondent bank has not only made full claims before the resolution professional but also adjusted the FDRs against the settlement of dues payable by the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p33)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Once a resolution plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority under Section 31(1) of the I&amp;B, Code the claims as provided in the approved resolution plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on all the stakeholders having interest. We rely upon the judgement passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Apex Court in
							<em>
								 Ghanashyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited v. Edelweiss
							</em>
							<em>
								 Asset Reconstruction Company Limited
							</em>
							 reported in&nbsp;(2021) 54 SC.
							<strong>
								 (p34)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of above finding, we direct the respondent bank to refund along with rate of interest applicable to the FDR and the refund may be made, once the matter is placed before the erstwhile member of the CoC of the Corporate Debtor and the distribution of this amount is decided by them in the CoC meeting which may include the Respondent, herein as well.
							<strong>
								 (p35)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of view of the above, we dismiss this application being I.A. (IB) No. 500/KB/2022.
							<strong>
								 (p36)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
