	<h1>
		The issue of fixing of EMD and its reasonableness in EOI is a matter which comes within the scope of Commercial Wisdom of the CoC and Adjudicating Authority has no jurisdiction to sit in appeal &ndash; Atharv Intertrade Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Shivadutt Bannanje, RP for Dnyanyogi Shri Shivakumar Swamji Sugars Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Bengaluru Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 30, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		This Application has been filed challenging the requirement of Rs.15 crore as EMD to be submitted along with EOI. The Applicant stated that as against the net worth criterion of Rs. 25 crore, the EMD of Rs.15 crore was exorbitant. NCLT Bengaluru Bench referred the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in Kanan Tiruvengadam v. M.K. Shah Exports Ltd. and Ors. (2018) 147 NCLAT and held that this issue of fixing of refundable EMD and its reasonableness is a matter which comes within the scope of &lsquo;Commercial Wisdom&rsquo; of the CoC.
	</p>
</div>

							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 758 NCLTCase Name:Atharv Intertrade Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Shivadutt Bannanje, RPCorporate Debtor:Dnyanyogi Shri Shivakumar Swamji Sugars Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:I.A 555 of 2023 in CP (IB) No.9/BB/2022Judgment Date:06-Oct-23Court/Bench:NCLT Bengaluru BenchMember (Judicial):Justice (Retd.) T. KrishnavalliMember (Technical):Shri Manoj Kumar Dubey
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case and contentions of the parties
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						This Application has been filed by Atharv Intertrade Pvt. Ltd. Limited, (Applicant), u/s. 60(5) of the IBC, 2016 r/w Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 against Resolution Professional(RP) of Corporate Debtor (Respondent), challenging the requirement of Rs.15 crore as Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) to be submitted along with Expression of Interest (EOI) as exorbitant , unreasonable and contrary to CIRP Regulations.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 Contentions of the Applicant:
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Fixing of Rs.15 crore as EMD for submission of EOI is unreasonable and lacks application of mind on the part of the COC.
						</li>
						<li>
							For this purpose, the Applicant has also referred to the fact that as against the net worth criterion of Rs.25 crore, the EMD of Rs.15 crore was exorbitant.
						</li>
						<li>
							In accordance with Section 25(2)(h) of the IBC, the criteria laid down with the approval of the CoC has to be with regard to the complexity and scale of operation of the business of the Corporate Debtor; and considering the scale of operation and financials of the Corporate Debtor including its tangible assets, the amount of Rs.15 crore as EMD was unreasonably high.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							 Contentions of the RP:
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This amount of Rs.15 crore as EMD was duly approved by the CoC in its 12th meeting held on 07.07.2023. During this meeting, the Chairperson of the CoC apprised the Members the provisions of Regulation 36A(4) of the IBBI CIRP Regulations, 2016; according to which the invitation for EOI shall not require payment of any fee or any nonrefundable deposit. Therefore, the Resolution was unanimously passed by the CoC for a refundable EMD of Rs.15 crore by the Prospective Resolution Applicant for participation in the CIRP.
						</li>
						<li>
							The requirement of Regulation 36A(4) has been fulfilled since the EMD is refundable.
						</li>
						<li>
							This is unanimously approved by the COC which is empowered as per Section 25(2)(h) as well as Regulation 36A(4).
						</li>
						<li>
							The forfeiture Clause would not make it violative of the Regulations, as the forfeiture was provided only for a specific set of circumstances.
						</li>
						<li>
							The RP has placed reliance on the judgment of
							<em>
								 Kanan Tiruvengadam v. M.K. Shah Exports Ltd. and Ors.&nbsp;(2018) 147 NCLAT
							</em>
							 in which, it has been observed that the question of eligibility criteria regrading requirement of minimum tangible net worth for one or other category of Resolution Applicants and other criteria are matter which can be dealt with by expert committee like Committee of Creditors, the Adjudicating Authority has no jurisdiction to sit in appeal over the decision of expert bodies relating to eligibility criteria till it is not shown that the same is perverse or against any of the provisions of I&amp;B Code or existing law.
						</li>
						<li>
							This matter of reasonability of the amount of EMD should be left to the wisdom of the CoC, as held by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the abovementioned judgment and therefore, this application is liable to be dismissed.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that having considered the abovementioned reasons including the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT&rsquo;s decision in
						<em>
							 Kanan Tiruvengadam v. M.K. Shah Exports Ltd. and Ors.&nbsp;(2018) 147 NCLAT
						</em>
						 , we are of the considered opinion that this issue of fixing of refundable EMD and its reasonableness is a matter which comes within the scope of &lsquo;Commercial Wisdom&rsquo; of the CoC. Hence, this Adjudicating Authority is not inclined to interfere with the decision of the CoC. Accordingly, I.A. No.555 of 2023 in C.P.(IB)No.09/BB/2022 is hereby
						<strong>
							 dismissed
						</strong>
						 .
						<strong>
							 (p12-13)
						</strong>
					</p>
