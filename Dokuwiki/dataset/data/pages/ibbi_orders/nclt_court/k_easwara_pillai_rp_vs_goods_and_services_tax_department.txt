	<h1>
		Search and Seizure of records of Corporate Debtor and issuance summons to Resolution Professional by GST Department during CIRP are violation of mortarium under p 14 of IBC, 2016 &ndash; K. Easwara Pillai &ndash; RP Mangomeadows Agricultural Pleasure Land Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Goods and Services Tax Department &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 31, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, GST Department after submitted its claim in CIRP, all of a sudden raided the premises of the Corporate Debtor after issued summon to Suspended Managing Director of the Corporate Debtor. The raid was conducted and seized all the account documents and also sent a summon to the Resolution Professional to appear for an inquiry. The question before NCLT Kochi Bench was whether the search and seizure of records of the corporate debtor and issuance of summons to applicant/resolution profession are violative of mortarium order passed under p 14 of IBC, 2016? Read here landmark decision of the NCLT Kochi Bench.
	</p>
</div>
<div>
	<div>
		<div>
			<div>
				<div>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 393 NCLTCase Name:K. Easwara Pillai RP Vs. Goods and Services Tax DepartmentCorporate Debtor:Mangomeadows Agricultural Pleasure Land Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA(IBC)/141/KOB/2023 IN CP(IBC)/06/KOB/2022Judgment Date:26-Jul-23Court/Bench:NCLT Kochi BenchMember (Judicial):Shri P. Mohan RajMember (Technical):Shri Satya Ranjan Prasad
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case and Submission
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In pursuance of the CIRP order, moratorium under p 14 of IBC 2016 is came into operation/effect from
							<strong>
								 25.01.2023
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							The Goods and Services Tax Department (GST Department/Respondent) after submitted its claim, all of a sudden on
							<strong>
								 10.03.2023,
							</strong>
							 raided the premises of the Corporate Debtor after issued summon dated
							<strong>
								 10.03.2023
							</strong>
							 to Suspended Managing Director of the Corporate Debtor in his presence and obtained his statement. The raid was conducted and seized all the account documents and also sent a summon dated 13.03.2023 to the Resolution Professional to appear for an inquiry on 20.03.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							The application is filed under p 60(5) of IBC, 2016 by the Resolution Professional for direction directing the&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;to release the assets (documents) which were seized on 10.03.2023 and to initiate proceedings against the&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;under p 70(2) of IBC, 2016.
						</li>
						<li>
							GST Department&nbsp;it submitted that the tax determination and tax assessment are not covered under moratorium. The recovery of tax alone prohibited by virtue of moratorium order passed under IBC. The summon were issued to erstwhile directors of corporate debtor to gather documents and to record their statements. There is no bar in the Act to issue summon to erstwhile directors of the corporate debtor. The corporate debtor is presently under the management of applicant hence summon was issued to him also to gather evidence and to record his statements to determine tax liability of corporate debtor under p 74 of GST Act. To arrest the leakage of revenue to the exchequer the search was conducted under p 67(2), the summons issued under p 70, for determination tax under p 74 of GST Act are unavoidable. The search and seizure of records made by&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;are not against the moratorium order of p 14 of IBC, 2016.&nbsp;
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue before the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether the search and seizure of records of the corporate debtor and issuance of summons to applicant/resolution professional are violative of mortarium order passed under p 14 of IBC, 2016?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The decision of the Adjudicating Authority is summarised in the following points:
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 a. Search and Seizure/issuance summons to Resolution Professional are violative of mortarium order passed under p 14 of IBC, 2016?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							There is no dispute regarding the facts of the case that&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;conducted raid in the premises of corporate debtor on 10.03.2023 during moratorium period and seized the documents in the presence suspended Board of director.
							<strong>
								 (p5)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							On&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;side justified the action that the acts of&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;come under the&nbsp;purview of part of assessment proceeding. Further submitted irrespective of&nbsp;mortarium the respondents are empowered to raid the premises of any person registered with under the GST, inclusive of corporate debtor under the management of resolution professional under p 67 of the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017. In support of his contention relies upon the following Apex court citations.
							<ul>
								<li>
									M/s Embassy Property Development Pvt Ltd vs State of Karnataka and Others.&nbsp;[2020] 12 SC
								</li>
								<li>
									Sundaresh Bhatt Liquidator of ABG shipyard vs Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs&nbsp;(2022) 103 SC
									<strong>
										 (p5)
									</strong>
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							In this case GST Department taken shelter that the raid conducted by&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;on 10.03.2023 is only part of proceeding to determine the tax due and it was not a proceeding to recover the dues.
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							&nbsp;The assessment is defined in Section 2(11) of the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017, and chapter XII of the said Act from ps 59 to 64 deals with the various kinds and ways of assessment.&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;under the guise of assessing or determining the dues, transgress and invoked the coercive provisions of law in Chapter XIV of the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017. In pursuance of search&nbsp;GST Department&nbsp;issued a summon to Resolution Professional under Section 70 the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017, to attend an inquiry to give evidence and to record statements. Section 70 (2) of the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017, runs as follow:
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;Every such inquiry referred to in sub-p (1) shall be deemed to be a &ldquo;judicial proceeding&rdquo; within the meaning of p 193 and p 228 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (Central Act 45 of 1860).&rdquo;
						</em>
						<strong>
							 (p10)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 It is made clear GST Department as an &ldquo;Authority&rdquo; under the Kerala State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017, initiated proceeding against the corporate debtor during the period of mortarium. This is prohibited under p 14(1)(a) of IBC 2016.(p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Karnataka High Court in
							<em>
								 Associate D&eacute;cor Ltd. Vs. Deputy Commissioner of Commercial Taxes&nbsp;(2021) 311 HC
							</em>
							 , after referring Apex court citations held that the notice issued under p 65 the State Goods and Services Tax Act 2017, R/w Rule 101(4) of KGST Rule, informing the discrepancies found in audit and asked&nbsp;the registered person to submit reply, is hit by mortarium order passed under&nbsp;p 14 of IBC 2016 and stayed/kept in abeyance, the proceedings pursuant to the said impugned notice till the lifting of moratorium.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Government of India, Ministry of Finance, finance Department also issued&nbsp;Circular No. 134/04/2020-GST&nbsp;under p 168(1) CGST Act, Annexure A1 of the petition explaining that no coercive action to be taken in respect of dues of GST pertaining to corporate debtor, under the CIRP. GST Department despite the supra guidance taken the coercive action.
							<strong>
								 (p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The acts of GST Department undermined the authority of Resolution professional and because of seizure of Books of accounts of the corporate debtor causes much inconvenience and paralyzed the Resolution process, the same shall be completed in time bound manner.(p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the circumstances it is concluded that
							<strong>
								 the search and seizure of records of the corporate debtor and issuance summons to applicant/resolution professional are violative of mortarium order passed under p 14 of IBC, 2016.(p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 b. Violation of moratorium order under p 74(2) of IBC, 2016 and impose a compensatory cost
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							On the Resolution Professional side prayed in the application to initiate proceeding against GST Department for violation of moratorium order under p 74(2) of IBC, 2016. This p is criminal in nature fall under Chapter VII under the heading Offences and Penalties, hence under p 236(1) of IBC, 2016 special court alone have jurisdiction. Further under p 236 (2) of IBC, 2016 cognizance of the offence can be taken only on the compliant of IBBI or Central Government.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence the Resolution Professional is granted liberty to approach the IBBI to proceed against the GST Department&rsquo;s erred officials in this regard.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above discussion we inclined to concede the prayer of the Resolution Professional and order the GST Department to return all the records seized from the premises of the corporate debtor and set aside the summon dated 13.03.2023 issued by GST Department and also impose a
							<strong>
								 compensatory cost of Rs.50,000/-
							</strong>
							 payable by the respondent to applicant towards CIRP cost, and respondent is granted liberty after paid the compensatory cost, to recover the said amount from the erred official(s).
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 c. Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the result:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							(i) The respondent is hereby directed to return all the documents seized from the premises of the corporate debtor to the applicant within a week from date of this order.
						</li>
						<li>
							(ii) The summon dated 13.03.2023 issued by the respondent to the corporate debtor is hereby set aside.
						</li>
						<li>
							(iii) The respondent is hereby directed to pay a compensatory cost of Rs.50,000/- (Rupees fifty thousand only) to the applicant towards the CIRP cost and
						</li>
						<li>
							(iv) Liberty is granted to the respondent, after paid the supra compensatory cost to recover it from the erred officials.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						The Registry is hereby directed to send e-mail copies of the order forthwith to all the parties and their counsel for information and for taking necessary steps.&nbsp;Let the certified copy of the order be issued upon compliance with requisite formalities.
						<strong>
							 (p17-19)
						</strong>
					</p>
