	<h1>
		Whether ex-Promoter/Director who resigned from Corporate Debtor before initiation of CIRP u/s 10 of IBC is eligible to submit a Resolution Plan under Sec. 29A of IBC &ndash; Vishram Narayan Panchpor RP of Blue Frog Media Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Committee of Creditors (Blue Frog Media Pvt. Ltd.) and Anr. &ndash; NCLAT New Delhi
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 16, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this landmark judgment, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT holds that: (i) Section 29A(c) is attracted when at the time of submission of the plan. (ii) Ineligibility is being held only on the ground that Successful Resolution Applicant was promoter of the Corporate Debtor till 2018 when he resigned. (iii) Section 29A does not make per se promoters and directors ineligible to submit a plan unless they are ineligible under clauses (a) to (g). Since in the present case, it is not the case that any of the clauses (a) to (g) are attracted on Successful Resolution Applicant, the mere fact that Successful Resolution Applicant was promoter and director shall not make him ineligible to submit a Resolution Plan. (iv) Set aside the order of NCLT Mumbai Bench.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Corporate Debtor, M/s. Blue Frog Media Pvt. Ltd. filed an Application under Section 10 of the Code which was admitted on
							<strong>
								 19.05.2021
							</strong>
							 by the Adjudicating Authority.
						</li>
						<li>
							A Resolution Plan was submitted by Mr. Mahesh Mathai, Ex-Director of the Corporate Debtor. Mr. Mahesh Mathai was one of the Directors in the Company who resigned on
							<strong>
								 01.03.2018
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							Committee of Creditors (CoC) on 08.11.2021 by 91.86% vote share has approved the Resolution Plan submitted by Successful Resolution Applicant(SRA), Respondent No.2.
						</li>
						<li>
							Resolution Professional filed an application under Section 30(6) of the Code seeking approval of the Resolution Plan.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Adjudicating Authority by impugned order, reported in&nbsp;(2023) 507 NCLT, rejected IA No. 2828 of 2021 holding that the Respondent No.2- Successful Resolution Applicant is not eligible under Section 29A.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether ex-promoter/directors are not eligible to submit a Resolution Plan under Section 29A if no disqualification is attached in any of the clauses under Section 29A.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Appellate Tribunal
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							A plain reading of Section 29A of IBC (inserted by Act 8 of 2018) indicates that a person shall not be eligible to submit a plan if such person, or any other person acting jointly or in concert with such person is covered by any of the clauses mentioned from (a) to (g).
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Section 29A(c) is attracted when at the time of submission of the plan, the person has an account or an account of a Corporate Debtor under the management or control of such person or of whom such person is a promoter, is classified as NPAs.
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Present is a case where as per submission of the Appellant, no bank is creditor of the Corporate Debtor. Form-H mentions the name of creditors of the Corporate Debtor and all creditors are only individual.
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Present is not a case even there is any suggestion or material that account of Successful Resolution Applicant or the Corporate Debtor is NPA on the date of submission of the Resolution Plan, therefore, there is no question of applicability of Section 29A(c).
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Present is not a case where it is pleaded or alleged that any of the clauses (a) to (g) are attracted with respect to Respondent No.2.
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court had occasion to consider Section 29A in reference to promoters of the Corporate Debtor in
							<em>
								 Hari Babu Thota
							</em>
							 (supra). In the above case, a plan proposed by the promoter was approved by the CoC and the application was filed by the Resolution Professional for approval of the plan which was dismissed on the ground that the promoters could not have presented the plan. In final appeal, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the above case has held that there is no per se disqualification under Section 29A. In the above case, MSME certificate was issued after commencement of the CIRP, hence, Section 240A was not relied by the Adjudicating Authority.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The present is not a case where any of the clauses of Section 29A are being pressed for ineligibility of Successful Resolution Applicant. Ineligibility is being held only on the ground that Successful Resolution Applicant was promoter of the Corporate Debtor till 2018 when he resigned. The view taken by the Adjudicating Authority is not as per the true and correct interpretation of Section 29A. Section 29A does not make per se promoters and directors ineligible to submit a plan unless they are ineligible under clauses (a) to (g). Since in the present case, it is not the case that any of the clauses (a) to (g) are attracted on Successful Resolution Applicant,
							<strong>
								 the mere fact that Successful Resolution Applicant was promoter and director shall not make him ineligible to submit a Resolution Plan.(p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Adjudicating Authority committed error in holding that the Respondent No.2/Successful Resolution Applicant is ineligible to submit a Resolution Plan. The rejection of IA No.2828 of 2021 is thus, unsustainable.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the foregoing discussions and our conclusions, we
							<strong>
								 set aside
							</strong>
							 the order of the Adjudicating Authority dated 18.08.2023 and revive IA No.2828 of 2021 in CP (IB) No.4360/MB/C-I/2018 before the Adjudicating Authority which may be heard and decided afresh in accordance with the law. Resolution Plan having been approved on 08.11.2021, the Adjudicating Authority shall endeavour to dispose of the application at an early date. The Appeal is disposed of accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p15-16)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
