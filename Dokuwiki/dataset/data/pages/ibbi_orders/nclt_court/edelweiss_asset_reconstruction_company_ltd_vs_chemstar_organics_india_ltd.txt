		<h1>
			Any extension of limitation by payment under Section 19 of the Limitation Act would ensure to the benefit of the Financial Creditor if the same is made within the prescribed period of limitation &ndash; Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited Vs. Chemstar Organics (India) Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 24, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that even if the documents produced by the Applicant are taken to be acknowledgment of liability, such documents cannot come to the aid of the applicant as the same are dated/issued beyond the expiration of prescribed period of limitation. In any case, documents/correspondence relied upon by the petitioner do not constitute any acknowledgment of debt under Section 18 of the limitation Act as the same do not meet the three essential ingredients of Section 18 of Limitation Act. Therefore, we believe that there is no acknowledgement of the debt by the respondent.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that the date&nbsp;of Default is in the month of May 2000 and the petition has been filed&nbsp;on 23.10.2019 i.e. 19 years 5 months after the date of default. It is a&nbsp;settled principle of limitation that the date of default triggers the period&nbsp;of limitation which is 3 years. It was also upheld by the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Supreme Court in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Jignesh Shah and another v. Union&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									of India and another&nbsp;[2019]  19 SC.
								</strong>
							</em>
							This petition is hit by&nbsp;limitation. It is admitted position that there was a huge round of&nbsp;litigation in this matter before different forums. However, the filing of&nbsp;any recovery proceedings (within limitation) does not extend the period&nbsp;of limitation for proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy&nbsp;Code, 2016 which has also been affirmed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme&nbsp;Court in the case of
							<em>
								<strong>
									&nbsp;Jignesh Shah Vs. Union of India.
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The petitioner has contended that the payments made on 07.01.2015,&nbsp;06.12.2018, 17.12.2018 &amp; 10.01.2019 would have the effect of extending&nbsp;the period of limitation under Section 19 of the Limitation Act. But we&nbsp;would like to clear here that any extension of limitation by payment under&nbsp;Section 19 of the Limitation Act would ensure to the benefit of the&nbsp;applicant if the same is made within the prescribed period of limitation.&nbsp;Even otherwise, let us examine the legality of the alleged payments relied&nbsp;upon by the petitioner in his books of accounts.
						</p>
						<p>
							There was no mention against the said payments in the books of accounts&nbsp;of the petitioner as to whether the respondent has paid the above&nbsp;amounts by way of cash, cheque or demand draft. The petitioner&nbsp;miserably failed to furnish the mode of the above payments despite&nbsp;questioning by this bench during the course of final argument. In this&nbsp;connection, it is appropriate to mention here that mere entries in the&nbsp;books of accounts of the petitioner are not enough to prove the payments&nbsp;as per Section 34 of the Indian Evidence Act more so when the petitioner&nbsp;is an ARC.&nbsp;Therefore, under these circumstances, the above payments relied&nbsp;upon by the petitioner cannot be said to be proved apart from they were&nbsp;not made within limitation even if they are considered to be true and&nbsp;valid.&nbsp;In the present case, the date of default has been pleaded as May&nbsp;2000. Therefore, the said payments are not within the prescribed period&nbsp;of limitation i.e. prior to May 2003. Hence the said alleged payments&nbsp;would not extend or renew any period of limitation.
						</p>
						<p>
							Further, we would also like to mention that the petitioner, in order to save&nbsp;itself from being hit by limitation has even failed to produce any&nbsp;acknowledgment of liability or prove any part payment within the&nbsp;prescribed period of limitation of 3 years as under Section 18 of the&nbsp;Limitation Act to save limitation under Article 137 of the Limitatioin Act.&nbsp;Under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, a document must meet the&nbsp;following ingredients to qualify as an acknowledgement in writing to&nbsp;extent the period of limitation:
						</p>
						<p>
							a. Acknowledgment must be made in writing;
							<br/>
							b. Acknowledgement must be made by person against whom claim is&nbsp;made;
							<br/>
							c. Acknowledgment must be made before expiration of limitation period;
						</p>
						<p>
							Here, we would like to refer to the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Supreme Court in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Sesh Nath Singh &amp; Anr. V.&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Baidyabati Sheoraphulu Co-operative Bank Ltd and Anr&nbsp;(2021)  49 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							&nbsp;wherein&nbsp;it has affirmed the above said ingredients and has held the following:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;66. Similarly under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, an&nbsp;acknowledgement of present subsisting liability, made in writing in&nbsp;respect of any right claimed by a party and signed by the party against&nbsp;whom the right is claimed, has the effect of commencing of a fresh period&nbsp;of limitation, from the date on which the acknowledgment is signed.&nbsp;However, the acknowledgment must be made before the period of&nbsp;limitation expires.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							In light of the aforesaid, it is observed that even if the documents&nbsp;produced by the Applicant are taken to be acknowledgment of liability,&nbsp;such documents cannot come to the aid of the applicant as the same are&nbsp;dated/issued beyond the expiration of prescribed period of limitation. In&nbsp;any case, documents/correspondence relied upon by the petitioner do&nbsp;not constitute any acknowledgment of debt under Section 18 of the&nbsp;limitation Act as the same do not meet the three essential ingredients of&nbsp;Section 18 of Limitation Act. Therefore, we believe that there is no&nbsp;acknowledgement of the debt by the respondent.
						</p>
						<p>
							Therefore, we believe that this petition is therefore, barred by&nbsp;limitation. On these grounds, the petition is found to be not maintainable&nbsp;and is liable to be dismissed
						</p>
