		<h1>
			Claim filing timeline is only directory and not mandatory, it cannot mean that the directory provision of the Code can be used to contravene or defeat the very objective of the Code itself &ndash; RD Fan Limited Vs. Soumitra Lahiri (RP of Vista Mining Private Limited) &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			May 31, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			While we appreciate the submission of the Applicant that the timeline is only directory and not mandatory, it cannot mean that the directory provision of the Code can be used to contravene or defeat the very objective of the Code itself. Condonation of delay cannot be had just for the asking but is a discretion to be exercised for good and sufficient reasons. Otherwise, the whole purpose of the substitution to regulation 12(2) of the CIRP Regulations would be rendered nugatory. Besides, while this Adjudicating Authority appreciates the submission of the Applicant that the timeline is only directory and not mandatory, it cannot mean that the directory provision of the Code be used to contravene the objective of the Code.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The CIRP against the corporate debtor in the present case had been initiated&nbsp;on 25.02.2020 and a public announcement concerning the same had been&nbsp;made by the IRP on 29.02.2020 fixing&nbsp;
							<strong>
								12.03.2020
							</strong>
							&nbsp;as the last date for&nbsp;submission of the claim.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Applicant first submitted that due to ill health and outbreak of Covid19 which resulted in lockdown being imposed from 25.03.2020, it failed to submit its claim with the IRP and then the RP. The Applicant later submitted that it was only on 02.11.2020 that it came to know about the&nbsp;CIRP of the corporate debtor. If it was only on 02.11.2020 that the&nbsp;Applicant came to know about the CIRP, a claim could definitely not have&nbsp;been filed by it before 02.11.2020, be the Applicant sick (no document&nbsp;supporting which has been filed) or the nation under lockdown (that had&nbsp;been lifted in parts since October 2020).Further, the Applicant came to know about the CIRP on 02.11.2020 but it&nbsp;filed its claim with the RP only on 24.12.2020, which is almost two months&nbsp;after it came to know about the CIRP. Nothing has been said in substantiation for this delay except the fact that the Government of West&nbsp;Bengal had decided to extend certain lockdown measures on 31.08.2020&nbsp;and 01.11.2020. However, it was only activities related to education and&nbsp;training institutions, cinema halls, entertainment parks, gathering and&nbsp;congregation that were prohibited by the Government of West Bengal. This&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority was very much functional through video conference&nbsp;and has been taking up matters and deciding them during the said period.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Decision of the Adjudicating Authority is summarised in following points
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								It is also pertinent to note that the CoC has already approved a Resolution&nbsp;Plan in the present matter and the same is pending consideration by this&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority. A Resolution Plan is based on the Information&nbsp;Memorandum prepared by the RP after receiving and compiling the claims&nbsp;received from all the creditors. If at this stage, the claim of this Applicant is&nbsp;allowed, the information memorandum will have to be changed and, in&nbsp;effect, the entire CIRP period will start again at the stage where it is about&nbsp;to end.
								<strong>
									(p30)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Further, allowing the Applicant&rsquo;s claim at such a belated stage, on grounds&nbsp;that seem specious at best, will not only be unfair to the other creditors who&nbsp;could not file their claim with the RP because of the delay but would also&nbsp;dilute the purpose of publication of Form A.
								<strong>
									(p31)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								We are all aware that the CIRP is a time-bound process and if the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority sets the clock back for the reason that the Applicant&nbsp;was not diligent enough in pursuing its claim, it would certainly go against&nbsp;the main objective of the Code.
								<strong>
									(p32)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is apposite to recall that the timeline for submission of claims was&nbsp;introduced vide Notification No.IBBI/2018-19/GN/REG031 dated&nbsp;03.07.2018 (w.e.f. 04.07.2018). Sub regulation (2) of regulation 12 before&nbsp;its substitution stood as follows-
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;12. A creditor, who failed to submit proof of claim within the time stipulated in the&nbsp;public announcement, may submit such proof to the interim resolution professional&nbsp;or the resolution professional, as the case may be, till the approval of a resolution plan&nbsp;by the committee.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							While we appreciate the submission of the Applicant that the timeline is&nbsp;only directory and not mandatory, it cannot mean that the directory&nbsp;provision of the Code can be used to contravene or defeat the very objective&nbsp;of the Code itself. Condonation of delay cannot be had just for the asking&nbsp;but is a discretion to be exercised for good and sufficient reasons. Otherwise,&nbsp;the whole purpose of the substitution to regulation 12(2) of the CIRP&nbsp;Regulations would be rendered nugatory.
							<strong>
								(p33)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								It is also noted that the Applicant submitted that on 16.12.2020, the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority had excluded 153 days and extended the CIRP&nbsp;period by a further ninety days and that the consideration of the Applicant&rsquo;s&nbsp;claim would not affect the CIRP. We, however, do not agree with this&nbsp;submission, since the extended period is also nearing the end. Moreover,&nbsp;the possibility of other creditors approaching this Adjudicating Authority&nbsp;for their claims to be allowed on the same ground cannot be denied; this&nbsp;would turn an otherwise time-bound process into a never-ending process.
								<strong>
									(p34)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Besides, while this Adjudicating Authority appreciates the submission of the&nbsp;Applicant that the timeline is only directory and not mandatory, it cannot&nbsp;mean that the directory provision of the Code be used to contravene the&nbsp;objective of the Code.&nbsp;In the light of the aforementioned discussion, we are not inclined towards&nbsp;granting the prayer of the Applicant.
								<strong>
									(p35-36)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
