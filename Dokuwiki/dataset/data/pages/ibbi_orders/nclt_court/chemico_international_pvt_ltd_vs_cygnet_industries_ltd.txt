	<h1>
		Whether mentioning of interest component in invoices and subsequent action by Corporate Debtor on these invoices would mean a novation of Contract and interest be combined with the principal debt to meet the threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore limit for filing a petition under Section 9 of IBC? &ndash; Chemico International Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Cygnet Industries Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 2, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench holds that: (i) Since neither the Operational creditor has claimed any discreet amount towards the purported interest in the invoices, nor the CD acknowledged the provision thereof by its conduct or otherwise. Hence mere mentioning of a condition as a footnote without any causative or remedial ramifications can be considered to be a part of the agreement. (ii) The Purchase Order clearly states that if there are any special conditions given by either side it has to be accepted in writing by the buyer (in this case the Corporate Debtor). Since the Purchase order was first accepted by the Operational Creditor, they agreed to this clause by sending the required goods. So, if they eventually gave any counter offer in their Tax Invoices, they too had to get it accepted clearly in writing by the Corporate Debtor. Since there was neither any exclusive writing about the 21% p. a interest which was accepted by the Corporate Debtor there is no question of Counter Offer.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This is a Company Petition filed under p 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016&nbsp;by&nbsp;Chemico International Pvt. Ltd. (Operational Creditor) seeking to initiate CIRP against Cygnet Industries Ltd. (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
						<li>
							Part-IV of the petition contains details of operational debt for an amount of Rs.1,10,98,763.08,/-which includes Rs. 87,74,191.08/- as Principal Amount along with 21% Interest per annum. Interest calculated till 31 August 2022 is Rs. 23,24,572/- as on 28.05.2021.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the instant case no agreement demonstrating that interest is payable&nbsp;has been presented by the Operational Creditor except a mention on the&nbsp;invoices that a 21% interest shall be charged on delay payments.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Can interest be combined with the principal debt to meet the threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore limit for filing a petition under Section 9 of the Code?
						</li>
						<li>
							Whether mentioning of the interest component in the invoices and subsequent action by the Corporate debtor on these invoices would mean a novation of Contract?
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observes that the imposition of 21% interest condition is solely a unilateral condition on the invoice, with no agreement or commitment from the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;It is evident that without combining this interest with the principal&nbsp;amount, the threshold under Section 4 of the Code is not met.
						<strong>
							 (p30-31)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						And refers:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Gandhar Oil Refinery (India) Limited v. City Oil Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 780 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Steel India v. Theme Developers Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2020] 192 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Pavan Enterprises v. Gammon India&nbsp;[2018] 46 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							SS Polymers vs. Kanodia Technoplast Ltd.&nbsp;[2019] 193 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Jyothi Limited vs. Boving Fouress Ltd.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In view of the provision of the Contract act, a contract could be construed to also have been established based on the conduct of the parties. In the instant case it is clearly seen that stated the Corporate&nbsp;debtor has never taken cognizance of the note on the invoices neither has&nbsp;the operational creditor added the interest figures, albeit unilateral to the&nbsp;claim amount in the invoice. Therefore, by the conduct of the CD, it is&nbsp;clear that it had not accepted the interest component which therefore does&nbsp;not give rise to a proper conduct which is essential for a novation. A&nbsp;proper conduct necessarily means that any changes in the contract has to&nbsp;be communicated directly to the other party. This is clearly not the case.
							<strong>
								 (p37)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Since neither the Operational creditor has claimed&nbsp;any discreet amount towards the purported interest in the invoices, nor&nbsp;the CD acknowledged the provision thereof by its conduct or otherwise.&nbsp;Hence mere mentioning of a condition as a footnote without any causative&nbsp;or remedial ramifications can be considered to be a part of the agreement.
							<strong>
								 (p39)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The main point of difference thus lies with the act of the parties. The Operational Creditor claims that the Tax invoices would gain precedence over the Purchase orders as it is construed as a counter offer and the Corporate Debtor accepted the offer when the CD paid the other tax invoices and accepts and leaves a company stamp on the same. On the other hand, this Bench finds that the Corporate Debtor issues Purchase orders which clearly states the General Clauses of Contract.
							<strong>
								 (p40)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Purchase Order clearly states that if there are&nbsp;any special conditions given by either side it has to be accepted in writing&nbsp;by the buyer (in this case the Corporate Debtor). Since the Purchase&nbsp;order was first accepted by the Operational Creditor, they agreed to this&nbsp;clause by sending the required goods. So, if they eventually gave any&nbsp;counter offer in their Tax Invoices, they too had to get it accepted clearly&nbsp;in writing by the Corporate Debtor. Since there was neither any exclusive&nbsp;writing about the 21% p. a interest which was accepted by the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor there is no question of Counter Offer.
							<strong>
								 (p40)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the light of the above precedents the amount claimed in the petition does not meet the threshold amount mentioned in Section 4 of the IBC and accordingly CP (IB) No. 307/KB/2022 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p41-42)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
