	<h1>
		An Insolvency Professional who is engaged to perform his/her duty as a Resolution Professional is entitled to be remunerated by way of paying fees for his labour and work and accordingly the fees and expenses are to be approved by the CoC &ndash; Mr. Sanjeev Jhunjhunwala, RP of Goouksheer Farm Fresh Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Bimal Kanti Choudhury, RP of PM Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		September 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) In our view that an Insolvency Professional who is engaged to perform his/her duty as a Resolution Professional is entitled to be remunerated by way of paying fees for his labour and work and accordingly the fees and expenses are to be approved by the CoC. (ii) Referred, the judgment passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in Jayesh N. Sanghrajka, R.P. (2021) 436 NCLAT and Mr. Mukesh Verma (2022) 948 NCLT. That it would not be prudent on our part to reduce the fees already fixed and approved at the meeting of the CoC. (iii) In regard to the fees paid to the Advocates and/or Counsels and /or Professionals appointed by the RP for defending himself, whether the same can be construed as &ldquo;directly related to acts done or expenses incurred which are necessary for the CIRP&rdquo; should be decided by the COC, and being its commercial wisdom, we do not intend to interfere with the same.
	</p>

							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 567 NCLTCase Name:Mr. Sanjeev Jhunjhunwala, RP of Goouksheer Farm Fresh Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Bimal Kanti Choudhury, RP of PM Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd.Corporate Debtor:Goouksheer Farm Fresh Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:I.A. (IB) No. 83/KB/2023 In C.P. (IB) No. 1582/KB/2019Judgment Date:01-Sep-23Court/Bench:NCLT Kolkata BenchMember (Judicial):Ms.&nbsp;Bidisha BanerjeeMember (Technical):Shri Balraj Joshi
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case and Contentions of the parties
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This instant Applicant is filed under Section 60(5) of the IBC read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 by Mr. Sanjeev Jhunjhunwala, Resolution Professional of Goouksheer Farm Fresh Pvt. Ltd., (Applicant), against Mr. Bimal Kanti Choudhury, Resolution Professional of PM Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. (CIRP), (Respondent No. 1) and against P.M. Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. (CIRP), (Respondent No. 2), seeking the following directions by an order passed by the Adjudicating Authority, as:
							<ul>
								<li>
									a) Necessary directions upon the Respondent to immediately pay the CIRP cost incurred, as mentioned in Annexure L;
								</li>
								<li>
									b) An Order be passed appointing another person as the Resolution Professional for the Corporate Debtor and discharging the Applicant his duties as the Resolution Professional;
								</li>
								<li>
									c) Such other order/orders as this Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal may deem fit and proper;
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant submits that on 8th January, 2020, in the First Meeting of the Committee of Creditors (COC), the COC was pleased to appoint the Applicant as the Resolution Professional (RP) of the Corporate Debtor and finalize the fees of the RP to be Rs. 50,000/- per month. Copy of the Minutes of the Meeting dated January 8, 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Respondent was the sole member of the COC. The Respondent had promised to pay of Rs. Two Lakh towards CIRP expenses and had resolved that RP should obtain OD against the fixed deposit maintained with HDFC Bank. Respondent failed to pay the committed amount and OD could not be obtained due the reason the lien was remarked by the sales tax department.&nbsp;It is contended further that the CD does not have any line of operation and&nbsp;does not have any material asset except for certain fixed deposits.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is contended further that, a complaint was filed against the Applicant with&nbsp;IBBI and after examining all the records placed before IBBI, the complained was dismissed vide an order dated 05/01/2021.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is contended that thereafter, in the 8th Meeting of the COC held on 26th September, 2022, the Applicant had once again placed the detail of expenses incurred by him and the outstanding dues. Such expenses were inter alia incurred for statutory compliances and are required to be reimbursed as per the provisions of I&amp;B Code, 2016. The Applicant states the sum of Rs. 20,51,168/- for conducting the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor which is incurred after the liquidation application was filed in July 2020 and 7th COC meeting was held, till date is pending.
						</li>
						<li>
							Also, it is stated that the RP has made the payment of Rs. 2,14,568/- from his own fund. However, the COC has not yet provided the means of further payments even in the 8th COC meeting. The applicant has also not received any fees for more than two years since August 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is further submitted that the Applicant is not ready and willing to continue&nbsp;as the Resolution Professional of the Corporate Debtor any further.&nbsp;The Applicant also submits that it is also necessary that an order is passed&nbsp;appointing another person as the Resolution Professional of the Corporate Debtor and hence discharging the Applicant from his duties as the Resolution Professional.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Order in
							<em>
								 P.M. Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Goouksheer Farm Fresh Pvt. Ltd.
							</em>
							<em>
								 (2022) 705 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT directed exemplifies that the conduct of Resolution Professional was questionable and it was directed to be investigated. However, no records of any subsequent investigation are initiated and taken up by the IBBI except issuing a notice by email dated November 07, 2022 to the Applicant. The Applicant has given his reply to the notice issued by the IBBI by email dated November 18, 2022.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is evident that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT while setting aside the order passed by this Adjudicating Authority has not directed the Resolution Professional to be penalized by the COC by withdrawing his due payments and CIRP Costs including costs incurred by the RP to defend himself in the prosecution initiated by the COC itself. However, the fact remains that the observation of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT as above stares at us.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In our view that an Insolvency Professional who is engaged to perform his/her duty as a Resolution Professional is entitled to be remunerated by way of paying fees for his labour and work and accordingly the fees and expenses are to be approved by the COC.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is evident from the Minutes of the First Meeting of Committee of Creditors of Corporate Debtor held on Wednesday, the 8th Day of January, 2020 at Kolkata 700 013, that the COC proposed and approved the fees of RP.
						</li>
						<li>
							We would rely upon the judgment passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
							<em>
								 Jayesh N. Sanghrajka, R.P. of Aristo Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. The Monitoring Agency nominated by the Committee of Creditors of Aristo Developers Pvt. Ltd.
							</em>
							 reported in&nbsp;(2021) 436 NCLAT. We would further rely upon the order passed by the Learned NCLT, Mumbai Bench in the matter of: Mr. Mukesh Verma, for SAB Global Entertainment Media Private Limited
							<em>
								 (2022) 948 NCLT.
							</em>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, in our considered opinion, it would not be prudent on our part to reduce the fees already fixed and approved at the First meeting of the COC on January 08, 2020. Hence, we do not propose to reduce the fees that was approved by the COC in its First Meeting, dated January 08, 2020.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, in regard to the
							<strong>
								 fees paid to the Advocates and/or Counsels and /or
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Professionals appointed by the RP for defending himself, whether the same can be construed as &ldquo;directly related to acts done or expenses incurred which are necessary for the CIRP&rdquo; should be decided by the COC, and being its commercial wisdom , we do not intend to interfere with the same. Thus, we leave it upon the COC to take a holistic view of the matter and address the issue at hand pragmatically.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, considering all the facts and circumstances abovementioned, we direct the Committee of Creditors to take necessary action in regard to reimbursing all the fees due including the CIRP costs and Professionals&rsquo; fees incurred by the Applicant herein, the Resolution Professional for the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, we also direct the Committee of Creditors to take proper action immediately towards release and discharge the RP from his duty as Resolution Professional, and propose new name for the Resolution Professional for Corporate Debtor for continuing the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP).
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, in view of the direction vide an Order dated 14.09.2022 passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 615 of 2020 that &ldquo;the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India to investigate this matter further regarding the conduct of RP and take necessary action under the regulations&rdquo;, the IBBI shall continue and conclude the investigation against the Applicant (RP) regarding the conduct of RP in this matter and take appropriate action under the regulation, if not already done.
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, the present Application, being, I.A. (IB) No. 83/KB/2023 in C.P. (IB) No. 1582/KB/2019 is disposed of, in terms of the order above.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, it has come to the fore that the Applicant has filed and interlocutory&nbsp;application, being I.A. (IB) No. 930/KB/2023 in C.P. (IB) No. 1582/KB/2019 by praying similar relief as sought for in the present I.A., as:
						</li>
						<li>
							<ul>
								<li>
									(i) Directions of the Respondents for payment of costs incurred by the applicant in accordance with their voting share on account of the corporate insolvency resolution process for a sum of Rs. 11,67,518/- as stated;
								</li>
								<li>
									(ii) Direction to provide all future expenses including Legal Expenses, Statutory Audit and the expenses incurred for ROC, WB compliances as and when incurred in proportion to voting share till al the applications are being disposed;
								</li>
								<li>
									(iii) Costs of this I.A.;
								</li>
								<li>
									(iv) Any other order that this Tribunal deem fit.
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>

(IB) No. 930/KB/2023 be listed for disposal on 05/ 09/ 2023. Urgent Certified copy of the order may be issued, if applied for, upon compliance with all the requisite formalities.
