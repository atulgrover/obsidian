	<h1>
		Can a single person CoC approve Resolution Plan? &ndash; Jaykay Enterprises Ltd. Vs. National Oil Company Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 9, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) The right regarding the land has not been defined, and it is not under our jurisdiction to decide upon the matter. (ii) The Resolution Professional is in no fault at not providing the information provided in the Information Memorandum to the application as the Applicant is not a member of the Committee of Creditors or a prospective Resolution Applicant, hence it is not entitled to the Information Memorandum. (iii) The word &ldquo;Committee&rdquo; has not been defined in the Part 1 or Part 2 of the Code which deals with Insolvency Resolution and Liquidation for Corporate Persons. (iv) There can be no meeting when a single person represents CoC (since other persons who attended are only invitees in this case and they don&rsquo;t form part of CoC) and consequently, &ldquo;meeting&rdquo; cannot been held as contemplated under Section 24 of the Code. (v) There can be no two view that stake holders in a CIRP process is not restricted only to financial and operational creditors. (vi) A decision to approve the resolution plan by the CoC should be in a validly convened and conducted CoC meeting. (vii) The resolution plan is sent back to the CoC. (viii) Once the Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority, then it will no more be a confidential document. (ix) The Workers&rsquo; Union have no locus standi to question the assignment of a debt by Bank.
	</p>
</div>

							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 683 NCLTCase Name:Jaykay Enterprises Ltd. Vs. National Oil Company Ltd.Corporate Debtor:Rishra Steel Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:C.A . No. 39/KB/2022 And I.A. (CA) No. 125/KB/2022 And I.A. No. 85/KB/2023 And I.A. (CA) No. 165/KB/2023 And IVN.P No. 10/KB/2023 in TP No. 4/KB/2022Judgment Date:05-Oct-23Court/Bench:NCLT Kolkata BenchMember (Judicial):Ms.&nbsp;Bidisha BanerjeeMember (Technical):Shri Arvind Devanathan
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 C.A . No. 39/KB/2022
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The C.A. has been filed by Jaykay Enterprises Ltd. seeking to implead itself as a party to the transfer petition and praying for leave to inspect all records, paper and proceeding of the transferred Company Petition No. 519 of 1989 and to obtain certified copies of relevant records, papers, documents and orders of the proceedings.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The present I.A. has been filed to allow the Applicant to intervene in the main T.P. in order to inspect the records and orders of the Corporate Debtor. The Applicant claims that the property situated at Rishra is the property of the Applicant as the mutation has carried out in the name of the Applicant.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The right regarding the land has not been defined, and it is not under our jurisdiction to decide upon the matter.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is prudent to allow only a person who can assist the Court to intervene in a case, not one who seeks an opportunity to look into the details for their own benefit.
						</li>
						<li>
							We cannot go into the nitty gritty of who the land belongs to as the matter is disputed and has not been settled by a civil court. This shadows the reason given by the Applicant to intervene in the matter.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above circumstances, C.A. No. 39/KB/2022 is hereby dismissed.
							<strong>
								 (p6-11)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I.A. (CA) No. 125/KB/2022
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Jaykay Enterprises Limited has filed the I.A. praying for the following reliefs:
					</p>
					<p>
						a. An order be made requiring the Respondent to provide a copy of the Information Memorandum to the Applicant;
					</p>
					<p>
						b. Leave be given to the Applicant to inspect the Information Memorandum for tackling the issue of the Applicant concerning inclusion of Lands at Rishra in Information Memorandum;
					</p>
					<p>
						c. Such further or other order or orders be made and/or direction or directions be given as to this Hon&rsquo;ble Court may seem fit and proper.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Information Memorandum has to include all the assets and liabilities including contingent liabilities of the Corporate Debtor and other details as given in regulation 36 of the CIRP Regulations, 2016 and p 29 lays down the method the information memorandum is to be prepared.
						</li>
						<li>
							Regulation 36 (4) of the CIRP Regulations, 2016 envisages that the Resolution Professional shall share the information memorandum only after taking&nbsp;an undertaking from the member of the committee and the Resolution&nbsp;Applicant and confidentiality is to be maintain with respect to the information provided in the Information Memorandum.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The Resolution Professional is in no fault at not providing the information provided in the Information Memorandum to the Applicant.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The Applicant is not a member of the Committee of Creditors or a prospective Resolution Applicant, hence it is not entitled to the Information Memorandum.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above observations, I.A. (CA) No. 125/KB/2022 is hereby rejected and disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p16-20)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I.A. No. 85/KB/2023
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This is an application filed by the Resolution Professional of Rishra Steel Ltd. seeking approval of Resolution Plan by Rishra Steel Ltd. by invoking provisions of Section 30 (6) of the IBC read with Regulation 39 (4) of the CIRP Regulations.&nbsp;
					</p>
					<p>
						Before examining the resolution plan submitted by the applicant in terms of Section 31 read with Section 30 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 along with Regulation 37, 38 and 39 of the CIRP Regulations, we are of the view that certain facts need to be brought on record for proper appreciation of the application seeking approval of the resolution plan.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether a single person CoC can be formed to take decision in such meetings in the context of Code.
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the given case the Committee of Creditors consists of only one Financial Creditor, who is assignee by virtue of assignment of debt by Allahabad Bank in (assigner) in favour of Deccan Traders Private Limtied (assignee). Therefore, it is literally a one-man Committee of Creditors, as others who can attend the meeting are persons who are entitled to attend i..e. the Suspended Members Board of Directors, Operational Creditors etc. who are merely invitees to the meeting and they have no right to vote on such meeting. Therefore, to take any decision in the Committee of Creditors meeting is left to a single Financial Creditor in this case, as he has 100% voting rights.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The word &ldquo;Committee&rdquo; has not been defined in the Part 1 or Part 2 of the Code which deals with Insolvency Resolution and Liquidation for Corporate Persons.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							With regard to constitution of Committee of Creditors, provisions have been made in Section 21(2) of the Code with provides that Committee of Creditors shall comprise all Financial Creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							However, in the CIRP Regulations it has been provided in Regulation 16 of the CIRP Regulations that in case the Corporate Debtor has no financial debt or Financial Creditors are related parties of the Corporate Debtor. The CoC can include Operational Creditors, one representative of the workman, and one representatives of the employee as provided in 16(2) of the said regulation.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The Code contemplates meeting of CoC for the purpose of taking all
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 decisions with regard to insolvency resolution of the corporate debtor.
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 There can be no meeting when a single person represents CoC (since
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 other persons who attended are only invitees in this case and they don&rsquo;t
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 form part of CoC) and consequently, &ldquo;meeting&rdquo; cannot been held as
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 contemplated under Section 24 of the Code.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Be that as it may, even assuming that in the absence of specific provision to deal with this kind of situation in the Code and Constitution of Committee of Creditors with one person and approval of the resolution plan by same person in the so-called &ldquo;meeting&rdquo; of the Committee of Creditors to be accepted, we have to examine the facts and circumstances under which the approval of the resolution plan has been sought by the applicant.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Average Fair Value submitted by two registered valuers is Rs. 74.26 crore and the Average Liquidation Value is Rs. 46.15 crore.
						</li>
						<li>
							From the valuation reports, it is evident that the value submitted by the registered valuers is only on the basis of the value of land and buildings and the fixed of deposit held in the name of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the given case, even the average liquidation value of the company is&nbsp;Rs.46.15 Cr (leave alone the average fair value which is Rs.74.26 Cr) is much more than the resolution plan value which is Rs.30.25 Cr.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 There can be no two view that stake holders in a CIRP process is not restricted only to financial and operational creditors.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The main objective of the Code has mentioned in the preamble to the Code, is to consolidate and amend the laws relating to re-organisation and Insolvency Resolution of Corporate Persons, partnership firms, individuals, in a time bound manner for maximation of value of assets of such persons, to promote entrepreneurship, availability of credit and balance the interest of all the stakeholders including alteration of the priority of payment of Government dues.
						</li>
						<li>
							While the commercial wisdom of the CoC is not questioned by the Adjudicating Authority by respectfully following several judgements of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT, NCLAT and Supreme Court, we are of the view that
							<strong>
								 a decision to approve the resolution plan by the CoC should be in a validly convened and conducted CoC meeting.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In light of above, we examined to see whether the stakeholders who were to be given notice as per Section 24 of the Code which deals with Meeting of Committee of Creditors have been given or not
						</li>
						<li>
							As per Section 24(3)(b) of the Code, notice is to be given the members&nbsp;of the suspended Board of Directors who represents the shareholders of the Corporate Debtor. Once the company is put back into CIRP instead of liquidation, in our view the erstwhile directors should be treated on par with suspended Board and notice ought to have been given to them in terms of Section 24(3)(b) of the Code following &ldquo;principle of fairness&rdquo;, as their stakes are very much involved in the current case.
						</li>
						<li>
							Though the suspended board of directors are not entitled to vote, they should be given opportunity to understand the &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo; of the CoC for handing over their company to the successful resolution applicant for a value far less then the liquidation value.
						</li>
						<li>
							On perusing all the minutes of meetings of the CoC held from 15 August,&nbsp;2022 to 16 May, 2023. We find that
							<strong>
								 the Members of Suspended Board of Directors have not been served notice as provided in Section 24(3)(b) of the Code and consequently they were never present in any of the CoC meetings.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In light of above, the resolution plan is sent back to the CoC with the direction to the Resolution Professional to serve notice on the Members of the Suspended Board of Directors/erstwhile Directors of the Corporate Debtor who represents the shareholders and conduct meeting to re-consider the plan for approval by CoC.
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						In view of the above observations. I.A. No. 85/KB/2023 in T.P. No. 04/KB/2022 is hereby disposed.
						<strong>
							 (p33-49)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I.A. (CA) No. 165/KB/2023
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Ld. Counsel for the Applicant contends that the Applicant is the State of West Bengal through its Land &amp; Land Reforms and Refugee Relief &amp; Rehabilitation Department. The Applicant claims that they are the owner of the land in Hooghly District which is presently possessed by the CD and the said land in question belongs to the State of W.B. and J.K. Steel Limited, which was later renamed as &ldquo;Rishra Steel Ltd.&rdquo; was only allowed to retain the said land for the purpose of running the mill in accordance with the provisions of the said law. The Ld. Counsel for the Applicant further contends that the Applicant vide a letter dated 12.09.2023 has requested the Respondent (RP) to give the Applicant a copy of the Resolution Plan, which is filed for final approval before this Tribunal, in order to ascertain how the said land, which belongs to the State of W.B., has been dealt with in such Resolution Plan. However, no response from the Respondent was received by the Applicant till date.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is evident that the Land and Land Revenue Department (Land Reforms Branch) of the Government of West Bengal vide Order dated April 06, 1962 (No. 4416-L Ref) declared that the lands measuring more or less 37.56 acres comprised in the mill held by M/s J.K. Steel Limited in
							<br />
							 mouza in Rishra and Kannagar, District-Hooghly, vested in the State Government free from encumbrances under the provisions of West Bengal Estates Acquisition Act, 1953 (West Bengal Act I of 1954) and also further declared that the said land being required for the purpose of the said mill may be retained by the said mill in accordance with the provisions of law.
						</li>
						<li>
							We are of the view that application made by the applicant is only as apprehension that pursuant to the Resolution Plan, there could be &ldquo;transfer&rdquo; of the said land to some other corporate. This apprehension in our view is mis-conceived. As long as, the Corporate Debtor exist and the land is proposed to be retained by the said Corporate Debtor, there is no transfer&rdquo; to attract levy by the Land and Revenue Department of the said West Bengal.
						</li>
						<li>
							We rely upon the judgment passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Himachal Pradesh in the case of
							<strong>
								<em>
									 Groupe SEB India Pvt. Ltd. and Another v. State of Himachal Pradesh [CWP No. 3569 of 2021] reported in (2023) 653 HC
								</em>
							</strong>
							 , where the question raised that:
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;Whether the change of name of a company with the approval of Registrar of Companies, would amount to transfer of assets of the company attracting imposition of stamp duty and registration fee?&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 The Hon&rsquo;ble High Court held at para 22 that: &ldquo;&hellip;change in name with approval of Registrar of Companies and issuance of Certificate of Incorporation by Registrar of Companies under Companies Act, 2013 does not mean transfer of land&hellip;.&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						(Emphasis Added)
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Further, we rely upon the decision passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, Delhi in the case of
							<em>
								 Association of Aggrieved Workmen of Jet Airways (India) Limited v. Jet Airways (India) Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 66 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , at para 25, that:
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						&ldquo;
						<em>
							 The above scheme of the Code also indicates that after Resolution Plan is submitted to the Adjudicating Authority and it is approved by the Adjudicating Authority, it no longer remains a confidential document, so as to preclude&nbsp;Regulator and other persons from access the said&nbsp;document.&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						(Emphasis Added)
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Thus, we are of the view that
							<strong>
								 once the Resolution Plan is approved
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 by the Adjudicating Authority, then it will no more be a confidential
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 document.
							</strong>
							 Therefore, the applicant will become aware as to how the land is dealt with in the Plan and accordingly, the Applicant shall have the liberty to claim all it&rsquo;s due fees and/or any charges under the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, 1955 along with any other Acts and/or Rules and/or Notifications for the time being in force, if there was a &ldquo;transfer&rdquo; and/or change in purpose of the land, as mentioned under Rule 6B of the Notification of Land &amp; Land Reforms and Refugee Relief &amp; Rehabilitation Department (Land Policy Branch) dated September 15, 2017 read with Section 14Y of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, 1955.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the Order above, this Application being I.A. (COMPANIES.ACT)/165(KB)2023 is dismissed and accordingly disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p58)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 IVN.P No. 10/KB/2023
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Be that as it may, we are of the view that
							<strong>
								 the Workers&rsquo; Union have
								<em>
									 no locus standi
								</em>
								 to question the assignment of a debt by Allahabad Bank.
							</strong>
							 It would be the assignor, or the Corporate Debtor who has taken the loan can challenge the assignment and not the Workers&rsquo; Union. On this ground alone this application is liable to be dismissed.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, we received an application seeking to withdraw this Intervention Petition being IVN Petition No. 10(KB)2023 on the ground that the said petition was filed by one Mr. Suraj Kumar Shaw on behalf of the Indian national Trade Union Congress (INTUC) on the misconception that the workers&rsquo; due in the Corporate Debtor, Rishra Steel Limited were due and unpaid. However, subsequently after filing the application has found out with all past dues and workers were settled and there are no further claims on accounts of the workers dues. This affidavit filed by said Mr. Suraj Kumar Shaw on behalf of the INTUC through its new Counsel, Adv. Somulaya Ganguly. However, it is appeared that NOC from the previous Counsel on record of the INTUC, Adv. Namrata Basu is not on record.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is not the case of the Applicant, that the workers&rsquo; dues were not paid. When that being the case there is no case made out to entertain this intervention petition and accordingly dismissed.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the Order above, the Interlocutory Application being I.A. (COMPANIES.ACT)/165(KB)2023 is dismissed and disposed of and Intervention Petition being IVN.P/10(KB)2023 is dismissed.
						</li>
						<li>
							Liberty is however granted to both the Applicants herein, and/or any other&nbsp;person(s) interested and/or aggrieved, to seek other remedies as may be available to it under any other provisions of law.
						</li>
