		<h1>
			Section 17 of the Code can be interpreted as suspension of powers of the board of directors and not their duties &amp; responsibilities, the Board is fastened with the responsibility of running &amp; managing the company&rsquo;s affairs &ndash; Wittur Elevator Components India Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Axiomata Elevators Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			December 28, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The AA held that a careful reading of Section 17 of the Code, this can be interpreted as suspension of powers of the board of directors(BOD) and not their duties and responsibilities. The Board is fastened with the responsibility of running and managing the company&rsquo;s affairs. If the powers of the board are suspended and the management of the affairs of the Corporate Debtor vests with the IRP after his appointment, then the responsibility also lies with the IRP. The suspension of the powers of the BOD means suspension of the role of directors, and responsibilities emanating from such role. Section 20 mandates the IRP to preserve and protect the value of the property and to manage the operations of the corporate debtor as a going concern. But in this case, even though the RP is empowered to take possession of the Registered Office and records of the Corporate Debtor, he has taken only symbolic possession of the same and allowed the suspended Directors to enjoy for their benefits. This is against the provisions of the Code. On verification of records of this case, it is seen that only one meeting of CoC took place with the presence of Resolution Professional, and without making any endeavour for inviting Expression of Interest, the CoC unanimously resolved to liquidate the Corporate Debtor.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The main allegation was that the Resolution Professional(Respondent)&nbsp;has failed to take over the affairs of the Corporate Debtor and to immediately take&nbsp;over custody and control of the assets without taking necessary steps to ascertain&nbsp;the financial position of the Corporate Debtor. To this, the contention of the&nbsp;Resolution Professional is that he has taken symbolic possession of the Registered&nbsp;Office and records of the Corporate Debtor. For the purpose of the resolution, the&nbsp;control and custody of the assets from the corporate debtor is taken over by the&nbsp;resolution professional as per Section 18(f).
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Adjudicating Authority observed that on hearing the parties, it comes to light that Section 17 has confused&nbsp;Corporate Debtors and practitioners. It may be stated that from the date of&nbsp;appointment of Interim Resolution Professional, &ldquo;
								<em>
									the powers of the Board of&nbsp;
								</em>
								<em>
									Directors or the partners of the Corporate Debtor, as the case may be, shall stand&nbsp;
								</em>
								<em>
									suspended and be exercised by the Interim Resolution Professional
								</em>
								&rdquo;. From a&nbsp;careful reading of this provision, this can be interpreted as suspension of powers&nbsp;of the board of directors and not their duties and responsibilities. The Board is&nbsp;fastened with the responsibility of running and managing the company&rsquo;s affairs. If&nbsp;the powers of the board are suspended and the management of the affairs of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor vests with the Interim Resolution Professional after his&nbsp;appointment, then the responsibility also lies with the Interim Resolution Professional. The suspension of the powers of the Board of Directors means&nbsp;suspension of the role of directors, and responsibilities emanating from such role.
								<strong>
									(p36)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								A plain reading of the Section 18 makes it clear that the Code empowers&nbsp;the Resolution Professional to take control and custody of any property which the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor has complete ownership. This power of the Resolution&nbsp;Professional extends to properties that are part of the court proceedings. Section 20 mandates the IRP to preserve and protect the value of the property and to&nbsp;manage the operations of the corporate debtor as a going concern. But in this&nbsp;case, even though the RP is empowered to take possession of the Registered&nbsp;Office and records of the Corporate Debtor, he has taken only symbolic possession&nbsp;of the same and allowed the suspended Directors to enjoy for their benefits. This is&nbsp;against the provisions of the Code.
								<strong>
									(p38)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								With the concept of Creditors in Control under IBC, after the initiation of the&nbsp;CIRP, the CoC assumes decision making powers for the management of the CD. IRP/ RP is an independent&nbsp;professional to take care of the interests of all the stakeholders. Thus both IRP/RP&nbsp;and CoC have to work in tandem and the overall interest of resolution while&nbsp;balancing the interests of all stakeholders. But here there is a clash of interest&nbsp;among the member of CoC and the Resolution Professional. In its first meeting of&nbsp;the CoC, it appointed the Resolution Professional, who was then convening and&nbsp;conducting the meetings of the committee. Further, as per Section 24(2), the&nbsp;resolution professional conducts all the meetings of the Committee of Creditors.&nbsp;Whereas, in this matter neither 2nd CoC nor 3rd CoC meetings were conducted by&nbsp;the Resolution Professional as the Chairman or even not attended the meeting.&nbsp;The meeting which was said to be convened was without his knowledge. As per the&nbsp;annexures submitted by the applicant (M/s. Wittur Elevator Components India Private Limited) in M.A. No./15/KOB/2020, only in the minutes of the 1st CoC Meeting,&nbsp;the RP has signed. It is also found that Applicants themselves issued agendas for&nbsp;the 3rd CoC Meeting and passed resolutions by themselves, even for change of the&nbsp;Resolution Professional.&nbsp;On verification of records of this case, it is seen that only one meeting of&nbsp;Committee of Creditors took place with the presence of Resolution Professional,&nbsp;and without making any endeavour for inviting Expression of Interest, the CoC&nbsp;unanimously resolved to liquidate the Corporate Debtor.
								<strong>
									(p39-41)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								The submission of the RP is that the 4th CoC Meeting was convened on&nbsp;07.03.2020, after issuing notice to Financial Creditor, Operational Creditor, and&nbsp;suspended Directors of Corporate Debtor. The CoC member (FC), a representative&nbsp;of OC, and suspended Director attended the meeting and after elaborate&nbsp;evaluation of the current situation, CoC by 100% voting resolved to liquidate the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and instructed the RP to file application before this Tribunal. It is&nbsp;also stated that the resolution passed for liquidation is before the expiry of 180&nbsp;days as stipulated in Section 12 of IBC, 2016.&nbsp;Section 33 of the Code enables that only after a resolution fails to yield&nbsp;Resolution Plan, the CD is ordered into liquidation, by an application filed by the&nbsp;Resolution Professional.
								<strong>
									(p42-43)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								This Tribunal is firmly rooted in&nbsp;the jurisprudence of &lsquo;Actus curiae neminem gravity- the act of the Court shall&nbsp;harm no man. We also noticed that M/s Bajaj Finance Ltd, who is a Financial&nbsp;Creditor of the Corporate Debtor came with their claim belatedly filing Form-C&nbsp;dated 12.02.2020 under Regulation 8. The claim of the Financial Creditor was&nbsp;admitted on 15.02.2020 and CoC was re-constituted with a sole Financial Creditor.&nbsp;Before that the CoC was constituted by the RP with the Operational Creditor.&nbsp;Without informing the Operational creditor and discussing the entry of new&nbsp;Financial creditor, the RP unanimously took a decision to accept the claims of&nbsp;Financial Creditor &ndash;M/s Bajaj Finance Limitedon 15.02.2020 reconstituting the CoC.&nbsp;No earlier meetings were attended by M/s. Bajaj Finance Limited and we could not&nbsp;understand, from where the Financial Creditor came and took over the CoC and&nbsp;unanimously decided to liquidate the Corporate Debtor before completion of 180 days. Even if such a decision is taken, the Resolution Professional should have&nbsp;objected to the same, as there is no expression invited and any resolution failed.&nbsp;This, in our opinion, is done in connivance with the Financial Creditor and to see&nbsp;that the Operational Creditors are put to trouble. It appears from the records that&nbsp;M/s Bajaj Finance Limited did not attend any of the CoC Meetings except the 4th&nbsp;Meeting in which the resolution for liquidating the Corpo ate Debtor was passed.
								<strong>
									(p44)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								The above discussions show that in the interest of justice the time spent till&nbsp;now before the Adjudicating Authority from the 2nd CoC Meeting till the date of this
								<br/>
								order should be excluded from calculating the period under Section 12 (1), (2) &amp;(3)&nbsp;of the IBC. Parties need not suffer for the time spent during this period before the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority.
								<strong>
									(p46)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Order:
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							a. The Report No. 28/KOB/2020 is taken on record, except the allegations&nbsp;made against the Operational Creditor and the reliefs sought for in the&nbsp;report.
							<br/>
							b. Since, the Committee of Creditors is reconstituted with the Financial&nbsp;Creditor M/s Bajaj Finance Limited and the Operational Creditor is not having any voting right in the CoC, the main prayer in MA/15/KOB/2020&nbsp;to permit them to change the Resolution Professional and refer the&nbsp;case to Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) for proposing&nbsp;the name of a new RP cannot be entertained, since they have no locus&nbsp;standi to do so. Hence, other prayers also cannot be granted by this&nbsp;Tribunal.
							<br/>
							c. In view of the fact that the Resolution Professional has without calling&nbsp;the Expression of Interest (EoI) and without following the mandates,&nbsp;accepted the approval of a CoC member to liquidate the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor and file this application hurriedly, this application for liquidation&nbsp;cannot be entertained now. The Resolution Professional is allowed to&nbsp;continue with the CIRP from the stage of reconstitution of CoC and&nbsp;proceed with the CIRP as per the Regulations. However, the fee of the&nbsp;Resolution Professional is to be restricted during the period of&nbsp;litigation before this Tribunal and the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Kerala.
							<br/>
							d. As we have directed to continue the CIRP with the present Resolution&nbsp;Professional following the mandate under the Regulations, this&nbsp;Tribunal is of the view that the application for the prosecution of&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and its Directors need not be considered at this&nbsp;juncture. Hence, IA/144/KOB/2020 is dismissed. It is hereby made clear that the officer/ erst-while Directors of the Corporate Debtor shall fully&nbsp;co-operate for the completion of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution&nbsp;Process.
