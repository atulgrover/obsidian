		<h1>
			Bad commercial business decision cannot be considered to be fraudulent or wrongful trading under provisions of Section 66 of the IBC &ndash; Mr. Venkatesan Sankaranarayanan, the RP for RTIL Ltd. Vs. Mr. Nitin Shambhukumar Kasliwal &amp; Ors. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			December 16, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority held that it is a fact that management of company have taken certain decision which has not worked out as intended by the management and eventually loss occurred. However, such bad commercial business decision cannot be considered to be fraudulent or wrongful trading under provisions of Section 66 of the IBC. The Bench in conclusion, therefore is of the view that the transaction review report has only highlighted that certain transaction may be potential fraudulent transaction. However, the transaction review report does not have any reference to siphoning of or diversion of funds in any manner. Therefore, there is no credible rational input based on which a Section 66 application can be entertained and application deserves to be dismissed.
		</p>
	</div>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							A Misc. Application has been filed by RP/Liquidator,&nbsp;Venkatesan Sankaranarayanan against the&nbsp;Promoters/Directors of the Corporate Debtor Company&nbsp;namely RTIL Limited under Section 66 of the&nbsp;Code. The present M.A. has been filed by&nbsp;RP /Liquidator based on the forensic Audit Report by the&nbsp;forensic Auditor KPMG. The Applicant has relied upon&nbsp;forensic Audit Report in which certain transaction by the&nbsp;forensic Auditor has been highlighted as potentially&nbsp;fraudulent.
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								<em>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</em>
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								i. Write-off by debtors:
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The Applicant has alleged that the&nbsp;assets base of the Corporate Debtor was reduced due to&nbsp;Write-off by the debtors of the Corporate Debtors&nbsp;amounting to Rs. 1019.48 crores as on 31.03.2016.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that the&nbsp;write off in an Internal Accounting Procedure which is&nbsp;necessarily to be followed by any corporate entity and these&nbsp;write off do not demonstrate any fraudulent trading and&nbsp;the Applicant is incorrect such write off to be fraudulent.
							<strong>
								(p3.1.5)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								ii. Reduction in carrying value of stock/inventory:
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The&nbsp;Applicant mentions that there was increase in the&nbsp;purchase of raw material by the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;during the years 2013 and 2014 and subsequently as of&nbsp;31st March, 2016 the value of inventory was reduced by&nbsp;551.74 crores by giving deep discounts.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that none of the investors, shareholders and&nbsp;lenders such as State Bank of India, IDBI, Bank, ICICI&nbsp;Bank Ltd. etc has ever raised any concerns relating to fraud&nbsp;being conducted by Respondent(s) and there is no&nbsp;reference to any such things in the financial statement of&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor for the relevant period. None of these&nbsp;entities have ever mentioned that adequate documentation&nbsp;relating to purchase of materials are not available.&nbsp;Therefore, the Bench finds these charges to be&nbsp;unsubstantiated.&nbsp;The Bench is also of the view that no gain was made by the&nbsp;Respondent(s) through these transactions. Therefore, the&nbsp;Applicant cannot call these reductions in inventory/stock&nbsp;to be a fraudulent act of the Respondent(s).
							<strong>
								(p3.2.3-3.2.4)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								iii. Linked entities:
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The Applicant mentions that the entities&nbsp;from whom purchases were made or whose debts have&nbsp;been written-off are linked to each other. The Applicant&nbsp;also mentions that these interse linked entities have&nbsp;common email IDs/common Auditors/common Directors etc. and that details of fixed assets of such&nbsp;entities were not available in public domain.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Applicant also alleges that it is fraudulent transaction&nbsp;because Mr. Shyam Malpani and Associates who were&nbsp;statutory auditor for Corporate Debtor from 01.03.2012 to&nbsp;30.09.2014 were also statutory auditor of some of the&nbsp;supplier/vendors. The Bench notes that Mr. Shyam&nbsp;Malpani and associates was setup in 1992 and in 2006, as&nbsp;mentioned by the Respondent(s), the staff strength of the&nbsp;statutory auditor group included 13 Chartered&nbsp;Accountants with locations at various cities in the country.&nbsp;The Bench notes that mere existence of common statutory&nbsp;auditors between the Corporate Debtor and some of&nbsp;suppliers/vendors in no way categories transactions as&nbsp;fraudulent or wrongful trading.
							<strong>
								(p3.3.2.)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								iv. Capital Advances written off:
							</strong>
							&nbsp;The Applicant mentions&nbsp;that the Corporate Debtor has written-off capital&nbsp;advance credit of Rs. 213.38 crores during the limited&nbsp;review period of the Forensic Audit conducted by KPMG.&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that the reasons for writing off&nbsp;capital advances has been clearly set out in the minutes of&nbsp;the board meeting dated 15.10.2015 of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor forming part of the Application.&nbsp;The Bench also notes that these are debit balances&nbsp;appearing against these parties and no payment by cheque&nbsp;or cash have been made in this respect and that the Respondent(s) had provided all relevant details and&nbsp;explanation in relation to the write off to its lender at&nbsp;relevant time. The Bench therefore is of the view that these&nbsp;advances write off can not be termed as fraudulent&nbsp;transaction. (
							<strong>
								p3.4.2.&amp;3.4.3
							</strong>
							)
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that&nbsp;the scope of Section 66 of&nbsp;IBC is to demonstrate that the business of Corporate Debtor&nbsp;has been carried on with the &ldquo;intent to defraud&rdquo; its creditor&nbsp;or for &ldquo;any fraudulent purpose&rdquo;. Therefore, while looking&nbsp;into the purported/alleged fraudulent transaction it has to&nbsp;be clearly established that the transaction were made with&nbsp;intension to defraud /fraudulent purpose.
							<strong>
								(p2.1)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Bench observes that it is a fact that management of&nbsp;company have taken certain decision which has not worked&nbsp;out as intended by the management and eventually loss&nbsp;occurred. However, such bad commercial business decision&nbsp;cannot be considered to be fraudulent or wrongful trading&nbsp;under provisions of Section 66 of the IBC. The Bench in conclusion, therefore is of the view that the&nbsp;transaction review report has only highlighted that certain&nbsp;transaction may be potential fraudulent transaction. However,&nbsp;the transaction review report does not have any reference to&nbsp;siphoning of or diversion of funds in any manner. Therefore,&nbsp;there is no credible rational input based on which a Section&nbsp;66 application can be entertained and application deserves to&nbsp;be dismissed.
							<strong>
								(p6-7)
							</strong>
						</p>
