	<h1>
		A Resolution Plan passed by a CoC, which is comprised of related parties of the Corporate Debtor, is void ab initio as it violates Section 21(2) read with Section 30(2)(e) of IBC, 2016 &ndash; M/s. Punjabi Accessoriezz Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-II
	</h1>
	<div>
		March 21, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The present IA has been filed by the Resolution Professional for approval of the Resolution plan. The entire Committee of Creditors (CoC) of the Corporate Debtor consists of its Shareholders only. There are only 04 shareholders in the present Corporate Debtor, and both the Members of the CoC are from amongst them. To appoint or remove a Director, an Ordinary Resolution is required to be passed. Voting by Show of Hands, is not excluded as a mode of voting for an Ordinary Resolution for either appointing or removing a Director of the Board. To pass an Ordinary Resolution by a show of hands, approval of more than 50% of the &ldquo;shareholders in number&rdquo; is required, which in the present case comes to 3. The Adjudicating Authority referring various provision of the Companies Act, 2013 with p 5(24(l) of IBC held that if voting by show of hands would have taken place for passing an Ordinary Resolution for the appointment or removal of a Director in the Corporate Debtor i.e., Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd., then the same could not have been possible without the participation of any of the CoC members. Therefore, we are of the firm view that the said two shareholders, who are also the members of the CoC of the Corporate Debtor, were capable of controlling the composition of the Board of Directors of the said Corporate Debtor. Hence, by virtue of their capability of controlling the composition of the Board of Directors of the Corporate Debtor, we conclude that both the CoC members/CoC as a whole comprised of &ldquo;related parties&rdquo; to the Corporate Debtor in terms of Section 5(24)(l) of IBC, 2016 and therefore, the entire constitution of CoC is erroneous in the eyes of law.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 107 NCLTCase Name:M/s. Punjabi Accessoriezz Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd.Corporate Debtor:M/s. Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA. No. 611/ND/2021 in Company Petition No. (IB)- 1164 (ND)/2019Judgment Date:17-Mar-23Court/Bench:NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IIMember (Judicial):Shri Ashok Kumar BhardwajMember (Technical):Shri L.N. Gupta
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The present IA has been filed by the Resolution Professional of Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd. for approval of the Resolution plan submitted by Ms. Vanshika Raheja jointly with Ms. Mridula Mangla (Resolution Applicant).
					</p>
					<p>
						The total claims of creditors/stakeholders admitted are to the tune of Rs. 382.98 Lakhs, against which the Successful Resolution Applicant had proposed to pay Rs. 4.07 Lakhs only. The Financial Creditors are getting paid 0.99% only of their claim amounts. In other words, the Resolution Plan involves a haircut of 99% plus for the Financial Creditors.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observed that when we compare the existing &ldquo;Shareholding Pattern&rdquo; of the Corporate Debtor with the &ldquo;Composition of CoC&rdquo;, the CoC members namely, &ldquo;Crickxon Trade &amp; Export Private Limited&rdquo; and &ldquo;Swift Builders Limited are the Shareholders of the Corporate Debtor. In other words, the entire Committee of Creditors (CoC) of the Corporate Debtor consists of its Shareholders only. From the the compliances in &ldquo;Form-H&rdquo;, it is observed that none of the two Financial Creditors&rsquo; (or the members of the CoC) names are reflected under the category of &ldquo;Unsecured Creditors in Column 2(a)&rdquo;, which means they are not considered as &ldquo;related party&rdquo; and therefore, they have not been debarred from the voting rights under Section 21(2) of the IBC, 2016.&nbsp;Hence, in the given backdrop, we would like to examine, if the CoC members, namely, M/s Crickxon Trade and Exports Private Limited and Swift Builders Limited, are &ldquo;unrelated parties&rdquo; of the Corporate Debtor.
						<strong>
							 (p15-17)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether members of CoC, who have voted in Resolution Plan are related party?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is a matter of fact that in the present case, the two CoC members hold a 19% voting share each. Therefore, individually they are not Related Parties to the Corporate Debtor in terms of Section 5(24)(j). However, in order to pass the test of being unrelated parties, the members of the CoC have to still sail through all the criteria stipulated under Section 5(24) of IBC, 2016. Therefore, we would like to examine if they are a related party under any of the other criteria stipulated under Section 5(24) of IBC, 2016.
						</li>
						<li>
							The other criteria, for declaring a person as a related party to the Corporate Debtor, is stipulated under Section 5(24)(l) of IBC, 2016, which is as &ldquo;
							<em>
								 (l) any person who can control the composition of the board of directors or corresponding governing body of the corporate debtor;
							</em>
							 &rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							The aforesaid criterion implies that any person, who is capable of controlling the composition of the board of Directors of the Corporate Debtor is a related party to the Corporate Debtor. When we say controlling the composition, it obviously includes the appointment and removal of Directors. In view of the above, we would like to examine at this stage, whether the two Shareholders/CoC members were in a position or legally capable to appoint or remove a Director in the Corporate Debtor Company i.e., Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd.
							<strong>
								 (p19-20)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Removal and appointment of a Director passing resolution by &ldquo;voting by show of hands&rdquo;
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In order to adjudicate this issue, we would like to examine the provisions regarding the removal and appointment of a Director. First, it can be inferred from Section 169(1) of the Companies Act 2013 that an Ordinary Resolution is required to be passed by the Members/Shareholders of the Company for the removal of a Director.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Similarly, the provisions for Appointing Directors are given under Section 152(2) read with 102 of the Companies Act, 2013. The appointment of Directors in place of those who are retiring is not considered a Special business at the Annual General Meeting of the Company. In other words, the appointment of Directors in place of those retiring is considered &ldquo;an ordinary business&rdquo;, and an ordinary resolution is required to be passed for its approval.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In order to perform various functions in a Company including appointment and removal of directors, the approval of Shareholders is required in the form of an Ordinary Resolution or Special Resolution, as the case may be. The Criteria for passing ordinary and special resolutions are stipulated under Section 114 of the Companies Act, 2013.
							<strong>
								 (p23)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In order to pass an ordinary resolution, the assent of more than 50%, of members/shareholders of a company is required and for passing a Special Resolution, the assent of at least 75% of members/shareholders is needed. Section 114 of the Companies Act, 2013 further recognizes the terms &ldquo;show of hands&rdquo; and &lsquo;poll&rsquo; as the voting criteria.
							<strong>
								 (p24)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							We would like to examine the difference between the two and in this context, refer to Section 107 of the Companies Act, 2013. In the normal course, the voting has to be done through the show of hands only, unless a Poll is demanded under Section 109 of the Companies Act, 2013, or the voting is carried out electronically.
							<strong>
								 (p24)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Now, if we compare the aforesaid provisions relating to &ldquo;voting by poll&rdquo; with &ldquo;the voting by show of hands&rdquo;, it can be inferred that a &ldquo;voting by poll&rdquo; has to be specifically demanded under Section 109, and if voting through a &ldquo;poll&rdquo; is conducted, then in that situation, the votes of a member shall be in proportion to the paid-up capital held by him. In other
							<br />
							 words, the higher the paid-up capital held by a member/shareholder in comparison to other members, the higher would be his voting share. In contrast, &ldquo;voting by show of hands&rdquo; works on the principle of &ldquo;one member &ndash; one vote&rdquo;, irrespective of the percentage of paid-up capital held by him in the Company.
							<strong>
								 (p26)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							When we again visit Section 114 of the Companies Act, 2013, we find that both &ldquo;voting by show of hands&rdquo; and &ldquo;voting by poll&rdquo; are recognized for passing of Ordinary and Special Resolutions. It goes without saying that the criteria of &ldquo;voting by show of hands&rdquo; is not excluded for the purpose of passing the resolutions.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Sum up the facts of the present case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Sum up the facts of the present case, as follows:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							a) There are only 04 shareholders in the present Corporate Debtor, and both the Members of the CoC are from amongst them.
						</li>
						<li>
							b) To appoint or remove a Director, an Ordinary Resolution is required to be passed.
						</li>
						<li>
							c) Voting by Show of Hands, is not excluded as a mode of voting for an Ordinary Resolution for either appointing or removing a Director of the Board.
						</li>
						<li>
							d) To pass an Ordinary Resolution by a show of hands, approval of more than 50% of the &ldquo;shareholders in number&rdquo; is required, which in the present case comes to 3.
							<strong>
								 (p28)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority concluded that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							If voting by show of hands would have taken place for passing an Ordinary Resolution for the appointment or removal of a Director in the Corporate Debtor i.e., Kredo Beauty Pvt. Ltd., then the same could not have been possible without the participation of any of the CoC members.
							<strong>
								 (p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, we are of the firm view that the said two shareholders, who are also the members of the CoC of the Corporate Debtor, were capable of controlling the composition of the Board of Directors of the said Corporate Debtor. Hence, by virtue of their capability of controlling the composition of the Board of Directors of the Corporate Debtor, we conclude that both the CoC members/CoC as a whole comprised of &ldquo;related parties&rdquo; to the Corporate Debtor in terms of Section 2(l) of IBC, 2016 and therefore, the entire constitution of CoC is erroneous in the eyes of law.
							<strong>
								 (p30)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							We are of the further view that a Resolution Plan passed by a CoC, which is comprised of related parties of the Corporate Debtor, is void ab initio as it violates Section 21(2) read with Section 30(2)(e) of IBC, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p31)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above, we have no other option but to dismiss the present Application i.e., IA No. 611 of 2021.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Since the maximum permissible period of the CIRP period has already elapsed, we have no other option but to order the Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. Ordered accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p33)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
