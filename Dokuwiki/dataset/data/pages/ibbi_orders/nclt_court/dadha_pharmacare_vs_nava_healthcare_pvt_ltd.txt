	<h1>
		Refundable Security Deposit made by Clearing and Forwarding Agent (CFA) in terms of the Clearing and Forwarding Agreement constitutes Financial Debt &ndash; Dadha Pharmacare Vs. Nava Healthcare Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 18, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT New Delhi Bench finds force in the plea raised on behalf of the Respondent that in terms of the provisions of Section 5(8)(f) of IBC, 2016, the security amount paid in terms of the agreement constitutes financial debt. A debt constitutes operational debt when the claim is in respect of provisions of goods and services including employment or a debt in respect of payment of dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, State Government, any State Government or any local authority. The security amount does not fall in any of the categories referred to in Section 5(21) of IBC, 2016.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Applicant (OC) is part of one of the oldest pharmaceutical Chemists and Druggist company and the Respondent (Corporate Debtor) is in the business of providing drugs and is a pharmaceutical company.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant and the Respondent entered into a &lsquo;Clearing and&nbsp;Forwarding Agreement&rsquo; dated 30.01.2020, having the ramification&nbsp;of appointment of the Applicant as Clearing and Forwarding Agent&nbsp;(CFA) for All India Operations with Mother Warehouse for the&nbsp;products manufactured, marketed and distributed by the&nbsp;Respondent (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of the agreement, the&nbsp;Operational Creditor deposited an amount of Rs. 1,50,00,000/-&nbsp;as fully secured and refundable&nbsp;Security Deposit, refundable within 14 days after the termination&nbsp;of the contract by either side.
						</li>
						<li>
							It was agreed between the parties that a Minimum Guarantee&nbsp;Commission (MGC) amounting to 2% of the net sales of Rs.&nbsp;6,50,000/- excluding&nbsp;GST less TDS whichever was higher per month was payable by the&nbsp;Respondent (CD) to the Applicant.
						</li>
						<li>
							The agreement was terminated on 15.09.2021, thus the&nbsp;security amount was to be refunded by the Respondent to the Applicant by&nbsp;29.09.2021.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Respondent did not pay the MGC/Interest/Expenses/ Hamali&nbsp;and Freight charges amounting to Rs. 51,04,773/-, for the months&nbsp;of October, November and December of the years 2020 and for the&nbsp;months of January, February and March of the years 2021.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is the case of the Applicant that the Security Deposit and the&nbsp;outstanding amount payable in terms of the agreement should fetch interest&nbsp;@ 18% per annum, if not paid. The total outstanding amount defaulted to be&nbsp;paid by the Respondent as mentioned in Part-IV (41) of the application is Rs.&nbsp;1,05,01,273/- + Rs. 1,50,00,000/-.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The NCLT finds force in the plea raised on behalf of the Respondent that in terms of the provisions of Section 5(8)(f) of IBC, 2016, the security amount paid in terms of the agreement constitutes financial debt. A debt constitutes operational debt when the claim is in respect of provisions of&nbsp;goods and services including employment or a debt in respect of payment of&nbsp;dues arising under any law for the time being in force and payable to the Central Government, State Government, any State Government or any local authority. The security amount does not fall in any of the categories referred to in Section 5(21) of IBC, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							As far as the balance amount is concerned, we find that the amount&nbsp;defaulted to be paid after 25.03.2021 including the amount mentioned in&nbsp;Part-IV (37) and the rent for Lock in period i.e. Rs. 26,69,160/- does not meet&nbsp;the threshold limit of Rs. One Crore. We may not be oblivious of the fact that&nbsp;with termination of the agreement on 15.09.2021, the clause 21 also stood&nbsp;terminated. In terms of the clause, the Applicant was entitled to refund of the&nbsp;security amount besides seeking adequate compensation to recover the&nbsp;costs/expenses incurred in connection with the warehouse.
							<strong>
								 (p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							As far as the proposal dated 17.11.2021 offered by the Respondent is&nbsp;concerned, it includes the amount of Security Deposit of Rs. 1,50,00,000/-&nbsp;and the interest on the total due amount. The amount agreed to be returned&nbsp;by the Respondent includes the amount of Security Deposit of Rs.&nbsp;1,50,00,000/- and interest thereon. If we reduce the interest on the security&nbsp;amount of Rs. 1,50,00,000/-, proportionately, the amount of 2,53,938/&ndash;&nbsp;Security amount plus interest thereon would not be Rs. One Core i.e. the&nbsp;threshold limit to order commencement of CIRP qua the CD.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the aforementioned, the present application is found devoid&nbsp;of merits and is accordingly rejected. The Applicant would, however, be&nbsp;entitled to file a fresh application under Section 7 of IBC, 2016 qua the&nbsp;Respondent for the default committed in refund of the security amount. The&nbsp;present order would not come in the way of the Applicant to resort to the&nbsp;remedies available to it to seek the relief regarding its entitlement to the dues&nbsp;payable to it by the Respondent.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The application stands disposed of accordingly. No cost.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
