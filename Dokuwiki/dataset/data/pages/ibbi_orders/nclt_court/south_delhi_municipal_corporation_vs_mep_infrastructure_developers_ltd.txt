		<h1>
			Claim based on an agreement where the Petitioner has appointed the Respondent as a &ldquo;Contractor&rdquo; to collect toll tax from commercial vehicles is not covered under Operational Debt &ndash; South Delhi Municipal Corporation Vs. MEP Infrastructure Developers Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			October 11, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			NCLT held that the claim is based on an Agreement where the Petitioner has appointed the Respondent as a &ldquo;Contractor&rdquo; to collect toll tax from commercial vehicles. Therefore, it is clear to the Bench that Petitioner is not providing any goods or services to the Respondent and to that extent is not covered under the definition of Operational Debt u/s 5(21). The Bench is of the view that, amount recoverable as &lsquo;arrears of tax&rsquo; is quite different from &lsquo;tax arising under statute&rsquo;. The Bench is of the view that what is covered in Section 5(21) is &lsquo;tax arising under the statute&rsquo; which is not the same thing as any due which only for the recovery mechanism is termed as &ldquo;an arrear of tax&rdquo;. The Bench is of the view that, what is covered under Section 5(21) is the dues which are arising under the statute and not dues which are recoverable as arrears of tax.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Petitioner is a statutory body incorporated under the Delhi&nbsp;Municipal Act, 1957 having the responsibility of monitoring, upgrading&nbsp;and developing civic amenities which are enshrined to it under the&nbsp;statute as detailed in Delhi Municipal Act, 1957. The Corporate Debtor-MEP Infrastructure Developers Limited (MEPL) was awarded the contract on 28.09.2017 for collection of Toll tax&nbsp;for a period of 5 years since it was the highest bidder at&nbsp;Rs. 1,206 crores per annum. Accordingly, it was agreed that the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor will pay the lumpsum amount of Rs. 1,206 crores in&nbsp;52 equal weekly instalments amounting to Rs. 23.19 crores (approx.)&nbsp;per week irrespective of the actual traffic or actual toll collection.&nbsp;This Petition has been filed under Section 9 of the Code&nbsp;by the South Delhi Municipal Corporation&nbsp;(SDMC) against MEP Infrastructure Developers Limited (MEPL). The
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Appellate Tribunal
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Bench is beset with mainly two sets of issues for&nbsp;consideration. The first one being whether the amount being claimed&nbsp;falls in the realm of the &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo;. The second issue for&nbsp;consideration under this Petition is whether the claim is a crystallized&nbsp;and undisputed sum.&nbsp;Therefore, it is to be seen in this case that this operational debt&nbsp;claimed by South Delhi Municipal Corporation is a claim in respect of&nbsp;at least one of the following :-
						</p>
						<p>
							a. Provision of goods; or
							<br/>
							b. Provision of services, including employment; or
							<br/>
							c. A debt arising under any statute and payable to Government/Local Authority.
						</p>
						<p>
							The claim is based on an Agreement where the Petitioner has&nbsp;appointed the Respondent as a &ldquo;Contractor&rdquo; to collect toll tax from&nbsp;commercial vehicles.&nbsp;Therefore, it is clear to the Bench that Petitioner is not providing&nbsp;any goods or services to the Respondent and to that extent is not&nbsp;covered under the definition of Operational Debt u/s 5(21).&nbsp;That the Petitioner is not providing any service to the&nbsp;Respondent is also made clear by several Judgments of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT.&nbsp;In this regard the Respondent has referred to the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT&nbsp;Judgment of 20.08.2020 in the matter of &ldquo;
							<em>
								Andal Bonumulla v/s Tomato&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Trading LLP [2020]  54 NCLAT
							</em>
							&rdquo;,&nbsp;&ldquo;
							<em>
								Kavita Anil Taneja v/s ISMT Limited (2019)  504 NCLAT
							</em>
							&ldquo;.
							<strong>
								(p21-27)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							However, let us see whether the operational debt claimed in this&nbsp;case, is with regard to payment of dues arising under any law and payable to Central Government/ State Government or any other local&nbsp;authority under Section 5(21) of IBC.&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							Further, it held that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								It is to be noted here that if the claim does not fall into any of&nbsp;these categories, then the claim cannot be categorised as an&nbsp;&lsquo;operational debt&rsquo;. In the present case, the Petitioner has not pleaded&nbsp;any supply of any goods or services being rendered by the Petitioner.&nbsp;However, the Petitioner claims that the debt falls under the ambit of&nbsp;&lsquo;operational debt&rsquo; in respect of payment of dues arising under any law&nbsp;for the time being in force and payable to Central Government or any&nbsp;State Government or any Local Authority. The Petitioner mentions that&nbsp;just because the Operational Creditor and Corporate Debtor had&nbsp;entered into a contract, does not render the dues as a due not arising&nbsp;out of a law.
							</li>
							<li>
								It is the claim of the Petitioner that in the instant matter the&nbsp;contract is an outcome of a specialised legislation, that is, Delhi&nbsp;Municipal Corporation Act (DMC Act). To buttress its claim, the&nbsp;Petitioner mentions that under Section 455 of the DMC Act, the&nbsp;Petitioner has power to recover any sum due to the DMC on any&nbsp;account under the DMC Act or any bye-law thereunder as an arrear of&nbsp;tax.&nbsp;The Petitioner mentions that the claim amount is a debt due&nbsp;under a statute. This, it mentions, is on the basis of Section 455 of&nbsp;DMC act.
							</li>
							<li>
								Here the Bench notes that these dues do not fall within the&nbsp;category of &ldquo;any charge, costs, expenses, fees, rates or rent or any&nbsp;other account&rdquo;. However, the Petitioner mentions that its claim is&nbsp;covered as &ldquo;recoverable from any person from whom such due is due&nbsp;as an arrear of tax under this act&rdquo;. In this regard it is very pertinent&nbsp;to see whether the phrases&nbsp;(a) dues which are recoverable as arrears&nbsp;of tax are the same as (b) dues which are tax arising under the statute.&nbsp;The Bench is of the view that there is a distinction that Section 455 is&nbsp;indicating regarding the recovery mechanism which exists for&nbsp;recovering taxes which can be used for recovering such dues.&nbsp;Therefore, the words as &ldquo;an arrear of tax&rdquo; and not &ldquo;dues which are&nbsp;tax&rdquo;.
							</li>
							<li>
								<strong>
									The Bench is of the view that, amount recoverable as &lsquo;arrears of&nbsp;tax&rsquo; is quite different from &lsquo;tax arising under statute&rsquo;. The Bench is of&nbsp;the view that what is covered in Section 5(21) is &lsquo;tax arising under the&nbsp;statute&rsquo; which is not the same thing as any due which only for the&nbsp;recovery mechanism is termed as &ldquo;an arrear of tax&rdquo;.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								The Bench is also of the view that the word &ldquo;recoverable from&nbsp;any person from whom such sum is due as an arrear of tax under this&nbsp;Act&rdquo; introduces deeming fiction that the dues can be recovered as an&nbsp;arrear of tax to take benefit of the machinery under the DMC Act.&nbsp;However, this does not make a contractual due which is the case in&nbsp;the instant matter, as a due converted into a tax. The distinction&nbsp;remains between (a) dues which are recoverable as arrears of tax, and (b) dues which are tax, arising under the statute.&nbsp;
								<strong>
									The Bench is of the&nbsp;
								</strong>
								<strong>
									view that, what is covered under Section 5(21) is the dues which are&nbsp;arising under the statute and not dues which are recoverable as&nbsp;
								</strong>
								<strong>
									arrears of tax.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;Emphasising upon this distinction, reliance has been&nbsp;placed by the Respondents on Judgment&nbsp;
								<em>
									SICOM Limited &amp; Ors v/s&nbsp;
								</em>
								<em>
									State of Maharashtra (this judgement relies on Builders Supply&nbsp;Corporation vs Union of India, (1965) 2 SCR 289).
								</em>
							</li>
							<li>
								The Bench notes that in the above Judgment Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme&nbsp;Court has observed that the legal fiction is not intended to convert an&nbsp;Award into a Decree for all purposes under all statutes &ndash; whether State&nbsp;or Central. In this case, the deeming fiction is restricted to the DMC&nbsp;Act and cannot be extended to cover another Act. In light of the above&nbsp;the Bench is of the view that the operational debt claim is not covered&nbsp;as &ldquo;a debt in respect of payment of dues arising under any law for the&nbsp;time being in force&rdquo;.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Bench therefore is of the considered view based on the&nbsp;above that the claim of the Petitioner as &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; does not&nbsp;meet the criteria as set out in Section 5(21) of IBC i.e. the claim of the&nbsp;SDMC does not fall in either if the following;
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							a. Claim in respect of goods and services;&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							b. Debt in respect of dues arising under any law in force and&nbsp;payable to the Government or any local authority.
							<strong>
								(28-36)
							</strong>
						</p>
					</div>
				</div>
			</p>
