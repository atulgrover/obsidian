		<h1>
			Once a Financial Creditor fails to file his claim before the CIRP, he is estopped from objecting the approval of the plan at a belated stage &ndash; Dewan Housing Finance Corporation Ltd Vs. Mr. Neehal Mahamulal Pathan (RP of Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd &amp; Anr.) &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			April 11, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Bench holds that the Code expressly/mandatory requires that the claim has to be filed and a claim is not filed, the claim is lost by acquiescence/ not filing of claims. The applicant being a secured creditor has failed to file his claim before the Resolution Professional after initiation of CIRP of the Corporate debtor. This Bench has no hesitation to conclude that the objection of the Secured Creditor is untenable.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The factual matrix in the present case revolves around the sole ground that the Secured Financial Creditor has not filed his claim in the process of the CIRP post the public announcement in the CIRP process. Therefore, the question remains that the rights of the Financial Creditors is taken away or lost vide its inaction of not filing the claim in the CIRP process. Therefore, once the Financial Creditor fails to file his claim before the CIRP, he is estopped from objecting the approval of the plan at a belated stage. The procedure as contemplated under the CIRP upon admission of the claim under p 7, or 9 or 10 the Resolution Professional is required to cause public announcement as per p 15 of the Code, in compliance of p 18 of the Code, the Resolution Professional shall collect all the information of the Creditors and constitute the Committee of Creditors. In terms of the IBBI Regulations for Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons). Regulation 8, as person claiming to be a Financial Creditor shall submit the claim and provide proof of existence of debt, the Resolution Professional under regulation 13 is duty bound to verify such claims filed. The Resolution Professional would then constitute the CoC. Hence, it can be said that the I&amp;B Code expressly / mandatory requires that the claim has to be filed and a claim is not filed, the claim is lost by acquiescence/ not filing of claims.
						</p>
						<p>
							This Bench relies on the judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Ghanashyam Mishra And Sons Private Limited Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited&nbsp;(2021)  54 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;wherein it was held that once the Resolution Plan is duly approved by the Adjudicating Authority under p 31(1), the claims as provided in the Resolution Plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on the Corporate Debtor, and its Employees, Members, Creditors including the Central Government, any State Government&nbsp;of Local Authority, Guarantors and Stake Holders. It was also further held that&nbsp;on the date of approval of the Resolution Plan by the Adjudicating Authority, all such claims, which are not part of the Resolution Plan shall stand extinguish and no person shall be entitle to initiate or constitute any proceedings in respect of the claim which is not the part of the Resolution claim.
						</p>
						<p>
							This Bench also refers to the judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the mater of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Office of the Assistant State Tax Commissioner, State Tax Department, Govt of Maharashtra Vs. Parthiv Parikh&nbsp;(2021)  164 NCLAT
							</em>
							&nbsp;wherein they have held that any interruption in the CIRP process at this stage by including the delayed claims would have meant setting the clock back and sending the matter back to the CoC and RP.
						</p>
						<p>
							Further judgement of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel Ltd. Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta and Ors.&nbsp;[2019]  07 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;has held that a Successful Resolution Applicant cannot suddenly be faced with undecided claims after Resolution plan is submitted by him has been accepted as this would amount to be a hydra head popping up which would throw into the uncertainty and the amounts payable by the prospective Resolution Applicant who can taken over the business of the Corporate Debtor. All the claims must be submitted to and decided by the Resolution Professional so that a prospective Resolution Applicant knows exactly what has to be paid in order that it may then take over and run the business of Corporate Debtor. This the Successful Resolution Applicant does on a fresh slate, as has been pointed out by us hereinabove.
						</p>
						<p>
							In view of the aforesaid observations of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the decision cited above and a conjoint reading of series of events as recorded by the Resolution Professional in the conduct of the CIRP and considering the most vital fact that the applicant being a secured creditor has failed to file his claim before the Resolution Professional after initiation of CIRP of the Corporate debtor. This Bench has no hesitation to conclude that the objection of the Secured Creditor is untenable and therefore the I.A. 2544 of 2021 and I.A. 2801 of 2021 are dismissed in the above terms.
						</p>
