					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The claim of the Operational Creditor is mainly arising out of damages/compensation claimed for detention/ idling of its containers for 10 years from 2007 to 2017.&nbsp;It is not understood and clear from the application as to why and how the Applicant, despite having the specific remedy available in Clause 22.3 of the Bill of Lading, did not take any steps to auction the goods for recovery of its dues/ charges as claimed.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that the Applicant has failed to demonstrate as to how it has crystallized its dues relating to the detention and other charges /debt to the tune of Rs 3,92,23,873/- in the absence of any direct explicit agreement or rate contract between the Applicant and the Respondent. Further, in our view, the compensation/ damage arising on account of detention of containers rendering services on behalf of a third company (the shipper, which booked the Petitioner), does not constitute an Operational Debt within the meaning of Section 5(21) of IBC, 2016. At this juncture, we consider it worthwhile to refer to the Judgement dated 24.02.2020 of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
						<em>
							 Neeraj Jain Vs. Cloudwalker Streaming Technologies Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2020] 221 NCLAT.
						</em>
						 Moreover, the claim for compensation/ damages is not based on any order by the Respondent in the instant case.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Respondent has also stated that the Bill of Lading dated 15.11.2017, on the basis of which the Operational Creditor has attempted to categorize the Respondent as merchant, bears forged signatures. Examination of such a document may require adducing of further evidence from all concerned, which is beyond the scope of the Summary Jurisdiction of this Adjudicating Authority.
					</p>
					<p>
						Lastly, we further note that the sole objective of the Applicant behind filing this Application is to recover its detention, on-carriage, terminal handling, container maintenance charges etc., which is not the objective of IBC 2016 as held by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme court in a catena of judgements. Here, we would like to refer to the recent judgement dated 15.07.2022 passed in
						<em>
							 M/s S.S. Engineers Vs. Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd. &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2022) 92 SC
						</em>
						 . The In sequel to the aforesaid discussion, the Application is
						<strong>
							 Dismissed
						</strong>
						 . It is, however, made clear that any of the observations made in this Order shall not be construed as an expression of opinion on the merits of the claim and the rights of the Applicant before any other forum shall not be prejudiced on account of dismissal of the present Application.
