		<h1>
			Whether the Guaranteed Minimum Royalties to be paid quarterly by the Corporate Debtor as a consideration to grant of license and right to use the Trademark of the Operational Creditor on its Licensed Product (for manufacture and sale purpose) is an Operational Debt or not? &ndash; Knight Riders Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Global Fragrances Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IV
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 12, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			Adjudicating Authority drew conclusion as per the judgment Vikas Sales Corporation vs. Commissioner of Commercial Taxes AIR 1996 SC 2082 and held that incorporeal rights like trademarks, copyrights, patents and rights in personam capable of transfer or transmission are included in the ambit of "goods". Further having considered the facts and circumstances and the material available on record the Adjudicating Authority is of the view that that for a claim to fall within the definition of 'operational debt', the operational creditor must establish that it has a "right to payment" "in respect of the provision of "goods or services" and also that Corporate Debtor has committed a "default" towards its "liability or obligation in respect of such outstanding claim". The Adjudicating Authority also placed it reliance on the judgment Broadcast Audience Research Council V. Mi Marathi Media Limited (2021)  150 NCLT.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Whether the Guaranteed Minimum Royalties to be paid quarterly by the Corporate Debtor as a consideration to grant of license and right to use the Trademark of the Operational Creditor on its Licensed Product (for manufacture and sale purpose) is an Operational Debt or not?
						</p>
						<p>
							Adjudicating Authority drew conclusion as per the judgment&nbsp;
							<em>
								Vikas Sales&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Corporation vs. Commissioner of Commercial Taxes AIR 1996 SC&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								2082
							</em>
							&nbsp;and held that incorporeal rights like trademarks,&nbsp;copyrights, patents and rights in&nbsp;
							<em>
								personam
							</em>
							&nbsp;capable of transfer or&nbsp;transmission are included in the ambit of &ldquo;goods&rdquo;. Further having&nbsp;considered the facts and circumstances and the material available on&nbsp;record the Adjudicating Authority is of the view that that for a claim to&nbsp;fall within the definition of &lsquo;operational debt&rsquo;, the operational creditor&nbsp;must establish that it has a &ldquo;right to payment&rdquo; &ldquo;in respect of the&nbsp;provision of &ldquo;goods or services&rdquo; and also that Corporate Debtor has&nbsp;committed a &ldquo;default&rdquo; towards its &ldquo;liability or obligation in respect of&nbsp;such outstanding claim&rdquo;. The Adjudicating Authority also placed it reliance on&nbsp;the judgment&nbsp;
							<em>
								Broadcast Audience Research Council V. Mi Marathi&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Media Limited
							</em>
							&nbsp;
							<em>
								(2021)  150 NCLT
							</em>
							&nbsp;and we hereby&nbsp;observe that in the present case, the MGR was a fixed payment due and&nbsp;payable by the Corporate Debtor to the Operational Creditor under the&nbsp;Agreement and the non-payment by the Corporate Debtor, for using&nbsp;the &ldquo;Trademark&rdquo; which is the Licensed &ldquo;product&rdquo; of the Operational&nbsp;Creditor, amounted to an &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; under the IBC. It has&nbsp;observed that time and again the Corporate Debtor has admitted its&nbsp;liability be it by way of making a part payment (first and second quarter&nbsp;payment) or by submitting before the Adjudicating Authority that &ldquo;admittedly the claim of the Applicant arises out of failure to pay the&nbsp;Minimum Guaranteed Royalties and were not paid on the condition that&nbsp;the Operational Creditor under the obligation to promote the brand for the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor&rdquo; therefore, it is a clear admission of default and this&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority does not have to indulge in the details or the&nbsp;terms of the Agreement.
						</p>
						<p>
							Further in order to deal with issue in hand&nbsp;with respect to &ldquo;pre-existence of dispute&rdquo; as the alleged by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor that the Operational Creditor was under the&nbsp;obligation to promote the brand for the Corporate Debtor, We are of the&nbsp;view that the Corporate Debtor did not raise any dispute in terms of&nbsp;Section 8(2)(a) read with Section 5(6) of the IBC, either with regard to&nbsp;the (a) existence of the amount of debt, (b) the quality of goods or&nbsp;service, or (c) the breach of a representation or warranty, either directly&nbsp;or indirectly. Therefore, the defense of pre-existence of dispute can be&nbsp;categorized as a moonshine dispute as explained in the judgment of&nbsp;&ldquo;
							<em>
								Mobilox Innovations Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Kirusa Software (P) Ltd.&nbsp;[2017]  01 SC
							</em>
							&ldquo;, of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court.&nbsp;Therefore, the Application is admitted and the commencement of the&nbsp;CIRP is ordered.
						</p>
