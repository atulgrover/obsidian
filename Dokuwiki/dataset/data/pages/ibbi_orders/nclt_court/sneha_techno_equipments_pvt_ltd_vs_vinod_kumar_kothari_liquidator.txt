	<h1>
		Period of 90 days to make balance payment is mandatory and not left to the discretion of liquidator while laying down terms and conditions of auction notice and IBBI circular dated 26.08.2019 as non-est in law &ndash; Sneha Techno Equipments Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Vinod Kumar Kothari, Liquidator &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 28, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT held that there is no doubt period of 90 days to make balance payment is mandatory and not left to the discretion of liquidator while laying down terms and conditions of auction notice. In the present case, EOI was issued by the liquidator on 08.07.2020, i.e. after the date of amendment of period for payment of balance sale consideration to 90 days. In the EOI issued on 08.07.2020, the period was mentioned to be 15 days by the liquidator, whereas applicant sought extension up to 90 days from the date of demand in terms of the amendment dated 25.07.2019. Therefore, we are of the considered view that the liquidator was required to issue EOI in terms of the amendment that was in place on the date of issuance of EOI, thereby providing 90 days for payment of balance sale consideration from the date of demand. Further, it is held that there is no power to issue such circular under Section 196 of IBC 2016. We hold that the circular dated 26.08.2019 as non-est in law.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case &amp; issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This IA has been filed under p 60(5) of IBC Code 2016 read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 by the applicant who was declared as the highest bidder in the e-auction held by the Respondent on 30th July, 2020. In the present case, EOI was issued by the liquidator on 08.07.2020, i.e. after the date of amendment of period for payment of balance sale consideration to 90 days in Liquidation Regulations(amended on 25.07.2019). In the EOI issued on 08.07.2020, the period was mentioned to be 15 days by the liquidator, whereas applicant sought extension up to 90 days from the date of demand in terms of the amendment dated 25.07.2019.
						</li>
						<li>
							Issue that arises for consideration is whether forfeiture of EMD by liquidator is bad in law?
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 a. Period of 90 days to make balance payment in Auction Sale is mandatory
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							While examining the plea regarding period of 90 days for deposit of balance amount of consideration and effect of circular dated 26-08-2019, in the first instance we seek to place reliance on an order passed by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT New Delhi, in
							<em>
								 Potens Transmissions &amp; Power Pvt. Ltd Versus Gian Chand Narang&nbsp;(2022) 359 NCLAT
							</em>
							 . Therefore, there is no doubt period of 90 days to make balance payment is mandatory and not left to the discretion of liquidator while laying down terms and conditions of auction notice.
							<strong>
								 (p9.7&amp;9.8)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 b. Effect of amendment in Liquidation Regulations for the period of balance payment from 15 days to 90 days
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							It is significant to note, Schedule I of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (Liquidation Process Regulations), 2016 i.e., Mode of Sale deals with sale through auction by liquidator in the manner as specified therein. Thus, providing for balance sale consideration within 90 days from the date of demand, is the obligation created by law after amendment on
							<strong>
								 25.07.2019
							</strong>
							 . Admittedly, in the present case, EOI was issued by the liquidator on
							<strong>
								 08.07.2020
							</strong>
							 , i.e. after the date of amendment of period for payment of balance sale consideration to 90 days. In the EOI issued on 08.07.2020, the period was mentioned to be 15 days by the liquidator, whereas applicant sought extension up to 90 days from the date of demand in terms of the amendment dated 25.07.2019. Therefore, we are of the considered view that the liquidator was required to issue EOI in terms of the amendment that was in place on the date of issuance of EOI, thereby providing 90 days for payment of balance sale consideration from the date of demand.
							<strong>
								 (p9.12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Notwithstanding the fact, liquidation order was passed prior to this amendment, there is no provision for providing any period for payment of EOI at the time of passing the order of liquidation by Adjudicating Authority. It is only in the EOI, this period is/was required to be provided in terms of Schedule I under Regulation 33 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India Liquidation Process Regulations), 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p9.13)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 c. IBBI Circular date 26.08.2019
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							With a view to analyse argument of validity of&nbsp;circular dated 26.08.2019, we are of the view there is no power to issue such circular under Section 196 of IBC 2016. We hold that the circular dated 26.08.2019 as non-est in law.
							<strong>
								 (p9.9-9.10, 9.14)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 d. Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							We therefore, with utmost regard to the order passed by the NCLT Ahmedabad Bench, are of the view, that the liquidator, after the amendment was required to grant 90 days for making payment of balance sale consideration in the EOI issued by him on 08.07.2020 and by not doing so in accordance 2nd proviso to Clause 1(12) under Schedule I of the Liquidation Process Regulations, 2016 after amendment on 25th July, 2019 and subsequently forfeiting the EMD, has acted contrary to law.
							<strong>
								 (p9.14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above position, it is held that forfeiture of EMD by liquidator is contrary to law and the same is, therefore, liable to be set aside. We accordingly allow the present application by setting aside forfeiture of EMD and direct the liquidator: &ndash; a. to refund the amount of EMD forfeited by him within 30 days the date of this order with an interest @ 4% from forfeiture date, failing which, liquidator will be liable to pay the forfeiture amount of EMD along with an interest of 7% till the actual payment is made to the applicant. With these observations, the application is disposed of accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p9.15-9.16)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
