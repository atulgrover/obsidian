	<h1>
		Mere institution of a civil suit or its pendency is not a situation similar to or that envisaged in para 88 of Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. judgment &ndash; Canara Bank v. BBT Elevated Road Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 22, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Kolkata Bench held that though the word &ldquo;example&rdquo; has been used in the Vidarbha Industries Power Limited v. Axis Bank Limited (2022) 91 SC, there has to be a situation/akin to award/decree in favour of the Corporate Debtor before the admission of an application under Section 7 of the Code can be considered to be kept in abeyance by the Adjudicating Authority. Mere institution of a civil suit or its pendency, in our opinion, is not a situation similar to or that envisaged in para 88 of the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court as referred by the Corporate Debtor.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Background of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Company Petition is filed under p 7 of the IBC&nbsp;by Canara Bank (formerly known as Syndicate Bank) seeking to initiate CIRP against BBT Elevated Road Private Limited (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
						<li>
							In para 88 of
							<em>
								 Vidarbha Industries Power Limited v. Axis Bank Limited&nbsp;(2022) 91 SC,
							</em>
							 Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court held that the Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) has to consider the grounds made out by the Corporate Debtor against admission, on its own merits. For example when admission is opposed on the ground of existence of an award or a decree in favour of the Corporate Debtor, and the Awarded/decretal amount exceeds the amount of the debt, the Adjudicating Authority would have to exercise its discretion under Section 7(5)(a) of the IBC to keep the admission of the application of the Financial Creditor in abeyance, unless there is good reason not to do so. The Adjudicating Authority may, for example, admit the application of the Financial Creditor, notwithstanding any award or decree, if the Award/Decretal amount is incapable of realisation. The example is only illustrative.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Though the word &ldquo;example&rdquo; has been used in the
							<em>
								 Vidarbha Industries Power Limited v. Axis Bank Limited&nbsp;(2022) 91 SC
							</em>
							 , there has to be a situation/akin to award/decree in favour of the Corporate Debtor before the admission of an application&nbsp;under Section 7 of the Code can be considered to be kept in abeyance by&nbsp;the Adjudicating Authority. Mere institution of a civil suit or its pendency,&nbsp;in our opinion, is not a situation similar to or that envisaged in para 88 of&nbsp;the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court as referred by the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the context of contribution of other parties, e.g: KMDA in this case, towards occurrence of default, we rely on para 15 of the judgment of&nbsp;Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of SBI Vs. N.S. Engineering Projects&nbsp;reported in&nbsp;(2023) 79 NCLAT.
							<strong>
								 (p34)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							There was a debt due which the Corporate Debtor had defaulted to repay. Therefore, the essential elements of a Section 7 application i.e., debt and default are satisfied. The present petition made by the Financial Creditor which is complete in all respects, should be
							<strong>
								 admitted
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p35-36)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
