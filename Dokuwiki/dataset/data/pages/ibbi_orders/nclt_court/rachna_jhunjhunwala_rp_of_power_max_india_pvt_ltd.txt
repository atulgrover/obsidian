		<h1>
			While approving Resolution Plan, since 92% haircut and Transactional Auditor examined only two years, NCLT directs RP to examine the last 6 years Financial Statements/IT Returns/GST Returns/Cash Flow/Fund Flow Statements in detail
		</h1>
		<p>
			In the present case, total admitted claims are of Rs. 48.36 Crore, the liquidation value is Rs. 4.08 Crore and the value of the Resolution Plan is of Rs. 4.01 Crore, leading to a &ldquo;haircut&rdquo; of about 92%. The RP has appointed a transactional auditor who has examined only two financials&rsquo; years i.e., 2020-2021 and 2021-2022, and unaudited Trial Balance of 2022-2023 and came to conclusion that there are no avoidance transactions. The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench, comprising of Ms. Bidisha Banerjee (Judicial Member) and Shri Arvind Devanathan (Technical Member) approving the Resolution Plan, has directed the Resolution Professional to examine the last 6 years Financial Statements, IT Returns, GST Returns, cash flow and fund flow statements in detail and place it before the CoC of the Corporate Debtor with appropriate justification and the basis for forming opinion that there were no avoidance transactions, in the form of a report. A copy of the said report also be filed with this Adjudicating Authority.
		</p>
	</div>

						<ul>
							<li>
								The Committee of Creditors in its 9th meeting convened on 27.12.2023 and e-voting conducted from 29.12.2023 to 02.02.2024, has approved the Resolution Plan dated 26.12.2023 submitted by M/s.

							</li>
							<li>
								The instant application is filed under Sections 30(6) and 31(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 read with Regulation 39 of the CIRP Regulations 2016, by the Resolution Professional of Power Max (India) Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) &ndash; Mrs. Rachna Jhunjhunwala.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The hon&rsquo;ble Adjudicating Authority observes that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Resolution dated 26.12.2023 submitted by M/s. Shyam Enterprises, has been approved by the Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor by 100% voting share on 02.02.2024.
							</li>
							<li>
								The LoI was issued on 02.02.2024, which has been unconditionally accepted by the SRA.
							</li>
							<li>
								Accordingly, the Resolution dated 26.12.2023 submitted by M/s. Shyam Enterprises, the Successful Resolution Applicant defeats all other plans submitted before the applicant and M/s. Shyam Enterprises, has unanimously declared as a &ldquo;Successful Resolution Applicant&rdquo;.
							</li>
							<li>
								This Adjudicating Authority has not interfered with the viability of the Commercial Wisdom as exercised by the Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
							</li>
							<li>
								Subject to the observations made in this Order, the Resolution dated 26.12.2023 submitted by M/s. Shyam Enterprises, the Successful Resolution Applicant is hereby
								<strong>
									 approved
								</strong>
								 and
								<strong>
									 finally sanctioned
								</strong>
								 this Adjudicating Authority.
								<strong>
									 (p67-68)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							Further, the Adjudicating Authority
							<strong>
								 observes that
							</strong>
							 :
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								It is evident that the CoC has been constituted with only one member which is Indian Bank (Secure Financial Creditor). The claim received from Indian Bank is for Rs. 43.08 Crore that has been admitted in full by the Resolution Professional (RP).
							</li>
							<li>
								The amount allocated to Indian bank in the Resolution Plan of SRA is of Rs. 3.82 Crore only resulting in a huge reduction of 91.15%, compared to the admitted claim. Similarly, in the case of Government dues, against the admitted claim of Rs. 2.01 Crore, only Rs. 0.03 Crore has been provided resulting in massive reduction of 98%. Further, in case of Operational Creditors other than the employees, workman and Government dues, against the admitted claim of Rs. 3.09 Crore, only a Rs. 0.03 Crore has been allocated, again leading to large scale reduction of 99%.
							</li>
							<li>
								Against total admitted claim from various creditors for Rs. 48.36 Crore, only Rs. 4.01 Crore has been proposed and allocated which is
								<strong>
									 almost 92% &ldquo;haircut&rdquo;.
								</strong>
								 If we consider the total claims received as against the admitted claim, then
								<strong>
									 the &ldquo;haircut&rdquo; is even more at 95%.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								The Resolution Professional in Form H as well as in the Supplementary Affidavit stated that no transactions were observed under Regulation 39(2) of the CIRP Regulations which envisages that the RP shall submit to the committee, all resolution plans which comply with the requirements of the Code and regulations made thereunder along with the details of following transactions, if any, observed, found or determined by him:
								<ul>
									<li>
										(a) preferential transactions under section 43;
									</li>
									<li>
										(b) undervalued transactions under section 45;
									</li>
									<li>
										(c) extortionate credit transactions under section 50; and
									</li>
									<li>
										(d) fraudulent transactions under section 66, and the orders, if any, of the adjudicating authority in respect of such transactions.
										<strong>
											 (p69-72)
										</strong>
									</li>
								</ul>
							</li>
							<li>
								Total admitted claims is of Rs. 48.36 Crore, the liquidation value is Rs. 4.08 Crore and the value of the Resolution Plan is of Rs. 4.01 Crore, leading to a &ldquo;haircut&rdquo; of about 92%.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								 And holds that:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 casts
								<strong>
									 huge responsibilities
								</strong>
								 on the Resolution professional to deal with avoidance transactions under Sections 43, 45, 50 and 66 during the CIRP of a corporate debtor. In fact, the Code contemplates that it is the Resolution Professional alone who would form an opinion and determine avoidance transactions and take it up with the Adjudicating Authority by way of application for appropriate orders.
							</li>
							<li>
								<strong>
									 The members of the CoC who participate in the CIRP neither can devote their time on a full-time basis nor equipped to form an opinion and determine the avoidance transactions in a corporate debtor undergoing CIRP/ liquidation.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								If the Resolution Professional misses to determine the avoidance transactions and fail to file applications before the Adjudicating Authority,
								<strong>
									 then no way diverted or syphoned of funds if any can be got back and made available for distribution and insolvency resolution of the corporate debtor.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is the duty of the Resolution Professional to bring it to the notice of the CoC, as to where the borrowed funds have gone, particularly in case where the &ldquo;haircut&rdquo; is as high as 92% like in the present case.
							</li>
							<li>
								During the CIRP, the RP has appointed a transactional auditor who has examined only two financials&rsquo; years i.e., 2020-2021 and 2021-2022, and unaudited Trial Balance of 2022-2023, and came to conclusion that there are no avoidance transactions.
								<strong>
									 While the look back period for fraudulent transactions under Sections 43, 45 and 50 is two years and there is no limit on look back period for fraudulent transactions under Section 66 of the I&amp;B Code.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Unless the audit period covers a period beyond two years, at least from the period when the corporate debtor starting making losses / default in payment of debts, we are of the view that no fraudulent transactions can meaningfully be found or unearthed.
								<strong>
									 Further, the transactional audit report merely reports changes in various accounts when compared to the previous year.(p73-79)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								 And directs that:
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								<strong>
									 Hence, we direct the Resolution Professional to examine the last 6 years Financial Statements, IT Returns, GST Returns, cash flow and fund flow statements in detail and place it before the CoC of the Corporate Debtor with appropriate justification and the basis for forming opinion that there were no avoidance transactions, in the form of a report. A copy of the said report also be filed with this Adjudicating Authority.
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								The RP shall comply with the direction within a period of four weeks from the date of pronouncement of the order. This direction shall not affect the approval of the Resolution Plan.
								<strong>
									 (p80-81)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>

								<strong>
									 (p82-90)
								</strong>
							</li>
