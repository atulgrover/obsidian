						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								On perusal of Report of Bankruptcy&nbsp;Law Reforms Committee, November 2015 (BLRC Report), regarding the role of&nbsp;the Resolution Professional about the preparation of the&nbsp;Information memorandum, it is clear that&nbsp;Resolution Professional is duty-bound to provide the most&nbsp;updated information about the entity as accurately as is&nbsp;reasonably possible to this range of solution providers.
							</li>
							<li>
								It is also the duty of the Resolution Professional to prepare the&nbsp;Information Memorandum, in order for the prospective investors to&nbsp;provide solutions to keep the entity as a going concern, the&nbsp;information memorandum must be made available to potential&nbsp;financiers within a reasonable period of time and if the information&nbsp;is not comprehensive, the Resolution Professional must put out the&nbsp;Information Memorandum with a degree of completeness and the&nbsp;information that she is willing to certify. In the report, an example&nbsp;is also given that the part of the Information Memorandum;&nbsp;the Resolution Professional must clearly state the expected&nbsp;shortfall in the coverage of liabilities and assets of the entity&nbsp;presented in the Information Memorandum. The Resolution&nbsp;Professional also must make sure that the information is readily&nbsp;available to whosoever is interested in bidding, a solution for&nbsp;Insolvency Resolution Process. Given the duties as prescribed in&nbsp;the BLRC Report, it is clear that Resolution Professional becomes&nbsp;the manager of the negotiation between the debtors and creditors&nbsp;in accessing the viability of the entity.
							</li>
							<li>
								It is clear that the Resolution Professional has a continuing&nbsp;statutory duty of adequate and correct disclosure during the CIRP.
							</li>
							<li>
								The Resolution Professional has had access to the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor&rsquo;s records, business and assets for several months whereas&nbsp;the Applicants have had no time for independent evaluation. It is&nbsp;submitted that the Resolution Professional cannot urge that the&nbsp;Applicants were not entitled to rely upon either the information and&nbsp;documents made available in the VDR or that it was obligatory on&nbsp;the Applicants to conduct independent due diligence. The VDR was&nbsp;created and maintained by the Resolution Professional for the&nbsp;admitted purpose of the due diligence of the resolution applicants.
							</li>
							<li>
								It is pertinent to mention that &ldquo;Due Diligence&rdquo; a key challenge&nbsp;when acquiring an asset under Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.&nbsp;The issue is that the asset you would not get everything &ndash;not all&nbsp;the information, not all the assets, as the Resolution Professional is&nbsp;himself is struggling in managing the business and is not an&nbsp;Industry Person in most of the cases.During Due Diligence process,&nbsp;a prospective acquirer takes stock of the Asset by looking at&nbsp;the company data,walking around sites and doing third party&nbsp;diligence, is one of the most critical areas of any, but is often&nbsp;considered as customary and left to advisers.
							</li>
							<li>
								The IBC neither confers the power or jurisdiction on the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority to compel specific performance of a plan by&nbsp;an unwilling resolution applicant. The letter and spirit of the I&amp;B&nbsp;Code mandate the acceptance of only a viable and lawful resolution&nbsp;plan being implemented at the hands of a willing resolution&nbsp;applicant. Absence of these factors renders the Section 31&nbsp;application liable to be rejected. The I&amp;B Code envisages a scheme whereby the Corporate Debtor is taken over by the successful&nbsp;resolution applicant. This Scheme must contain a provision for its&nbsp;implementation and supervision under Section 30(2)(d) and as&nbsp;required by the proviso to Section 31(1).
							</li>
							<li>
								It is also well-established that the object of the I&amp;B Code is not&nbsp;liquidation of the Corporate Debtor but resolution of the insolvency&nbsp;situation. This is clear by the Preamble of the I&amp;B Code and more&nbsp;specifically explained by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
								<em>
									<strong>
										Swiss&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. &amp;Anr. Vs Union of India &amp;Ors.
									</strong>
								</em>
								&nbsp;judgment dated&nbsp;25.01.2019.
							</li>
							<li>
								Further, in a recent order of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court dated&nbsp;24.09.2019 in&nbsp;
								<em>
									<strong>
										Committee Of Creditors Of Amtek Auto Limited&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Through Corporation Bank vs. Dinkar T. Venkat&nbsp;
									</strong>
								</em>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Subramanian &amp; Ors.
									</strong>
								</em>
								, the&nbsp;Resolution Professional was permitted to invite the fresh offers and&nbsp;the COC was directed to take a final call on the fresh offers so&nbsp;received, if any, within a time bound manner. The order was&nbsp;passed after considering that a resolution plan was prepared but&nbsp;has failed owing to non-fulfilment of the commitment by the&nbsp;resolution applicant which resulted in expiry of CIRP period&nbsp;available under p 12 of the I&amp;B Code. Further, the Apex&nbsp;Court also considered that as per The Insolvency and Bankruptcy&nbsp;Code (Amendment) Act, 2019 (No. 26 of 2019) with effect from&nbsp;16.08.2019, by which the resolution process may be permitted to&nbsp;be completed within 90 days from the date of the commencement&nbsp;of the Amendment Act and there were eight other parties which&nbsp;have submitted their expression of interest.
							</li>
						</ul>
