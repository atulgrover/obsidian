	<h1>
		If principal amount has been paid during the pendency of CIRP application but that was returned by the Operational Creditor, application u/s 9 of IBC is not maintainable as the spirit of the legislation of the Code is for &lsquo;resolution of debt&rsquo; and not for &lsquo;recovery&rsquo; &ndash; Shah Paper Mills Ltd. Vs. Shree Rama News Print &amp; Papers Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Ahmedabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 30, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this Section 9 application, the Corporate Debtor made an attempt to pay the principal amount twice &ndash; once by sending demand draft and then by transferring the amount through RTGS but that was returned by the Operational Creditor. NCLT Ahmedabad Bench held that: (i) The Operational Creditor&rsquo;s intent of filing p 9 application is not at all resolution of Corporate Debtor but the recovery of its dues. (ii) In the process of resolution, the priority of Operational Creditors are much below the Financial Creditors. (iii) The provisions of the Code are essentially intended to bring the Corporate Debtor to its feet and are not for money recovery proceedings. (iv) IBC cannot be used as a tool of recovery. (v) Rejected the Section 9 application.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The present application has been filed on 28.03.2019 by M/s. Shah Paper Mills Ltd. (Operational Creditor) against the M/s. Shree Rama Newsprint &amp; Papers Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) under Section 9 of the IBC for initiation of CIRP against the Respondent/Corporate Debtor, to appoint Interim Resolution&nbsp;Professional (IRP) and declare the&nbsp;moratorium for making default in payment of outstanding amount of Rs.55,23,253/- along with interest @18% until the date of its realization.
						</li>
						<li>
							In the present case, the Corporate Debtor made an attempt to pay the principal amount twice &ndash; once by sending demand draft and then by transferring the amount through RTGS but that was returned by the Operational Creditor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Operational Creditor wants to pursue application u/s 9 only for payment of interest also on the basis of invoices.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority referred the
						<em>
							 S.S. Polymers Vs. Kanodia Technoplast Ltd.&nbsp;[2019] 193 NCLAT
						</em>
						 and
						<em>
							 Rohit Motawat Vs. Madhu Sharma, proprietor of Hindu Chem Corporation &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2023) 128 NCLAT
						</em>
						 and held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							By considering the subsequent events and decisions relied upon by the Corporate Debtor we are of the considered opinion that the Operational Creditor&rsquo;s intent of filing p 9 application is not at all resolution of Corporate Debtor but the recovery of its dues. It is also noteworthy that in the process of resolution, the priority of Operational Creditors are much below the Financial Creditors. The provisions of the Code are essentially intended to bring the Corporate Debtor to its feet and are not for money recovery proceedings.
						</li>
						<li>
							We rely upon the judgement of Hon&rsquo;ble Principal Bench in the matter of
							<em>
								 Ms. Rita Malhotra and Ors. Vs. M/s. Orris Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 622 NCLT
							</em>
							 wherein it is said that IBC cannot be used as a tool of recovery.
						</li>
						<li>
							As per the direction of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT after considering the subsequent events as placed in I.A. No. 123 of 2023, we are of the view that p 9 Petition in C.P. No.251 of 2019 does not deserve to be admitted therefore, in view of the above observations, I.A. No. 123 of 2023 is allowed and consequently, C.P. No. 251 of 2019 stands
							<strong>
								 rejected
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
