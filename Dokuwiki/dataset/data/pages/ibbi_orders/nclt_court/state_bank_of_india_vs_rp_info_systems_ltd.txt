	<h1>
		Whether attachment of property of Corporate Debtor under PMLA prior to the initiation of CIRP bars admission of Section 7 application under IBC and a Creditor&rsquo;s right of secured interest over the property remains secured &ndash; State Bank of India Vs. R.P. Info Systems Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 20, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In Section 7 application, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT, Kolkata Bench holds that: (i) Properties attached under the PMLA prior to the initiation of the CIRP, should still became available to fulfil objects of the IBC. (ii) PMLA and I&amp;B Code subserve completely different, divergent and distinct purpose. (iii) The subject property which stands attached under the PMLA can be released in the event a Corporate Debtor is admitted by the competent authority in CIRP and a creditor&rsquo;s right of secured interest over the property remains secured, it would still have the right to enforce its secured interest over the property in question. (iv) Irrespective of the prior attachment order under the PMLA, the tainted properties of the Corporate Debtor would always be available to fulfil the object and achieve the goal of IBC. (v) Attachment order under PMLA will not bar admission under IBC. (vi) Hon&rsquo;ble Bench has admitted Section 7 application.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This application/petition has been preferred under Section 7 of the IBC, by the State Bank of India (Applicant/Financial Creditor), against the R.P. Info Systems Ltd. (Respondent/Corporate Debtor), seeking for a direction to initiate the CIRP, in respect of the Corporate Debtor herein.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Ruling on interplay between PMLA and IBC
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority refers following ruling:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Varrsana Ispat Ltd v Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, reported in [2019] 67 NCLAT.
						</li>
						<li>
							Andhra Bank v. Sterling Biotech Ltd.&nbsp;(2019) 470 NCLAT.
						</li>
						<li>
							Rotomac Global Pvt Ltd v Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement&nbsp;[2019] 114 NCLAT.
						</li>
						<li>
							Directorate of&nbsp;Enforcement, New Delhi v Manoj Kumar Agarwal&nbsp;(2021) 182 NCLAT.
						</li>
						<li>
							Kiran Shah, RP of KSL and Industries Ltd v Enforcement&nbsp;Directorate, Kolkata, reported in&nbsp;(2022) 10 NCLAT.
						</li>
						<li>
							<em>
								 Rajiv Chakraborty Resolution Professional of EIEL vs. Directorate of
							</em>
							<em>
								 Enforcement
							</em>
							 reported in&nbsp;(2022) 257 HC
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Thus, it has been succinctly spelt out that
							<strong>
								 properties attached under the PMLA prior to the initiation of the CIRP, should still became available to fulfil objects of the IBC
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p49)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The&nbsp;object of the PMLA is to prevent the suspected from enjoying the&nbsp;fruits of a &ldquo;
							<em>
								 tainted property
							</em>
							 &rdquo; or &ldquo;
							<em>
								 proceeds of crime
							</em>
							 &rdquo;, and not to&nbsp;allow the Government to don the hat of a creditor. Juxtaposed to&nbsp;the above, the object of the IBC essentially is to pay off the&nbsp;creditors of a corporate debtor by way of insolvency resolution or&nbsp;liquidation in a time bound manner maximizing the value of the&nbsp;assets of the corporate debtor and balancing the interest of all&nbsp;stakeholders of it.
							<strong>
								 (p51)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. Gist of the principles
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							i. The PMLA provides for the confiscation of the illegal acquired assets (tainted properties) out of proceeds of crimes to prevent a person from enjoying fruits of such tainted properties or the &ldquo;proceeds of crime&rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							ii. A Provisional Attachment Order (PAO) does not invest in the authority a superior or overriding right in the property.
						</li>
						<li>
							iii. Similarly, the attachment of tainted properties does not divest a person of a right to establish that the properties attached are not out proceeds of crime.
						</li>
						<li>
							iv. A third party having a legitimate interest over such property can always seek its release by showing that its interest in such property was acquired bona fide and for lawful (adequate) consideration.
						</li>
						<li>
							v. An order of attachment under PMLA if it meets with the prerequisites, is as lawful as an action initiated by a bank or a financial institution or a secured creditor for recovery of its dues or enforcement of its secured interest in accordance with RDBA or SARFAESI Act.
						</li>
						<li>
							vi. An order of PMLA is not rendered illegal only because a secured creditor has a prior security interest in the subject property.
						</li>
						<li>
							vii. PMLA does not result in the corporate debtor or the Resolution Professional facing a fait accompli.
						</li>
						<li>
							viii. The statutes provide adequate means and avenues for redressal of claims and grievances.
						</li>
						<li>
							ix. It could be open to a Resolution Professional to approach the competent authorities under the PMLA for such reliefs&nbsp;in respect of tainted properties as may be legally&nbsp;permissible.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 PMLA and I&amp;B Code subserve completely different, divergent and distinct purpose
						</strong>
						 . The rights of the Enforcement Directorate over the properties subject to attachment would stand restricted to the extent as recognised in the judgments rendered in
						<em>
							 Deputy Director Directorate of Enforcement Delhi Vs. Axis Bank and Ors.&nbsp;[2019] 06 HC
						</em>
						 and later in
						<em>
							 Rajiv Chakraborty (Supra)
						</em>
						 .
						<strong>
							 (p54)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 C. Whether attachment of Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s property by the Enforcement Directorate under the PMLA prior to the filing of IBC Section 7 application would bar CIRP admission
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Coming to the issue that has cropped up for determination, in this matter, i.e., whether that property which already stands attached by the Enforcement Directorate under the PMLA prior to the filing of an application under Section 7 of the Code for the initiation of the CIRP, would bar admission of the application bearing the present claim of Financial Creditor.
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In
							<em>
								 Axis Bank (Supra)
							</em>
							 and
							<em>
								 Rajiv Chakrabarty (Supra)
							</em>
							 have been elaborately dealt with the interplay of the PMLA and IBC and have clearly and categorically held that PMLA is a legislation on money laundering, independent of the I&amp;B Code, 2016 and Section 8(8) of the PMLA explicates that a person aggrieved by an attachment order under PMLA can always seek its &ldquo;release&rdquo; and &ldquo;restoration&rdquo; by showing that his interest in the property was acquired bona fide, and PMLA then takes a back seat. Thus, there is no conflict between the two provisions.
							<strong>
								 The subject property which stands attached under the PMLA can be released in the&nbsp;event a Corporate Debtor is admitted by the competent authority&nbsp;in CIRP and a creditor&rsquo;s right of secured interest over the property&nbsp;remains secured, it would still have the right to enforce its secured&nbsp;interest over the property in question.(p56)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Irrespective of the prior attachment order under the PMLA, the tainted properties of the Corporate Debtor would always be available to fulfil the object and achieve the goal of IBC. Attachment order under PMLA will not bar admission under IBC.(p57)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In our considered opinion that since the PMLA attachment order is in regard to the assets of the Corporate Debtor, whereas the debts are almost admitted there is no escape from the conclusion that the application needs to be admitted.
							<strong>
								 (p58)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 D. Conclusions
						</strong>
						<br />
						 In terms of the foregoing discussion, we are of the view that this instant application under Section 7 of the IBC is squarely maintainable and therefore,
						<strong>
							 ALLOW
						</strong>
						 the application filed under Section 7 of the I&amp;B Code, and accordingly, order the initiation of CIRP in respect of the Corporate Debtor.
						<strong>
							 (p59)
						</strong>
