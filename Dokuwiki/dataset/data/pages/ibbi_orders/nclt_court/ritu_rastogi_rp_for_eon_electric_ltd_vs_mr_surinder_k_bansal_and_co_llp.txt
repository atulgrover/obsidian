						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The present application is filed by Resolution Professional of Eon Electric Limited (Applicant) against Mr. Surinder K Bansal, Bansal and Co. LLP, Chartered Accountants (Respondent) under Section 19(2) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 and Rule 11 of NCLT Rules.&nbsp;The applicant has prayed to direct the respondent to cooperate with the applicant and to provide the audit report and audited financial statements for the financial year 2019-2020 related to the corporate debtor in order to make compliance as mandated under Section 17(2)(e) of the Code, 2016 or in alternate to appoint another Chartered Accountant for the purpose of audit of corporate debtor for the financial year 2019-2020.
					</p>
					<p>
						In the third meeting of the CoC, it is resolved to replace the respondent i.e. Statutory Auditor namely M/s Bansal and Co. LLP in terms of Section 28(1)(m) &amp; appointed new statutory auditor for the audit of the financial year 2019-2020. The applicant had requested the respondent to provide a resignation letter along with no objection certificate to file Form ADT-3 with the ROC by email dated 20.04.2021. Since, no reply has been received, the applicant has been sent another email dated 13.05.2021 to do the needful on urgent basis. The respondent has intimated by Email dated 11.05.2021 to the applicant that they had initiated the audit of the financial year 2019-2020 and the fees for the audit services to the corporate debtor is still pending. It is further stated that the respondent by Email dated 04.06.2021 has refused to provide resignation letter and no objection certificate in terms of the decision of the CoC. The applicant has replied by Email dated 10.06.2021 that the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code has an overriding effect and non-issuance of the No objection by Chartered Accountant is unprofessional and unethical.
					</p>
					<p>
						It is submitted that the respondent had requested the applicant to provide information and documents pertaining to the corporate debtor for the completion of the audit process and the applicant has provided the same. The respondent has assured the applicant that the balance sheets for the financial year 2019-2020 shall be provided by 16.08.2021 but the respondent has failed to do so.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the present case, the applicant has sought directions against the statutory auditors of the corporate debtor to provide the audit report and audited financial statements for FY 2019-20. The respondent has also not provided the resignation letter as sought by the applicant by Email dated 20.04.2021 and no audit report and financial statements for the FY 2019-20 has been provided by the statutory auditor. Section 17(2)(e) of the Code casts a duty upon the Resolution Professional for complying with the requirements under any law for the time being in force on behalf of the corporate debtor which includes getting the balance sheets and financial statements of the corporate debtor prepared as stipulated under the law.
					</p>
					<p>
						This is neither a forum for deciding the merits of charges and counter-charges levelled by the parties, nor any effective result will emerge from doing the same. The mandate of this adjudicating authority is to ensure the smooth progress of the CIRP. In response to the allegations of the auditor that the signed financial statements have not been supplied by the applicants, we direct the applicant-Resolution Professional to furnish these signed financial statements and related documents within 15 days of this order. He is further directed to extend all the cooperation required to the auditor to get the audit completed. As clarified in various judicial decisions cited in, the suspended directors or the officers in the employment of the corporate debtor authorized to sign prior to the initiation of the CIRP are required to sign these financial statements. On the other hand, the auditor is reminded of his responsibilities under the Companies Act, 2013 and take necessary steps to complete the audit process and make available within 45 days of this order. If, the auditor not inclined to take up and complete the audit work, he is directed to issue No Objection Certificate to the RP within 7 days of this order to enable him to appoint any other statutory auditor and complete the audit of the corporate debtor in accordance with law.
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the aforesaid discussions, IA No.639/2021 is allowed with aforesaid directions.
