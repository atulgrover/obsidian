	<h1>
		Conditions to exercise residuary jurisdiction under Section 60(5)(c) of IBC to adjudicate upon the contractual dispute between the parties &ndash; Ms. Vineeta Maheshwari RP of M/s Aaj ka Anand Paper Ltd. Vs. M/s Kwality Catering Services &amp; Ors. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 20, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, NCLT Mumbai Bench points out five major conditions to exercise residuary jurisdiction under Section 60(5)(c) of IBC to adjudicate upon the contractual dispute between the parties.
	</p>
</div>
<div>
	<div>
		<div>
			<div>
				<div>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 348 NCLTCase Name:Ms. Vineeta Maheshwari RP Vs. M/s Kwality Catering Services &amp; Ors.Corporate Debtor:M/s Aaj ka Anand Paper Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA -1121/2023 in CP (IB) No.760/MB-IV/2021Judgment Date:18-Jul-23Court/Bench:NCLT Mumbai BenchMember (Judicial):Shri Kishore VemulapalliMember (Technical):Mr. Prabhat KumarCase Status:(2024) 31 NCLAT
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						This is an application filed by the Resolution Professional Ms. Vineeta Maheshwari in the matter of M/s Aaj Ka Anand Papers Limited, the Corporate Debtor under p 60(5) of the IBC and Rule 11 of the NCLT&nbsp;Rules 2016 to seek directions to M/s Kwality Catering Services, the licensee of the premises owned by the Corporate Debtor, to pay outstanding rent of Rs. 38,19,200/- and to vacate the licensed premises, in case such dues are not paid.
					</p>
					<p>
						The following issues emerge for consideration-
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							(i) Whether the NCLT can exercise its residuary jurisdiction under Section 60(5)(c) of the IBC to adjudicate upon the contractual dispute between the parties; and
						</li>
						<li>
							(ii) Whether in the exercise of such a residuary jurisdiction, it can hold that the license agreement came to be determined, and consequently, direct the respondent to evict the licensed premises.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							<em>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</em>
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority found that under p 60(5)(c) of IBC, the tribunal has been conferred with the jurisdiction to entertain and dispose of any question of law or facts &ldquo;
						<em>
							 arising out of or in relation to the Insolvency resolution or liquidation process of Corporate Debtor
						</em>
						 &rdquo; and referred following judgments:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Gujarat Urja Vikas Nigam Ltd. Vs. Mr. Amit Gupta (2021) 44 SC
						</li>
						<li>
							Tata Consultancy Services Ltd. Vs. Vishal Ghisulal Jain, RP, SK Wheels Pvt. Ltd. (2021) 167 SC
						</li>
						<li>
							SICOM Ltd. Vs. Kitply Industries Ltd. : (2023) 236 NCLAT
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And held that from the above judicial decisions, it emerges that &ndash;
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							i. Section 60(5)(c) grants residuary jurisdiction to the NCLT to adjudicate any question of law or fact, arising out of or in relation to the insolvency resolution of the Corporate Debtor;
						</li>
						<li>
							ii. The residuary jurisdiction can be exercised to adjudicate disputes which arise solely from or which relate to the insolvency of the corporate debtor i.e. the nexus with the insolvency of the corporate debtor must exist, as held in
							<em>
								 Gujarat Urja (Supra)
							</em>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							iii. Even if the contractual dispute arises in relation to the insolvency, a party can be restrained from terminating the contract only if it is central to the success of the CIRP. Crucially, the termination of the contract should result in the corporate death of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							iv. An Agreement is an instrument having an effect virtue of law can be overridden u/s 238 of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							v. The existence of a clause for referring the dispute between parties to arbitration does not oust the jurisdiction of the NCLT to exercise its residuary powers under Section 60(5)(c) to adjudicate disputes relating to the insolvency of the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p5.4.1&amp;5.4.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						In the light of above discussion, this bench finds that, issue of termination of the agreement arose subsequent to commencement of CIRP in this case i.e. 31.3.2022. Accordingly, this Tribunal has power to decide on the dispute, even though there exists an Arbitration clause i.e. Clause 31 in the said agreement, as held in the case of
						<em>
							 Indus&nbsp;Biotech Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Kotak India Venture (Offshore) Fund &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2021) 52 SC
						</em>
						 .
						<strong>
							 (p5.4.7)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Now, the question arises whether the dispute, in question, arises from the insolvency and is central to the success of the CIRP, as held in case of
						<em>
							 Tata Consultancy Services Limited (Supra)
						</em>
						 . In the present case, it is not in dispute that the Corporate Debtor is in liquidation, and it is the case of the applicant that the rentals from property, in question, will facilitate in meeting CIRP/liquidation costs; and the Applicant filed this application to seek eviction on the ground that the property can be licensed to a new licensee, thus fetching rentals to the Corporate Debtor. Accordingly, it can not be said that the eviction from the premises is central to the success of the CIRP.
						<strong>
							 (p5.4.8)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Decision on the present case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Bench finds that the facts in case of
							<em>
								 Adinath Jewellery Exports (Supra)
							</em>
							 are distinguishable, as in that case, the license agreement came to an end by efflux of time, and there was no further extension of the license agreement so as to entitle the licensee to occupy the premises owned by the Corporate Debtor. In the present case, the licensee has disputed the adherence to procedure in clause 21.1 of the Agreement to content that it can not be said that the License agreement came to be terminated on account of breach in payment of rent for three consecutive terms, and accordingly the licensor is precluded from the terminating the agreement before expiry of the lock in period contemplated in the said Agreement.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is not in dispute that the Licensee has failed to pay the rent under the License agreement for more than three consecutive months. This Bench had also given an opportunity to the Respondent to clear such outstanding dues, vide its interim-order dated 4.5.2023, and it failed to pay such dues even then also. This Bench feels that the Respondents are relying heavily on the lock-in-period of 5 years, which excludes the licensor to terminate the agreement, but the said stipulation of lock in period of 5 years is to be read in conjunction and subject to clause 21 of the same agreement. The Respondents can not read the clause(s) of the agreement in isolation or in pieces.
						</li>
						<li>
							This Bench directs the Respondents to pay the outstanding rent, having become due upto the date of this order, immediately to the Corporate Debtor, and not to cause any obstruction to the liquidation process.
						</li>
						<li>
							However, as pointed out at Para 5.3.2 above, it can not be said that the procedure contemplated in Clause 21.1 of the Agreement was followed in the manner stated therein. From the perusal of the prayers, it is noticed that the applicant has sought eviction from the premises if the total outstanding rent is not paid immediately in the CIRP account of CD. Accordingly, this bench is of the considered view that the Applicant Corporate Debtor be directed to&nbsp;issue a notice to the Respondents, specifically intimating the Respondent the&nbsp;consequence of non-payment payment of outstanding dues, as are due and payable upto the date of this order, within 7 days, in terms of the clause 21 of the Leave and License Agreement. Thereafter, upon such failure, the Applicant Corporate Debtor shall be at liberty to exercise its right to terminate the said Agreement in terms of Clause 21.1 and evict the Licensee in consequence thereof. In case, the Agreement stands terminated on such failure, the consequences stated in clause 21.1, 21.3 &amp; 21.4 of the Agreement shall follow, and the Licensee shall be a trespasser, liable to be evicted forthwith. Upon such termination of the Agreement, the Police Station Incharge, exercising jurisdiction over the area where the Premises in question is situated, shall assist the Liquidator to take vacant possession of the said premises upon production of this Order.
						</li>
						<li>
							With aforesaid directions in Para 6 &amp; 7, this IA-1121/2023 is partly
							<strong>
								 allowed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p5.5.1, 5.6, 6-8)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
				</div>
