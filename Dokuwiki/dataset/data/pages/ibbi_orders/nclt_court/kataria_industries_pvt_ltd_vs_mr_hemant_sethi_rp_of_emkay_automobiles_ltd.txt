					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Expression of Interest(EOI) filed by the Applicant Consortium has been declared as ineligible by the Resolution Professional(RP) and the same was confirmed by the CoC in terms of the eligibility criteria approved by the CoC and provided under Form-G dated 19.012022. The contention of the RP is that the het worth submitted by the Applicant Company had not been calculated in accordance with the definition of net worth as provided under the Section 2(57) of the Companies Act, 2013 and that on the basis of calculations made as per the definition of net worth under Section 2(57) the net worth falls short of the minimum requirement of Rs. 25 Crore as specified in the eligibility conditions, thereby making the Applicant ineligible. The Applicant is a company which was in operation from before the enactment of the Companies Act, 2013. It has been contended by the RP that as per Section 1(4) of the Companies Act, 2013 which reads as under: &ldquo;
						<em>
							 The provisions of this Act shall apply to (a) Companies incorporated under this Act or under any provisions Company law.
						</em>
						 &ldquo;
					</p>
					<p>
						The calculation of the acceptable net worth of the company would be the one which has been calculated on the basis of the provisions [Section 2(57)] of the Companies Act, 2013 and not by any other methodology. We are of the view that the stand of the RP as above is in accordance with law and satisfies the propriety and the sanctity implicit in a free and fair bidding process. Further the decision has been made by the CoC after deliberating upon all the aspects of the EOI with its commercial wisdom.
					</p>
					<p>
						It is also evident that inspite of the multiple opportunities provided by the CoC and the RP the Applicant failed to file fresh EOI which would conform to eligibility criteria. The claim of Applicant had hence been rejected in exercise of its statutory responsibilities and after the deliberation by the CoC. The arguments presented by the Applicant are not convincing to this bench and we do not find any cause to interfere with the apparently fair bidding process adopted and the commercial decision of CoC which was taken after due deliberation and in the presence of the representative of the Applicant. Our view is further strengthened by the case of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
						<em>
							 K. Sashidhar Vs. Indian Overseas Bank &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2019] 08 SC
						</em>
						 .
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the aforesaid , the present application is devoid of merits, hence the same is dismissed without costs.
						<strong>
							 (p22-23)
						</strong>
					</p>
