		<h1>
			A Retired Partner of a Firm has no right under the IBC to file claim against other Partner for retirement dues- Anil Vora HUF Vs. Kavya Build-Con Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			February 28, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observes that the Operational Creditor and the Corporate Debtor were the Partners of the Firm. This Bench is well aware about the relationship between the Partners with its Partnership Firm and also accepts the contentions of the Operational Creditor with respect to the joint and several liability with the other partners or with the Firm. However, the Bench is of considered view that even the liability of the Corporate Debtor is proved in all aspect, the IBC does not protect the interest or claim of the Partner against another Partner or the Firm. The claim and the cause of action arose on the transaction between the Partners. Therefore, the Petition itself is not maintainable in the eye of law.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Operational Creditor was a Partner in a Partnership Firm M/s Kavya KCD Developers (Firm).&nbsp;The Corporate Debtor is also a partner of the Firm.&nbsp;The Firm approached the Operational Creditor with a proposal whereby the Operational Creditor retire from the Firm and for this purpose it was agreed to execute a Retirement Deed, which was executed on 31.12.2015. As per the said Retirement Deed, the Firm agreed to jointly and severally pay a lumpsum consideration of Rs.75,00,000/- including the amount standing towards the Operational Creditor&rsquo;s credit in the Books of Accounts as on 31.12.2015 in full and final settlement of all the Operational Creditor&rsquo;s claims in Capital, Goodwill Profits and Assets (including movable/immovable properties) and interest in capital of the Firm as also the benefit directly or indirectly attached to the Firm and its business or rights or properties as also its share in net assets of the Firm. The Firm issued a cheque dated 20.01.2016 drawn on Oriental Bank of Commerce. The cheque was dishonoured by a return memo dated 18.04.2016 by the Operational Creditor&rsquo;s Bank with remark Fund Insufficient.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Corporate Debtor submits that the Operational Creditor seeking the enforcement of the Deed of Retirement dated 31.12.2015 executed between the Partners of M/s. Kavya KCD Developers., who is a Partnership Firm duly registered under the provisions of the Partnership Act, 1936. The Operational Creditor and the Corporate Debtor were the Partners of the said Firm. There is no specific provisions under IBC with respect to the proceedings to be initiated against the Partnership Firm.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								Can the Retirement amount arising out of the Partnership Firm constitutes the Operational Debt?
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observes that the Operational Creditor and the Corporate Debtor were the Partners of the Firm. This Bench is well aware about the relationship between the Partners with its Partnership Firm and also accepts the contentions of the Operational Creditor with respect to the joint and several liability with the other partners or with the Firm. However, the Bench is of considered view that&nbsp;
							<strong>
								even the liability of the Corporate Debtor is proved in all aspect, the IBC does not protect the interest or claim of the Partner against another Partner or the Firm. The claim and the cause of action arose on the transaction between the Partners. Therefore, the Petition itself is not maintainable in the eye of law.
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Operational Creditor may be liable to the claims against the Corporate Debtor not under the IBC but under the any other law which provides the remedy to the Operational Creditor.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								The Retired Partner has no right under the IBC to file claim against the Partner or the Firm.&nbsp;
							</strong>
							The Adjudicating Authority relies upon the NCLAT Judgment in&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Gammon India Ltd V. Neelkanth Mansions and Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2018]  103 NCLAT&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							wherein it was held that&nbsp;even if one of the partners or more than one partner is&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor as the amount is due from the partnership firm, the&nbsp;application under p 9 of the Code against one of the partners of&nbsp;such partnership firm will not be maintainable.&nbsp;In view of the above observations, the Adjudicating Authority rejects the Application filed by Operational Creditor under p 9 of the Code.
							<strong>
								(p10-13)
							</strong>
						</p>
