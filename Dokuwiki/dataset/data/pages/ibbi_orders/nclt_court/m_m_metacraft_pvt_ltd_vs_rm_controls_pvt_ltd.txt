	<h1>
		Even if debt was due prior to 24.03.2020 and demand notice was sent prior to 24.03.2020, a petition u/s 9 of IBC cannot be filed against a Corporate Debtor if the amount of unpaid debt is less than the threshold limit of Rs. 1 crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016 &ndash; M/s. M. M. Metacraft Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. R.M. Controls Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IV
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 25, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that the date of filing the petition has to be considered to determine the applicability of threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016 and the date of issue of demand notice or date of default are irrelevant in determining the applicability of threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016. The intent of legislation to fix the threshold limit was to save the Companies from being rotted to NCLT for initiation of CIRP proceedings, due to COIVD-19 effect. The said notification was always prospective in nature but having retrospective also. In the present application the Operational Creditor demanded outstanding operational debt amounting Rs.17,91,274/- and the said amount is much below the threshold limit of Rs. 1 crore as envisaged under p 4 of the Code, 2016 as the date of the filing of the instant petition i.e., 18.09.2020 has to be considered and not the date of the demand notice i.e. 04.02.2020 to be considered.
	</p>
</div>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2022) 659 NCLTCase Name:M/s. M. M. Metacraft Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. R.M. Controls Pvt. Ltd.Operational Creditor:M/s. M. M. Metacraft Pvt. Ltd.Corporate Debtor:M/s. R.M. Controls Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:Company Petition No. (IB)-86(ND)/2021Judgment Date:21-Jul-22Court/Bench:NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IVMember (Judicial):Shri Dharminder SinghMember (Technical):Dr. Binod Kumar Sinha
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The total amount of debt claimed by the applicant from the corporate debtor is Rs. 17,91,274/- which includes Rs. 14,62,264/- as the unpaid operational debt and as interest computed @18% p.a.. The Applicant had issued the demand notice to the corporate debtor on 04.02.2020 demanding outstanding operational debt amounting Rs. 16,37,698 wherein Rs. 14,62,264 is the unpaid operational debt and Rs. 1,75,434 as interest computed @18% on the outstanding principle amount. However, the applicant had filed the instant petition on 1809.2020.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							With reference to the reliance placed by the applicant in case
							<em>
								 Madhusudan Tantia vs. Amit Choraria &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2020) 294 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT has held that the notification dated 24.03.2020 issued by MCA whereby and where under the minimum amount of default was specified as Rs. 1 Crore is only prospective in nature and not retrospective. We are of the view that the date of filing the petition has to be considered to determine the applicability of threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016 and the date of issue of demand notice or date of default are irrelevant in determining the applicability of threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016. The intent of legislation to fix the threshold limit was to save the Companies from being rotted to NCLT for initiation of CIRP proceedings, due to COIVD-19 effect. The said notification was always prospective in nature but having retrospective also.
							<strong>
								 (p7)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							At this juncture, we refer to the Judgement of Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Kerala passed in the matter of
							<em>
								 M/s. Tharakan Web Innovations Pvt. Ltd. Vs. National Company Law Tribunal Kochi Bench and Anr.&nbsp;(2022) 28 HC.
							</em>
							<strong>
								 (p8)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, reference can be made to the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
							<em>
								 Jumbo Paper Products V. Hansraj Agrofresh Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 497 NCLAT
							</em>
							 wherein it was held that the any statute/law can be applied retrospectively only if explicit provision regarding its retrospective application is made in the statute. It is seen that notification of MCA dated 24.3.2020 makes it unambiguously clear that the threshold limit to be considered will be Rs. 1 crore.
							<em>
								 This threshold limit will be applicable for application filed u/s 7 or 9 on or after 24.3.3020 even if the debt is of date earlier than 24.032020.
							</em>
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the light of the reasons quoted above, when we consider the case in hand, we find that in the present application the Operational Creditor demanded outstanding operational debt amounting Rs.17,91,274/- and the said amount is much below the threshold limit of Rs. 1 crore as envisaged under p 4 of the Code, 2016 as the date of the filing of the instant petition i.e., 18.09.2020 has to be considered and not the date of the demand notice i.e. 04.02.2020 to be considered. Hence, even if the amount was due prior to 24.03.2020 and the demand notice was send prior to 24.03.2020, the petition under p 9 of the Code cannot be filed against the Corporate Debtor if the amount of unpaid debt is less than the threshold limit of Rs. 1 crore as stipulated under p 4 of the Code, 2016. Henceforth, for the above-mentioned reasons, the present petition cannot be admitted.
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, the instant petition stands
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 being not maintainable with no order to costs.
							<strong>
								 (p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
