	<h1>
		The monies held by Bank as margin towards the live bank guarantee reverts to Corporate Debtor only in case where the corresponding Bank Guarantee has expired and in no other case &ndash; Dhiren Shantilal Shah Vs. Indian Bank &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		June 12, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that it is not the case of the applicant that the bank guarantee(s), against which the respondent bank is holding fixed deposits as margin money, are not live. The Adjudicating Authority notices that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of Indian Overseas Bank Vs. Arvind Kumar (2020) 285 NCLAT has categorically held that the monies held as margin towards the live bank guarantee reverts to the Corporate Debtor only in case where the corresponding Bank Guarantee has expired and in no other case. Further, it is settled legal proposition that the bank guarantee(s) do not expire with the commencement of moratorium in case of corporate debtor.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The present Application is filed by the Resolution Professional (RP) in the case of M/s Tuticorin Coal Terminal Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) seeking transfer of the amount of Rs. 60,00,000/- kept as Fixed Deposit with the Respondent Bank i.e. Indian Bank on the ground that such Fixed Deposit is wrongly appropriated and/or marked lien towards the Bank Guarantee issued by the Respondent Bank on behalf of the Corporate Debtor as well as against Respondent&rsquo;s claim against the Corporate Debtor arising from the encashment of one of the bank guarantee by the beneficiary during CIRP period.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority&nbsp;finds that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
						<em>
							 IDBI Bank Ltd. Vs.
						</em>
						<em>
							 Indian Oil Corporation Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 35 NCLAT
						</em>
						 held that Bank Guarantees are outside the scope of the moratorium under Section 14 of the Code and Section 3 (31) specifically excludes Performance Bank Guarantees (PBGs). It is not the case of the applicant that the bank guarantee(s), against which the respondent bank is holding fixed deposits as margin money, are not live. This Bench further finds that the applicant has submitted incorrect facts in stating that the respondent bank is holding the FDRs wrongfully and has marked a lien, whereas these FDRs were purchased by the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor as margin money to the Bank Guarantee in accordance with the&nbsp;terms of sanction of such non fund-based facility.
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority notices that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
						<em>
							 Indian Overseas Bank Vs. Arvind Kumar&nbsp;(2020) 285 NCLAT
						</em>
						 has categorically held that the monies held as margin towards the live bank guarantee reverts to the Corporate Debtor only in case where the corresponding Bank Guarantee has expired and in no other case. Further, it is settled legal proposition that the bank guarantee(s) do not expire with the commencement of moratorium in case of corporate debtor. The applicant has also not filed any evidence to suggest that such bank guarantee(s) have expired. In view of the above, this Bench does not find any merit in the present application and is of considered view that the present application (IA-148/2021) deserve to be
						<strong>
							 dismissed
						</strong>
						 .
