		<h1>
			The assets and properties, including any claim, interest therein, of Holding Company held through Subsidiary Company will have to be said to be the property of the Corporate Debtor, for the purpose of CIRP of the Subsidiary Company &ndash; Sanghvi Movers Limited Vs. M/s. Albanna Engineering (India) Pvt. Ltd., Represented by its Ex-Director Mr. Mathew Kavalan &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 31, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority held that the corporate veil between the subsidiaries and holding company are to be lifted depending on facts of each case and no straight formula can be defined. The corporate veil can be lifted for unlimited reasons and it is not only limited to the extent of the cases of fraud, impropriator. Each case needs to be tested with the unique facts of arrangement applicable to it. The instant application is a fit case for piercing &lsquo;Corporate Veil&rsquo;. Accordingly, we came to the same view that lifting of Corporate Veil is very much needed and permitted in the instant case as the promoters/ directors of a company diversify the business in various field by creating several independent entities, call it subsidiaries, with the constitution of common directors and at some point of time if the Group gets financially stressed due to default in repayment of debt, at that juncture a right of recourse is required to be adopted. That is why, the right recourse shall be to examine the necessity of &lsquo;Consolidation&rsquo; as there is a clear case of principal-agent relationship. Section 221 (Agent&rsquo;s lien on principal&rsquo;s property) and Section 222 (Agent to be indemnified against consequences of lawful acts) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 can also be involved in this instant application. As already pointed out, the management and ownership of these companies are same. We have also come to a conclusion that there is no other fall-back mechanism for recovery of dues from the parent company unless they are not ready to settle the dues.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							M/s. Albanna Engineering LLC is a Foreign Company incorporated overseas&nbsp;in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) with limited liability and is also&nbsp;having its project office at Ambalamugal, Ernakulam District, Kerala&nbsp;State. The Respondent, M/s Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited.&nbsp;(BPCL) invited global tenders for the execution of Mechanical Works of&nbsp;VGO HDT Unit and DHDT Unit for Integrated Refinery Expansion&nbsp;Project (IREP) at Kochi Refinery, Kerala in the year 2014. The tenders&nbsp;submitted by the Appellant was accepted by the Respondent BPCL and&nbsp;Purchase Orders were issued to the Appellant. The estimated contract&nbsp;value of the works was Rs. 205,59,20,098/-. For execution of works&nbsp;the Appellant appointed its subsidiary, the Respondent No. 5 (herein) &ndash;M/s. Albanna Engineering (India) Private Limited, under an agreement&nbsp;as sub-contractor and carried out the works through them.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that the corporate veil&nbsp;between the subsidiaries and holding company are to be lifted&nbsp;depending on facts of each case and no straight formula can be&nbsp;defined. The corporate veil can be&nbsp;lifted for unlimited reasons and it is not only limited to the extent&nbsp;of the cases of fraud, impropriator. Each case needs to be tested&nbsp;with the unique facts of arrangement applicable to it.&nbsp;We have referred&nbsp;
							<em>
								Hindustan Construction Company Limited&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								and Ors. Vs. Union of India (UOI) and Ors. [2019]  21 SC
							</em>
							, where the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court have&nbsp;referred to their judgment in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Mobilox Innovations Pvt. Ltd. v.&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Kirusa Software Pvt. Ltd. [2017]  01 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;and stated that Section 5(6) of the Insolvency Code,&nbsp;which defines &lsquo;disputes&rsquo;, read with Section 8(2) of the Insolvency&nbsp;Code, would make it clear that there is no bar to applying an&nbsp;Order VIII-A of the Code of Civil Procedure type procedure to&nbsp;proceedings under the Insolvency Code, so that when his client&rsquo;s&nbsp;sub-contractor triggers the Insolvency Code against his client, his&nbsp;client in-turn should be able to make its principal employer a&nbsp;party to such proceedings, so that the subcontractor may then&nbsp;recover these amounts from the principal employer directly,&nbsp;thereby absolving his client from the clutches of the Insolvency&nbsp;Code.
						</p>
						<p>
							The instant application is a fit case for piercing &lsquo;Corporate Veil&rsquo;.&nbsp;Accordingly, we came to the same view that lifting of Corporate&nbsp;Veil is very much needed and permitted in the instant case as the&nbsp;promoters/ directors of a company diversify the business in&nbsp;various field by creating several independent entities, call it&nbsp;subsidiaries, with the constitution of common directors and at some point of time if the Group gets financially stressed due to&nbsp;default in repayment of debt, at that juncture a right of recourse&nbsp;is required to be adopted. That is why, the right recourse shall be&nbsp;to examine the necessity of &lsquo;Consolidation&rsquo; as there is a clear case&nbsp;of principal-agent relationship. Section 221 (Agent&rsquo;s lien on&nbsp;principal&rsquo;s property) and Section 222 (Agent to be indemnified&nbsp;against consequences of lawful acts) of the Indian Contract Act,&nbsp;1872 can also be involved in this instant application.&nbsp;As already pointed out, the management and ownership of these&nbsp;companies are same. We have also come to a conclusion that&nbsp;there is no other fall-back mechanism for recovery of dues from&nbsp;the parent company unless they are not ready to settle the dues.&nbsp;It is evidently interconnected, interwoven and interlaced to&nbsp;much greater extent. As there is no revenue for M/s Albanna&nbsp;Engineering (India) Private Limited, the due amount could not be recoverable. So, we are of the view that the obligations of each&nbsp;other are also intermingled and is treated as one single economic&nbsp;entity.
						</p>
						<p>
							In the backdrop of aforesaid discussion, it has to be held that the&nbsp;assets and properties, including any claim, interest therein, of&nbsp;Albanna Engineering LLC (Dubai) held through M/s Albanna&nbsp;Engineering (India) Private Limited will have to be said to be the&nbsp;property of the Corporate Debtor, for the purpose of the present&nbsp;CIRP. Further, we have concluded that the bank guarantee of&nbsp;parent company for completing the work for BPCL is done by M/s&nbsp;Albanna Engineering (India) Private Limited (sub-contractors),&nbsp;wherein the applicants, i.e., Sanghvi Movers Limited, MNB Nair,&nbsp;Kerala Metal Distributors and Brothers Engineering are the&nbsp;service providers and material suppliers for engineering projects&nbsp;for BPCL. Therefore, the assets held by them can be said to be&nbsp;&ldquo;its&rdquo; assets i.e., the assets of present Corporate Debtor, which is&nbsp;under the CIRP. Further, the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court Order in the&nbsp;writ petition filed by the parent company may not come in the&nbsp;way of our passing any order in the instant application. The order&nbsp;as informed to us, clearly directed the parent company to get all&nbsp;the injunctions/ garnishee order lifted in the civil courts. Further,&nbsp;as per the agreement between the parent company and the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, the agreement will be construed and enforced&nbsp;in accordance with the laws of India including its recognition of applicable law, as mentioned under clause 17(2) of the&nbsp;agreement. (
							<strong>
								p13)
							</strong>
						</p>
