	<h1>
		The provision of some amount should be made for Operational Creditors as well as Dissenting Financial Creditors, and the amount so provided cannot be NIL | Reference to Section 53(1) of IBC in Sec. 30(2)(b) is only for the purpose of calculating the amount payable to Operational Creditors and Dissenting Financial Creditors, otherwise, there is no place for Sec 53(1) when it comes to resolution of a Corporate Debtor under CIRP &ndash; Shankar Mukherjee and Anr. v. Ravi Sethia, RP of Suasth Healthcare Foundation and Ors. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 5, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Important decision of NCLT Kolkata Bench: (i) Code contemplates a scenario where a provision made to an operational creditor or dissenting financial creditor in a Resolution Plan could be lesser than what they would have got in the event of liquidation in terms liquidation value as per p 53(1). (ii) That is the reason for the word &ldquo;not less than&rdquo; used in Section 30(2)(b). If the legislature wanted to restrict the amount payable to them to liquidation value at the most, then the words &ldquo;not more than liquidation value&rdquo; would have been used. (iii) The code contemplates mandatory allocation to dissenting financial creditors and to operational creditors and the allocation would be the amount provided in the plan or liquidation value whichever is higher and the contention that such creditors can be paid NIL value because liquidation value for them is NIL, would defeat the very purpose of the beneficial amendment made in Section 30(2) of the I&amp;B Code. (iv) On careful examination of Section 30(2)(b) of the I&amp;B Code, 2016 in our view, two legal propositions emerge: (a) Reference to Section 53(1) of the I&amp;B Code is only for the purpose of calculating the amount payable to operational creditors and dissenting financial creditors. Otherwise, there is no place for Section 53 (1) when it comes to the resolution of a corporate debtor under the CIR Process. (b) The provision of some amount should be made for operational creditors as well as dissenting financial creditors, and the amount so provided cannot be NIL.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether a suspended member of the board, the applicant herein is aggrieved by the approval of the resolution plan by the CoC which is pending before this Adjudicating Authority
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Applicant has no locus standi to intervene and file this application challenging the approval of the resolution plan by the CoC. In this regard, we rely on the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India in the case of
							<em>
								 Adi Pherozshah Gandhi vs H.M Seevai
							</em>
							 reported in
							<em>
								 1970 (2) SCC 484
							</em>
							 , wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court has held that grievance must be legal grievance; the applicant must not come merely saying &ldquo;
							<em>
								 I do not like this thing to be done, it must be shown that it tends to his injury or to his damage, in the legal sense of the word.
							</em>
							 &rdquo;
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							One who feels disappointed with the order is not the person aggrieved. He must be disappointed by a benefit that he would have received if the order (plan in this case) had gone the other way. It is very clear that regardless of the plan value or its distribution, the Applicant is not entitled to anything and therefore cannot be an aggrieved party to come before us.
							<strong>
								 (p32)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, we think that there is no necessity to deal with his objections regarding the amount of plan value or liquidation value being more than the plan value or the distribution of plan value.
							<strong>
								 (p33)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of the foregoing observations and findings, we are of the view that this application needs to be dismissed as not maintainable.&nbsp;Accordingly, we are dismissing this application being I.A. (IB) No. 1733/KB/2023.&nbsp;No costs.
							<strong>
								 (p34-36)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 IVN.P (IBC)/34(KB)2023 In I.A. (IB) No. 1551/KB/2023 And IVN.P
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 (IBC)/37(KB)2023 In I.A. (IB) No. 1381/KB/2022
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether an unsecured financial creditor can be paid the NIL amount in a resolution plan
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Approval of a resolution plan is done by the CoC based on its &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo; and the Adjudicating authority has a limited role. All that the Adjudicating Authority is to see whether provision contained in Section 30(2)(b) of IBC and regulations 37, 38 and 39 of IBBI (Insolvency process of corporate persons) Regulations 2016 have been complied before approving the plan.
							<strong>
								 (p48)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							On careful examination of Section 30(2)(b) of the I&amp;B Code, 2016 in our view, two legal propositions emerge:
							<ul>
								<li>
									Reference to Section 53(1) of the I&amp;B Code is only for the purpose of calculating the amount payable to operational creditors and dissenting financial creditors. Otherwise, there is no place for Section 53 (1) when it comes to the resolution of a corporate debtor under the CIR Process.
								</li>
								<li>
									The provision of some amount should be made for operational creditors as well as dissenting financial creditors, and the amount so provided cannot be NIL.
									<strong>
										 (p53)
									</strong>
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							This being a beneficial amendment as observed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court, in our view code contemplates a scenario where a provision made to an operational creditor or dissenting financial creditor in a Resolution Plan could be lesser than what they would have got in the event of liquidation in terms liquidation value as per p 53(1). In such a situation the code provides for provision as per liquidation value.
							<strong>
								 (p54)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Therefore, we are of the view, that is the reason for the word &ldquo;not less than&rdquo; used
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 in Section 30(2)(b). If the legislature wanted to restrict the amount payable to
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 them to liquidation value at the most, then the words &ldquo;not more than liquidation
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 value&rdquo; would have been used.(p56)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In view of the above analysis, we are of the view that the code contemplates
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 mandatory allocation to dissenting financial creditors and to operational creditors
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 and the allocation would be the amount provided in the plan or liquidation value
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 whichever is higher and the contention that such creditors can be paid NIL value
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 because liquidation value for them is NIL, would defeat the very purpose of the
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 beneficial amendment made in Section 30(2) of the I&amp;B Code. Such contention
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 made by Ld. Senior Counsels, in our view, will not be correct proposition in a&nbsp;CIRP proceedings, though the same would be correct in a liquidation proceeding
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 under Section 53(1) of the I&amp;B Code.
								<strong>
									 (p57)
								</strong>
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Commercial wisdom of CoC
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							While the commercial wisdom of the CoC must be held in primacy, this Tribunal cannot act as a mute spectator to endorse and put its seal of approval on whatever decision the COC takes in the name of exercising its &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo;.
							<strong>
								 (p70)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is a settled legal position that distribution of plan value including categorizing different classes of creditors and subclass of creditors based on security, the value of debt etc. should be left to the commercial wisdom of CoC and as long as the plan is not in violation of Section 30(2) the I&amp;B Coe Code and the regulations made thereunder, the Adjudicating Authority will have to approve.
							<strong>
								 (p72)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							While we do see the violation of the said p of the code, we are conscious of the legal position that the Adjudicating Authority cannot direct the allocation of a particular amount to such creditor as the applicant. Therefore, we leave it upon the Committee of Creditors to take a pragmatic and holistic view to allocate a reasonable amount as per its &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo;, against the claim of the applicant.
							<strong>
								 (p73)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, the applications are allowed and disposed of accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p74)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
