		<h1>
			Whether an ex-employee of the Corporate Debtor has any locus standi to file an application under Section 33(3) of the IBC for liquidating the Corporate Debtor when a Resolution Plan has already been approved by NCLT &ndash; N.P. Rajan Vs. Hindustan Newsprint Ltd. &amp; 3 Others &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			February 21, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Tribunal holds that a liquidation proceeding shall be initiated only when the CIRP fails. Section 33 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 sets out the conditions laying down three scenarios wherein liquidation proceedings can be initiated against the Corporate Debtor. In a situation where a Resolution Applicant succeeds as Corporate Debtor but it fails to comply with its assurance in terms of the resolution plan, what step is to be taken has already been laid down in Sub-Section (3) of Section 33 of the I&amp;B Code. We are of the considered opinion that the expression &lsquo;person aggrieved&rsquo; does not include a person who suffers from a psychological or an imaginary injury; a person aggrieved must, therefore, necessarily be one, whose right or interest has been adversely affected or jeopardized.(

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							This application has been filed by an ex-employee of the Corporate Debtor under Section 33(3) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code,2016 seeking the following reliefs:
						</p>
						<p>
							a) That the Hon&rsquo;ble Adjudicating Authority be pleased to allow the present Application by passing an order for liquidation of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern;
						</p>
						<p>
							b) That the Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal may pass an order forfeiting the performance security and to initiate penal action as per p 74 (3) of The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								In the backdrop of the facts, the question which arises for our consideration is as to&nbsp;
								<strong>
									whether the applicant has any locus standi to file the above applications, especially for liquidating the Corporate Debtor when a Resolution Plan has been already approved by this Tribunal and the Resolution Applicant has taken over the Corporate Debtor and its properties, as also the revival of the Corporate Debtor factory is under process.&nbsp;
								</strong>
								If the Corporate Debtor acts in a manner that infringes the norms of the Resolution Plan, then that can be contested. Moreover, any stakeholder&rsquo;s right stands violated as a consequence of this infringement, he can make an application to the Adjudicating Authority for liquidation of the Corporate Debtor as per Section 33(3) of the Code.
								<strong>
									(p33-34)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								A liquidation proceeding shall be initiated only when the CIRP fails. Section 33 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 sets out the conditions laying down three scenarios wherein liquidation proceedings can be initiated against the Corporate Debtor. In a situation where a Resolution Applicant succeeds as Corporate Debtor but it fails to comply with its assurance in terms of the resolution plan, what step is to be taken has already been laid down in Sub-Section (3) of Section 33 of the I&amp;B Code. We are of the considered opinion that the expression &lsquo;
								<strong>
									person aggrieved
								</strong>
								&rsquo; does not include a person who suffers from a psychological or an imaginary injury; a person aggrieved must, therefore, necessarily be one, whose right or interest has been adversely affected or jeopardized.
								<strong>
									(p35-36)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is significant to point out that pre-occupation of the Code with &lsquo;timely resolution of insolvency&rsquo; is an important factor. In so far as Liquidation is concerned, it destroys the organizational capital etc. Code allows Liquidation only on failure of CIRP and it facilitates/encourages resolution in several manners.
								<strong>
									(p37)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								At this stage, it is relevant to point out the submission of the ex-Resolution Professional (R4) that the total admitted claim of FC is 209.09 Crore, whereas, the liquidation value of the Corporate Debtor is Rs.162.70, and if Corporate Debtor goes into liquidation, the amount payable to the Applicant being an employee is NIL under Section 53(1). However, in this case the applicant herein has received the eligible amount as per the CIRP from the RP and thereafter came forward with this application for liquidation. In such an event, the applicant himself will be at a loss, as he has to return the amount received, in case, the company is put under liquidation.
								<strong>
									(p38)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								This Bench approved the Resolution Plan submitted by Kerala Industrial Infrastructure Development Corporation (KINFRA), a Statutory authority of the Government of Kerala pursuant to the approval of the Resolution Plan by the COC with 92.72% voting rights. The claim of the applicant was also settled in accordance with the Resolution Plan on 19th March 2021 paying Rs.12,87,926 within 45 days as provided under the Resolution Plan. The mere allegation that CoC has not constituted the Monitoring Committee and that the conduct of the first meeting of the purported Board would not hold water.
								<strong>
									(p39)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								In the light of foregoing detailed discussions, this Tribunal, taking note of the entire conspectus of the attendant facts and circumstances of the instant case in an encircling manner and also keeping in mind the plea taken by the ex- Resolution Professional that out of 145.60 Crores, 144.96 Crore has been disbursed to the stakeholders immediately and balance 0.64 Crore is pending in the accounts of Hindustan Newsprint Limited due to incorrect account details of certain claimants, returned amount due to dormant account details, for which the communication has been sent to claimants to the available Email. The ex-Resolution Professional to disburse the balance amount finding out the correct account details and complete the process without further delay.
								<strong>
									(p40)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								With regard to the contention of the applicant regarding bringing a new industrial park in the name of Kerala Rubber Products, since the same is considered to be a part of the action plan of the Resolution Applicant at Chapter V of the Plan, bringing a new industrial establishment for the purpose of feasibility and viability of the Plan, in the unused land of the Corporate Debtor was already approved by the CoC in its 10th meeting held on 24.12.2020 with 92.72% voting share and simultaneously approved by this Tribunal on 29.01.2021, we find that no deviation in the Resolution Plan has been made by the Resolution Applicant. In view of what is stated above, we do not find any merit in M.A(IBC)/25/ KOB/2021 filed for liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. Hence, M.A(IBC)/25/ KOB/2021 is dismissed. Since MA (IBC) 25/ KOB/ 2021 has been dismissed, no orders are necessary in IA (IBC)/ 190/ KOB/ 2021 and IA (IBC)/151/ KOB/ 2021.
								<strong>
									(p41-42)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
