	<h1>
		Advance deposit against lease rents is Operational Debt, not Financial Debt &ndash; M/s Spicejet Ltd. Vs. M/s Affordable Infrastructure and Housing Projects Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IV
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 17, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in Consolidated Construction Consortium Ltd. Vs. Hitro Energy Solutions Pvt. Ltd. (2022) 09 SC clearly laid down at any advance payment has been made for providing goods and services, that would fall within the definition of Operational Debt'. Herein, the applicant paid money Rs. 1.20 crore to the corporate debtor for taking the premise situated in Gurgaon on lease and the said amount was not repaid as the respondents failed to execute the lease-deed. In view of the law laid down by the Hon'ble Apex Court, the said amount was taken up by the respondent-corporate debtor towards goods and services to be provided to the applicant herein, therefore, the same falls within the definition of 'Operational Debt'.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India in
						<em>
							 Consolidated Construction Consortium Ltd. Vs. Hitro Energy Solutions Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 09 SC
						</em>
						 clearly laid down at any advance payment has been made for providing goods and services, that would fall within the definition of Operational Debt&rsquo;. Herein, the applicant paid money Rs. 1.20 crore to the corporate debtor for taking the premise situated in Gurgaon on lease and the said amount was not repaid as the respondents failed to execute the lease-deed. In view of the law laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court, the said amount was taken up by the respondent-corporate debtor towards goods and services to be provided to the applicant herein, therefore, the same falls within the definition of &lsquo;Operational Debt&rsquo;. So far as &lsquo;Operational Debt&rsquo; is concerned, the applicant was entitled to file an application under Section 9 of the and not under Section 7 of the Code. Further, in the case of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
						<em>
							 Vibrus Homes Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Ashimara Housing Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2022) 299 NCLAT
						</em>
						 vide judgment 22.04.2022 had clarified that security deposit relating to transaction in immovable will fall within the definition of Operational Debt.
					</p>
					<p>
						Further, it is also observed in this context that advance made of Rs. 1.2 crore to the respondent Corporate Debtor was without any interest accrued thereon, therefore, the same does not come to the purview of the definition of &lsquo;Financial-Debt&rsquo; as 5(8) of the Code as the same was not for the time value for money, as defined under Section 5(8) of the Code. No doubt, Ld. Counsel for the applicant relied upon of citations i.e.,
						<em>
							 Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd. &amp; Anr. vs. UOI &amp; ors.; [2019] 13 SC
						</em>
						 ,
						<em>
							 Orator Marketing Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Samtex Desinz,&nbsp;(2021) 68 SC
						</em>
						 &amp;
						<em>
							 Nikhil Mehta &amp; Sons Vs. AMR Infrastructure
						</em>
						<em>
							 Ltd.; [2017] 02 NCLAT
						</em>
						 , but the same have no in the present matter, in view of the law laid down by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India in the matter of
						<em>
							 M/s. Consolidated Construction Consortium Limited (Supra)
						</em>
						 , as a less Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
						<em>
							 Ashimara Housing (Supra)
						</em>
						 . Although, on behalf of the applicant, earlier a petition under Section 9 of Code was filed, but the same was dismissed by this Tribunal vide order dated 21.05.2019. The said order was challenged before the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT, but later on the said appeal was withdrawn voluntarily. Hence, the said order has attained finality. Moreover, after passing the said order, one application was filed on behalf of the applicant herein for clarification the order dated 1802.2020. Accordingly, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT vide order dated 03.11.2020 granted liberty to the applicant herein to approach Adjudicating Authority. However, after passing the aforesaid judgement by Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India, the present petition under Section 7 of the Code qua the &ldquo;advance payment&rdquo; for the goods and services is not maintainable as the said advance payment does not come under the purview of definition &lsquo;Financial Debt&rsquo; rather the same amounts to &ldquo;Operational Debt&rdquo;.
					</p>
					<p>
						In sequel of the above-said discussion, this Tribunal is of the view that the present petition under Section 7 of the Code is not maintainable. Accordingly, the present petition u/s 7 stands rejected, with no order as to costs.
