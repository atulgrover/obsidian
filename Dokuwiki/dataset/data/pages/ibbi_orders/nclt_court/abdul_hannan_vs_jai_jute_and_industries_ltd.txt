	<h1>
		Whether a Police Complaint, prior to the issuance of the Demand Notice under Section 8 of IBC prosecuting a dispute regarding the supply of inferior goods and/or service is a pre-existing dispute? | Whether the issue relating to forgery and fabrication can be decided in a summary proceeding under I&amp;B Code &ndash; Abdul Hannan Vs. Jai Jute and Industries Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		March 9, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) The parties agreed not to proceed any further with respect the said police complaint. No evidence of withdrawal or closure of complaint is placed before us. The complaint being a fact and the allegations levelled therein being lodged in 2017, i.e., long prior to this company petition. (ii) The Police Complaint, prior to the issuance of the statutory notice of demand under Section 8 of the I&amp;B Code, prosecuting a dispute regarding the supply of inferior goods and/or service is a &ldquo;pre-existing dispute&rdquo;. (iii) In a summary proceeding under the I&amp;B Code, the dispute relating to forgery and fabrication of document cannot be adjudicated by this Adjudicating Authority.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The instant application is filed under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 by one Abdul Hannan, (Operational Creditor/Applicant) against M/s. Jai Jute and Industries Limited, (Corporate Debtor/Respondent) seeking for a direction to commence the CIRP.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Operational Creditor has supplied the raw jute and issued several bills of its service from 08.05.2017 to 14.07.2017.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Complaint before the Posta Police Station against the Operational Creditor along with others was lodged on 17.07.2017 alleging forgery, manufactory of false documents, short supply of raw jute, conspiracy with the employees of the Corporate Debtor to manufacture and fabricate inspection reports, inferior quality of raw jute is a fact which cannot be denied by the Operational Creditor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Settlement agreement dated 20.07.2017 made between the applicant and the respondent wherein both the parties agreed to and decided that the Corporate Debtor should pay the amount of Rs. 66 Lakh to the Operational Creditor in instalments within a period of a year from the execution of the settlement agreement sans any levy of interest, finds mention in case of the complaints lodged before police in the year of 2017.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor may have submitted the postdated security cheques in favour of the Operational Creditor subject pursuant to the settlement agreement.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, both the parties had agreed to execute the settlement agreement that the Corporate Debtor would withdraw its complaint dated 17.07.2017 against the Operational Creditor and would not proceed further, hence there was no further follow up of the complaint.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the backdrop of given facts the issues that fell for determination:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							(i) Whether a Police Complaint can be considered as preexisting dispute.
						</li>
						<li>
							(ii) Whether the issue relating to forgery and fabrication can be decided in a summary proceeding under I&amp;B Code.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue 1: Whether a Police Complaint can be considered as preexisting dispute
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Hon&rsquo;ble Adjudicating Authority refers following judgments:
							<ul>
								<li>
									<em>
										 Sherbahadur D. Yadav Vs. M/s. Rohan Dyes and Intermediates Ltd. in&nbsp;(2022) 78 NCLAT
									</em>
								</li>
								<li>
									<em>
										 Mr. Anil J. Nemaavarkar vs. M/s.&nbsp;Kumar Builders Mumbai Realty Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 270 NCLAT
									</em>
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							And holds that in the case at hand, the Corporate Debtor made a complaint on 17.07.2017 before the Posta Police Station by alleging for supplying inferior quality of goods (raw jutes) by the Operational Creditor. However, the record has been placed before us that the said dispute was settled through a Settlement Agreement on 20.07.2017 and accordingly, the parties agreed not to proceed any further with respect the said police complaint. No evidence of withdrawal or closure of complaint is placed before us. The complaint being a fact and the allegations levelled therein being lodged in 2017, i.e., long prior to this company petition.
							<strong>
								 (p30.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Bench concludes that
							<strong>
								 the Police Complaint, prior to the issuance of the statutory notice of demand under Section 8 of the I&amp;B Code, prosecuting a dispute regarding the supply of inferior goods and/or service is a &ldquo;pre-existing dispute&rdquo;.(p30.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue 2: Whether the issue relating to forgery and fabrication can be decided in a summary proceeding under I&amp;B Code
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Hon&rsquo;ble Adjudicating Authority refers following judgments:
							<ul>
								<li>
									<em>
										 Radha Exports (India) Pvt. Limited vs. K.P. Jayaram reported at&nbsp;[2020] 19 SC.
									</em>
								</li>
								<li>
									<em>
										 Jaginder Singh Lather v. AU Small Finance Bank Ltd. reported at [2018] 71 NCLAT
									</em>
								</li>
								<li>
									<em>
										 Shelendra Kumar Sharma v. DSC Ltd., reported in&nbsp;(2019) 482 NCLAT
									</em>
								</li>
								<li>
									Satori Global Limited v. Shailja Krishna reported at
									<em>
										 (2023) 360 NCLAT
									</em>
								</li>
								<li>
									NCLT, New Delhi Bench in the case of Shri T.R. Arya v. Dilawari Motors Pvt. Ltd. reported in&nbsp;(2024) 44 NCLT
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							And holds that
							<strong>
								 in a summary proceeding under the I&amp;B Code, the dispute relating to forgery and fabrication of document cannot be adjudicated by this Adjudicating Authority.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							To examine an application under Section 9 of the I&amp;B Code, the Adjudicating Authority requires to determine whether there is an &ldquo;operational debt&rdquo; exceeding the threshold limit as prescribed, whether the documentary evidence furnished with the application substantiating the debt is due and payable and whether there is existence of a dispute between the parties before the receipt of the demand notice of the unpaid operational debt in relation to such dispute. (
							<em>
								 Mobilox Innovations Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Kirusa Software Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2017) 01 SC
							</em>
							 )(
							<strong>
								 p32
							</strong>
							 ).
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The plea of the Corporate Debtor regarding the dispute is not a moon shine defence and there are pre-existing disputes between the parties regarding the supply of goods.
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, this Company Petition being C.P. (IB) No. 154/KB/2022 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p33-34)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
