	<h1>
		NCLT issues show cause notice, why penalty stipulated u/s 65 of IBC, 2016 shall not be imposed on Assenting CoC members, who voted in favour of Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor without even exploring the possibility of resolution &ndash; Mr. Pawan Kumar Goyal, IRP Vs. Alchemist XXXVII &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court- II
	</h1>
	<div>
		March 20, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT held that until EOI in FORM-G is published, there is no mechanism under the regime of IBC to discover Prospective Resolution Applicants for a Corporate Debtor. Without publishing Form-G, CoC could not have been in a position to formulate an opinion that there were no prospective buyers available for the CD. The scheme of IBC gives every Corporate Debtor a fair chance to stand on their own feet and to come out of financial distress. That is why every Corporate Debtor must go through the IBC-mandated CIR process before facing the liquidation proceedings.
	</p>
</div>
<div>
	<div>
		<div>
			<div>
				<div>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 106 NCLTCase Name:Mr. Pawan Kumar Goyal, IRP Vs. Alchemist XXXVIICorporate Debtor:M/s SARE Realty Projects Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:IA. No. 3818/ND/2021 in Company Petition No. (IB)-684(ND)/2020Judgment Date:16-Mar-23Court/Bench:NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-IIMember (Judicial):Shri Bachu Venkat Balaram DasMember (Technical):Shri L.N. GuptaCase Status:Set aside by NCLAT, reported in&nbsp;(2024) 40 NCLAT.
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The present IA No. 3818 of 2021 has been filed by the IRP of the Corporate Debtor M/s. SARE Realty Projects Private Limited under Section 33(2) of IBC, 2016 seeking pass an order for Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor under Section 33(2) read with explanation and in terms of sub-clauses (i), (ii) and (iii) of clause (b) of sub-p (1) of Section 33 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
						</li>
						<li>
							In this case, Form-G (Expression of Interest) although prepared by the IRP and placed before the CoC in its 2nd Meeting, was never approved by it and published. Rather, the CoC proposed an early liquidation of the Corporate Debtor and approved the same in its 5th Meeting.
						</li>
						<li>
							The IRP has filed its written submissions and stated that the CoC in its commercial wisdom has passed the resolution for recommending the liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. The IRP has relied upon the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
							<em>
								 Sunil S. Kakkad v. Atrium Infocom Private Limited &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2020] 42 NCLAT
							</em>
							 to demonstrate that CoC can directly pass a resolution for Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor even without publication of Form-G.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The CoC has not taken any steps for the resolution of the Corporate Debtor. Rather, the CoC right from its second meeting held on 27.05.2021 started considering the proposal for an early Liquidation of the CD at the instance of Mr. Stuti Mitra of ACRE. It is further noticed that the IRP was never confirmed as RP despite the CoC having 05 meetings, for reasons which are not available on record. These events show that the CoC was being driven in a particular direction with a pre-set mind.
						<strong>
							 (p13)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						From the minutes of the 2nd meeting of CoC held on 27.05.2021 and the subsequent email dated 21.08.2021, it is observed that the early liquidation was proposed due to the non-availability of any prospective buyer for the CD.
						<strong>
							 In our view, until EOI in FORM-G is published, there is no mechanism under the regime of IBC to discover Prospective Resolution Applicants for a Corporate Debtor.
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 Without publishing Form-G, CoC could not have been in a position to formulate an opinion that there were no prospective buyers available for the CD. The scheme of IBC gives every Corporate Debtor a fair chance to stand on their own feet and to come out of financial distress. That is why every Corporate Debtor must go through the IBC-mandated CIR process before facing the liquidation proceedings. Hence, in our considered view, without taking any steps for seeking resolution of the Corporate Debtor, the CoC has acted contrary to the Scheme of IBC.(p14)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						At this juncture, we consider it appropriate to refer to the Judgement of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of
						<em>
							 Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr. Vs. Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) 03 SC
						</em>
						 , in which the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court observed that:
						<strong>
							 (p15)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;11. &hellip;&hellip;&hellip;What is interesting to note is that
							<strong>
								 the Preamble does not, in any manner, refer to liquidation, which is only availed of as a last resort if there is either no resolution plan or the resolution plans submitted are not up to the mark.
							</strong>
							 Even in liquidation, the liquidator can sell the business of the corporate debtor as a going concern.&rdquo;
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						(Emphasis placed)
					</p>
					<p>
						We are fully aware that the CoC in its commercial wisdom has approved the liquidation of the CD. However, in the background and context of the present matter, we refer to the Judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT passed in the matter of
						<em>
							 Hero Fincorp Limited vs. M/s Hema Automotive Private Limited&nbsp;(2023) 21 NCLAT
						</em>
						 , which, reads thus:
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;13. There is no doubt that in Section 33, sub-ps (1) and (2) legislature has used the expression &ldquo;shall&rdquo;. However, the obligation of the Adjudicating Authority to direct for liquidation shall rise only when decision of the CoC is in accordance with the Code. Judicial review of the decision of the CoC in a particular case is not precluded. In Sreedhar Tripathy, it has been clearly held that judicial review of the decision of the CoC is not precluded and it depends on facts of each case.&rdquo;
						</em>
						<strong>
							 (p16)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Had the Corporate Debtor was not having any Assets; it would be justified that CoC had recommended early Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor. However, in the instant case, the Corporate Debtor is a real estate company having projects and assets, by not taking any steps for the Resolution of the Corporate Debtor on the premise that there are no bids available for the Corporate Debtor, without actually publishing the Form-G, in our view, is arbitrary and unsustainable. Further, non-cooperation from the suspended board of management, which as per the submissions of the IRP have resigned
						<em>
							 en masse
						</em>
						 , is no ground for recommending an early liquidation of the Corporate Debtor.
						<strong>
							 (p17)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the aforesaid discussion, we are of the prima facie opinion that the present IA for the Liquidation of CD has been filed with malicious intent, and it is a fit case for issuance of show cause notice to the Assenting CoC members jointly, who voted in favour of the Liquidation of the CD without even exploring the possibility of resolution of the Corporate Debtor, that as to why the penalty stipulated under Section 65 of IBC, 2016 shall not be imposed on them. Let the Assenting members of the CoC file a joint reply to the show cause notice within 2 weeks from the passing of this order.
						<strong>
							 (p18)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Simultaneously,
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							a) the IRP is also directed to bring on record the Valuation reports of the Corporate Debtor,
						</li>
						<li>
							b) the list of both movable and immovable assets of the Corporate Debtor along with Fair Market Value and Liquidation value of the CD,
						</li>
						<li>
							c) the date on which the security interest was created in the assets of the Corporate Debtor,
						</li>
						<li>
							d) who is presently having possession over those assets /projects of the Corporate Debtor; whether they are in possession of IRP, or in the possession of the Creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						Let a response to these queries be filed by the IRP by filing a Separate Affidavit within 2 weeks from the passing of this order.
						<strong>
							 (p19)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						&nbsp;It is made clear that we have not finally adjudicated the liquidation Application, the fate of which would depend upon the outcome of the Section 65 proceedings initiated against the Assenting CoC members.&nbsp;List the matter before the Regular Bench for further hearing on&nbsp;17.04.2023.
						<strong>
							 (p20-21)
						</strong>
					</p>
