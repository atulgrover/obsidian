	<h1>
		Filing of default information with Information Utility in compliance of Rule 20(1A) of IBBI (Information Utilities) Regulations 2017 is not mandatory compliance | Whether simultaneous CIRP can be initiated against Principal Borrower and its Corporate Guarantor &ndash; State Bank of India Vs. Anupriya Management Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 17, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench hold that: (i) Financial Creditor can also prove default with own records as provided in Sec 7(3) of the Code which says that the Financial Creditor shall, along with the application furnish the record of the default recorded with the information utility or such other record or evidence of default as may be specified. (ii) The Financial Creditor can produce certified copies of the entries in the relevant account in the banker&rsquo;s book as defined in clause (3) of Section 2 of the Bankers Book Evidence Act 1891, in this Financial Creditor has furnished the same and thus no fault can be attributed. (iii) The Corporate Debtor has built a few apartments on the very same land as a Joint venture Partner and is in the process of seeking Liquidation. When the Corporate Debtor himself could not seek any resolution plan as a builder, the question of the Corporate Guarantor getting Resolution Plan does not arise.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This instant application is filed under Section 7 of the IBC, by State Bank of India(Financial Creditor/Applicant) against Anupriya Management Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Guarantor/Respondent) seeking direction to initiate CIRP&nbsp;in respect of the Corporate Guarantor.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Records of default
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The only relevant issue that has been taken is on the non-compliance of Rule 20 (1A) of IBBI (Information Utilities) Regulations 2017, about non-filing of &ldquo;default information&rdquo; with information utility.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is not mandatory compliance. Financial Creditor can also prove default with own records as provided in Sec 7(3) of the Code which says that&nbsp;the Financial Creditor shall, along with the application furnish the record of the&nbsp;default recorded with the information utility or such other record or evidence of&nbsp;default as may be specified. In terms of Regulation 2A of CIRP&nbsp;Regulation, 2016. The Financial Creditor can&nbsp;produce certified copies of the entries in the relevant account in the banker&rsquo;s book&nbsp;as defined in clause (3) of Section 2 of the Bankers Book Evidence Act 1891, in&nbsp;this Financial Creditor has furnished the same and thus no fault can be attributed.
							<strong>
								 (p22-23)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. Whether the simultaneous CIRP can be initiated against the
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 Principal Borrower and its Corporate Guarantor
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor, Avani Projects &amp; Infrastructure Ltd. (now under CIRP vide an order dated March 19, 2013) had entered into a joint venture agreement with the Respondent for the development of real estate and the Financial Creditor, SBI has granted a loan and various credit facilities to the Corporate Debtor since 2013, where the Respondent herein was the Corporate Guarantor of the Corporate Debtor. At this juncture, it would be appropriate to go through the proviso laid down under Sections 127 and 128 of the Contract Act, 1872, as well as Section 60(2) of the I&amp;B Code, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p24-25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble Bench refers:
							<ul>
								<li>
									Laxmi Pat Surana v. Union Bank of India&nbsp;(2021) 53 SC
									<br />
									 State Bank of India v. Athena Energy Ventures Private Limited&nbsp;(2020) 344 NCLAT.
								</li>
								<li>
									Mohan Kumar Garg v. Omkara Assets Reconstruction Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 547&nbsp;NCLAT
								</li>
								<li>
									Hi-Tech Designs Pvt. Ltd. v. Sri Sai Car Sales Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 338&nbsp;NCLT
								</li>
								<li>
									IFCI Limited v. NTCIL Infrastructure Private Limited&nbsp;(2023) 774&nbsp;NCLT
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, refers to the observation of the Insolvency Law Committee in its Report, dated February 20, 2020, at Pages 30-31 in Para 7.
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of the foregoing discussions and decisions, no reason to dismiss the application on the other grounds concassed by Corporate Guarantor. The Corporate Debtor has built a few apartments on the very same land as a Joint venture Partner and is in the process of seeking Liquidation. When the Corporate Debtor himself could not seek any resolution plan as a builder, the question of the Corporate Guarantor getting Resolution Plan does not arise.
							<strong>
								 (p29)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the light of the facts stated in the application bearing Company Petition (IB) No. 18/KB/2023, and the evidence placed on record and the discussion hereinabove, we
