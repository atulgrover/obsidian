	<h1>
		When scheme of compromise and arrangement proposed by Applicant is rejected at its first motion by Creditors, Proviso to Rule 2B(3) of Liquidation Process Regulations 2016 does not apply and Liquidator is not entitled to claim his fees and other costs from the Applicant &ndash; Radhasiriya Properties Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Jai Narayan Gupta Liquidator of Barcle Enterprises Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 11, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, Applicant who is a proponent of a composite scheme of compromise and arrangement has deposited Rs. 23,01,000/- and however, the scheme has been rejected by the Creditors. The Liquidator has refused to refund the amount to the applicant. Issue before the NCLT is &ldquo;whether the Liquidator is entitled to claim his fees and other costs in relation to liquidation, from the Applicant who is a proponent of a composite scheme of compromise and arrangement, when the scheme proposed by him is rejected by the creditors&rdquo;. NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) A combined going through of Regulation 2B (3) of the IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016 and Section 230(6) of the Companies Act, 2013 envisages that if any cost incurred by the Liquidator, in relation to compromise or arrangement sanctioned by the Tribunal, shall be borne by the corporate debtor. (ii) Proviso to Rule 2B(3) of the Liquidation Process regulations 2016, provides for bearing of cost by the parties who proposed compromise or arrangement only when such a scheme is not sanctioned by the Tribunal under sub p (6) of Section 230 of the Companies Act. (iii) The Scheme proposed by the Applicant was rejected unanimously at its first motion of compromise or arrangement and this Adjudicating Authority did not have any scope to sanction the scheme and therefore there is no case made out by the liquidator for invoking proviso to Rule 2B(3) of liquidation process regulations 2016. (iv) Thus, we are in view that the all the costs, incurred by the Liquidator in relation to compromise or arrangement including liquidator fee were wrongly claimed from the Applicant.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Facts of the case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In this case, since no Resolution Plan was received in CIRP, the Adjudicating Authority passed an order of Liquidation of the Corporate Debtor (Barcle Enterprises Ltd.).
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant (Radhasiriya Properties Pvt. Ltd.) submitted its Scheme of Compromise and Arrangement to the Liquidator and upon request from the Liquidator, the Applicant deposited
							<strong>
								 Rs. 7,88,280/-
							</strong>
							 in the account of Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Liquidator accepted the proposed Scheme of Compromise and Arrangement and filed an application being I.A. praying NCLT to direct the Liquidator to conduct the meeting of the Creditor under Regulation 2B of the IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulation, 2016 read with Section 230(1) of the Companies Act, 2013.
						</li>
						<li>
							Vide an order dated
							<strong>
								 06.01.2023
							</strong>
							 , NCLT allowed the Application bearing I.A. (IB) and directed the Liquidator to hold a meeting of the Creditors u/s. 230(1) of the Companies Act, 2013.
						</li>
						<li>
							On 02.02.023, due to incessant demands of the Liquidator to make payment, the Applicant transferred Rs. 5,31,000/- with an outstanding that the said amount would be adjusted against the final payment to be made under the Scheme proposed. On 03.02.2023, the Applicant received another email from the liquidation demanding a further sum of money towards liquidation cost and on 16.02.2023, the Applicant made a payment of Rs. 6,00,000/- and on 28.02.2023, the Applicant made again payment of Rs. 4,69,000/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							On 01.03.2023, the Liquidator informed the Applicant that a meeting of creditors was held on February 17, 2023, wherein the Scheme had been
							<strong>
								 rejected
							</strong>
							 by the Creditors.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant wrote a letter on 21.03.2023 to the Liquidator requesting him to forthwith refund the aggregated amount of
							<strong>
								 Rs. 23,88,280/-
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							This instant application is filed under Section 60(5) of the IBC by Applicant against Jai Narayan Gupta, Liquidator of the Corporate Debtor seeking the relief that to direct the Liquidator to forthwith refund the amount of Rs. 23,88,280/- to the Applicant.
						</li>
					</ul>

						<em>
							<strong>
								 Submission of the Liquidator
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>

							The Liquidator has relied upon various statutory provisions and claimed that he has rightly taken money from the Applicant. In the Reply Affidavit, he has furnished a detail of the liquidation cost during the period of compromise or arrangement in which Rs. 23,01,000/- Liquidator fee and other publication, IA filing etc. fee and charges, totaling Rs. 24,12,172/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Ld. Counsel for the Liquidator claims that the overall costs uncured during the liquidation process till 17.02.2023 amounts to Rs. 24,12,172/- in which, the applicant has paid Rs. 23,88,280/- as he claims. Thus, the applicant is liable to remit the rest amount of Rs. 23,892/- to the Respondent as Liquidation Cost.
						</li>
					</ul>

						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether the Liquidator is entitled to claim his fees and other costs in relation to liquidation, from the Applicant who is a proponent of a composite scheme of compromise and arrangement, when the scheme proposed by him is rejected by the creditors.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Adjudicating Authority referred Section 34(9) and 34(8) of the Code, Regulation 2(ea) and Regulation 2B(3) of the IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016, Section 230(6) of the Companies Act, 2013 and held that a
							<strong>
								 combined going through of Regulation 2B (3) of the IBBI
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016 and Section 230(6) of the
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Companies Act, 2013 envisages that if any cost incurred by the
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Liquidator, in relation to compromise or arrangement sanctioned by
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 the Tribunal, shall be borne by the corporate debtor.(p8.1-8.5)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Proviso to Rule 2B(3) of the Liquidation Process regulations 2016, provides for bearing of cost by the parties who proposed compromise or arrangement only when such a scheme is not sanctioned by the Tribunal under sub p (6) of Section 230 of the Companies Act.
							<strong>
								 (p8.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is evident that pursuant to the Order dated 06.01.2023, the Liquidator&nbsp;conducted a meeting among the Creditors of the Corporate Debtor on 17.02.2023 and the scheme was voted upon by 95.58% of the creditors by value wherein the proposal was rejected by the majority of the creditors and the same was intimated to the Applicant vide an email dated March 01, 2023.
							<strong>
								 (p8.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 The
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Scheme proposed by the Applicant was rejected unanimously at its
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 first motion of compromise or arrangement and this Adjudicating
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Authority did not have any scope to sanction the scheme and therefore
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 there is no case made out by the liquidator for invoking proviso to
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Rule 2B(3) of liquidation process regulations 2016 .Thus, we are in
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 view that the all the costs, incurred by the Liquidator in relation to
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 compromise or arrangement including liquidator fee were wrongly
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 claimed from the Applicant.(p8.6)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, we direct the Liquidator of Barcle Enterprises Limited (Corporate Debtor &ndash; in Liquidation) to refund the entire amount of Rs.&nbsp;23,88,280/- to the Applicant within two weeks from the date of&nbsp;pronouncement of this order and report compliance.
							<strong>
								 (p8.7)
							</strong>
						</li>
