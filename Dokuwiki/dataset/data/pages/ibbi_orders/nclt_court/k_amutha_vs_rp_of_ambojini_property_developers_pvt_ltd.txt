	<h1>
		Can claim of Homebuyer paid in cash to Promotors of Real Estate Company be admitted even no records found in the books of Corporate Debtor about the cash portion paid by Allottee &ndash; K. Amutha Vs. RP of Ambojini Property Developers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Chennai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 6, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		The Applicant paid Rs.2,50,000/- through cheque and remaining Rs.17,50,000/- in cash as booking advance. The applicant had submitted claim with the IRP for a sum of Rs.35,20,000/-, however, RP admitted only to the extent of Rs.3,77,178/- and RP stated that there is no entry in the books of the Corporate Debtor that it has received cash payment from the Applicant for the purchase of residential units from the Project.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Applicant had booked a 2 BHK apartment in the Project costing Rs.82,23,072/- and is required to pay Rs.20,00,000/- as advance to the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant paid Rs.2,50,000/- through cheque and remaining Rs.17,50,000/- in cash as booking advance. The receipts of the said transactions is attached in this application.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor was admitted to CIRP vide&nbsp;order dated 10.09.2019.
						</li>
						<li>
							The applicant had submitted claim with the IRP for a sum of Rs.35,20,000/-. However, she has not received any reply from the IRP.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant came to understand that several members of the Association had filed claims before the Respondent and were waiting for the confirmation. The Applicant was under the bona fide belief that her claim would also be considered and admitted by the Respondent.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							On 05.01.2021 the list of admitted claims published by RP and her claim was admitted only to the extent of Rs.3,77,178/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							On enquiry, the Resolution Professional stated that there is no entry in the books of the Corporate Debtor that it has received cash payment from the Applicant for the purchase of residential units from the Project.
						</li>
						<li>
							Some of the homebuyers made payments to the account of Real Value Promoters for the project being developed by the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The RP had preferred an application under Section 66 of IBC, 2016 to secure the interest of the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The erstwhile promoter of the Corporate Debtor had provided an Affidavit along with a list of homebuyers which provides the details of some homebuyers who paid in cash under the instruction of the erstwhile management. Applicant name is not found in that affidavit.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							A Resolution Plan approved by the 95% of the CoC is filed before this Tribunal for approval
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal observes that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The cheques for the amount of Rs.1,00,000/- and Rs.1,50,000/- have been drawn in the name of the Corporate Debtor. Further, the cash receipt for Rs.12,50,000/- and Rs.5,00,000/- dated 02.05.203 and 06.05.2013 respectively in the name of Applicant is placed at pages 12-13 of this application.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Amount totaling Rs.20,00,000/- has been paid by the Applicant to the Corporate Debtor. All the Cheques and receipts were in the name of the Corporate Debtor. The transaction of the Applicant is not reflected in the books of the Corporate Debtor, and the name of the Applicant is not in the affidavit submitted by erstwhile management before the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Bombay.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And holds that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Regulation 8A of the IBBI (CIRP) Regulation 2016 prescribes that the existence of the debt may be proved on the basis of other relevant documents including receipt of payment made.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Reading the CIRP Regulation 13 shows that the RP shall verify every claim, but there is no specific obligation upon the Resolution Professional to strictly compare the claim submitted by the claimants only with the books of the Corporate Debtor
							</strong>
							 .
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 If the Resolution Professional verifies the claims only based on the books of the Corporate Debtor and if the Corporate Debtor does not manage its books and records properly, it would be detrimental to the creditors
							</strong>
							 like the Applicant.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Due to improper maintenance of the records of the Corporate Debtor, the Applicant/homebuyer cannot be made to suffer. The Resolution Professional must verify the authenticity of the supporting documents that have been filed by the claimant in support of their claim by other means within the boundaries of law.(p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And concludes that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The copy of the receipts and the cheques produced by the Applicant are bona fide. The Corporate Debtor had Received Rs.20,00,000/- from the Applicant as a booking advance. Accordingly, the Resolution Professional is directed to admit the claim of the Applicant with the applicable interest rate and include her in the respective class of creditors.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							With the aforesaid directions this Application stands allowed and disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p20)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
