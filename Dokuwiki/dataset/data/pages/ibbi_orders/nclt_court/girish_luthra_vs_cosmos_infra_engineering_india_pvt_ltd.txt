	<h1>
		NCLT disposes of IBC Section 7 petition filed by Homebuyer in Real Estate Insolvency resolving the issue through Advocate Commissioner &ndash; Girish Luthra and Ors. Vs. Cosmos Infra Engineering (India) Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Principal Bench, New Delhi
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 27, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, the Home Buyers are aggrieved by the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s conduct in not completing the project in time; and therefore creditors in a class filed Section 7 Petition. NCLT appointed an Advocate Commissioner vide order dated 23.05.2023. The Home Buyers giving their assent to Plan-A proposed by the Corporate Debtor, the details of the proposal was agreed by the Corporate Debtor. Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Principal Bench, New Delhi disposes of the petition holding that since the parties have come to certain terms for resolving the issue we deem it fit to record the same stating that it will be binding on the parties for implementation. We do so keeping in mind the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the similar proceedings in the case of Amit Katyal vs. Meera Ahuja &amp; Ors. reported in (2022) 14 SC as well as in the matter of Anand Murti vs. Soni Infratech Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr. reported in (2022) 27 SC.
	</p>

							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The present Section 7 Petition has been filed by the Home Buyers against the Corporate Debtor namely Cosmos Infra Engineering (India) Pvt. Ltd. in a housing project called &ldquo;Cosmos Express 99&rdquo;.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Home Buyers are aggrieved by the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s conduct in&nbsp;not completing the project in time; and therefore creditors in a class&nbsp;filed Section 7 Petition.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate&nbsp;Debtor
							<em>
								 inter alia
							</em>
							 pleading that the project is by and large complete&nbsp;except 30% which remains incomplete due to several factors which&nbsp;includes delayed payment by the allottees; and therefore, the project is&nbsp;being delayed.
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, NCLT appointed an Advocate&nbsp;Commissioner&nbsp;vide order dated 23.05.2023.
						</li>
						<li>
							The meeting was held under in the presence of Advocate&nbsp;Commissioner and the Home Buyers were given the option of&nbsp;suggesting ways and means to resolve the issue and it is not in dispute&nbsp;that the Home Buyers met and decided the two proposals given by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, which is Plan-A and Plan-B.
						</li>
						<li>
							Both the proposals given by the Corporate Debtor were considered in&nbsp;the meeting held on 29.10.2023 and majority of Home Buyers totalling&nbsp;to 78.48% have voted in favour implementing Plan-A of the proposal&nbsp;given by the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							Consequent to the Home Buyers giving their assent to Plan-A proposed&nbsp;by the Corporate Debtor, the details of the proposal was agreed by the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor and the Promoter of the Corporate Debtor has filed an affidavit dated 20.01.2024.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Effectively, the Corporate Debtor has consented to implement Plan-A proposed and also accepted by the Home Buyers and the parties are ad idem on resolution of the project in the manner suggested in Plan-A and undertaken by the Corporate Debtor in the affidavit dated 20.01.2024.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Since the parties have come to certain terms for resolving the issue we deem it fit to record the same and dispose of the petition i.e. (IB)- 462(PB)/2022 stating that it will be binding on the parties for implementation. We do so keeping in mind the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the similar proceedings in the case of
							<em>
								 Amit Katyal vs. Meera Ahuja &amp; Ors.
							</em>
							 reported in&nbsp;(2022) 14 SC&nbsp;as well as in the matter of
							<em>
								 Anand Murti vs. Soni Infratech Pvt. Ltd. &amp; Anr.
							</em>
							 reported in&nbsp;(2022) 27 SC.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							One other aspect which remains unfinished here is that there is an IA6397/2023 which relates to 06 Home Buyers. The Respondent undertakes to hear the parties with their records and deal with the same in accordance with law so that their grievance will be addressed on merits.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							If the parties still have further grievances, they are entitled to seek&nbsp;appropriate recourse as per law.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The fees of Advocate Commissioner will be paid by the Corporate Debtor. The initial remuneration of the Advocate Commissioner will be Rs.2.5 Lakhs. The further remuneration will be paid to the Advocate Commissioner from time to time as directed by the Adjudicating Authority.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, present Petition i.e. (IB)-462(PB)/2022 is disposed of. Connected applications in the present matter also stands disposed of.
							<strong>
								 (p20)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
