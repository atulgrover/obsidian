		<h1>
			Can a creditor initiate proceedings for recovery of any of the dues which are not part of the resolution plan, from the Corporate Debtor after approval of resolution plan by the NCLT? &ndash; Prabhat Pincha, Proprietor of Shubham Vs. Ultratech Cement Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 12, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority held that when a Resolution Plan is being approved by the CoC there is an intrinsic assumption that the Financial Creditors are fully informed about the viability and feasibility of the proposed Resolution Plan. There is no provision in the Code, which envisages challenge to the commercial or business decision of the Financial Creditors taken collectively or their individual opinions, as the case may be. Nevertheless, in this instant case, the payments to the Operational Creditors have been made by Ultratech Cement Limited in terms of the approved Resolution Plan. Further, these applications have been filed three years after the date of approval and implementation of the resolution plan. The Applicants should have taken proper recourse under the Code at the relevant point of time, against the admission or rejection of their claims by the RP. It is a bit too late in the day at this point of time to ask the Corporate Debtor under new management to make payment of operational debts which stand extinguished by the approval of the resolution plan way back in 2018.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Binani Cement Limited (Corporate Debtor) was engaged in the business of manufacturing and marketing of cement and had appointed Binani Business Developers (BBD) for networking, collection from dealers, sales and marketing within specific geographical locations. As per the agreements between the Corporate Debtor and the BBDs, the BBDs shall be responsible for supervising the activities of the dealers/distributors appointed by them and shall also be responsible for making all payments due and payable to the Corporate Debtor. While executing an agreement with the BBD, the Corporate Debtor used to collect security amount from the BBD, depending upon the region/districts.
						</p>
						<p>
							An application under p 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy, Code, 2016 was filed by one of the Financial Creditors of the Corporate Debtor before this Adjudicating Authority. The Resolution Plan of Ultratech Cement Limited(UCL or Successful Resolution Applicant or SRA) was unanimously approved by the CoC and was approved by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT&nbsp;on 14 November 2018. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court&nbsp;vide Order dated 19 November 2018&nbsp;also upheld the Order of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT.
						</p>
						<p>
							These are applications under p 60(5) of the Code, filed by the Applicants, against UCL. Submission of the Applicants that the Applicants kept on providing their services during the CIRP period, to help the Corporate Debtor run as a going-concern. Some of the Applicants had also entered into BBD Agreement with the Corporate Debtor. In pursuance public announcement the Applicants also submitted their claims with the RP. From time to time they also about the same. However, on enquiry the Applicants were informed that their claims are under the verification process and shall be considered by the RP in due course. Nevertheless, the verification of the claims of the Applicant was pending even when during the final hearing of the approval of the Resolution Plan.. The Resolution Plan of UCL, as approved by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT vide order dated 14 November 2018 provides for payment of 100% dues of the Operational Creditors, except related parties. But no payments have been received by the Applicant despite several requests and meetings.
						</p>
						<p>
							The instant Applications has been filed with a view to harass the respondents into paying sums which are neither due nor payable by the Successful Resolution Applicant.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							In all the IAs, there is only one Respondent, i.e.,Ultratech Cement Limited, who is also the Successful Resolution Application but all the replies on record has been filed on behalf of the Respondent No.2. As envisaged under p 31 of the Code, the Adjudicating Authority approves a Resolution Plan if it is satisfied that the same has been approved by the Committee of Creditors by the requisite 66% votes, after taking into consideration the feasibility and viability of the Resolution Plan[
							<em>
								p 30(4) of the Code
							</em>
							]. However, in this instant case, the Resolution Plan of Ultratech Cement Limited was unanimously approved by the CoC and was approved by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT on 14 November 2018. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court vide Order dated 19 November 2018 also upheld the Order of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT.
							<strong>
								(p5.1-5.3)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							It is also pertinent to mention that the issue arising here have been dealt by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in a plethora of cases. Every now and then, the Apex Court has made it explicit that the commercial wisdom of the Committee of Creditors has to be given paramount status without any judicial intervention.(
							<em>
								Kalpraj Dharamshi v Kotak Investment Advisors Limited&nbsp;(2021)  40 SC&nbsp;decided by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court on 10 March 2021
							</em>
							).&nbsp;Further,&nbsp;
							<strong>
								when a Resolution Plan is being approved by the CoC there is an intrinsic assumption that the Financial Creditors are fully informed about the viability and feasibility of the proposed Resolution Plan.
							</strong>
							<em>
								(Kalpraj Dharamshi v Kotak Investment Advisors Limited&nbsp;(2021)  40 SC).&nbsp;
							</em>
							<strong>
								There is no provision in the Code, which envisages challenge to the commercial or business decision of the Financial Creditors taken collectively or their individual opinions, as the case may be.&nbsp;
							</strong>
							<em>
								(K. Sashidharv Indian Overseas Bank &amp; Others,&nbsp;[2019]  08 SC&nbsp;decided by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court on 05 February 2019).
							</em>
							<strong>
								(p5.4)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Further, in
							<em>
								&nbsp;Ghanashyam Mishra &amp; Sons Private Limited v Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited&nbsp;(2021)  54 SC
							</em>
							, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has held that any debt in respect of the payment of dues arising under any law, including the ones owed to the Central Government, any state Government or any local authority, which does not form a part of the approved Resolution Plan shall stand extinguished.
							<strong>
								(p5.5)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Nevertheless, in this instant case, we have observed that the payments to the Operational Creditors have been made by Ultratech Cement Limited in terms of the approved Resolution Plan. Further,
							<strong>
								&nbsp;these applications have been filed three years after the date of approval and implementation of the resolution plan.(p5.6)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								The Applicants should have taken proper recourse under the Code at the relevant point of time, against the admission or rejection of their claims by the RP. It is a bit too late in the day at this point of time to ask the Corporate Debtor under new management to make payment of operational debts which stand extinguished by the approval of the resolution plan way back in 2018.
							</strong>
							&nbsp;It is precisely such claims popping up long after the approval of the resolution plan, that the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court cautioned against, when it said in the&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Essar Steel&nbsp;[2019]  07 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							&nbsp;judgment that the successful resolution applicant should not be required to face these hydra heads popping up.
							<strong>
								(p5.7)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							In view of the above facts and circumstances and the precedents laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, each of the IAs, i.e.,IA (IBC) No.582/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.596/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.597/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.598/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.606/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.609/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.610/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.611/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.728/KB/2021, IA (IBC) No.729/KB/2021 and IA (IBC) No.1004/KB/2021, shall stand dismissed as not maintainable after the approval of the resolution plan.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								(p5-6)
							</strong>
						</p>
					</div>

			</p>
