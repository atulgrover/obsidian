			<h1>
				Balance Sheet of the Corporate Debtor reflecting the loan of the applicant, should have been the sufficient ground for acceptance of the claim submitted by the Creditors &ndash; Champa Impex Pvt. Ltd. Vs. P.M.Cold Storage Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
			</h1>
			<div>
				April 19, 2022
			</div>
			<p>
				The Adjudicating Authority held that the claim of the applicant should have been considered and accepted on merit because as per the latest judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India, the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor reflecting the loan of the applicant, should have been the sufficient ground for acceptance of the claim submitted by the Applicant. There is no necessity for the Applicant to file any loan agreement in its support, particularly, when the parties were related to each other and had common Directorship in the two companies, and, therefore did not think it necessary to execute such a loan Agreement.

							<p>
								<strong>
									II. Brief about the decision
								</strong>
							</p>
							<p>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Facts of the case
									</strong>
								</em>
							</p>
							<p>
								The Applicant had sent email dated 3rd August, 2021 attaching therewith the claim form, Ledger, Bank Statements, PAN, Form-3 etc. It is specifically mentioned therein that the amount advanced as loan to the Corporate Debtor was given on various dates highlighted in the Bank account statement in Form-C. The RP has not disputed the advancing of loan by the Applicant to the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;Part of the loan amount has been set off on account of supply of goods.&nbsp;All these entries are reflected in the books of accounts and the amount has been transferred through the banking channels, the existence of these entries cannot be denied by the RP. The only thing disputed by the RP is that there is no loan agreement relating to the said loan. The Applicant has sent along with the claim all the material documents like ledger, Bank Statement etc. which has probably not satisfied the RP.
							</p>
							<p>
								<em>
									<strong>
										Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
									</strong>
								</em>
							</p>
							<ul>
								<li>
									The claim of the applicant should have been considered and accepted on merit because as per the latest judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India, the balance sheet of the Corporate Debtor reflecting the loan of the applicant, should have been the sufficient ground for acceptance of the claim submitted by the Applicant. There is no necessity for the Applicant to file any loan agreement in its support, particularly, when the parties were related to each other and had common Directorship in the two companies, and, therefore did not think it necessary to execute such a loan Agreement.
								</li>
								<li>
									Moreover, the RP has also not produced any document to show that the Corporate Debtor had refunded the said outstanding amount to the applicant by any mode.&nbsp;In the absence of any such evidence, the RP could not have rejected the claim of the applicant.
								</li>
								<li>
									Even if part of the amount has been set-off against goods supplied by the Corporate Debtor to the Financial Creditor, it would not make the whole loan amount as an operational transaction or operational debt, when it was a loan advanced to the Corporate Debtor. It will not make the balance loan of&nbsp;Rs.39,84,516/- also as operational debt.
								</li>
								<li>
									After having considered all the documents placed on record, we direct the RP to accept the claim of the applicant and recognize it as financial debt. The delay in filing the claim is condoned. The RP is directed to take this outstanding loan of the Financial Creditor, as a financial debt to be payable by the Corporate Debtor.
									<strong>
										(p8-10)
									</strong>
								</li>
							</ul>
							<p>
								The RP has referred to and relied upon the judgment/order dated September 4, 2021 passed by this Adjudicating Authority in CA(IB) No.1039/KB/2019 [ Asshish Jhunjhunwala Vs. Kshitiz Chhawchharia(RP) of Ramsarup Industries Ltd. &amp; Ors] arising out of CP(IB) No. 349/KB/2019. The facts involved in the instant case are identical to the facts in in CA(IB) No. 1039/KB/2019. While dismissing the application in CA(IB) No. 1039/KB/2019, this adjudicating authority in its judgment/order has held that so only on cause production of financial statement itself cannot be held that all the requirements for substantiating the claim has been fulfilled on the side of the Applicants. The aforesaid judgment has been upheld by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT reported at&nbsp;
								<em>
									(2021)  213 NCLAT
								</em>
								.
							</p>
							<p>
								The Adjudicating Authority further held that the judgments cited by the RP to our mind do not apply to the facts of the present case, so far as written loan agreement is concerned. However, as regards other documents, the Financial Creditor has satisfied the requirements.
								<strong>
									(p12)
								</strong>
							</p>
