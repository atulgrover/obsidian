						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The prayer made in I.A. No. 2085/ND/2020 filed by the applicant is reproduced below:
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;a) Declare that the invocation/encashment of Bank Guarantee No. 0162191GPER0021 dated 22.04.2019 is not covered by Moratorium under Section 14 of the Code.
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								(b) Consequently, direct the Respondent Bank to encash the Bank Guarantee No. 0162191GPER0021 dated 22.04.2019 and,
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								(c) Pass any other order as this Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal may deem fit in the facts and circumstances of the present case.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								The issue that arose for our consideration is as to whether the bank guarantee in question can be invoked during the moratorium declared under Section 14(1) of IBC 2016?
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT held that as per p 14(1)(c) any action to foreclose, recover or enforce any&nbsp;
							<em>
								<u>
									security interest
								</u>
							</em>
							&nbsp;created by the corporate debtor in respect of its property including any action under the SARFAESI Act, 2002 is prohibited. It is well settled position that the bank guarantee falls within the purview of the definition of&nbsp;
							<em>
								&ldquo;Security interest&rdquo; as&nbsp;
							</em>
							defined under Section 3(31) of the IBC, which is already quoted under para 12 herein above. Therefore, during the moratorium the bank guarantee cannot be invoked as the same is prohibited under Section 14(1)(c) of the IBC.
						</p>
						<p>
							In this connection this Authority rely upon the order of NCLT, Ahmedabad Bench passed in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Nitin Hasmukhlal Parikh (Diamond Power Transformers Ltd.) v. Madhya Gujarat Vij Company Ltd. &amp; Others
								</strong>
							</em>
							, wherein under Para No, 10 of the order Adjudicating Authority has observed as follows;&nbsp;
							<em>
								&ndash; &ldquo;The moratorium order passed by this Tribunal applies in respect of Bank Guarantees other than Performance Guarantees furnished by the Corporate Debtor in respect of its property since it comes within the meaning of &lsquo;security interest&rsquo;. Therefore, Respondent no. 1 is not entitled to invoke Bank Guarantees other than that comes within the meaning of performance Guarantees, during Moratorium period.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							We are conscious of the fact that NCLT, PB New Delhi in the case of&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									<u>
										M/s Levcon Valves (P) Ltd.
									</u>
									&nbsp;v/s&nbsp;
									<u>
										Enerqo Engineering Projects Ltd,&nbsp;
									</u>
								</em>
							</strong>
							[C.A.No.453(PB)/2017, filed in C.P (IB)-160 (ND)/2017, Order dated 24.08.2018] and in&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									<u>
										GUDEARTH HOMES INFRACON&nbsp;
									</u>
								</em>
								<u>
									PVT LTD &amp; ORS
								</u>
								<em>
									&nbsp;Vs.&nbsp;
									<u>
										VEEBRO TECHNOPLAST&nbsp;
									</u>
								</em>
								<u>
									PVT LTD &ndash;&nbsp;
									<em>
										GUDEARTH HOMES 1NFRACON&nbsp;
									</em>
									PVT LTD..
								</u>
							</strong>
							&nbsp;[CA-580 (PB)/2017 filed in (IB)-159 (PB)/2017 order dated, 06.09.2018] allowed invocation of a bank guarantee during moratorium, even without seeking leave of the Adjudicating Authority. But the orders did not provide any reasonable distinction between&nbsp;
							<em>
								bank guarantee&nbsp;
							</em>
							and&nbsp;
							<em>
								performance bank guarantee.&nbsp;
							</em>
							The NCLT, Ahmedabad Bench in&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Nitin Hasmukh Lal Parikh&rsquo;s&nbsp;
								</strong>
							</em>
							case (supra) brought out a clear distinction between&nbsp;
							<em>
								bank guarantee&nbsp;
							</em>
							and&nbsp;
							<em>
								performance bank guarantee,&nbsp;
							</em>
							which is plausible.
						</p>
						<p>
							ln view of the legal position stated above, the I.A-2085/ND/2020 filed by Applicant viz., Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd., stands dismissed. The I.A. No. 2572/ND/2020 filed by non-applicant No.2 Union Bank of India (erstwhile Andhra Bank) is allowed with direction to the Applicant viz., Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd., not to demand the release of Bank Guarantee amount from the non-Applicant No. 2 viz., Union Bank of India (erstwhile Andhra Bank) in view of the Moratorium declared under Section 14, of IBC 2016 in relation to the Corporate Debtor.
						</p>
