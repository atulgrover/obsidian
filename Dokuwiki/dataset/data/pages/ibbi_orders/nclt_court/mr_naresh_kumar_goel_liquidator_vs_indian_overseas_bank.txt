	<h1>
		During Liquidation process, a Bank is not bound to release Margin Money held in form of Fixed Deposit against a Bank Guarantee issued to Customs Department under Customs Act,1962 on behalf of Corporate Debtor &ndash; Mr. Naresh Kumar Goel, Liquidator Vs. Indian Overseas Bank &ndash; NCLT Chandigarh Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 3, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The present application has been filed by the liquidator of the Corporate Debtor under p 60(5) of the IBC, 2016 for seeking directions, in respect of the release of the Fixed Depository Receipts the Corporate Debtor has provided a Bank Guarantee to Customs Department(R2). Indian Overseas Bank(R1) has asked the petitioner to obtain NOC from Customs Department before releasing the amount of bank guarantee to the Corporate Debtor. The said bank guarantee is kept as a security by the Indian Overseas Bank in relation to the bank guarantee issued by the bank to the Customs Department. NCLT Chandigarh Bench observed that the bank guarantee is in respect of the EPCG licenses obtained by the Corporate Debtor. The bank guarantee is in the nature of financial guarantee where respondent-bank has undertaken to assume the responsibility to meet the possible debt if the applicant-Corporate Debtor defaults. The Bench after referring the judgment of NCLAT in Bharat Aluminium (2021) 91 NCLAT held that the bank guarantee is being revoked for violation of an undertaking given under the Customs Act by the Corporate Debtor. We are of the view that the corporate debtor under the liquidation cannot escape the consequences of violation of contractual obligations.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observed that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The bank guarantee is in respect of the EPCG licenses obtained by the Corporate Debtor. The bank guarantee is in the nature of financial guarantee where respondent-bank has undertaken to assume the responsibility to meet the possible debt if the applicant-Corporate Debtor defaults.
						</li>
						<li>
							In this connection, a reference is made to the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in&nbsp;the matter of
							<em>
								 Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd. vs M/s. J.P. Engineers Pvt .Ltd.&nbsp;(2021) 91 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , which deals with the dual issue of invocation of bank guarantee and also whether the same can be invoked even during moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC. On the issue, whether the Bank guarantee is an asset of the Corporate Debtor, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT has considered the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble
							<em>
								 AP Court in the case of Haryana Telecom Ltd. Vs. Aluminium industries Ltd. (1995) SCC Online AP 721.
							</em>
							 Further, reference is made to the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 UP State Sugar Corporations Vs. Sumac International Ltd. in Civil Appeal No. 15357 of 1996 dated 04.12.1996.
							</em>
						</li>
						<li>
							After considering all the facts and the related judicial decisions, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in case of Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd. vs M/s. J.P. Engineers Private Limited (Supra) has observed that &ldquo;
							<em>
								 the Corporate Debtor has issued bank guarantee for ensuring the price of goods. The bank guarantee is irrevocable and unconditional and payable on demand without demur. The assets of the surety are separate from those of the corporate debtor, and proceedings against the corporate debtor may not be seriously impacted by the actions against assets of a third party like a surety. Bank guarantee can be invoked even during the moratorium period issued under p 14 of the IBC in view of the amended provision under p 14 (3)(b) of the IBC.
							</em>
							 &rdquo;
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 We note the fact that the bank guarantee is being revoked for violation of an&nbsp;undertaking given under the Customs Act by the Corporate Debtor. We are of the view that the corporate debtor under the liquidation cannot escape the consequences of violation of contractual obligations.
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above discussion and the decision in the case of
							<em>
								 Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd. vs M/s. J.P. Engineers Private Limited (Supra)
							</em>
							 , the prayers of the applicant cannot be accede to. In these circumstances, IA No. 924/2020 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 and disposed of accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p7-14)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
