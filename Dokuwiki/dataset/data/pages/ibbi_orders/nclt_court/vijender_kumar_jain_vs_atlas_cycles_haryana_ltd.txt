		Legal heir of an Operational Creditor who expired during proceedings of Section 9 of IBC can continue the proceedings &ndash; Vijender Kumar Jain Vs. M/s. Atlas Cycles (Haryana) Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Chandigarh Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		October 21, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		Operational creditor has filed a petition under Section 9 of IBC, 2016 for initiation of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process. However, during the said proceedings, Mr. Vijender Kumar Jain expired intestate leaving behind 5 legal heirs. The four legal heirs have executed relinquishment deed in favour of the applicant. It is stated that the said application is filed with limitation i.e. 90 days of the death. The respondent has stated that legal heirs are not covered under the definition of the operational creditors as defined under Section 5(20) of IBC, 2016. The definition of person as used under Section 5(20) of IBC, 2016 is prescribed under Section 3(23) of IBC, 2016.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Operational creditor has filed a petition under Section 9 of IBC, 2016 for initiation of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process. However, during the said proceedings, Mr. Vijender Kumar Jain expired intestate leaving behind 5 legal heirs. The four legal heirs have executed relinquishment deed in favour of the applicant. It is stated that the said application is filed with limitation i.e. 90 days of the death.
					</p>
					<p>
						The respondent has stated that legal heirs are not covered under the definition of the operational creditors as defined under Section 5(20) of IBC, 2016. The definition of person as used under Section 5(20) of IBC, 2016 is prescribed under Section 3(23) of IBC, 2016. The respondent has relied upon the judgment of M/s. Double Seven Enterprises Vs. M/s. Vijay Fine Art Press on 30 August, 2018, District Court(Delhi-Central) bearing number Suit No.: 07/14 (Old); 99188/16 (New).
					</p>
					<p>
						The applicant has filed a rejoinder vide Dairy No. 00904/5 Dated 03.01.2022 wherein it has been stated that the applicant is Class I legal heir of the deceased has subrogated into his shoes, so the applicant has become the operational creditor. The very definition under Section 5(20) of IBC, 2016 as relied upon by the Respondent clearly provides that the Operational creditor includes a person to whom the debt is assigned or transferred. Here, the debt has been transferred in the name of the applicant by operation of law and hence the applicant is the operational creditor and relied upon the judgement of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
						<em>
							 Fipola Retail (India) Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M2N Interiors&nbsp;(2021) 415 NCLAT
						</em>
						 dated 01.09.2021.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						It is observed that the applicant is a legal heir along with four other legal heirs(daughters) of Vijender Kumar Jain, who was running a proprietorship concern under the name of Arihant Cycles.&nbsp;In this regard, reliance may be placed on the judgment of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court of India in case of
						<em>
							 Ashok Transport Agency vs. Awadhesh Kumar and Ors.
						</em>
						 C.A. No. 1873 of 1998 dated 31.03.1998.
					</p>
					<p>
						Thus, judgment of
						<em>
							 M/s. Double Seven Enterprises Vs. M/s. Vijay Fine Art Press (Supra)
						</em>
						 relied by learned counsel for respondent is not applicable to the present case. To conclude, in the present case, the applicant has stepped into the shoes of the petitioner in CP (IB) No. 217/Chd/Hry/2020 being the legal heir/wife of Vijender Kumar Jain(Since Deceased). Thus, the present application is allowed and the applicant is to continue the proceedings in the present petition and the amended memo of parties is also taken on record. Hence, the present application is disposed of accordingly.
