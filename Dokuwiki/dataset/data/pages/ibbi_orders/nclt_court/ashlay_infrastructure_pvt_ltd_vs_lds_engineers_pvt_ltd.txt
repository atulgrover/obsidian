		<h1>
			The representative of the Operational Creditor being not available in India is not a ground to condone the delay for filing application under Section 9 of IBC &ndash; Ashlay Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. Vs. LDS Engineers Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court &ndash; II
		</h1>
		<div>
			December 5, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that&nbsp;the grounds taken do not seem to be cogent and logical, because limitation period to file the petition is three years, and during this entire period, the representative of the Operational Creditor was travelling outside India and could not file the application, it cannot be accepted. Therefore, the representative of the Operational Creditor being not available in India is not a ground to condone the delay. So far as the other ground that he had come to file the application on 28.022017, but the Registry refused to accept the application on the ground that the application was not defect free, is also not a ground to condone the delay. We are of the considered view that these are not sufficient reasons to prevent the applicant to file an application within the period of limitation.
		</p>
	</div>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The applicant also prayed to condone the delay in&nbsp;filing the application. However, the applicant has not filed the&nbsp;Condonation application along with the main petition. The main petition&nbsp;was filed on 25.04.2017, whereas this Condonation application is filed on&nbsp;25.09.2019 when the matter was remanded.
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that&nbsp;the grounds taken do not seem to be cogent and logical,&nbsp;because limitation period to file the petition is three years, and during this&nbsp;entire period, the representative of the Operational Creditor was travelling&nbsp;outside India and could not file the application, it cannot be accepted.&nbsp;Therefore, the representative of the Operational&nbsp;Creditor being not available in India is not a ground to condone&nbsp;the delay. So far as the other ground that he had come to file the application&nbsp;on 28.022017, but the Registry refused to accept the application on the&nbsp;ground that the application was not defect free, is also not a ground to&nbsp;condone the delay. We are of the considered view that these are not&nbsp;sufficient reasons to prevent the applicant to file an application within the&nbsp;period of limitation.
						</p>
						<p>
							A bare perusal of the Section 5 of the Limitation Act shows that the application for&nbsp;condonation of delay may be admitted after the prescribed period, if the&nbsp;appellant or the applicant satisfies the Court that he had sufficient cause for&nbsp;not preferring the appeal or making the application within said period. In&nbsp;our considered view the grounds taken by the applicant are not covered&nbsp;under Section 5 of the Limitation Act. Therefore, the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble&nbsp;Supreme Court in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Seshnath Singh
							</em>
							&nbsp;Case reported&nbsp;(2021)  49 SC&nbsp;in our considered view is not applicable. Hence, we are not inclined to&nbsp;condone the delay in filing the main application. Accordingly, the prayer to&nbsp;condone the delay is hereby rejected.&nbsp;Hence, we find and hold that the present application&nbsp;is barred by limitation. Accordingly, the same is&nbsp;dismissed on the point of limitation.
						</p>
