<section>
	<h1>
		Whether, after approval of Resolution Plan and issuance of LoI, an Unsuccessful Resolution Applicant can question the conduct of the Committee of Creditors(CoC) and challenge Resolution Plan of Successful Resolution Applicant(SRA) &ndash; Dhansagar Dealers Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Anubrata Gangoly, RP of Carnation Industries Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, while approving resolution plan in the matter of Carnation Industries Ltd., Bench of Kolkata NCLT comprising of Ms. Bidisha Banerjee and Shri Arvind Devanathan, has held that: (i) Any modifications or revisions of any plan after the approval of the plan by the CoC, even if undertaken as per directions of the CoC, shall not be entertained unless the CoC grants the subsequent approval. (ii) An unsuccessful resolution applicant has no vested right to challenge the approval of a resolution plan. (iii) The approval of Resolution Plan falls within the arena of &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo; which cannot be questioned unless there is a violation of law as enshrined under Sections 30(2) and 31 of the I&amp;B Code. (iv) Once a resolution applicant fails to succeed in the bid, it neither has a locus to question the action of the stakeholders qua members sitting in and controlling the CoC, nor the right to enhance or revise the monetary value of its Resolution Plan to compete with the plan of the Successful Resolution Applicant.
	</p>
</section>

						<p>
							<strong>
								 A. I.A. (IB) No. 969/KB/2024
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Resolution Plans of the applicant and SRA were placed and discussed and the authorized representatives were called in at appropriate times for clarifications and explanations. Both the plans were put in for e-voting and the plan submitted by the SRA has been approved by the CoC by 100% voting share.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the instant application preferred under Section 60(5) of the IBC, the Applicant, Dhansagar Dealers Pvt. Ltd., is an unsuccessful resolution applicant, challenging the resolution plan of the successful resolution applicant as the same has been illegally and arbitrarily voted upon and approved by the Committee of Creditor (CoC). Thus, the applicant has prayed to consider its enhanced/ revised plan and direct the respondents to opt for a challenge mechanism procedure to enable the applicant and the other resolution applicants to improve their plans.
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							Hon&rsquo;ble Adjudicating Authority holds that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								As per section 30(6) of the I&amp;B Code, the Resolution Professional shall submit the resolution plan as approved by the CoC to the Adjudicating Authority.
								<strong>
									 Thus, any modifications or revisions of any plan after the approval of the plan by the CoC, even if undertaken as per directions of the CoC, shall not be entertained unless the CoC grants the subsequent approval.(p17)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Further, in the context of challenging the approval of the resolution plan, Hon&rsquo;ble Bench refers to the following judgment rendered by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT:
								<ul>
									<li>
										PNC Infratech Ltd. Vs. Deepak Maini reported in&nbsp;(2022)  612 NCLAT.
									</li>
									<li>
										Interups Inc v. Kuldeep Kumar Bassi, reported in&nbsp;(2021)  141 NCLAT
									</li>
									<li>
										Steel Strips Wheels Ltd. v. Shri Avil Menezes, (RP) &amp; Ors. reported at&nbsp;(2022)  297 NCLAT.
										<strong>
											 (p18-19)
										</strong>
									</li>
								</ul>
							</li>
							<li>
								In the present case in hand, the Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor within its ambit of &ldquo;Commercial Wisdom&rdquo; has taken the decision and the Adjudicating Authority has very limited scope to interfere in their decision which is unanimously taken.&nbsp;To fortify the view, we would refer to the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court in
								<em>
									 Kalpraj Dharamshi &amp; Anr. vs Kotak Investment Advisors Ltd. &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2021)  40 SC
								</em>
								 wherein, it was observed that
								<em>
									 the legislative scheme, as interpreted by various decisions
								</em>
								<em>
									 of this Court, is unambiguous. The commercial wisdom of CoC is not to be
								</em>
								<em>
									 interfered with, excepting the limited scope as provided Under Sections 30
								</em>
								<em>
									 and 31 of the I&amp;B Code.
								</em>
								<strong>
									 (p21)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Thus, from the foregoing enumerations,
								<strong>
									 an unsuccessful resolution applicant has no vested right to challenge the approval of a resolution plan.
									<strong>
										 (p22)
									</strong>
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								In the instant case,
								<strong>
									 the plan value of the Unsuccessful Resolution Applicant is less than the plan value as proposed by the Successful Resolution Applicant and the resolution plan submitted by the SRA has unanimously been approved by the CoC with the majority voting share. Thus, the approval of the plan falls within the arena of &ldquo;commercial wisdom&rdquo; which cannot be questioned unless there is a violation of law as enshrined under Sections 30(2) and 31 of the I&amp;B Code.
									<strong>
										 (p22)
									</strong>
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Where are no irregulates in approval of the resolution plan by the CoC.
								<strong>
									 Once a resolution applicant fails to succeed in the bid, it neither has a locus to question the action of the stakeholders qua members sitting in and controlling the CoC, nor the right to enhance or revise the monetary value of its Resolution Plan to compete with the plan of the Successful Resolution Applicant.
								</strong>
								 Thus, the Applicant herein being an Unsuccessful Resolution Applicant cannot be allowed to cry foul.
								<strong>
									 (p22)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								In terms of the view above, the Application is dismissed. No cost.
								<strong>
									 (p23-24)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							<strong>
								 B. I.A. (IB) (Plan) No. 7/KB/2024
							</strong>
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The Resolution Plan application preferred by Mr. Anubrata Gangoly, the Resolution Professional (RP) through this I.A. under Section 30(6) and 31(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, (I&amp;B Code) read with Regulation 39(4) of IBBI (Insolvency Regulations Process of Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016, (CIRP Regulations) seeking for the final approval and sanction of the resolution plan as approved by the CoC on 18.04.2024.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the case at hand, we would note that the Resolution dated April 13, 2024, submitted by Mr. Vikas Garg, has been approved by the Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor by 100% voting share on 02.02.2024. We have further noted that the LoI was issued on 02.02.2024, which has been unconditionally accepted by the SRA.
							</li>
							<li>
								Accordingly, the Resolution dated April 13, 2024, submitted by Mr. Vikas Garg, defeats all other plans submitted before the applicant and Mr. Vikas Garg, has unanimously declared as a &ldquo;Successful Resolution Applicant&rdquo;. Hence, given the aforesaid decisions of the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court as well as in light of the overall facts and circumstances of the present case, this Adjudicating Authority has not interfered with the viability of the Commercial Wisdom as exercised by the Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor.
							</li>
							<li>
								Subject to the observations made in this Order, the Resolution dated April 13, 2024, submitted by Mr. Vikas Garg, the Successful&nbsp;Resolution Applicant, is hereby
								<strong>
									 APPROVED
								</strong>
								 and FINALLY&nbsp;SANCTIONED by this Adjudicating Authority.
								<strong>
									 (p80-81)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
