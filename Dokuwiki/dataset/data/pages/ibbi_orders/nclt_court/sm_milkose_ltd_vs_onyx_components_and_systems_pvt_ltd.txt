		<h1>
			A separate application to deal with a new issue cannot be entertained under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016, after approval of Resolution Plan &ndash; SM Milkose Ltd. Vs. M/s Onyx Components and Systems Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-II
		</h1>
		<div>
			September 12, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that the period of 180 days may be extended under Section 12 of IBC, 2016 and in terms of the amended provision of Section 12 of IBC, 2016, the total period of insolvency resolution process is 330 days, which means that the period so referred to in Section 5(14) of IBC, 2016 is subject to the extension made under Section 12 of IBC, 2016 or when the Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority. Here in the case in hand, the Resolution Plan has already been approved by the Adjudicating Authority on 02.04.2019. Therefore, no insolvency proceeding is pending before the Adjudicating Authority. Under such circumstances, in our considered view, the application filed by the applicants under Section 60 (5) of IBC, 2016 is not maintainable under the law.
		</p>
	</div>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that Section 60(5)(c) of IBC, 2016 states that any question of priorities or any question of law or facts, arising out of or in relation to the insolvency resolution or liquidation proceedings of the corporate debtor or corporate person under this Code shall be considered by the Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;In the present matter, the Resolution Plan has already been approved. Therefore, at this juncture, we would like to consider the meaning of `insolvency resolution process period&rsquo;, which has been defined in Section 5 (14) of IBC, 2016, which is quoted below:
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								<em>
									5(14) : &ldquo;insolvency resolution process period&rdquo; means the period of one hundred and eighty days beginning from the insolvency commencement date and ending on one hundred and eightieth day;
								</em>
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							A bare perusal of the provision shows that the insolvency resolution process period means the period of one hundred and eighty days beginning from the insolvency commencement date and ending on one hundred and eightieth day.
						</p>
						<p>
							This period of 180 days may be extended under Section 12 of IBC, 2016 and in terms of the amended provision of Section 12 of IBC, 2016, the total period of insolvency resolution process is 330 days, which means that the period so referred to in Section 5(14) of IBC, 2016 is subject to the extension made under Section 12 of IBC, 2016 or when the Resolution Plan is approved by the Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;Here in the case in hand, the Resolution Plan has already been approved by the Adjudicating Authority on 02.04.2019. Therefore, no insolvency proceeding is pending before the Adjudicating Authority.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Under such circumstances, in our considered view, the application filed by the applicants under Section 60 (5) of IBC, 2016 is not maintainable under the law.
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							So far as the application maintainable under Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016 is concerned, in our considered view, during the pendency of any matter before the Tribunal, when there is no specific provision under the Act/Code is given only then the Tribunal may pass order by exercise of its power under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules to deal with such situation. But herein the case in hand, no such matter is pending after the approval of Resolution Plan by the Adjudicating Authority, therefore, in our considered view, a separate application to deal with a new issue cannot be entertained under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016.&nbsp;
							<strong>
								Therefore, in our considered view, Rule 11 of NCLT Rules, 2016 is also not applicable in the case in hand. And the application filed by the applicants is also not maintainable under Rule 11 of NCLT Rules.
							</strong>
							(p26-31)
						</p>
