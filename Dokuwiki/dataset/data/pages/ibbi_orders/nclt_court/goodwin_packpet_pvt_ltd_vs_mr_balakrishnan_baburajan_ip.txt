		<h1>
			NCLT grants 30 days more time to complete compromise or arrangement proposed under Section 230 of the Companies Act, 2013 r/w Reg. 2B of Liquidation Process Regulations, 2016 &ndash; Goodwin Packpet Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Balakrishnan Baburajan IP &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 5, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			NCLT held that to arrive at a conclusion whether further time can be granted to the applicants to settle the matter by restructuring the company, we have gone through the 2005 Report of the Expert Committee on Company Law (JJ Irani Committee Report) and in the matter S.C. Sekaran vs. Amit Gupta and Ors. [2019]  02 NCLAT, in this case, the Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal directed the liquidator appointed under the IBC, to &ldquo;take steps in terms of Section 230&rdquo; for the revival of the corporate debtor before undertaking the sale of its assets. NCLT exercising the discretionary power of this Tribunal under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016, disposed of the IA with the order that the Applicants are granted 30 days&rsquo; time from today to clear off the debt due to the Respondent in the IBA and another Creditor CSB Bank, so as to get re-structured the Applicant Company.
		</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the Case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							An Application was allowed on&nbsp;
							<strong>
								11.01.2021
							</strong>
							&nbsp;putting the Corporate Debtor under liquidation. On 26.2.2021, the Applicants, suspended directors of the Corporate Debtor , requested for granting 3 months time so that they could re-structure the company by clearing off the debt due to the Respondent in the IBA and the other creditor CSB Bank. The Liquidator stated that as per Regulation 2B of the Liquidation Process Regulations, 2016, compromise or arrangement proposed under Section 230 of the Companies Act, 2013 should be completed within 90 days from the date of commencement of liquidation. Hence, compromise or arrangement proposed under Section 230 of the Companies Act, 2013 should have been completed on or before&nbsp;
							<strong>
								11th April, 2021
							</strong>
							. The applicants could not come up with any proposal to restructure the Company within the said period of 90 days as specified in IBC 2016.
						</p>
						<p>
							The question to be decided in this matter is whether some time more time can be granted to the Applicants to clear off the dues to the Respondent and other Creditor CSB Bank so as to get re-structured the Applicant Company.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Decision of the adjudicating authority is summarised in following points:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								To arrive at a conclusion whether further time can be granted to the applicants to settle the matter by restructuring the company, we have gone through the&nbsp;
								<em>
									2005 Report of the Expert Committee on Company Law (JJ Irani Committee Report)
								</em>
								&nbsp;in which it is stated that to provide an opportunity for genuine effort to explore restructuring/ rehabilitation of potentially viable businesses with consensus of stakeholders reasonably arrived at. Where revival / rehabilitation is demonstrated as not being feasible, winding up should be resorted to. Where circumstances justify, the process should allow for easy conversion of proceedings from one procedure to another. This will provide opportunity to businesses in liquidation to turnaround wherever possible. Similarly, conversion to liquidation might be appropriate even after a rehabilitation plan has been approved if such a plan was procured by fraud or the plan can no longer be implemented.
							</li>
							<li>
								In the matter&nbsp;
								<em>
									S.C. Sekaran vs. Amit Gupta and Ors.&nbsp;[2019]  02 NCLAT
								</em>
								, in this case, the Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal directed the liquidator appointed under the IBC, to&nbsp;
								<em>
									&ldquo;take steps in terms of Section 230
								</em>
								&rdquo; for the revival of the corporate debtor before undertaking the sale of its assets.
							</li>
							<li>
								NCLT exercising the discretionary power of this Tribunal under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016, disposed of the IA with the order that the Applicants are granted 30 days&rsquo; time from today to clear off the debt due to the Respondent in the IBA and another Creditor CSB Bank, so as to get re-structured the Applicant Company. It is made clear that no further time will be granted for this purpose and if this order is not complied with by the applicants, the Liquidator is free to proceed further under the relevant rules.
								<strong>
									(p7-10)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
