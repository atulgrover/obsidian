	<h1>
		Can sale of assets of Corporate Debtor be converted from sale as a going concern to slump sale basis even Liquidation auction has already been completed &ndash; Shree Krishna Recycling India Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mr. Sanjay Gupta Liquidator, Shamken Multifab Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Allahabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		February 9, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, Successful H1 Bidder filed IA for treating the present sale as a going concern to be as a sale under slump sale basis in view of the certain eventualities/difficulties faced by the Successful Bidder/Applicant.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The applicant in pursuance of the sale notice published by the Liquidator for sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor as a
							<strong>
								 going concern
							</strong>
							 , made offer for bidding and became the Successful H1 Bidder and accordingly, a Letter of Intent was issued calling upon the&nbsp;applicant to deposit the sale consideration as per the&nbsp;stipulated date mentioned in the LoI.
						</li>
						<li>
							After receipt of the LoI, the applicant deposited&nbsp;total amount&nbsp;Rs.29,36,000,00/-&nbsp;including the initial&nbsp;amount paid towards EMD&nbsp;and the only&nbsp;balance consideration of Rs.7,20,000,00/- remained due and&nbsp;payable by the applicant.
						</li>
						<li>
							The present application has been moved for&nbsp;treating the present sale as a going concern to be as a sale&nbsp;under slump sale basis in view of the certain&nbsp;eventualities/difficulties faced by the Successful&nbsp;Bidder/Applicant.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Grounds for Application to convert going concern sale to slum sale
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Even though the Corporate Debtor was contemplated to be sold as a going concern, the financial status of the Corporate Debtor indicates as follows:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							a. Corporate Debtor has not been operational since&nbsp;2006.
						</li>
						<li>
							b. The machineries available at the factory of the Corporate Debtor are outdated, based on obsolete technologies and presently has no market value.
						</li>
						<li>
							c. Several machineries of the corporate debtor have broken down and not in the repairable condition.
						</li>
						<li>
							d. There are no workers left in the corporate debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							e. The factory of the Corporate Debtor has been built on land encompassing Khasra numbers 316, 317, 318, and 319. However, the Debtor only owns land parcel numbers 31 and 318. This is discrepancy likely triggers a dispute over the factory portion constructed on numbers 317 and 319 at Village Kotvan, Tehsil Chatta, Mathura, Uttar Pradesh-281401.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The fragmented portion of the assets of the Corporate Debtor in terms of its land and building as the debtor is stated to be only the owner for the land parcel comprising Khasra No.316 and 318, whereas the factory of the Corporate Debtor has been built on the land comprising Khasra No.316 to 319. Moreover, several machineries of the Corporate Debtor seem to have broken down and are not in reparable conditions. We also notice that the Liquidator on the basis of the recommendations of the SCC as well as in the reply has observed that a substantial amount of Rs.29,36,000,00/- has already been deposited and only the balance amount which is remaining is liable to be deposited.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further the issues raised by the applicant in its application would render the purchase of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern untenable. The Liquidator, therefore, has observed in its reply that the request of the applicant to consider the present sale of the assets as on slump sale basis would not prejudice the entitlement of the creditors of the Corporate Debtor or any other person. It is also stated that the applicant has under taken to bear the requisite stamp duty as may be applicable for the purchase of the Corporate Debtor on slump sale basis over and above the total amount which has been provided in the bid submitted by the applicant.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The SCC has also resolved and granted its consent for considering the sale of the assets as on slump sale basis subject to the payment of the entire balance amount within three days from the date of approval by this Adjudicating Authority.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Consider the request made in the application as being bona fide based upon the Resolution duly passed by the SCC which comprises of the Life Insurance Corporation of India, ARCIL, Stressed Assets Stabilization Fund and UTI Trustee Company Private Limited amongst others. Some of the Members of the Stake Holder Committee however, either were not present or were present but did not vote in the meeting.
							<strong>
								 (p20)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Pass the following order: &ndash;
							<ul>
								<li>
									(1). The sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor would be deemed to be on slum sale basis instead of the sale as a going concern.
								</li>
								<li>
									(2). The remaining/ balance amount to be paid by the applicant, shall be paid within a period of three days from the date of passing of this order.
								</li>
								<li>
									(3). The applicant shall bear all expenses on conversion of sale of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern to sale on slump sale basis such as stamp duty, registration expenses and travel expenses of the Liquidator.
									<strong>
										 (p21)
									</strong>
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
						<li>
							Since, by our interim orders dated 25th January, 2024 and 30th January, 2024 as well as the order dated 1st February, 2024, the time period for deposit of the remaining amount to be paid by the applicant/ Successful Auction Bidder has been extended, therefore, we order that the remaining amount be paid within a period of three days from the date of passing of this order and would not attract any further interest, as the last date of deposit of the remaining amount (90 days from the issuance of the LoI) would be deemed to be 3 days after the date of passing of the present order.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The above IA No.68 of 2024 is thus disposed off in terms of the above directions.&nbsp;Ordered accordingly.
							<strong>
								 (p23-24)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
