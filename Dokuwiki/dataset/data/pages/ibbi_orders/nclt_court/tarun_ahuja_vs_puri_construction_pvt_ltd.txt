	<h1>
		Number of Allottees has to be seen on date of IBC Section 7 petition filing and subsequent withdrawal of certain allottees in view of the settlement arose would not affect the maintainability of the petition | Home buyers approached RERA or NCDRC before approaching NCLT remain Financial Creditors under Section 5(8)(f) of IBC &ndash; Tarun Ahuja and Ors. Vs. Puri Construction Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 27, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment in Real Estate Insolvency, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT New Delhi Bench holds that: (i) In view of Vishal Chelani &amp; Ors. Vs Debashis Nanda, (2023) 117 SC judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, it is concluded that irrespective of the fact that the home buyer allottees approached RERA or NCDRC before approaching this Adjudicating Authority, the status of the home buyers being &lsquo;financial creditors&rsquo; under Section 5(8)(f) of the Code remains intact. Hence, the averment of the corporate Debtor that the allottees who approached RERA or NCDRC ceases to be considered as &lsquo;financial creditors&rsquo; under Section 5(8)(f) of the Code does not hold any ground. (ii) In view of the Judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the Manish Kumar Vs Union of India (2021) 16 SC, the number of allottees has to be seen on the date of the filing of the petition and subsequent withdrawal of certain allottees in view of the settlement arose between settled allottees and the Corporate Debtor would not affect the maintainability of the present petition. (iii) The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd. &amp; Anr. Vs Union of India &amp; Ors. (2019) 13 SC has upheld the status of allottees of a real estate project as financial creditors provided &ldquo;the allottees are interested in completion of the project&rdquo;, however, such is not the position in the present case. In the present case, the homebuyers are concerned only with seeking refund of the money and have no longer interest in taking over the possession of the Flats.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This is a Company Petition filed under p 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 by Tarun Ahuja &amp; Ors. (homebuyers/Financial Creditors), seeking to initiate CIRP against M/s Puri Construction Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
						<li>
							In the present case, the Applicants are the group of allottees who have booked their flats in a project namely &ldquo;Aanand Vilas&rdquo; of the Corporate Debtor, M/s Puri Construction Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) and the Corporate Debtor is engaged in the business of real estate activities which includes developing, buying, selling, renting, managing and appraising real estate amongst others. Further, it is envisaged under Section 5(8)(f)(ii) of the Code that the definition of &lsquo;allottees&rsquo; for the purpose of IBC, shall have same meaning as defined under Section 2(d) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Issue
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Following issues need to be considered:
					</p>
					<p>
						i. Whether homebuyers who have approached RERA or NCDRC for refund would still be treated as &lsquo;Financial Creditors&rsquo; in terms of Section 5(8)(f) of the Code?
					</p>
					<p>
						ii. Issue of threshold on the date of filing of the present Section 7 petition.
					</p>
					<p>
						iii. Whether there is a default by the Corporate Debtor in giving possession to the homebuyers?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue No. 1: Whether homebuyers who have approached RERA or NCDRC for refund would still be treated as &lsquo;Financial Creditors&rsquo; in terms of Section 5(8)(f) of the Code?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the present case, the petition has been filed by 83 allottees holding 51 units. However, subsequently, settlement arose between certain allottees and the Corporate Debtor and therefore, such allottees withdrew their names from the present petition. Further, it is the contention of the Corporate Debtor that as on the date of filing 310 out of 512 units were ready to move for the general public. Further, it is contended by the Corporate Debtor that 41 out of 51-unit holders had approached either RERA or NCDRC for seeking refund of the amount already paid to the Corporate Debtor, however, such&nbsp;applications before RERA or NCDRC were either dismissed or disposed of as withdrawn,&nbsp;therefore, such allottees ceases to be considered as &lsquo;financial creditors&rsquo; in the light of&nbsp;Section 5(8)(f) of the Code. However, it is pertinent to refer to the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Vishal Chelani &amp; Ors. Vs Debashis Nanda
							</em>
							 ,
							<em>
								 (2023) 117 SC
							</em>
							 , wherein, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has clarified that once an allottee seeks remedies under the RERA Act and opts for a refund of money, they are still considered homebuyers.
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the above-mentioned judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, it is concluded that
							<strong>
								 irrespective of the fact that the home buyer allottees approached RERA or NCDRC before approaching this Adjudicating Authority, the status of the home buyers being &lsquo;financial creditors&rsquo; under Section 5(8)(f) of the Code remains intact.
							</strong>
							 Hence, the averment of the corporate Debtor that the allottees who approached RERA or NCDRC ceases to be considered as &lsquo;financial creditors&rsquo; under Section 5(8)(f) of the Code does not hold any ground. Therefore, the contention of the Corporate Debtor in this regard is not sustainable.
							<strong>
								 (p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue No. 2:&nbsp;Issue of threshold on the date of filing of the present Section 7 petition
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As to the fact that certain allottees withdrew their application after filing of the main petition under Section 7 of the Code, reference is taken from the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar Vs Union of India&nbsp;(2021) 16 SC
							</em>
							 , wherein, it was held that the quorum of 100 is to be seen as &ldquo;
							<strong>
								 on the date of presentation of the petition&rdquo; and not at the time of hearing or admission.(p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, in view of the Judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the
							<em>
								 Manish Kumar (Supra)
							</em>
							 ,
							<strong>
								 the number of allottees has to be seen on the date of the filing of the petition and subsequent withdrawal of certain allottees in view of the settlement arose between settled allottees and the Corporate Debtor would not affect the maintainability of the present petition.
							</strong>
							 Thus, this Adjudicating Authority is of the view that the total number of allottees being 83, at the date of the filing of the instant application, fulfills the threshold limit of minimum 10 percent as envisaged under Section 7 of the IB Code, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue No. 3: Whether there is a default by the Corporate Debtor in giving possession to the homebuyers?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the present case, the Applicants being the homebuyer allottees booked 51 units out of 512 units in the March-April 2013 in the project named &ldquo;Aanand Vilas&rdquo; developed by the Corporate Debtor. The Financial Creditors were allotted units in April 2013 vide provisional allotments letters after depositing the booking amount in lieu of the Units. The Applicants paid principal amount to the tune of Rs. 73,22,93,308 as payment in&nbsp;lieu of allotment of flats. The total amount claimed to be due as on 30.01.2020 is Rs.&nbsp;1,11,93,76,405 which is inclusive of interest of Rs. 38,70,83,097.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is observed that as per clause 11(a) of the Apartment Buyer&rsquo;s Agreement (ABA) dated 07.11.2013, the construction of the apartment had to be completed within a period of 48 months from the date of the execution of the agreement. Further, if due to Force Majeure circumstances, the project fails to be completed within the prescribed period of 48 months, an additional grace of 180 days shall be provided to the company to complete the project and apply for the occupation certificate. Therefore, the possession was to be handed over to the Applicants by June 2018. However, the Corporate Debtor failed to handover the possession to the Applicants within the mentioned period citing the reasons for delay in the completion of the project as delay in making remaining payment by some of the allottees, stay on construction activities in NCR in the year 2015 due to increased levels of pollution which could not be foreseen in the year 2013 at the time of the execution of the Agreement. The Corporate Debtor further stated that the unexpected demonetization in the year 2016 lead to labor shortage which lead to the hindrance in the completion of the project. It is observed that the Occupancy Certificate was received by the Corporate Debtor on 24.01.2019 and further, the offer of possession was sent to the allottees on 20.05.2019.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is observed that the delay in offering possession was less than one year, however, the Applicants herein, refused to make payment and take the possession of the apartments rather sought refund of the money on the basis of delay in offering possession of the apartments. On the perusal of the clause 11(b), 11(c) and 15 of the ABA, it is observed that where there is a delay in offering possession to the allottees, however, the Corporate Debtor pays compensation to the allottees from the date of delay till the date of offering of possession, then such, shall not be treated as a default on the part of the Company.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In the present case, the Corporate Debtor adjusted compensation @ 10.75 % p.a. on the amounts paid by each allottee for the alleged period of delay in the 2019 itself before filing of the present petition. Further, as per the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Vineet Kumar &amp; Anr. Vs DLF Universal Ltd.
							</em>
							<em>
								 2019 SCC Online NCDRC 9
							</em>
							 , the delay of less than one year is considered as &lsquo;reasonable&rsquo;. Therefore, in view of the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Vineet Kumar (Supra)
							</em>
							 , it is observed that the delay of less than one year in the real estate project was not unreasonable and hence, the Applicants in the present case are not justified in seeking refund of the amount paid and refusing to take possession of the apartments. Further, the Applicants had approached RERA or NCDRC for the purpose of seeking refund, however, their applications were either dismissed or withdrawn and the RERA vide its order dated 09.04.2019 was of the view that Applicants are not entitled to receive refund merely on the ground of lapse in deemed date of possession and such an order of the RERA attained finality. Further, the fact that the Applicants are seeking refund of the money reflects the intent of the Applicants that the Applicants are concerned only with refund of money paid and they are no longer interested in the completion of the project. Therefore, we are of the view that &lsquo;no debt&rsquo; is owed to the home buyer allottees in the present case.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is further observed that the occupancy certificate was received on 24.01.2019 and the offer of possession was sent on 20.05.2019. Further, the project is complete with all the basic amenities and as on date allottees of 445 apartments had made the payment of the final demand and taken possession of their units. It is further observed that the project is complete and the p of unit holders who approached this Adjudicating Authority are in default of refusing to make payment of their remaining dues and taking possession of their apartments rather emphasizing on seeking refund of the amount paid. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd. &amp; Anr. Vs Union of India &amp; Ors.&nbsp;(2019) 13 SC
							</em>
							 has upheld the status of allottees of a real estate project as financial creditors provided &ldquo;the allottees are interested in completion of the project&rdquo;, however, such is not the position in the present case. In the present case, the homebuyers are concerned only with seeking refund of the money and have no longer interest in taking over the possession of the Flats. Further, the Corporate Debtor relies upon the post completion Brochure of the project which clearly demonstrates by way of actual images that the project in question is complete and nothing remains to be done in the project. Hence, we are of the view that there is &lsquo;no default&rsquo; on the part of the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is further observed that
							<strong>
								 the Corporate Debtor is a fully solvent company having net worth of Rs. 491 Crores as on 31.03.2022.
							</strong>
							 Further, as per the decision of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Vidarbha Industries Power Limited Vs Axis Bank Limited
							</em>
							<em>
								 (2022) 91 SC
							</em>
							 , CIRP shall not be initiated against companies which are fully solvent and capable of paying its debts.
							<strong>
								 (p18)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							This Adjudicating Authority is further of the view that had the CIRP been initiated against the Corporate Debtor, it would result in irreparable damage to the allottees of 445 apartments who had made the final payment and taken possession of their respective units. Hence, this Adjudicating Authority is not inclined towards initiating CIRP against the Corporate Debtor.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In view of the conspectus of facts and applicable law as discussed above, the present application filed under Section 7 of the IB Code, 2016 filed by the Applicant against the Corporate Debtor lacks merit, is
							<strong>
								 not maintainable
							</strong>
							 , and is liable to be
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 . In the light of the above, the instant petition COMPANY PETITION IB (IBC)/755/(PB)/2020 being devoid of merit, is hereby dismissed.
							<strong>
								 (p20-21)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Respondent has also filed an Interlocutory Application bearing I.A. No. 6091/ND/2022 seeking dismissal of the Section 7 petition bearing CP IB No. 755/PB/2020 on the ground of the petition being filed fraudulently. However, in light of the observations made hereinabove, in the CP IB No. 755/PB/2020, the I.A./6091/ND/2022 stands dismissed as infructuous.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
