							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Ld. Counsel for the RP submits that the forensic audit report was discussed at the CoC meeting held 20.03.2020 and from the perusal of the said report, it appeared that the Corporate Debtor had repaid unsecured loans/advances/outstanding balances which are preferential in nature. The payments have put the unsecured creditors in a beneficial position over the Secured Financial Creditors, who were not paid or even if paid, at marginal percentage. That the Forensic Auditor in its report has concluded that the repayment of the aforesaid unsecured loans to the related party entities is preferential transaction within the meaning of p 43 of the Code. The following issues are to be taken into consideration:
						</p>
						<p>
							a. Whether the Resolution Professional has filed the application is in consonance with the Code and Regulations governing Corporate Insolvency Process?
						</p>
						<p>
							b. Whether this application can survive after the Resolution Plan is approved? Submission of the Resolution Professional
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								<em>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</em>
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that before entering into the merits of the case, it is important to examine whether the RP has adhered to the timeframe in bringing the application for avoidance of preferential transactions under p 44 of the Code. The timeline as provided in regulation 35A CIRP Regulations have not been adhered to. That RP has filed the application on the 389th day of the CIRP period. Moreover, the most vital essence of regulation 35A(3) of the CIRP Regulations is missing in the present case, i.e., the determination to be made by the RP that there are preferential transactions. The RP has heavily relied on the Forensic Auditor&rsquo;s Report and has not given any independent reasons for determination of preferential transactions other than the explanation as per the Forensic Auditor&rsquo;s report. We are also satisfied with the explanations preferred by the Respondents in regard to the transactions in question.
						</p>
						<p>
							It held that we cannot avoid the fact that the RP had filed an application for approval of the Resolution Plan on 14.07.2020 and thereafter filed an application under Section 43 of the Code on 24.07.2020. The Resolution Plan was approved on 12.08.2020. On perusal of the order dated 12.08.2020, it is apparent that the RP had prayed for urgent hearing of the application for approval of the Resolution Plan. The RP was duty bound to file the application for preferential transaction within time and also seek for urgent hearing of the application before the plan is approved. Once the Resolution Plan is approved, the Corporate Debtor is managed by a new management and the RP becomes functus officio. An application for avoidance of preferential transaction cannot be carried on by the Resolution Professional on behalf of the Corporate Debtor. The order passed by the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court of Delhi in&nbsp;
							<em>
								Venus Recruiters Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India and Others (2020)  41 HC
							</em>
							, (paras 73, 77, 80, 89, 92) is crystal clear with respect to the second issue.
						</p>
						<p>
							The feeling is inescapable that the RP has filed the application under p 43 read with p 44 of the Code only to avoid adverse scrutiny on the part of the IBBI and not with any real intention to pursue the alleged preferential transactions to their logical end. That may be the only plausible reason for filing the p 43 application after filing the application for approval of resolution plan.&nbsp;Given the circumstances of the case, we are satisfied that the prayer made in the IA should not be allowed.
							<strong>
								(p21-24)
							</strong>
						</p>
