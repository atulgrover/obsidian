	<h1>
		Petition u/s 9 of IBC arising from default in consent terms, having been taken on record by Tribunal on earlier occasions, is maintainable | Default in payment of settlement amount, even if it includes the interest component also, can be sustained, as the settlement amount itself becomes the principal amount when admitted as lump sum obligation &ndash; Panchmahal Steel Ltd. Vs. Kundan Industries Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 10, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Mumbai Bench held that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of Ahluwalia Contracts (India) Ltd. vs. Jasmine Buildmart Pvt. Ltd. (2023) 601 NCLAT has distinguished the decision in the case of Trafigura India Private Limited vs. TDT Copper Ltd. (2022) 714 NCLAT and Amrit Kumar Agrawal vs. Tempo Appliances Pvt. Ltd. (2020) 425 NCLAT and held that Settlement during earlier Section 9 proceeding was only mode of payment to the operational debt and it allowed the appeal of the Operational Creditor reversing the Order passed by Coordinate Bench of this Tribunal where the Petition filed on ground of breach of settlement terms was held not maintainable. In view of the foregoing decision of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of Ahluwalia Contracts (India) Ltd. (2023) 601 NCLAT, we have no hesitation to hold that the present petition arising from default in consent terms, having been taken on record by this Tribunal on earlier occasions, is maintainable.
	</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Operational Creditor filed Company Petition (18) No. 4573 (MB)/2018 in the National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench, (&ldquo;said Company Petition&rdquo;), to initiate CIRP against the Corporate Debtor under Section 9 of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							Pursuant to filing of the said Company Petition, negotiations&nbsp;towards settlement were initiated by the Corporate Debtor with the&nbsp;Operational Creditor and ultimately, Consent Terms dated 2 May,&nbsp;2019 were executed between the Corporate Debtor and the&nbsp;Operational Creditor (&ldquo;said Consent&nbsp;Terms&rdquo;).
						</li>
						<li>
							However in complete breach of its binding obligations under the said&nbsp;Consent Terms, the Corporate Debtor defaulted in its payment&nbsp;obligations and failed to make payment from seventh instalment&nbsp;onwards. Clauses 7 and 8 of the said Consent Terms inter alia set out&nbsp;the consequences of default, acceleration of outstanding settlement&nbsp;amount, interest thereon and right of Operational Creditor to reinitiate CIRP.
						</li>
						<li>
							In these circumstances, the Operational Creditor issued its Demand&nbsp;Notice dated 18 December 2019, to the Corporate Debtor,&nbsp;(&ldquo;said Demand Notice&rdquo;), under Section&nbsp;8 of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							In light thereof, the Operational Creditor filed another&nbsp;Company Petition (IB) No. 1489 (MB)/2020 in the NCLT, Mumbai Bench, (&ldquo;said 2nd Company Petition&rdquo;) to initiate CIRP&nbsp;against the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							At that time, the Corporate Debtor agreed and undertook to&nbsp;make balance payment of Rs. 2,50,00,000/- to the Operational Creditor by 31.05.2022. This Tribunal recorded this obligation of&nbsp;the Corporate Debtor and disposed of the 2nd Company Petition.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As regards contention that the petition u/s 9 can not lie for claim of interest, we find that the facts in case of
							<em>
								 Rohit Motawat vs. Madhu Sharma Proprietor Hind Chem Corporation &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2023) 128 NCLAT
							</em>
							 are distinguishable.
							<strong>
								 (p5.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							It is undisputed fact that this Tribunal had dismissed earlier two company petitions for initiating of CIRP in the matter of Corporate Debtor on the ground of this settlement only and no such ground was raised therein. On the contrary, the Corporate Debtor had admitted to pay a sum of Rs. 2,50,00,000/- during proceedings in C.P. (IB) No. 1489 (MB)/2020 as recorded in Order dated 31.03.2022, which clearly shows that this admission became the basis of dismissal of the said petition. Accordingly, we have no hesitation to hold that the default in payment of settlement amount, even if it includes the interest component also, can be sustained, as the settlement amount itself becomes the principal amount when admitted as lump sum obligation.
							<strong>
								 (p5.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
							<em>
								 Ahluwalia Contracts (India) Ltd. vs. Jasmine Buildmart Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 601 NCLAT
							</em>
							 has distinguished the decision in the case of
							<em>
								 Trafigura
							</em>
							<em>
								 India Private Limited vs. TDT Copper Ltd
							</em>
							 .
							<em>
								 (2022) 714 NCLAT
							</em>
							 and
							<em>
								 Amrit Kumar Agrawal vs. Tempo Appliances Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2020) 425 NCLAT
							</em>
							 and held that Settlement during earlier Section 9 proceeding was only mode of payment to the operational debt and it allowed the appeal of the Operational Creditor reversing the Order passed by Coordinate Bench of this Tribunal where the Petition filed on ground of breach of settlement terms was held not maintainable.
							<strong>
								 (p5.4)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In view of the foregoing decision of Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the case of
							<em>
								 Ahluwalia Contracts (India) Ltd.&nbsp;(2023) 601 NCLAT
							</em>
							 , we have no hesitation to hold that the present petition arising from default in consent terms, having been taken on record by this Tribunal on earlier occasions, is maintainable.
							<strong>
								 (p5.5)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Since the present petition is complete and is within limitation period; and&nbsp;there exists an operational debt of more than Rs. 1.00 crores and the same&nbsp;is in default without any dispute, we find it fit case for commencing the&nbsp;CIRP in terms of p 9 of the Code.
							<strong>
								 (p6)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
