	<h1>
		Can an insolvency proceeding be initiated against the Corporate Debtor on the grounds of the foreign award? &ndash; Trans Sea Transport B.V. Vs. Lords Polymer [India] Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		November 23, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority dismissed the application filed u/s 9 of IBC referring Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in Government of India v. Vedanta Limited (2020) 165 SC, Usha Holdings LCC &amp; v. Francorp Advisors Pvt. Ltd. [2018] 115 NCLAT and PEC ltd. v. Austbulk Shipping Sdn. Bhd. (2018) 154 SC and held that it is imperative to mention that for the enforcement of foreign award in India, an enforcement/ execution petition is required to be filed before the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court, as per the amendment to p 47 by Act 3 of 2016 (which came into force on 23 October, 2015). A proceeding seeking recognition and enforcement of a foreign award has different stages: in the first stage, the Court would decide about the enforceability of the award having regard to the requirements of p 47 and 48 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. Once the enforceability is decided, it would further proceed to take further effective steps for execution of the award.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>

					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						This is a Company Petition filed under p 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 by Trans Sea Transport B.V. (Operational Creditor), for initiation of CIRP against Lords Polymer [India] Private Limited (Corporate Debtor). The prima-facie issue that arises is this case is that can an insolvency proceeding be initiated against the Corporate Debtor on the grounds of the foreign award?
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The position was well settled by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								<strong>
									 Government of India v. Vedanta Limited&nbsp;(2020) 165 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							 , where in it has been held that &ldquo;
							<em>
								 a foreign award does not become a &ldquo;foreign decree&rdquo; at any stage of the proceedings. The foreign award is enforced as a deemed decree of the Indian Court which has adjudicated upon the petition filed under Section 47, and the objections raised under Section 48 by the party which is resisting enforcement of the award.
								<strong>
									 A foreign award is not a decree by itself which is executable as such under Section 49 of the Act. The enforcement of the foreign award takes place only after the court is satisfied that the foreign award is enforceable under Chapter 1 in Part II of the 1996 Act.
								</strong>
								 After the stages of Sections 47 and 48 are completed, the award becomes enforceable as a deemed decree, as provided by Section 49. The phrase &ldquo;that court&rdquo; refers to the Indian court which has adjudicated on the petition filed under Section 47, and the application under Section 48.
							</em>
							 &rdquo;&nbsp;[emphasis implied]
							<strong>
								 (p9)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In similar situation in
							<em>
								<strong>
									 Usha Holdings LCC &amp; v. Francorp Advisors Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;[2018] 115 NCLAT
								</strong>
							</em>
							 , the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT has held that &ldquo;
							<em>
								 In view of the aforesaid decision in &ldquo;Binani Industries Limited&rdquo;, we hold that the Adjudicating authority not being a Court or &ldquo;Tribunal&rdquo; and &lsquo;Insolvency Resolution Process&rsquo; not being a litigation, it has no jurisdiction to decide whether a foreign decree is legal or illegal. Whatever findings the Adjudicating Authority has given with regard to legality and propriety of foreign decree in question being without jurisdiction is nullity in the eye of law.
							</em>
							 &rdquo;
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, it is imperative to mention that for the enforcement of foreign award in India, an enforcement/ execution petition is required to be filed before the Hon&rsquo;ble High Court, as per the amendment to p 47 by Act 3 of 2016 (which came into force on 23 October, 2015). A proceeding seeking recognition and enforcement of a foreign award has different stages: in the first stage, the Court would decide about the enforceability of the award having regard to the requirements of p 47 and 48 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. Once the enforceability is decided, it would further proceed to take further effective steps for execution of the award. (PEC ltd. v. Austbulk Shipping Sdn. Bhd.&nbsp;(2018) 154 SC).
							<strong>
								 (p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The above provision of law made it clear that High Court only has exclusive jurisdiction to deal with foreign award and give effect to the same. In this context, the Petition being C.P. (IB) No. 186 /KB/2019 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 . Needless to say that the Operational Creditor is at liberty to resort to other remedies that may be available to it under any other law.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
