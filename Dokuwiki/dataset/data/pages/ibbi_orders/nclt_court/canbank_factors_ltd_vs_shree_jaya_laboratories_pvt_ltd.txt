	<h1>
		Factoring transactions on recourse basis only will fall within the mischief of Section 5(8)(e) of the IBC- M/s Canbank Factors Ltd. Vs. M/s. Shree Jaya Laboratories Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Hyderabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 23, 2022
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that in the background of the RBI Circular "Provision of Factoring Services by Banks &mdash; Review", dated 30 July 2015, when we refer the definition of Financial Debt in p 5(8) of the Code, more particularly the clause (e) thereof, it becomes immediately clear that only the factoring transactions in respect of "receivables other than on non-recourse basis" are included therein. In other words, factoring transactions on recourse basis only will fall within the mischief of Section 5(8)(e) of the Code. The reason to restrict the mischief of Section 5(8)(e) to debt arising from discounting of bills otherwise than on non-recourse basis only fits perfectly into the concept of Financial Debt under the Code having the two intrinsic ingredients of 'disbursal" of funds for "time value of money" which would obtain in the case of an Assignor only.
	</p>
</div>
<div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2022) 712 NCLTCase Name:M/s Canbank Factors Ltd. Vs. M/s. Shree Jaya Laboratories Pvt. Ltd.Financial Creditor:M/s Canbank Factors Ltd.Corporate Debtor:M/s. Shree Jaya Laboratories Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:Company Petition IB/83/2021Judgment Date:21-Jul-22Court/Bench:NCLT Hyderabad BenchMember (Judicial):Shri Bhaskara Pantula Mohan (B.P.Mohan)Member (Technical):Dr. Binod Kumar Sinha
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The RBI makes it abundantly clear that in the case of factoring &ldquo;with recourse&rdquo;, the exposure is to be reckoned on the assignor and in the case of factoring &lsquo;&rdquo;without recourse&rdquo;, the exposure is to be reckoned on the debtor (person liable to pay the receivable).&nbsp;In other words, in a case of factoring with recourse, it is the assignor, who will be the borrower and in cases of factoring without recourse, it will be the debtor (purchaser of invoiced goods or person liable to pay the receivable) who will be the borrower.
						<strong>
							 (p17-18)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the background of the RBI Circular &ldquo;
						<strong>
							 Provision of Factoring Services by Banks &mdash; Review
						</strong>
						 &ldquo;, dated 30 July 2015, when we refer the definition of Financial Debt in p 5(8) of the Code, more particularly the clause (e) thereof, it becomes immediately clear that only the factoring transactions in respect of &ldquo;receivables other than on non-recourse basis&rdquo; are included therein. In other words, factoring transactions on recourse basis only will fall within the mischief of Section 5(8)(e) of the Code. The reason to restrict the mischief of Section 5(8)(e) to debt arising from discounting of bills otherwise than on non-recourse basis only fits perfectly into the concept of Financial Debt under the Code having the two intrinsic ingredients of &lsquo;disbursal&rdquo; of funds for &ldquo;time value of money&rdquo; which would obtain in the case of an Assignor only.
						<strong>
							 (p19)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In any case, we have referred earlier to the RBI guidelines which provide for recognizing the debt in the hands of the customer/ debtor only if the factoring has been done on non-recourse basis which clearly and admittedly is not the case before us. We also hasten to observe here that even if such an event would have occurred, i.e., the factoring transaction was on &ldquo;non-recourse&rdquo; basis, the Factor/ Assignee may have a right to reckon the debt in the hands of the Customer/ debtor and may have legal remedies to recover the debt but will have no eligibility to file an Application U/ s 7 of the Code against the Customer/ debtor / purchaser of goods as Section 5(8)(e) shall not be applicable in such factoring &ldquo;with non-recourse&rdquo; cases.
						<strong>
							 (p21)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						In the instant case, it is seen that the Applicant/ Financial Creditor has nowhere discounted the bills on request of the Corporate Debtor nor has there been any disbursal of funds from the Applicant to the Respondent so as to claim the existence of a financial debt. The terms and conditions in the agreement between the Financial Creditor and M/s P. Praful &amp; Company Agency (India) Pvt. Ltd clearly states about the Type of factoring Facility, which clearly shows &lsquo;Sale Bill Factoring Limit &mdash; With Recourse&rsquo;. Therefore, in view of the above discussion based on the provisions of the
						<strong>
							 Factoring Regulation Act, 2011
						</strong>
						 and the RBI Guidelines, we are of the considered view that while the Applicant here may have recourse against the assignor namely M/s P. Praful &amp; Company Agency (India) Pvt. Ltd. u/s 7 of the Code, it certainly has no eligibility to file and maintain the instant Application against the respondent herein namely M/s Jaya Laboratories Pvt Ltd. as a Financial Creditor.
						<strong>
							 (p22-24)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						On a consideration of all the facts and the legal position, we have no doubt in holding that in view of the explicit clause in the factoring agreement entered into between the Applicant/ Assignee and the Client/ Assignor in relation to the purchase of debts ON RECOURSE basis, read with the RBI Guidelines discussed supra, there is no jural relationship of creditor and borrower between the Applicant and Respondent. Consequently, the Applicant herein cannot claim to be a Financial Creditor qua the Respondent so as to maintain the instant Application filed u/s 7 of the Code against the Respondent.&nbsp;Accordingly, instant Application is dismissed with liberty to the Applicant to seek remedy elsewhere in a manner known to Law.&nbsp;With the above, Company Petition 1B/83/2021 stands disposed. No Order as to costs.
						<strong>
							 (p26-28)
						</strong>
					</p>
