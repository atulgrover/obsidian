		<h1>
			No notice issued under Section 79 of the State GST/CGST Act can be acted upon by any Central/ State Authority against the Corporate Debtor undergoing CIRP &ndash; Mr. Ashutosh Agarwala, RP of Cox &amp; Kings Ltd. Vs. Joint Commissioner of State Tax, Kolkata &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			December 16, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			NCLT observed that as far as Section 83 of the GST Legislation is concerned, it is a settled position of law that attachment of assets of Corporate Debtor during CIRP drastically affects the stakeholders of the Corporate Debtor and therefore NCLT/ NCLAT time and again have issued directions to release the assets so attached u/s 83 of the GST Legislation following Sections 14 and 238 of the IBC. With respect to attachment of assets of a Company undergoing CIRP following Section 79 of the GST Act 2017 constitute violation of the &lsquo;moratorium&rsquo; imposed under Section 14 of the IBC which explicitly prohibits any action to recover or enforce any security interest created by the Corporate Debtor. Therefore, no notice issued u/s 79 of the State GST/ CGST Act can be acted upon by any Central/ State Authority against the Corporate Debtor undergoing CIRP.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT observed that as far as Section 83 of the GST&nbsp;Legislation is concerned, it is a settled position of law that&nbsp;attachment of assets of Corporate Debtor during CIRP&nbsp;drastically affects the stakeholders of the Corporate Debtor&nbsp;and therefore NCLT/ NCLAT time and again have issued&nbsp;directions to release the assets so attached u/s 83 of the GST&nbsp;Legislation following Sections 14 and 238 of the IBC. With&nbsp;respect to attachment of assets of a Company undergoing&nbsp;CIRP following Section 79 of the GST Act 2017 constitute&nbsp;violation of the &lsquo;moratorium&rsquo; imposed under Section 14 of the&nbsp;IBC which explicitly prohibits any action to recover or enforce&nbsp;any security interest created by the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;Therefore, no notice issued u/s 79 of the State GST/ CGST Act&nbsp;can be acted upon by any Central/ State Authority against the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor undergoing CIRP. The Bench has clarity&nbsp;that any payment to the State Tax Authority would be in terms&nbsp;of the IBC, as per the Resolution Plan that is approved or as&nbsp;per the waterfall prescribed u/s 53 of the IBC. The Bench also&nbsp;notes that as per Section 83(2) of the State GST/ CGST Act&nbsp;any provisional attachment u/s 83(1) will have no effect after&nbsp;expiry of one year. Therefore, the West Bengal GST Notice&nbsp;and Maharashtra GST Notice would have been operational&nbsp;only till 08.09.2020 and 24.10.2020 respectively and&nbsp;thereafter ceased to have effect.
