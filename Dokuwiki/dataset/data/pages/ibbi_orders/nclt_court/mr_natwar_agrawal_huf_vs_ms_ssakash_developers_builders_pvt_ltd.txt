	<h1>
		An allottee in Real Estate Project, who subsequently becomes a Decree Holder under RERA Act, 2016 continues to be a Financial Creditor in the class of Home Buyers and shall continue to be governed by the threshold limit prescribed under Second Proviso to Section 7(1) of the Code &ndash; Mr. Natwar Agrawal (HUF) Vs. Ms. Ssakash Developers &amp; Builders Pvt. Ltd &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		August 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		An CIRP application filed by the applicant, had obtained a Decree from RERA in capacity of allottee in a Real Estate Project, under Section 7 of IBC In this important decision, NCLT Mumbai Bench held that: (i) Allottee in Real Estate Project is covered under the definition of Financial Debt contained in under Explanation to Section 5(8)(f) of the Code. Accordingly, the applicant, being holder of a decree in capacity of allottee is a Financial Creditor. (ii) Further, the Bench finds that second proviso to p 7(1) prescribes the threshold limit specifically in relation to Home Buyers Class so as to discourage multiple applications being filed by the allottees in a Real Estate Project. This bench feels that an allottee in Real Estate Project, who subsequently becomes a Decree Holder under RERA Act, continues to be a creditor in the class of Home Buyers and shall continue to be governed by the threshold limit prescribed under second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code. (iii) In the present case, it is not in dispute that the threshold prescribed under second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code is not met in the present case, the application could not have been maintainable had the applicant filed this application without obtaining decree under RERA.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							An application is filed by HUF through Karta, the Financial Creditor/Applicant, under Section 7 of the IBC for initiating CIRP in the case of M/s. Ssakash Developers &amp; Builders Pvt. Ltd, Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
						<li>
							The applicant states that as the Corporate Debtor did not execute the agreement for sale as per Section 13 of the Real Estate Regulations and Development Act, 2016(RERA) and hence, aggrieved by the aforesaid conduct the applicant was coerced to file a Complaint before the Maharashtra Real Estate Regulation Authority (MRERA) u/s 13 of the Real Estate Regulations and Development Act, 2016. The said complaint came to be heard by the Hon&rsquo;ble RERA Tribunal wherein the Hon&rsquo;ble RERA Tribunal was pleased to pass the order in favour of the Applicant.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 I. A Decree from RERA in capacity of allottee in a Real Estate Project and allottee in Real Estate Project is covered under the definition of Financial Debt
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Bench referred judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble Madras High Court in the case of
							<em>
								 Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company Ltd. Vs. Navrang Roadlines Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 331 HC
							</em>
							 , following the decision of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in case of
							<em>
								 Kotak Mahindra Bank v. A. Balakrishnan &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2022) 62 SC
							</em>
							 and held that decree would be categorised as either financial or operational debt depending on the nature of the underlying claim which stands crystallised through the arbitral or court the nature of the debt due under decree would depend on the nature of transaction from which the decretal debt has arisen.
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 In the present case the applicant had obtained a Decree from RERA in capacity of allottee in a Real Estate Project and allottee in Real Estate Project is covered under the definition of Financial Debt contained in under Explanation to Section 5(8)(f) of the Code. Accordingly, the applicant, being holder of a decree in capacity of allottee is a Financial Creditor.(p3.1&amp;3.2)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Whether an allottee holding a decree from RERA would fall under the class of Home Buyers within category of Financial Creditor under second proviso to Section 7(1) or remain a Financial Creditor u/s 7(1) of IBC
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							At this juncture, this bench considers appropriate whether an allottee holding a decree from RERA would fall under the class of Home Buyers within category of Financial Creditor or it would cease to be an allottee under the class of Home Buyers, but shall remain a Financial Creditor, to determine whether the threshold limit prescribed under second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code or under p 4 of code would apply.
						</li>
						<li>
							The Bench finds that second proviso to p 7(1) prescribes the threshold limit specifically in relation to Home Buyers Class so as to discourage multiple applications being filed by the allottees in a Real Estate Project. This bench feels that
							<strong>
								 an allottee in Real Estate Project, who subsequently becomes a Decree Holder under RERA Act, continues to be a creditor in the class of Home Buyers and shall continue to be governed by the threshold limit prescribed under second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code.(p3.3)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 III. Decision on the present case
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the present case, it is not in dispute that the threshold prescribed under&nbsp;second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code is not met in the present case, the application could not have been maintainable had the applicant filed this application without obtaining decree under RERA. Accordingly, the applicant cannot be allowed to claim eligibility to file application u/s 7 in terms of p 4 of the Code, because if it is made permissible it would tantamount to circumventing the provisions of second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code. In other words, what is not permissible directly shall become permissible via RERA decree route. Accordingly, this bench feels that present petition is hit by bar under second proviso to p 7(1) of the Code.
						</li>
						<li>
							Considering the facts placed before us, this bench is of the view that in such circumstances, that the present case deserves to be
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p3.5&amp;4)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
