		<h1>
			Any increase in the claim amount of the Assenting Financial Creditors due to the invocation of a Bank Guarantee cannot be a ground for challenge by the Dissenting Financial Creditors on discrimination basis &ndash; Union Bank of India Vs. Ms. Vandana Garg (Erstwhile RP/Monitoring Committee Chairperson of Jyoti Structures Ltd.) &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			December 28, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that Section 30(2)(b) of the Code provides for the payment of debts of the Dissenting FCs in such manner as may be specified by the Board, which shall not be less than the amount to be paid to such creditors in accordance with Section 53(1) of the Code in the event of liquidation. Any increase in the claim amount of the Assenting FCs due to the invocation of such BG cannot be a ground for challenge by the Dissenting FCs on grounds of discrimination. Further, the decision to include the invoked amount of the BG to the fund-based debts is a commercial decision of the CoC. Further, Resolution Plan once approved by the AA shall stand frozen and binding on all stakeholders including FCs
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Three Application have been filed by Union Bank of India (Dissenting Financial&nbsp;Creditor), Bank of Maharashtra (Abstaining Financial&nbsp;Creditor) and Central Bank of India (Dissenting&nbsp;Financial Creditor) who have prayed that the discrimination in payment under the&nbsp;Resolution Plan on the basis of the Assenting and Dissenting/Abstaining Financial&nbsp;Creditor be modified to the extent that all Secured Financial Creditors (FCs) inter&nbsp;alia Applicants be treated equally for payment of plan value subject to their&nbsp;individual exposure with the same terms as that of Assenting FCs.
						</p>
						<p>
							Applicant submits that in the approved Resolution Plan, there is&nbsp;glaring inequality in the payment between the Assenting/ Dissenting FCs&nbsp;and Operational Creditors (OCs). OCs are paid 10% more than that of the&nbsp;Dissenting/ Abstaining FCs and Assenting FCs are getting around 18 times&nbsp;more under the Resolution Plan.&nbsp;The Apex Court in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Committee of creditors of Essar Steel India&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Limited Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta&nbsp;[2019]  07 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;held that, &ldquo;equitable treatment is to be&nbsp;accorded to each creditor depending upon the class to which it belongs:&nbsp;secure or unsecure, financial or operational&rdquo;. Therefore, although Section&nbsp;30(2)b)(ii) provides for liquidation value to be given to Dissenting FCs but&nbsp;that does not mean there shall be any Discrimination between the&nbsp;Dissenting and Assenting Creditors under similar class.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that Section 30(2)(b) of the Code provides for the payment of debts of&nbsp;the Dissenting FCs in such manner as may be specified by the Board, which shall&nbsp;not be less than the amount to be paid to such creditors in accordance with&nbsp;Section 53(1) of the Code in the event of liquidation. Explanation 1 to Section 30(2)(b) of the Code further clarifies that distribution in accordance with the&nbsp;provisions of this clause shall be fair and equitable to such creditors.&nbsp;It is pertinent to note that the invocation of BG is as per the terms of Resolution&nbsp;Plan. Thus, any increase in the claim amount of the Assenting FCs due to the&nbsp;invocation of such BG cannot be a ground for challenge by the Dissenting FCs&nbsp;on grounds of discrimination. Further, the decision to include the invoked&nbsp;amount of the BG to the fund-based debts is a commercial decision of the CoC.
						</p>
						<p>
							It held that Resolution Plan once approved by the AA shall stand frozen and binding&nbsp;on all stakeholders including FCs. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Pvt. Ltd. vs. Edelweiss Asset&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Reconstruction Co. Ltd.&nbsp;(2021)  54 SC
							</em>
							&nbsp;held &ldquo;
							<em>
								That once a Resolution Plan is duly approved by the&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Adjudicating Authority under sub-p (1) of Section 31, the claims as&nbsp;provided in the Resolution Plan shall stand frozen and will be binding on the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor, and its employees, members, creditors, including the Central&nbsp;Government any State Government or any local authority, guarantors and other&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								stakeholders.
							</em>
							&rdquo;&nbsp;It is noted that the BG invocation and the revision in the amounts of Assenting&nbsp;FCs is as per the terms of the Resolution Plan. Similarly, in the case of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Ghanshyam Mishra (supra)
							</em>
							&nbsp;it is laid down that Resolution Plan once approved&nbsp;by the AA shall stand frozen and shall be binding on all stakeholders including&nbsp;FCs. In view of this, the prayers are liable to be rejected. Hence ordered.&nbsp;IAs 2025, 2028 and 2035 of 2021 are rejected and dismissed.
							<strong>
								(p6-9)
							</strong>
						</p>
