	<h1>
		The amount invested in a JV project of a promoter and investor is not a Financial Debt &ndash; Deccan Charters Pvt. Ltd. Vs. GSEC Monarch and Deccan Aviation Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Ahmedabad Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 20, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Ahmedabad Bench holds that: (i) The transactions between the applicant and the respondent were intended to be a Joint Venture Agreement for share of business, expertise as narrated in the Business Transfer Agreement. The intention of investment or debt was for the purpose of having a joint venture and the debt and its service and its disbursement as evidenced from the documents do not reveal that it was intended to be given as a creditor-debtor arrangement. (ii) In view of the same and the documents and submissions made, this application is not to be further considered under the provisions under which the submissions have been made. 19. In this regard the judgment passed by Hon'ble NCLAT in the matter of Jagbasera Infratech Pvt. Ltd. v Rawal Variety Construction Ltd. (2022) 324 NCLAT held that the amount invested in a JV project of a promoter and investor is not a financial debt.
	</p>

						<strong>
							 Brief about the decision:
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Under consideration, is an application filed by the Financial Creditor viz. Deccan Charters Private Limited under Section 7 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 against the Corporate Debtor viz. GSEC Monarch and Deccan Aviation Pvt Ltd seeking thereof to initiate CIRP as against the Corporate Debtor.
					</p>
					<p>
						The transactions between the applicant and the respondent were intended to be a Joint Venture Agreement for share of business, expertise as narrated in the Business Transfer Agreement. The intention of investment or debt was for the purpose of having a joint venture and the debt and its service and its disbursement as evidenced from the documents do not reveal that it was intended to be given as a creditor-debtor arrangement.
					</p>
					<p>
						The nature of transactions and the disbursements do not correctly bring the status of the debt which is in-built in an agreement which is intended to be a joint venture for furthering business interests. The same is whether continued or discontinued and whether any liability is to be paid to the applicant or any other party has become a matter of dispute and reference before the Arbitration Counsel as stated in the application.
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the same and the documents and submissions made, this application is not to be further considered under the provisions under which the submissions have been made. In this regard the judgment passed by Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the matter of
						<em>
							 Jagbasera Infratech Pvt Ltd v Rawal Variety Construction Ltd.&nbsp;(2022) 324 NCLAT
						</em>
						 held that the amount invested in a JV project of a promoter and investor is not a financial debt.
					</p>
					<p>
						The application CP(IB) 100 of 2022 filed under Sec 7 is rejected.
					</p>
