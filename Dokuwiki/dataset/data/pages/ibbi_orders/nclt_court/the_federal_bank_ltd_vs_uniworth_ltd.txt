		<h1>
			DRT is not a wrong forum for exclusion of time under p 14 of the Limitation Act since a financial creditor is perfectly entitled to maintain the proceedings initiated by it under the RDDB Act before a DRT for recovery of its dues &ndash; The Federal Bank Limited Vs. Uniworth Limited &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 31, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that the fundamental difference between the facts of Sesh Nath Singh (2021)  49 SC and the present case is this: in Sesh Nath Singh (supra), the financial creditor was a cooperative bank. The Hon'ble Calcutta High Court, before whom the SARFAESI proceedings were challenged, was prima facie of the view that a cooperative bank could not invoke the provisions of the SARFAESI Act. Therefore, the proceedings were stayed pending a final decision on the writ petition. Therefore, both the appellate courts felt that a case was made out for exclusion of time under p 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963. In case the writ proceedings confirmed the prima facie view of the Hon'ble Calcutta High Court that a cooperative bank could not initiate SARFAESI proceedings, then it would have resulted in the proceedings before DRT being quashed. That would have left the creditor therein with only one remedy: to approach the civil court. In these circumstances, the Hon'ble Supreme Court thought it fit to hold that p 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963 would have applicability. As noticed by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Sesh Nath Singh (supra) in para 77 thereof, p 14 provides that an applicant who has prosecuted another civil proceeding with due diligence before a forum which is unable to entertain the same on account of jurisdiction or any other cause of like nature, is entitled to exclusion of time spent in prosecuting such proceeding in computing the period of time. However, such is not the case here. The financial creditor is perfectly entitled to maintain the proceedings initiated by it under the RDDB Act before the learned DRT-I, Kolkata, for recovery of its dues. Neither is the DRT a wrong forum. Therefore, in this conspectus, it is not a fit case for exclusion of time under p 14.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Uniworth Limited, the corporate debtor, has filed the present application seeking dismissal of CP (IB) No.277/KB/2019 on the ground that the debt is barred by law. The financial creditor has filed OA No.160/2003 before the learned Debts Recovery Tribunal-I, Kolkata, on 12.12.2003.
						</p>
						<p>
							Decision of the Adjudicating Authority is summarised in below points:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The fundamental difference between the facts of&nbsp;
								<em>
									Sesh Nath Singh&nbsp;(2021)  49 SC&nbsp;
								</em>
								and&nbsp;the present case is this: in&nbsp;
								<em>
									Sesh Nath Singh (supra)
								</em>
								, the financial creditor was&nbsp;a cooperative bank. The Hon&rsquo;ble Calcutta High Court, before whom the&nbsp;SARFAESI proceedings were challenged, was prima facie of the view that a&nbsp;cooperative bank could not invoke the provisions of the SARFAESI Act.&nbsp;Therefore, the proceedings were stayed pending a final decision on the writ&nbsp;petition. Therefore, both the appellate courts felt that a case was made out&nbsp;for exclusion of time under p 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963. In case&nbsp;the writ proceedings confirmed the prima facie view of the Hon&rsquo;ble Calcutta&nbsp;High Court that a cooperative bank could not initiate SARFAESI proceedings,&nbsp;then it would have resulted in the proceedings before DRT being quashed.&nbsp;That would have left the creditor therein with only one remedy: to approach&nbsp;the civil court. In these circumstances, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court thought&nbsp;it fit to hold that p 14 of the Limitation Act, 1963 would have&nbsp;applicability.
							</li>
							<li>
								As noticed by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in&nbsp;
								<em>
									Sesh Nath Singh (supra)
								</em>
								&nbsp;in para&nbsp;77 thereof, p 14 provides that an applicant who has prosecuted&nbsp;another civil proceeding with due diligence before a forum which is unable&nbsp;to entertain the same on account of jurisdiction or any other cause of like&nbsp;nature, is entitled to exclusion of time spent in prosecuting such proceeding&nbsp;in computing the period of time. However, such is not the case here. The&nbsp;financial creditor is perfectly entitled to maintain the proceedings initiated&nbsp;by it under the RDDB Act before the learned DRT-I, Kolkata, for recovery&nbsp;of its dues. Neither is the DRT a wrong forum. Therefore, in this&nbsp;conspectus, it is not a fit case for exclusion of time under p 14.&nbsp;We have deemed it fit to notice&nbsp;
								<em>
									Sesh Nath Singh (supra)
								</em>
								&nbsp;even though the&nbsp;same was not placed by either side.
							</li>
							<li>
								Now adverting to the documents attached to the petition, it is seen that&nbsp;except for the two balance confirmation letters, all the other documents are&nbsp;dated 16.07.1998, i.e., nearly twenty-three years ago. The two balance&nbsp;confirmation letters are of relatively more recent vintage, dated 10.01.2001&nbsp;and 30.09.2001, i.e., about twenty years ago. Even if we take the latter of&nbsp;the two dates and apply Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963, limitation&nbsp;ran its full course on 29.09.2004, when the IBC was nowhere on the&nbsp;firmament.
								<strong>
									(p5)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
						<p>
							In this view of the matter, we hold that the main company petition bearing&nbsp;CP (IB) No.277/KB/2019 is hopelessly barred by limitation, in so far as&nbsp;the IBC is concerned. Consequently, IA No.1508/KB/2019 is entitled to succeed, being in the nature of a demurrer application and which&nbsp;specifically raises the issue of limitation.Since we have held that the main company petition cannot survive&nbsp;limitation, IA No.1222/KB/2019 for intervention has become infructuous&nbsp;and shall stand dismissed.
							<strong>
								(p6)
							</strong>
						</p>
