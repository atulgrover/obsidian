	<h1>
		Debt Assignment Deed cannot be challenged in petition under Sec. 7 of IBC | Proceedings under IBC are summary proceedings and it is beyond the ambit of Adjudicating Authority to delve into the details regarding the requirement or exemption of registration of Assignment Agreement &ndash; CFM Asset Reconstruction Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M.G. Finvest Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 27, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		In this important judgment, NCLT New Delhi Bench holds that: (i) The assignment of debt essentially being a transaction between the Creditor and the Assignee and assignment being recognized by the Code, 2016 as a valid mode of transfer of rights across the ambit of Section 5(7) of the Code, therefore, the entity who received the said assignment of debt falls within the fold of &ldquo;Financial Creditor‟. (ii) In Palm Products Pvt. Ltd. Vs T.V.L. Narsimha Rao &amp; Anr. (2021) 115 NCLAT, the issue is with regard to admission of claim by the RP not for admission of an application filed under Sec 7 of the Code, 2016. (iii) Proceedings under Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 are summary proceedings and it is beyond the ambit of this Adjudicating authority to delve into the details regarding the requirement or exemption of registration of the Assignment Agreement dated 18.01.2021. Therefore, the assignment agreement cannot be challenged in the petition under Section 7 of the Code, 2016. (iv) Further, the assignment does not affect the liability and obligations of the Corporate Debtor to discharge the debt.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The instant Company Petition is filed under p 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (the Code) by M/s. CFM Asset Reconstruction Pvt. Ltd. (Applicant), a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 and registered with Reserve Bank of India as a of SARFAESI Act, 2002 (Financial Creditor) seeking to initiate CIRP against M.G. Finvest Pvt. Ltd. (Corporate Debtor).
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor has raised an objection on the status of the Applicant as Financial Creditor under Section 5(7) of the Code, 2016 on the ground that the Assignment Agreement dated 18.01.2021 executed between the SBI (original borrower) and Applicant is not legally enforceable and there exist no privity of contract between the Applicant and the Corporate Debtor.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 A. Challenge of Debt Assignment before NCLT
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The assignment of debt essentially being a transaction between the Creditor and the Assignee and assignment being recognized by the Code, 2016 as a valid mode of transfer of rights across the ambit of Section 5(7) of the Code, therefore, the entity who received the said assignment of debt falls within the fold of &ldquo;Financial Creditor‟.
						</li>
						<li>
							Further, Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
							<em>
								 Lalan Kumar Singh v. Phoenix ARC (P) Ltd.,&nbsp;(2018) 225 NCLAT
							</em>
							 observed that the declaration of Assignment Agreement is essentially a civil proceeding.
							<strong>
								 (p10)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor has relied upon
							<em>
								 Palm Products Pvt. Ltd. Vs T.V.L.
							</em>
							<em>
								 Narsimha Rao &amp; Anr.&nbsp;(2021) 115 NCLAT
							</em>
							 to submit that if an assignment deed is not registered, it cannot fall within the ambit of claim and consequently, will not fall within the ambit of debt under the Code.
							<strong>
								 On a perusal
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 of given citation, the issue is with regard to admission of claim by the Resolution
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Professional not for admission of an application filed under Sec 7 of the Code,
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 2016.(p11)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							As regards to the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s contention that the Assignment Agreement dated 18.01.2021 lacks legal enforceability due to non-registration under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908, whereas on the other hand the Applicant contended that the Applicant being an Asset Reconstruction Company is exempted from Stamp Duty Payment under Section 5(1A) of SARFAESI Act in conjunction with Section 8F of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, we find that Section 5 (1) of the SARFAESI Act, 2002 empowers a Bank to assign loan to an Asset Reconstruction Company and clause 1A of Section 5 of the SARFAESI Act, 2002 particularly elucidates that documents executed by any bank or financial institution under sub-p (1) in favour of the asset reconstruction company acquiring financial assets for the purposes of asset reconstruction or securitization shall be exempted from stamp duty. Therefore, considering the exemption provided under Section 5(1A) of SARFAESI Act, 2002 we are not inclined to accept the contention that the said assignment agreement being unregistered is not legally enforceable.
							<strong>
								 (p12)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Proceedings under Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 are summary
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 proceedings and it is beyond the ambit of this Adjudicating authority to delve
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 into the details regarding the requirement or exemption of registration of the
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Assignment Agreement dated 18.01.2021.
							</strong>
							 Therefore,
							<strong>
								 the assignment
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 agreement cannot be challenged in the petition under Section 7 of the Code,
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 2016.(p13)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							<strong>
								 Further, the assignment does not affect the liability and obligations of the
							</strong>
							<strong>
								 Corporate Debtor to discharge the debt.
							</strong>
							 When this is so, the Applicant herein i.e., CFM Asset Reconstruction Private Limited would step into the shoes of SBI (original lender) with the Assignment Deed dated 18.01.2021 executed in its favour. The contentions raised by the Corporate Debtor is accordingly repelled.
							<strong>
								 (p14)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							From the perusal of aforesaid facts, it is clear that the applicant is a Financial&nbsp;Creditors and the debt owed by the Corporate Debtor is a Financial Debt, and&nbsp;there has been a default, as stipulated in Sections 3(12), 5(7) and Section 5(8)&nbsp;of the IBC.
							<strong>
								 (p15)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 B. The liability of
						</strong>
						<strong>
							 the Corporate Debtor cannot be extinguished simply because CIRP has been initiated against the Principal Borrower
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As regard to the Corporate Debtor&rsquo;s contention that applicant has made a mala fide attempt to file duplicate claims with respect to the same debt on same facts and for the same default, it is pertinent to note that under p 128 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 the liability of the surety is coextensive with that of the principal debtor, unless otherwise provided under the contract. The Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in the matter of
							<em>
								 Laxmi Pat Surana Vs. Union Bank of India &amp; Anr.
							</em>
							<em>
								 (2021) 53 SC
							</em>
							 wherein it was held that the liability of the Corporate Guarantor is coextensive with that of the Principal Borrower and that acknowledgment given by the Principal Borrower also binds the Corporate Guarantor.
							<strong>
								 (p16)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Corporate Debtor failed to bring on record any document to prove that there exists any such provision in the Guarantee Agreement which provides for discharge of the surety before the entire dues guaranteed under the Guarantee Agreement are repaid. Therefore, we are of considered view that the liability of the Corporate Debtor cannot be extinguished simply because CIRP has been initiated against the Principal Borrower. Therefore, this contention of the Corporate Debtor is overruled.
							<strong>
								 (p17)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 C. Limitation issue
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							From the facts which are borne on record, as narrated above, we are of the considered view that the financial debt is not barred by limitation and the submissions of the Corporate Debtor as to the present application being barred by limitation is not sustainable. Hence, in all respects the debt as claimed by the Financial Creditor is well within the period of limitation. It is not denied that the Corporate Debtor has committed &lsquo;default&rsquo; in repayment of the said &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo;.
							<strong>
								 (p19)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 D. Conclusion
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the light of the above facts and circumstances, and in terms of Section 7(5) (a) of the Code, the instant Company Petition IB (IBC)/115(PB) 2022 filed by the CFM Asset Reconstruction Private Limited, the Financial Creditor, under p 7 of the Code read with Rule 4(1) of the Insolvency &amp; Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 for initiating CIRP against M.G. Finvest Pvt. Ltd., the Corporate Debtor, stands
							<strong>
								 admitted.(p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
