	<h1>
		The status of Financial Creditor cannot be accorded to a person who, in the garb of a lender comes in a Real Estate Project as a Speculative Investor and for mere recovery of monies files exorbitant claims &ndash; Mr. Rohit Prasad Vs. M/s S and N Lifestyle Infraventures Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-V
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that while determining both Issue ii and Issue iii together, on bare perusal of Section 5 (7) of Insolvency and Bankruptcy code "financial creditor" means any person to whom a financial debt is owed and includes a person to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred; Section 5 (8) &ldquo;financial debt&rdquo; means a debt along with interest, if any, which is disbursed against the consideration for the time value of money. The status of &ldquo;Financial Creditor&rdquo; cannot be accorded to a person who, in the garb of a lender comes in the project as a speculative investor and for mere recovery of monies files exorbitant claims. Therefore, the benefit of p 5(8)(f) of IBC will not enure in his favour and the amount claimed in Part IV of the application doesn&rsquo;t amount to become Financial Debt as per code. Therefore, the present application filed under p 7 of the IB Code 2016 against the corporate debtor is not maintainable.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 130 NCLTCase Name:Mr. Rohit Prasad Vs. M/s S and N Lifestyle Infraventures Pvt. Ltd.Financial Creditor:Mr. Rohit PrasadCorporate Debtor:M/s S and N Lifestyle Infraventures Pvt. Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:Company Petition No. (IB)- 1026/PB/2020Judgment Date:28-Mar-23Court/Bench:NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-VMember (Judicial):Shri P.S.N. 					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The instant application is filed by Mr. Rohit Prasad (Applicant) son of Gopal Krishna Prasad under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 with a prayer to initiate Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process in respect of M/s. S and N Lifestyle Infraventures Pvt. Ltd (respondent Company), referred to as the corporate debtor.
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue i : Whether the parties have entered into contingent or forward sale agreement?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						The applicant and respondent entered in the general agreement dated 18.10.2014 which is an admitted document. According to applicant, a sum of Rs 99,99,999 was paid to Respondent via cheque dated 28.05.2014 (approximately 5 months prior to memorandum of understanding) on condition that Respondent will give 5% equity in the Housing Project of respondent in Dehradun. After four years (lock in period) from signing the agreement Respondent will capitalize or purchase the equity holding in project from applicant and will repay at least Rs 2,00,00,000 and total sum of Rs. 3,50,00,000 comprising of repayment and 5% profit in the Project as per equity holding on pro-rata basis. In case the respondent fails to repay, a grace period of 3 months will be given to Respondent by mutual agreement between the parties&rsquo; subject to payment of interest @1.5% on remaining amount payable by the end of 4 years. After the grace period if any dues are remaining or&nbsp;respondent fails to capitalize Applicant&rsquo;s equity, then respondent&nbsp;will be bound to transfer land equivalent to the total payable amount as per market rate to the applicant, such land being fully paid-up and freely transferable.
						<strong>
							 (p8)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						As clearly stated in the Clause 1 of said agreement &ndash; the subject matter of the agreement was purchase of 5% equity share by the applicant in the housing project of Respondent at The Highlands, Aures valley, Dehradun. The clause 5 of the agreement states that the said amount has been paid by the Applicant (Second Party) to the Respondent (First Party) by cheque no: 088992 dated 28-05- 2014 drawn on ICICI Bank favouring M/s. S and N Lifestyle Infraventures Pvt. Ltd. towards 100% payment is respect of 5 percent equity share in the said Project. That, Respondent represents and the Applicant agrees that the estimated period required for execution, completion and handing over of Project is 4 years.
						<strong>
							 (p9)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Therefore, we are not convinced by Applicant&rsquo;s contention that Applicant advanced a loan to the Corporate Debtor to enable construction for commercial sale of their housing project at The Highland, Aures Valley, Village Challang, Rajpur Road, Dehradun for a consideration of Rs 99,99,999/-. We have examined the terms and conditions of the agreement to understand the intention of parties. On reading the agreement in entirety it was manifestly clear that the Agreement is a sale agreement with settled base return and profits amounting to contingencies with a maximum ceiling. It was an investment done by the applicant in the housing project where he was 5% equity holder in the project and Therefore had a share in the profit to the tunes of 5%. It can be said that the clauses of the Agreement clearly demonstrating the terms, should be read in accordance with Doctrine of Strict Interpretation.
						<strong>
							 (p10)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Issue ii : Whether the amount claimed by the applicant as per Part IV&nbsp;of Application amounting to Rs. 4,76,00,000 (Principle Amount claimed of Rs 3,50,00,000 plus interest Rs 1,26,00,000) is a &lsquo;financial debt&rsquo; under p 5 (8) (f) of I &amp; B code? and Whether the Applicant falls in the category of Financial Creditor as per Section 5 (7) of I &amp; B code?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						While determining both Issue ii and Issue iii together, on bare perusal of Section 5 (7) of Insolvency and Bankruptcy code &ldquo;financial creditor&rdquo; means any person to whom a financial debt is owed and includes a person to whom such debt has been legally assigned or transferred; Section 5 (8) &ldquo;financial debt&rdquo; means a debt along with interest, if any, which is disbursed against the consideration for the time value of money and includes&ndash;
						<br />
						 (a) money borrowed against the payment of interest;
						<br />
						 (f) any amount raised under any other transaction, including any forward sale or purchase agreement, having the commercial effect of a borrowing;
					</p>
					<p>
						Explanation. -For the purposes of this sub-clause, &ndash; (i) any amount raised from an allottee under a real estate project shall be deemed to be an amount having the commercial effect of a borrowing; and (ii) the expressions, &ldquo;allottee&rdquo; and &ldquo;real estate project&rdquo; shall have the meanings respectively assigned to them in clauses (d) and (zn) of p 2 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (16 of 2016).
					</p>
					<p>
						We are of the view that as per the debt claimed by Applicant i.e against the investment of Rs 1 crore, claiming Rs 3.5 Crore as principle and Rs 1.26 Crore interest is a Lucrative Agreement Situation. Moreover, in event of Non-payment by Respondent, Applicant has secured his interests as per clause 12 of agreement, by way of transfer of land by respondent to applicant for amount payable as per the market value. The Adjudicating Authority referred the following judgments:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Ankit Goyal vs. Sunita Agarwal&nbsp;(2021) 387 NCLAT
						</li>
						<li>
							Anuj Jain Interim Resolution&nbsp;Professional for Jaypee Infratech Ltd. vs Axis Bank Ltd. &amp; Ors.&nbsp;[2020] 06 SC
						</li>
						<li>
							Sudha Sharma vs Mansi Brar and Anr.&nbsp;(2020) 338 NCLAT.&nbsp;Also the subsequent order of Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 Mansi Brar Fernandes versus Sudha Sharma and Anr.&nbsp;(2020) 74 SC
							</em>
							 which affirms the order of appellate Tribunal in the matter of Nidhi Rekhan vs M/s Samyak Projects Private&nbsp;Limited&nbsp;(2022) 109 NCLAT.
							<sup>
								 1
							</sup>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						We are of the view that the status of &ldquo;Financial Creditor&rdquo; cannot be accorded to a person who, in the garb of a lender comes in the project as a speculative investor and for mere recovery of monies files exorbitant claims. Therefore, the benefit of p 5(8)(f) of IBC will not enure in his favour and the amount claimed in Part IV of the application doesn&rsquo;t amount to become Financial Debt as per code.
						<strong>
							 (p11)
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						Therefore, the present application filed under p 7 of the IB Code 2016 against the corporate debtor is not maintainable.&nbsp;In the light of the above this Tribunal concludes that, this&nbsp;application cannot be admitted and is hereby dismissed.
						<strong>
							 (p12-13)
						</strong>
					</p>
