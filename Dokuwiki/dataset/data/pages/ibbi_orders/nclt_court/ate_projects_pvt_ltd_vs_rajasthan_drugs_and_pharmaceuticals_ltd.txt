		<h1>
			Whether an application to initiate CIRP is maintainable against the Corporate Debtor which is a Government Company &amp; Whether existence of the Arbitration clause in the agreements executed between the parties bars on initiation of CIRP &ndash; M/s. ATE Projects Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s. Rajasthan Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Jaipur Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			November 12, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			On question whether an application to initiate CIRP is maintainable against the Corporate Debtor which is a Government Company, AA held that from the Judgment Hindustan Construction Company [2019]  21 SC of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, it is observed that a &ldquo;Government Company&rdquo; as defined under Section 2(45) of the Companies Act, 2013 is covered under the definition of a &ldquo;Company&rdquo; as defined under Section 2(20) of Companies Act 2013. Since the Corporate Debtor, has not been performing any Governmental Functions or the Functions of State, the Corporate Debtor cannot be immune from the IBC proceedings merely on the basis of being a Government Company. Therefore, we find that the present Application is maintainable and worth considering on merits. Further it held that that the objection taken by the Corporate Debtor that the present Application is not maintainable in view of the Arbitration clause in the Agreements between the Parties and Section 5 of Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 bars this Adjudicating Authority from hearing this Application, does not merit consideration since Section 238 of IBC, 2016 is having overriding effect over the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.
		</p>
	</div>

							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Facts of the case
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Applicant/Operational Creditor has filed the present Petition&nbsp;under Section 9 of the&nbsp;IBC, 2016&nbsp;with a prayer to initiate the&nbsp;CIRP against M/s. Rajasthan&nbsp;Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Ltd. (Respondent/&nbsp;Corporate Debtor).&nbsp;The Corporate Debtor is a&nbsp;Central Public Sector Enterprise having its Manufacturing Unit &amp;&nbsp;Registered office in VKI Area, Jaipur (Rajasthan). It has been added&nbsp;that the Corporate Debtor is a Government of India enterprise under&nbsp;the administrative control of the Ministry of Chemicals &amp; Fertilizers,&nbsp;Department of Pharmaceuticals, Government of India.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								1. Whether&nbsp;an application to initiate CIRP is maintainable against the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor which is a Government Company.
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							It held that from the Judgment&nbsp;
							<em>
								<strong>
									Hindustan Construction Company Limited and Anr. vs. Union of India&nbsp;[2019]  21 SC
								</strong>
							</em>
							&nbsp;of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court, it is&nbsp;observed that a &ldquo;Government Company&rdquo; as defined under Section&nbsp;2(45) of the Companies Act, 2013 is covered under the definition of a&nbsp;&ldquo;Company&rdquo; as defined under Section 2(20) of Companies Act 2013.&nbsp;Since the Corporate Debtor, has not been performing any&nbsp;Governmental Functions or the Functions of State, the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor cannot be immune from the IBC proceedings merely on the&nbsp;basis of being a Government Company. Therefore, we find that the&nbsp;present Application is maintainable and worth considering on merits.
							<strong>
								(p28-29)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<strong>
								2. Existence of the&nbsp;Arbitration clause in the Agreements executed between the parties
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							It is stated by the Corporate Debtor that the Present Application&nbsp;filed under Section 9 is not maintainable in view of the existence of the&nbsp;Arbitration clause in all the Agreements executed between the parties.&nbsp;That the Corporate Debtor has further placed emphasis on&nbsp;Section 5 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 in order to&nbsp;support its submission that the present petition is not maintainable.
						</p>
						<p>
							It held that that the objection taken by the Corporate Debtor that the&nbsp;present Application is not maintainable in view of the Arbitration&nbsp;clause in the Agreements between the Parties and Section 5 of Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 bars this Adjudicating Authority&nbsp;from hearing this Application, does not merit consideration since&nbsp;Section 238 of IBC, 2016 is having overriding effect over the&nbsp;Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.
							<strong>
								(p30)
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							It further held that the Operational Creditor has been able to establish the&nbsp;default on the part of Corporate Debtor in payment of the operational&nbsp;debt. Therefore, the petition is admitted in terms of Section 9(5)&nbsp;of the IBC, 2016. Accordingly, the CIRP is initiated and&nbsp;moratorium is declared in terms of Section 14 of the IBC, 2016.
							<strong>
								(p35)
							</strong>
						</p>
