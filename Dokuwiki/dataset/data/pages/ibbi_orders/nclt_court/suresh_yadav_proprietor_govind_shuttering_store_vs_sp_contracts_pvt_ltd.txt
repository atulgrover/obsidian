		Whether settlement agreement for payment of outstanding amount comes under the ambit of Operational Debt? &ndash; Suresh Yadav, Proprietor, Govind Shuttering Store Vs. S.P Contracts Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT New Delhi Bench Court-V
	</h1>
	<div>
		April 7, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		The Adjudicating Authority held that as to the second issue, it is pertinent to mention that Mr. Satish Gupta is the Director of the Corporate Debtor company and the Settlement has been made by Mr. Satish Gupta while discharging his duties towards the corporate debtor and in the course of the business between the parties. Therefore, it is observed that the alleged unpaid share of 34.67% failed to be fulfilled. Hence it is to be considered as an unpaid amount as per the settlement agreement. It is relevant to mention that the Hon&rsquo;ble National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Allahabad Bench, vide its decision in Delhi Control Device (P) Ltd. vs. Fedders Electric and Engineering Ltd. (2019) 451 NCLT has made clear its stand regarding the claiming of debt due on account of settlement agreement between the parties.

					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether the present application is within the Limitation?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Petition has been filed by the Operational Creditor before this Tribunal on 05.07.2019. Further, the last alleged invoices, were raised by the Operational Creditor only in the year 2015. Therefore, more than 3 years have elapsed from the dates of the alleged invoices.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is pertinent to note that for the extension of limitation u/s 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, where there has to be a clear acknowledgement of debt within a period of 3 years from the date of default i.e.31.8.2015 and further that the documents provided by the Applicant in support of his application failed to show any clear acknowledgment of the operational debt that is due.
						</li>
						<li>
							We further find that no unequivocal or clear cut acknowledgement of the debt was found in various payment invoice and e-mails produced by the Applicant. Further, the Applicant had not been able to establish an extension of limitation as required under p 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963 on the basis of a valid acknowledgement provided by the corporate debtor to the operational debt, which was in default from 2015. Therefore, it is observed that the p 9 application is barred by limitation. In
							<em>
								 B.K. Educational Services Private Limited v. Parag Gupta and Associates
							</em>
							 .
							<strong>
								 (p7-9)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<strong>
							 Whether settlement agreement for payment of outstanding amount comes under the ambit of &lsquo;Operational Debt&rsquo;?
						</strong>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As to the second issue, it is pertinent to mention that Mr. Satish Gupta is the Director of the Corporate Debtor company and the Settlement has been made by Mr. Satish Gupta while discharging his duties towards the corporate debtor and in the course of the business between the parties. Therefore, it is observed that the alleged unpaid share of 34.67% failed to be fulfilled. Hence it is to be considered as an unpaid amount as per the settlement agreement.
						</li>
						<li>
							It is relevant to mention that the Hon&rsquo;ble National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Allahabad Bench, vide its decision in
							<em>
								 Delhi Control Device (P) Ltd. vs. Fedders Electric and Engineering Ltd.&nbsp;(2019) 451 NCLT
							</em>
							 has made clear its stand regarding the claiming of debt due on account of settlement agreement between the parties. The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT has observed that:
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							 &ldquo;Unpaid instalments as per the settlement agreement cannot be treated as Operational debt within the meaning of its definition as prescribed to it under p 5 (21) of the code and any failure(s) or breach(es) of settlement agreement cannot be a ground to trigger CIRP against Corporate debtor under the provisions of the code and remedy may lie elsewhere but, not before the Adjudicating Authority&rdquo;.
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						In view of the above facts and circumstances, we are of the&nbsp;considered issue that the present petition fails to fulfil the criteria laid down under the provisions of this code. Hence, this petition is sought to be dismissed on the grounds stated above.
						<strong>
							 (p10-12)
						</strong>
