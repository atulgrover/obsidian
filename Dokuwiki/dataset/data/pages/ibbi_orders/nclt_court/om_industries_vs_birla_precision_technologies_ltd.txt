	<h1>
		In absence of the due date in invoice, the due date of each invoice can be treated as immediate and in case of due date of invoices are accessed separately, the invoices which fall within the period of 10A of IBC will be excluded for calculation of the threshold limit u/s 4 of IBC &ndash; OM Industries Vs. Birla Precision Technologies Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		July 31, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		In this case, NCLT Mumbai Bench held that: (i) In absence of the due date in invoice, the due date of each invoice can be treated as immediate. (ii) It is well settled that for the purposes of limitation as well as Section 10A of the Code due date or date of default of each invoices has to be taken into account. (iii) Referred M/s K12 Techno Services Private Limited (2023) 39 SC and held that in a case where several invoices are issued and some of the invoices are time barred, then the court must consider the remaining invoices which are within the limitation and whether they cross the minimum threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore or not. (iv) Eligibility of each invoice needs to be considered separately in order to determine the threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore. (v) In the instant case, if the due date of all the invoices are accessed separately and the invoices which fall within the period of 10A are excluded, the claimed amount of the remaining invoices do not meet the threshold limit requirement. (vi) CIRP application filed u/s 9 of IBC has been dismissed.
	</p>
</div>

						<strong>
							 I. Case Reference
						</strong>
					</p>
					Case Citation:(2023) 397 NCLTCase Name:OM Industries Vs. Birla Precision Technologies Ltd.Operational Creditor:OM IndustriesCorporate Debtor:Birla Precision Technologies Ltd.Application/Appeal No.:C.P. 1260/IB/MB/2021 &amp; IA. 					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							This Company Petition has been filed by OM Industries (Operational Creditor) seeking to initiate CIRP against Birla Precision Technologies Ltd. (Corporate Debtor) u/s 9 of IBC alleging that the Corporate Debtor has committed a default to an extent of Rs. 1,55,57,967.00/-.&nbsp;
						</li>
						<li>
							The Applicant issued 62 invoices during the period starting from 07.11.2020 to 30.07.2021.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The Adjudicating Authority observed that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							As per the invoices, there is no indication regarding the time span within which the amount of the invoice is to be paid. Therefore, the due date of each invoice can be treated as immediate. It is well settled that for the purposes of limitation as well as Section 10A of the Code due date or date of default of each invoices has to be taken into account.
						</li>
						<li>
							The due date for 31 invoices issued between 07.11.2020 and 21.03.2021, the aggregate of which amounts to Rs. 74,76,147.00/-, falls within the Section 10A period. Therefore, this Bench is of the considered view that out of 62 invoices, 31 invoices are covered under Section 10A period regarding which the claim cannot be ever filed by the Operational Creditor.&nbsp;As regards, the remaining 31 invoices, which are not covered under&nbsp;Section 10A period, the total outstanding amount of such invoices comes to Rs. 83,53,220.00/-, which falls below the minimum threshold of Rs. 1 Crore. The Corporate Debtor is stated to have paid a sum of Rs. 2,71,400/- and Rs. 2,49,240/- on 30.07.2021 and 13.08.2021 respectively, which should further be deducted from the total outstanding amount of Rs. 83,53,220.00/-.
						</li>
						<li>
							In this regard, reference can be made to
							<em>
								 M/s Next Education India Private Limited vs M/s K12 Techno Services Private Limited&nbsp;(2023) 39 SC
							</em>
							 whereby the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court has held that in a case where several invoices are issued and some of the invoices are time barred, then the court must consider the remaining invoices which are within the limitation and whether they cross the minimum threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore or not.
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, it is evident that eligibility of each invoice needs to be considered separately in order to determine the threshold limit of Rs. 1 Crore. In the instant case, if the due date of all the invoices are accessed separately and the invoices which fall within the period of 10A are excluded, the claimed amount of the remaining invoices do not meet the threshold limit requirement.
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						And held that:
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							In the light of the law laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court and considering the facts and circumstances of the present case, this Bench is of the view that the present petition under Section 9 of the Code is liable to be dismissed as the claimed amount falls below the threshold amount of Rs. 1 Crore.
						</li>
						<li>
							Accordingly, CP no. 1260 of 2021 is
							<strong>
								 dismissed
							</strong>
							 and disposed of and IA. No. 847 of 2022 filed by the Operational Creditor shall be dismissed to have been rendered infructuous. This order, however, will not preclude the Operational Creditor from proceeding against the Corporate for the recovery of the outstanding due at any other forum as per law as the debt is not extinguished with the passing of this order.
							<strong>
								 (p17-19)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
