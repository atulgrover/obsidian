					<p>
						<strong>
							 II. Brief about the decision
						</strong>
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						The &lsquo;Corporate Debtor&rsquo; SBM Paper Mills had filed an Application under p 10 of the Insolvency Code which was admitted vide an order dated 17/10/ 2017 by declaring commencement of insolvency proceedings (CIRP). Moratorium was declared and IRP was appointed. As per the said order, the admitted factual position was that the Corporate Debtor had filed the said application in respect of a default in payment of loan of one &lsquo;Financial Creditor&rsquo; that is Allahabad Bank. This bank is the sole member of &lsquo;Committee of Creditors&rsquo; (CoC).
					</p>
					<p>
						The Resolution Professional convened few meetings of Committee of Creditors and finally an improved Resolution Plan was approved by the CoC. Facts have revealed that an Earnest Money of ₹50 lakhs was deposited by the Resolution Applicant. Further on demand also furnished a Bank Guarantee of ₹95 lakhs to show its bona fides.
					</p>
					<p>
						It is interesting to note that on one hand the Resolution Professional has submitted the said Resolution Plan for approval by the Adjudicating Authority, side-by-side, the Applicant of this Miscellaneous Application, Mr. Satyanarayan Malu, was carrying a parallel negotiation with Allahabad bank. Mr. Malu, had made a OTS proposal to Allahabad bank to settle the account of the corporate debtor SBM paper Mills. One of the main contention of this Applicant before the COC was that the offer he had made was a better proposal than the Resolution Plan under consideration.
					</p>
					<p>
						In this application an unexpected development is reported. The sole Resolution Applicant had expressed its desire to withdraw the Resolution Plan. As a consequence, there was no person as a Resolution Applicant for consideration before the Committee of Creditors. In that position when there was no Resolution Plan the only remedy left with the Committee of Creditors is to go for &ldquo;Liquidation&rdquo;. On the other hand, if the applicant is allowed to withdraw U/s 12A IBC the present petition being filed by him as an Authorised person of the Corporate Debtor, all the stakeholders shall get benefit. The applicant had given an undertaking that The Operational Creditors and Unsecured Creditors shall be benefited since 100% dues shall also be paid in the ordinary course of business.
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Questions before the NCLT
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						(i) Whether an Applicant who has filed an Application/Petition u/s 10 of the IBC is entitled to withdraw its own petition u/s 12A of IBC 2016?
					</p>
					<p>
						(ii) Whether a Resolution Applicant who has submitted a Resolution Plan which was approved with majority vote by CoC can be allowed to withdraw the said Resolution Plan which is under consideration for approval before the NCLT?
					</p>
					<p>
						(iii) Whether ex-director of the Corporate Debtor, which is under Insolvency can offer One Time Settlement (OTS) with the Financial Creditor/Creditors if qualified u/s 29A of the Insolvency Code 2016?
					</p>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the NCLT
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<p>
						Under the fitness of circumstances, I have taken a conscious decision that a workable solution is to first decide the question framed above that whether an Applicant who has filed a petition u/s 10 is entitled to withdraw its own petition&nbsp;u/s 12A of the Code, especially when the said applicant&nbsp;has furnished the impugned Petition(CP 1362/2018) and&nbsp;now offering a onetime settlement as a Director in the&nbsp;suspended management of the Debtor Company. Section&nbsp;12A is inserted w.e.f. 06.06.2018 which prescribes that the&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority may allow the withdrawal of application&nbsp;admitted either u/s 7 or u/s 9 or u/s 10, on an application made&nbsp;by the Applicant with the approval of 90% voting share of the&nbsp;CoC. The first reaction during the course of hearing of this&nbsp;Bench was that how it is justifiable on the part of an applicant&nbsp;who has moved a Petition u/s 10 to declare itself insolvent (as&nbsp;happened in this case) at one point of time and thereafter at a&nbsp;later stage suo-moto seeking permission for withdrawal of the&nbsp;said Petition? It has also been questioned that in this manner&nbsp;the procedure laid down in the provisions of the IBC may be&nbsp;wrongly utilized? How a person can be allowed to play with the&nbsp;precious time of a Court by moving a Petition with the prayer to&nbsp;commence CIRP and at the fag end of the process seeking&nbsp;permission of withdrawal of the said Petition? But, the answer&nbsp;is simple and single that the Code has now subscribed such procedure through which withdrawal is possible in respect of a&nbsp;petition filed u/s 10 of the Code. The condition subscribed is&nbsp;that &ldquo;on an Application made by the Applicant&rdquo; withdrawal can&nbsp;be allowed, if approved by 90% voting share of the CoC. On&nbsp;this point, the Applicant has clarified that as a Director of the&nbsp;Debtor Company the said Application u/s 10 was signed by him&nbsp;and now he is the signatory of this withdrawal application, hence&nbsp;qualified.
					</p>
					<p>
						In this regard, provisions of p 10 are unambiguous &ndash;&nbsp;where a corporate debtor has committed a default, a corporate&nbsp;applicant thereof may file an application for initiating CIRP with&nbsp;the AA. The term &ldquo;corporate applicant&rdquo; is defined u/s 5 of&nbsp;&lsquo;Definitions&rsquo; of IBC, means a member of the corporate debtor&nbsp;company who is authorized to make an application for CIRP&nbsp;under the constitutional document of the Corporate Debtor or a&nbsp;person who has the control and supervision over the financial&nbsp;affairs of the Corporate Debtor. In the present case an admitted&nbsp;position is that Mr. Satyanarayan Malu is the person who has&nbsp;control and supervision over the financial affairs of the Corporate&nbsp;Debtor. It has also been clarified that he is one of the directors&nbsp;in the Board of the Corporate Debtor as well as duly authorized&nbsp;to act on behalf of the company. At the time when the Petition&nbsp;u/s 10 was filed, he was the signatory and now also signed this&nbsp;withdrawal application.&nbsp;
					</p>
					<p>
						As far as the applicability of Regulation 30A of IBBI&nbsp;(Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Person)&nbsp;Regulations 2016 is concerned, it is inserted as a (3&nbsp;rd&nbsp;amendment) Regulations 2018 w.e.f. 03.07.2018. It is&nbsp;clarified that this Regulation shall apply to CIRP commencing on&nbsp;or after 03.07.2018. A procedure is laid down in this Regulation&nbsp;for withdrawal of application after the insertion of p 12A in&nbsp;the statute. A question was that as per the procedure prescribed&nbsp;under Regulation 30A an application for withdrawal is to be&nbsp;submitted to IRP or RP in Form No. FA but before issue of&nbsp;invitation for expression of interest under Regulation 36A. So,&nbsp;a query has been raised that in a situation when EoI had already&nbsp;been invited then how this application for withdrawal can be&nbsp;entertained? It is vehemently pleaded that when the CIRP has&nbsp;commenced as per the order of this Bench dated 17.10.2017&nbsp;the said Regulation was not in the statute Book. Moreover,&nbsp;Section 12A was introduced w.e.f. 06.06.17 and even at that&nbsp;time Regulation 30A was not in the statute Book being&nbsp;introduced w.e.f. 03.07.2018. It is worth to add one more&nbsp;important feature about the introduction of this Regulation is&nbsp;that the Notification No. IBBI/2018-19/GN/REG031 introduced&nbsp;by The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (Insolvency&nbsp;Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) (Third Amendment)&nbsp;Regulations, 2018, it was made clear that the Regulations shall&nbsp;come into force on their publication in the Official Gazette&nbsp;and shall apply to CIRP commencing on or after the said&nbsp;date i.e. 03.07.2018. Because of the reason that the Statute&nbsp;itself has clarified about the date from which this Regulation&nbsp;should be applicable i.e. on or after 03.07.2018, no uncertainty&nbsp;was left in the statute Book about its enforceability. As a&nbsp;consequence, as far as the applicability of the provisions of the&nbsp;Code is concerned and to be applied on this Application, only&nbsp;p 12A is relevant for judicial consideration. It is also a&nbsp;known Law that the Regulations do not control the main ps&nbsp;of a statute, but it is vice versa. Since the provisions of p 12A has not laid down a condition of pre-EoI advertisement,&nbsp;therefore, the present &lsquo;withdrawal&rsquo; application is&nbsp;maintainable.&nbsp;
					</p>
					<p>
						Whether withdrawal application can be allowed when a&nbsp;Resolution Plan is subjudice for the pronouncement of an order
						<br/>
						 u/s 31(1) i.e. approval of Resolution Plan. The Resolution&nbsp;Professional has submitted a Resolution Plan of M/s.&nbsp;Khandesh Roller Floor Mills Ltd. annexed with&nbsp;Miscellaneous Application No. 827/2018 dated&nbsp;21.08.2018 seeking an order u/s 31(1) of the Code. As&nbsp;per this plan prima-facie the restructuring of liabilities of the&nbsp;Financial Creditor i.e. Allahabad Bank was only to the extent of Rs. 1,250 lakhs as a full and final payment. While it was pending&nbsp;for disposal strangely an Application was moved by the&nbsp;Resolution Applicant (MA No. 1142/2018) seeking&nbsp;permission for withdrawal of the Resolution Plan. To my&nbsp;knowledge, this has happened for the first time in the two and&nbsp;half years (Approx.) that a Resolution Applicant is withdrawing&nbsp;a Resolution Plan which is approved by the CoC with majority&nbsp;vote. In the following paragraph I shall deal with the relevant&nbsp;provisions, if any, available to deal with the situation when a&nbsp;Resolution Applicant is seeking permission for withdrawal of an&nbsp;approved Resolution Plan pending for an order u/s 31(1). But&nbsp;before that, at the moment this situation is to be tackled keeping&nbsp;in mind that if the sole Resolution Plan be allowed to be&nbsp;withdrawn then there shall be no option left but to declare&nbsp;&lsquo;Liquidation&rsquo; of the Corporate Debtor. One of the important&nbsp;provision is unambiguous that in case all attempts have failed to&nbsp;invite Resolution Applicant but no one come forward showing&nbsp;interest in the revival of the Debtor Company, the option&nbsp;available to CoC is to vote for Liquidation. Refer Section 33(1)&nbsp;of the Code prescribing &lsquo;Initiation of Liquidation&rsquo; that where&nbsp;Adjudicating Authority does not receive a Resolution Plan U/s&nbsp;30(6) of the Code or reject a Resolution Plan U/s 31 of the Code,&nbsp;it shall pass an order requiring the Corporate Debtor to be&nbsp;&lsquo;&lsquo;Liquidated&rsquo; by issuing a public announcement that the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor is in &lsquo;Liquidation&rsquo;. Hence, a serious contention&nbsp;before this Bench has been raised that whether a Liquidation is&nbsp;beneficial vis-&agrave;-vis OTS proposal. The contention is that due to&nbsp;the unexpected and unprecedented development on the part of&nbsp;the Resolution Applicant, the CoC as well as the RP have been&nbsp;put in a strange situation that what to do and how to proceed&nbsp;when the CIRP period has also expired. Prima-facie it is&nbsp;conveyed to me that this situation is at par with a situation when&nbsp;there is no Resolution Plan available which automatically leads&nbsp;to &lsquo;Liquidation&rsquo;. The parties present before me, especially the&nbsp;Bank, is not at all ready for Liquidation of the assets of the Debtor Company, naturally so, due to lower Liquidated value of&nbsp;all those assets.
					</p>
					<p>
						Subject to the fine imposed (supra) this withdrawal application&nbsp;is hereby allowed by invoking the jurisdiction prescribed u/s&nbsp;12A of the IBC.
					</p>
