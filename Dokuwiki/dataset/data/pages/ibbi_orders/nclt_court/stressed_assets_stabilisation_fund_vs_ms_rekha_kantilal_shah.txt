	<h1>
		Whether Liquidator is authorized to sell the assets of the Corporate Debtor in blocks? &ndash; Stressed Assets Stabilisation Fund v. Ms. Rekha Kantilal Shah &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		December 21, 2023
	</div>
	<p>
		NCLT Mumbai Bench held that: (i) The Liquidator has been provided with ample powers not only to sell the land in parcels but also not to be bound by the consultation with the stakeholders. (ii) Section 36 of the IB Code 2016 categorically stipulates the scope and ambit of the liquidation estate. Sub-Section (4) of Section 36 is with respect to the assets which shall not be included in the liquidation estate and shall not be used for recovery in the liquidation. (iii) The Liquidator is authorized to sell the immovable and movable property of the Corporate Debtor in liquidation through a public auction or a private contract, either collectively, or in a piecemeal manner. (iv) The proviso to Section 35 (2) of the IBC makes it clear that the opinion of the stakeholders would not be binding on the Liquidator.
	</p>
</div>

	<p>
		<strong>
			 Brief about the decision:
		</strong>
	</p>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Issue
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<p>
		This application is preferred by the Applicant i.e. Stressed Assets Stabilisation Fund to restrain the Liquidator (Ms. Rekha Kantilal Shah/Respondent) from deletion of all assets of the Corporate Debtor from the Liquidation estate and subsequent amendment of the asset memorandum and also to restrain the Liquidator from selling the assets of the Corporate Debtor i.e. land parcels bearing block Nos. 77 and 79 till the disposal of I.A.1305 of 2020.
	</p>
	<p>
		The following issues arise for consideration in the present case:
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			a) Whether the land belonging to third party can be made part of the Liquidation estate?
		</li>
		<li>
			b) Whether the Liquidator is authorized to sell the&nbsp;assets of the Corporate Debtor in blocks?
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<em>
			<strong>
				 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
			</strong>
		</em>
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>
			 Selling of assets
		</strong>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			The Respondent/liquidator is correct to the extent that he has to perform his duties, firstly, of closing the liquidation within the stipulated time period and, secondly, for ensuring that the actions taken are as per the law. The Powers and Duties of the Liquidator have been elaborated under Section 35 of the IBC, 2016.
			<strong>
				 (p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>
				 The Liquidator has been provided with ample powers not only to sell the land in parcels but also not to be bound by the consultation with the stakeholders.(p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>
				 Section 36 of the IB Code 2016 categorically stipulates the scope and ambit of the liquidation estate. Sub-Section (4) of Section 36 is with respect to the assets which shall not be included in the liquidation estate&nbsp;and shall not be used for recovery in the liquidation.(p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In the present case, there has been no attempt on the part of the Applicant to verify the actual position on the ground either by visiting the land or verifying the revenue records. The conduct of the Corporate Debtor right from the beginning seems to be fraudulent and malicious. The loan amount of Rs. 7 crores obtained from the original borrower, i.e. IDBI in the year 1998 was defaulted right from the first instalment itself. The Applicant having been assigned the debt of IDBI in the year 2004 also never bothered to reverify the actual position or the status of the land.
			<strong>
				 (p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			As is evident, Block No. 81 was transferred to the Government of Gujarat on 30.07.2002, Block No. 82 was transferred by the deceased tribal occupant to Mr. Arjun Singh Solanki on 09.05.2015 and Block No. 80 was transferred to Government of Gujarat due to non-compliance of the provisions of Section 43 and 63AA of the Bombay Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act 1948 on 01.01.2023. Therefore, the promoters of the Corporate Debtor are not the owners of the said three Blocks. These three Blocks not being in the ownership of the Corporate Debtor cannot be stated to be part of the liquidation estate.
			<strong>
				 (p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			The&nbsp;blocks i.e. Block No. 81 was transferred to the Government of&nbsp;Gujarat on 30.07.2002, Block No. 82 was transferred by the&nbsp;deceased tribal occupant to Mr. Arjun Singh Solanki on 09.05.2015&nbsp;and block No. 80 was transferred to Government of Gujarat due to&nbsp;non-compliance of the provisions of Section 43 and 63AA of the&nbsp;Bombay Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act 1948 on&nbsp;01.01.2023, are not under the ownership of the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;On all these three Blocks, the third-party rights have been created.
			<strong>
				 (p13)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			Thus, in view of Section 36(4)(a) these Blocks of Land cannot be included as part of the liquidation estate. Therefore, the issue (a) is decided in favour of the liquidator by holding that the land which does not belong to the Corporate Debtor cannot be made part of the liquidation estate.
			<strong>
				 (p19)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			On a conjoint reading of the provisions of the IBC and the&nbsp;Liquidation Regulations, it is evident that the
			<strong>
				 Liquidator is
			</strong>
			<strong>
				 authorized to sell the immovable and movable property of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor in liquidation through a public auction or a
			</strong>
			<strong>
				 private contract, either collectively, or in a piecemeal manner.
			</strong>
			 The&nbsp;underlying object of the statute is to protect and preserve the assets&nbsp;of the Corporate Debtor in liquidation and proceed to sell them at&nbsp;the best possible price. The Liquidator is permitted to consult the&nbsp;stakeholders who are entitled to distribution of the sale proceeds.
			<strong>
				 (p23)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			However, the proviso to Section 35 (2) of the IBC makes it clear that the opinion of the stakeholders would not be binding on the Liquidator. Regulation 8 of the Liquidation Regulations refers to the consultative process with the stakeholders, as specified in Section 35 (2) of the IBC and states that they shall extend all necessary assistance and cooperation to the Liquidator for completing the liquidation process. Regulation 31A has introduced a Stakeholders&rsquo; Consultation Committee that may advise the Liquidator regarding sale of the assets of the Corporate Debtor and such committee must&nbsp;be furnished all relevant information to provide such advice to the&nbsp;liquidator. Though the advice offered is not binding on the&nbsp;Liquidator, he must give reason in writing for acting against&nbsp;such advice.
			<strong>
				 (p23)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<strong>
			 Liquidator is in a hurry to dispose of the assets of the Corporate Debtor
		</strong>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Another contention advanced by the Applicant on the issue is that the Respondent/Liquidator is in a hurry to dispose of the assets of the Corporate Debtor and is not working as per the objective of the IBC.
			<strong>
				 (p20)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			As per the settled proposition of law also, the Liquidator is to work&nbsp;in the best interest so as to maximise the value of assets of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor as held in case of
			<em>
				 Swiss Ribbons Private
			</em>
			<em>
				 Limited and Another v. Union of India and Others
			</em>
			 and further in
			<em>
				 Jaypee Kensington Boulevard Apartments
			</em>
			<em>
				 Welfare Association and Others v. NBCC (India) Limited and Others
			</em>
			 it has been held that the object of the IBC is to ensure Resolution/liquidation in a time bound manner for maximization of value of assets in order to balance the interests of all the stakeholders.
			<strong>
				 (p21)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		<strong>
			 Conclusion
		</strong>
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Firstly, that the Corporate Debtor from the beginning had the intention to defraud the Creditors as after taking loan in 1998 from IDBI, he defaulted from the very first instalment. After assignment of the debt of IDBI to the Applicant, the Applicant did not take any proactive steps either to verify the status/ownership of the land or to secure the assets after true and correct facts were brought to its attention. The connivance on the part of the promoters of Corporate Debtor is further established from the submission of the liquidator wherein it has been informed that the promoter of M/s Jord Engineering Group is the son-in&ndash; law of the promoter of the Corporate Debtor who is constructing a wall to block the access of the Respondent/liquidator on the land mortgaged with the Applicant by the Corporate Debtor. Strangely, the Applicant too instead of taking any steps to either get back the land or to get the status of the land converted from &ldquo;agriculture&rdquo; to &ldquo;non-agriculture&rdquo; is only resorting to the Deed of Indemnity knowing it fully well that when the Corporate Debtor is not the owner of the land, his indemnifying the Applicant with respect to the land belonging to the third party is going to be of no avail.
			<strong>
				 (p24)
			</strong>
		</li>
		<li>
			In the present case, the Respondent/Liquidator had addressed the issues with the Promoters in various meetings and an application for non-cooperation was pending which was disposed of when the liquidation order was passed by this Hon&rsquo;ble Tribunal. From&nbsp;perusal of the report of the Respondent/Liquidator, when she&nbsp;visited the said land, there was no problem, the wall was not&nbsp;constructed. The wall was erected by the son-in-law of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor subsequently. The Promoters were creating road&nbsp;blocks and indirectly intimidating the Respondent/Liquidator&nbsp;rather than working together with the Respondent/Liquidator to&nbsp;maximise value which in turn would have benefitted the promoter&nbsp;by reducing the liability on their personal guarantees. The&nbsp;Applicant now ought to persuade the promoters to cooperate and&nbsp;address the non- compliance issues in respect of these blocks to&nbsp;maximise value. Hence, the contention of the Applicant that the&nbsp;Respondent/Liquidator is not working as per the objective of the&nbsp;IBC does not survive. In view of the above facts, the issue (b) is&nbsp;resolved. The Liquidator is authorized to sell the assets of the&nbsp;Corporate Debtor in Blocks.
			<strong>
				 (p25)
			</strong>
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		Therefore, in view of the above stated facts, specific provisions of law and the law laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court. The Interlocutory Application Number I.A. 883 of 2022 is
		<strong>
			 dismissed.(p26)
		</strong>
	</p>
