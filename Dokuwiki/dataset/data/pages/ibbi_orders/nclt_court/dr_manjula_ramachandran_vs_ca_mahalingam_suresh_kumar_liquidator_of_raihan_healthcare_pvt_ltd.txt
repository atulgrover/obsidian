		<h1>
			Doctors of the Corporate Debtor are working as consultants, they cannot be considered as workmen/ employees of the Corporate Debtor &ndash; Dr. Manjula Ramachandran Vs. C.A Mahalingam Suresh Kumar, Liquidator of Raihan Healthcare Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Kochi Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			October 17, 2021
		</div>
		<p>
			The Appellants are appointed as Consultant Doctors for a fixed remuneration and they are acting as Consultants of the Corporate Debtor. In the appointment letter it is agreed between the parties that the tax shall be deducted from the payment of the doctors. It is also seen that the appellants were not registered as a part of corporate debtor&rsquo;s Employee Provident Fund Scheme and no agreement to show that provident fund can be deducted from their professional fees. There is no employment contract between the appellants and the Corporate Debtor. There is a clear demarcation between the Doctors who are the employees and the doctors who are consultants. The appellants here are doctors of the Corporate Debtor working as consultants. Therefore, they cannot be considered as workmen/ employees of the Corporate Debtor, as rightly decided by the Liquidator. In view of the aforesaid discussions and findings, we see no error in the impugned order passed by the Liquidator.
		</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The appellants filed their claim&nbsp;in Form E even though the Liquidator directed them to file the claim in Form&nbsp;C, stating that they are not Operational Creditors. In this respect the Tribunal&nbsp;consider it necessary to see whether the appellants being consultant doctors of&nbsp;the corporate debtor comes under the purview of workmen?&nbsp;According to the appellants doctors doing private practice along with&nbsp;other employment will not come under the category of workman. But the&nbsp;appellant-doctors are working under the management and doing work as per&nbsp;the directions of the Corporate Debtor M/s. Raihan Health Care Pvt Ltd. Since&nbsp;doctors appointed in M/s. Raihan Health Care Pvt Ltd are not permitted for&nbsp;any private practice or they were engaged in any private practice and also not&nbsp;raised or shared any profit arising out of such patients which the doctors&nbsp;recommend to the hospital and the appellants were full time practicing&nbsp;doctors working under the management, they come under the category of&nbsp;workmen/employees.
						</p>
						<p>
							NCLT held that a person can acquire the status of a workman under Section 2(s) only&nbsp;if he falls within the first part of Section 2(s) of the Industrial Disputes Act,&nbsp;1947. They must be employed to do manual, unskilled, skilled, technical,&nbsp;operational, clerical or supervisory work for hire or reward. If they fail in any&nbsp;of the above categories in the first part of Section 2(s), then it is to be&nbsp;ascertained whether such person stands excluded by the four exceptions&nbsp;carved out in the latter half of Section 2(s). Whether the appellants are&nbsp;workmen or not is a question of fact and that a finding of fact can be arrived&nbsp;at by this bench only on the basis of evidence on record.&nbsp;In order to verify whether the appellants are paid salary and whether&nbsp;the T.D.S was made, vide order dated 02.08.2021 the appellants were directed&nbsp;to produce the acknowledgement of IT returns showing the income and the&nbsp;tax paid by them during their engagement with the Corporate Debtor.&nbsp;However, the appellants have not produced the same. The learned counsel&nbsp;appearing for appellants could not properly answer why these documents&nbsp;could not be produced. Their contention is that even though the tax is&nbsp;deducted under Section 194 (j) of the Income Tax Act,1961 they have to be classified as workmen and that this provision is only to ensure the tax liability&nbsp;of an assessee.&nbsp;
						</p>
						<p>
							In the cases on hand, the Appellants could not prove that they are fulltime employees of the Corporate Debtor and their names entered in the&nbsp;muster rolls of the Corporate Debtor as &ldquo;Employee&rdquo;. A perusal of the&nbsp;appointment letters annexed with the appeals, it is discernible that the&nbsp;Appellants are appointed as Consultant Doctors for a fixed remuneration and&nbsp;they are acting as Consultants of the Corporate Debtor. In the appointment&nbsp;letter it is agreed between the parties that the tax shall be deducted from the&nbsp;payment of the doctors. It is also seen that the appellants were not registered&nbsp;as a part of corporate debtor&rsquo;s Employee Provident Fund Scheme and no&nbsp;agreement to show that provident fund can be deducted from their&nbsp;professional fees. There is no employment contract between the appellants&nbsp;and the Corporate Debtor. There is a clear demarcation between the Doctors&nbsp;who are the employees and the doctors who are consultants. The appellants&nbsp;here are doctors of the Corporate Debtor working as consultants. Therefore,&nbsp;they cannot be considered as workmen/ employees of the Corporate Debtor,&nbsp;as rightly decided by the Liquidator.&nbsp;In view of the aforesaid discussions and findings, we see no error in the&nbsp;impugned order passed by the Liquidator. Hence, we find no merit in the&nbsp;above MA&rsquo;s. All the MA&rsquo;s are dismissed.
						</p>
