						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							Regulation 32(e) of the IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016, after its substitution vide Notification No. IBBI/2018-19/GNB/REG037dated22.10.2018, permits the Liquidator to sell the corporate debtor as a going concern. The earlier regulation 32(c) also permitted the Liquidator to sell the corporate debtor as a going concern. Regulation 32A, inserted vide Notification No. IBBI/2019-20/GN/REG047 dated 25.07.2019, enjoins the Liquidator first to attempt the sale of corporate debtor as a going concern or the business of the corporate debtor as a going concern first, if the Committee of Creditors (CoC) has so recommended or where the liquidator is of such opinion by himself.
						</p>
						<p>
							The concept of &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; has not been defined either in the Code or the Regulations framed thereunder. Therefore, we have to seek assistance of external aids to arrive at an interpretation of the term &lsquo;going concern.&rsquo; Our first pitstop in this endeavour is the Insolvency Law Committee Report dated26.03.2018. Under the Chapter,&nbsp;
							<strong>
								<em>
									Recommendations Proposing Amendments to the Code and Relevant Subordinate Legislation
								</em>
							</strong>
							, Page 36 @page 37, para 8.1, the concept of &lsquo;going concern&rsquo; has been referred to. While this Report also does not define the phrase, it has been stated that the phrase &ldquo;as a going concern&rdquo; imply that the corporate debtor would be functional as it would have ben prior to initiation of CIRP, other than the restrictions put by the Code.
						</p>
						<p>
							In insolvency and bankruptcy law, a going concern should be distinguished as a business that can be continued without the necessity of being liquidated. In accounting, it is defined in Accounting Standards (AS)-1 as follows: &ndash;
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								&ldquo;The enterprise is normally viewed as a Going Concern, that is, as continuing in operation for the foreseeable future. It is assumed that the enterprise has neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or of curtailing materially the scale of the operations.&rdquo;
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							Viewed from this perspective, sale as a going concern implies that the corporate debtor will continue to exist, albeit under a new management. There is a seamless transition from the Liquidator to the purchaser. It envisages a whole some transfer of immovable property, goods, employees, goodwill, etc. Further, there should not be a change of business after transfer.
						</p>
						<p>
							We are of the view that in case of sale of the corporate debtor as a going concern by the Liquidator, it is necessary to pave the way for the smooth transition by grant of appropriate concessions and waivers prior to the completion of the sale by the Liquidator by issue of Certificate of Sale.
							<strong>
								(p8-12)
							</strong>
						</p>
