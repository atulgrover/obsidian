		<h1>
			Whether the sale of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern under the IBC and the Regulations includes both assets and liabilities or assets alone without any liabilities? &ndash; Gaurav Agarwal Vs. CA Devang P Sampat, Liquidator &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 4, 2022
		</div>
		<p>
			The present Applicant being an auction purchaser who purchased the Corporate Debtor Company as a going concern is not bound by any of its liabilities. The issue whether the sale of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern includes assets and liabilities or assets alone is no longer res-integra. It referred judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in M/s Visisth Services Ltd. (2022)  33 NCLAT, in the similar circumstances of the present case on hand. Similarly, the coordinate bench of NCLAT, Chennai Bench in M.S. Viswanathan (2022)  33 NCLAT by relying on the above judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in M/s Visisth Services Limited clearly held that the Sale of Corporate Debtor as a Going Concern &lsquo;as is where is&rsquo; basis under Regulation 32A and the Code means sale of assets as well as liabilities and not assets sans liabilities. However, the learned counsel appearing for the Petitioner relied on the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports (2022)  192 NCLAT which is later in point of time and argued that as per the judgment in M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports, the sale does not include liabilities and the above decision being the later decision will prevail over the earlier decision. Therefore, the only issue is which judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT is a ratio.
		</p>
	</div>
</p>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Ground of the Application
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The I.A. 1253/2021 is filed by one Mr. Gaurav Agarwal who is the successful auction purchaser in respect of auction dated 03.03.2021 conducted by the Liquidator claiming several among the following relief claimed in terms of prayer clause &lsquo;ii&rsquo; of the Application that the Applicant shall not be responsible for any other claims/ liabilities/ obligations etc. payable by the Corporate Debtor as on this date to the Creditors or any other stakeholders including Government dues. All the liabilities of the Corporate Debtor as on the date stand extinguished, as far as the Applicant is concerned.
						</p>
						<p>
							<em>
								<strong>
									Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
								</strong>
							</em>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority held that:
						</p>
						<ul>
							<li>
								The present Applicant being an auction purchaser who purchased the Corporate Debtor Company as a going concern is not bound by any of its liabilities. The issue whether the sale of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern includes assets and liabilities or assets alone is no longer&nbsp;
								<em>
									res-integra
								</em>
								. It referred judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT vide its order dated 11.01.2022 in&nbsp;
								<em>
									M/s Visisth Services Limited VS. S.V. Ramani&nbsp;(2022)  33 NCLAT,
								</em>
								&nbsp;in the similar circumstances of the present case on hand. Similarly, the coordinate bench of NCLAT, Chennai Bench in its order dated 15.02.2022 in I.A./1215/CHE/2021 in CP/699/IB/2017 in&nbsp;
								<em>
									M.S. Viswanathan Vs. Pixtronic Global Technologies Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;(2022)  33 NCLAT
								</em>
								&nbsp;by relying on the above judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in&nbsp;
								<em>
									M/s Visisth Services Limited VS. S.V. Ramani&nbsp;(2022)  33 NCLAT&nbsp;
								</em>
								clearly held that the Sale of Corporate Debtor as a Going Concern &lsquo;as is where is&rsquo; basis under Regulation 32A of the IBBI (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016 and the Code means sale of assets as well as liabilities and not assets sans liabilities.
								<strong>
									(p6-7)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								However, the learned counsel appearing for the Petitioner relied on the judgment of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in&nbsp;
								<em>
									M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s KTC Foods Private Limited and others&nbsp;(2022)  192 NCLAT&nbsp;
								</em>
								which is later in point of time and argued that as per the judgment in&nbsp;
								<em>
									M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports,&nbsp;
								</em>
								the sale does not include liabilities and the above decision being the later decision will prevail over the earlier decision. Therefore, the only issue is which judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT is a ratio.
								<strong>
									(p8)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								In order to examine the above issue, it is important to mention here the background and scope of the appeal in
								<em>
									&nbsp;M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports Pvt. Ltd. Vs. M/s KTC Foods Private Limited
								</em>
								&nbsp;
								<em>
									and others&nbsp;(2022)  192 NCLAT.&nbsp;
								</em>
								The above Appeal was filed by the auction purchaser against the rejection of reliefs claimed in terms of prayer clause &lsquo;c&rsquo; and &lsquo;e&rsquo; in the above application filed by the liquidator. It is very clear from the order that the findings of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the above order was while dealing a situation after distribution of the proceeds of the sale of Corporate Debtor as a going concern as per the order on priority provided Section 53 of the Code. Therefore in those circumstances, the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT held that the remaining unpaid outstanding liabilities of the Corporate Debtor extinguished and all consequences of past non-compliance of the Corporate Debtor under applicable laws in the period prior to e-auction shall be waived.
								<strong>
									(p9-11)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Thus, it is very clear that the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in the above appeal has made its observations in the Application filed by the liquidator which is not the circumstance of the present case. It is also worthwhile to note that the sale proceeds have already been distributed as per the order of priority provided in Section 53 of the Code in the above case which is not the same in the present case. In the present case on hand, the application is filed by auction purchaser himself seeking the declaration without distribution of the liquidation proceeds to the creditors and before issuing sale certificate and possession of the Corporate Debtor. In addition to this, the liquidator in this case clearly put the Applicant on notice that sale of the Corporate Debtor as going concern as is where is&rsquo; basis and the Applicant is duty bound to make due diligence with regard to the local taxes/maintenance fees /electricity expenses/water charges etc., outstanding as on date or yet to fall due in respect of the relevant asset should be ascertained by the E-Auction process applicant and would be borne by the successful bidder.
								<strong>
									(p12)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It was also made it very clear to the auction purchaser in the bid documents that the liquidator does not take or assume any responsibility for any dues, statutory or otherwise, of the Company, including such dues, if any, which may affect transfer of the liquidation assets in the name of the Successful Bidder and such dues, if any, will have to be borne/paid by the Successful Bidder.&nbsp;Similarly, the liquidator also put the Applicant notice that the bidders prior to submitting the bid should make their independent inquiries regarding the encumbrances, title of property claims/right/dues/affecting the property, inspect the&nbsp;property at their own expenses and satisfy themselves. The date and time of inspection of the property will be any time at site email Mr. CA Devnag P. Sam********@sampatassoicates.in.
								<strong>
									(p13-14)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								Therefore, it is very clear from the above facts that the auction purchaser was put on notice regarding all the above liabilities. The Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in M/s Visisth Services Limited VS. S.V. Ramani, after discussing paragraphs 3.2, 3.2.1, 3.2.2, 4.2 and 4.2.1 of the IBBI discussion paper of Corporate Liquidation Process along with approved Regulations dated 27.04.2019 and various rulings of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court held that sale of the Corporate Debtor as going concern includes both assets and liabilities not assets sans liabilities.
								<strong>
									(p15)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is also important to observe here that both the above orders of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT were passed by the same Bench comprising of Hon&rsquo;ble Justice Anant Bijay Singh, Member (Judicial) and Ms. Shreesha Merla, Member (Technical).&nbsp;
								<strong>
									It is also very important to observe here that the same Bench having observed that the sale of Corporate Debtor as a going concern under the Regulations includes assets and liabilities, not assets alone sans liabilities in their earlier order cannot re-write their order in a different way. Therefore, this Bench has no hesitation in holding that the order passed by the same Bench of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in M/s Shiv Shakti Inter Globe Exports Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;Vs. M/s KTC Foods Private Limited and others is distinguishable&nbsp;from the facts of the present case on hand and also from logic.(p16)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								It is also very clear from the plain reading of Clause 3 of Regulation 32A of the Liquidation Process Regulations, 2016&nbsp;that in fact a duty is cast upon the stakeholders committee as well as the liquidator is find out the liabilities of the Corporate Debtor.
								<strong>
									(p17)
								</strong>
							</li>
							<li>
								In view of the above observations and the law laid down by the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT in
								<em>
									&nbsp;M/s Visisth Services Limited VS. S.V. Ramani
								</em>
								, this Bench has no hesitation in holding that&nbsp;
								<strong>
									the Applicant is not entitled for the relief sought in prayer clause (&lsquo;ii&rsquo;) in the above Application and the above Application deserves to be dismissed.
								</strong>
								&nbsp;Accordingly, the above Application is dismissed. It is once again hereby clarified at the cost of repetition that since the Applicant has given up the other reliefs other than the relief in prayer clause (&lsquo;ii&rsquo;) no discussion nor any finding needs to be given by this Bench in respect of the other reliefs.
								<strong>
									(p18)
								</strong>
							</li>
						</ul>
