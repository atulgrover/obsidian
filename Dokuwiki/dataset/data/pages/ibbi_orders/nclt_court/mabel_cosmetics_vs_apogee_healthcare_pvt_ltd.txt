		<h1>
			Claiming back amount paid for the goods/services returned by purchaser upon payment qualifies as operational debt &ndash; Mabel Cosmetics Vs. Apogee Healthcare Pvt. Ltd. &ndash; NCLT Mumbai Bench
		</h1>
		<div>
			July 16, 2020
		</div>
		<p>
			The Adjudicating Authority observed that the person seeking initiation of CIRP under Section 9 of Code, must be a person to whom a claim in respect of the provision of goods or services including employment or a debt in respect of the repayment of dues arising under any law and payable to the Central Government, State Government or local authority is payable. In this case initially the goods were supplied by the corporate debtor to the petitioner on the basis of the super stockist agreement executed between the parties, the corporate debtor raised invoices on the petitioner, subsequently the goods were returned by the petitioner to the corporate debtor and hence petitioner is claiming back amount paid for the stock duly returned by the petitioner. The relationship between the petitioner and the corporate debtor is that of purchaser/stockist and seller and subsequently due to the return of goods the relationship turned as creditor and debtor, hence, the petitioner is entitled to claim the amount as an operational creditor under the code.
		</p>
	</div>

						<p>
							<strong>
								II. Brief about the decision
							</strong>
						</p>
						<p>
							The Adjudicating Authority observed that the&nbsp;person seeking initiation of CIRP under Section 9 of Code,&nbsp;must be a person to whom a claim in respect of the provision of goods or services including employment or a debt in respect&nbsp;of the repayment of dues arising under any law and payable to&nbsp;the Central Government, State Government or local authority&nbsp;is payable. In this case initially the goods were supplied by the&nbsp;corporate debtor to the petitioner on the basis of the super&nbsp;stockist agreement executed between the parties, the&nbsp;corporate debtor raised invoices on the petitioner,&nbsp;subsequently the goods were returned by the petitioner to the&nbsp;corporate debtor and hence petitioner is claiming back amount&nbsp;paid for the stock duly returned by the petitioner. The&nbsp;relationship between the petitioner and the corporate debtor is&nbsp;that of purchaser/stockist and seller and subsequently due to&nbsp;the return of goods the relationship turned as creditor and&nbsp;debtor, hence, the petitioner is entitled to claim&nbsp;the amount as an operational creditor under the code.
						</p>
						<p>
							The judgement in&nbsp;
							<em>
								TATA Chemicals Ltd. Vs. Raj Process Equipments and Systems Pvt.&nbsp;(2018)  85 NCLT
							</em>
							&nbsp;case referred supra,&nbsp;wherein it was held that the claim for refund of advance is not&nbsp;an operational debt, is of no avail to the corporate debtor view&nbsp;of the judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble NCLAT the case of&nbsp;
							<em>
								Overseas Infrastructure Alliance (India) Pvt. Ltd.&nbsp;
							</em>
							<em>
								Vs. Kay Bouvet Engineering Ltd.&nbsp;[2018]  101 NCLAT
							</em>
							,&nbsp;wherein it was held that the claim for refund of advance in a&nbsp;contractual transaction is an operational debt. Further the&nbsp;petitioner herein is claiming the money back for the return of&nbsp;goods purchased by it.&nbsp;In view of the above discussion, it is clear that the debt of&nbsp;the petitioner is proved and the default is also proved. Hence,&nbsp;the petition deserves admission.
							<strong>
								(p11-12)
							</strong>
						</p>
