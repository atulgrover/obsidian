	<h1>
		NCLT dismisses application filed for defer or dismiss CIRP application on the basis of Vidarbha Judgement and levies a penalty of Rs. 1 lac &ndash; Simplex Infrastructures Ltd. Vs. Canara Bank &ndash; NCLT Kolkata Bench
	</h1>
	<div>
		January 11, 2024
	</div>
	<p>
		Hon&rsquo;ble NCLT Kolkata Bench held that: (i) Two-Judge Bench of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank (2023) 67 SC distinguished Vidarbha case on facts and noted that when the existence of debt and default had been proved, the NCLT is bound to admit the petition under Section 7 of the Code. (ii) Vidarbha case could not be understood as taking a contrary position in law as opposed to Innoventive Industries [2017] 02 SC case and also the case law rendered in E.S. Krishnamurthy (2021) 173 SC by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court. (iii) This application has been filed with an intent to derail this Tribunal from considering the Application filed by the Respondent under Section 7 of IBC This is in our view is an attempt to abuse the process of Tribunal and therefore levy a penalty of Rs. 1 Lac (Rs. One Lac only) in terms of Rule 11 of NCLT Rules 2016.
	</p>

					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Facts of the case
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							The Financial Creditor has preferred an application under Section 7 of IB to initiate CIRP of Corporate Debtor and pending for disposal before this Adjudicating Authority.
						</li>
						<li>
							Meanwhile, the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court pronounced a Judgement on 12th July,&nbsp;2022 in the case of
							<em>
								 Vidarbha Industries Power Limited vs. Axis Bank Limited&nbsp;(2022) 91 SC.
							</em>
						</li>
						<li>
							The Ld. Senior Counsel states that there are 36 awards pronounced in favour of the applicant and the applicant is in the process of realizing a sum of Rs. 7330.60 crores and out of such sum, a sum of Rs. 554.17 Crore inclusive of interest has already been awarded in applicant&rsquo;s favour and another 1212.58 crores shall be realized at the earliest.
						</li>
						<li>
							This application has been preferred to seek the following reliefs, inter alia:
							<ul>
								<li>
									(a) The company petition being C.P. (IB) No. 94/KB/2021 be dismissed;
								</li>
								<li>
									(b) In the alternative, the hearing of the company petition being C.P. (IB) No. 94/KB/2021 be deferred for a period of six months or any other period as this Tribunal deems fit and proper;
								</li>
								<li>
									(c) The proceedings instituted under the said company petition, be stayed till the disposal of this instant application.
								</li>
							</ul>
						</li>
					</ul>
					<p>
						<em>
							<strong>
								 Decision of the Adjudicating Authority
							</strong>
						</em>
					</p>
					<ul>
						<li>
							Two-Judge Bench of the Hon&rsquo;ble Supreme Court in
							<em>
								 M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank&nbsp;(2023) 67 SC
							</em>
							 distinguished Vidarbha case on facts and noted that when the existence of debt and default had been proved, the NCLT is bound to admit the petition under Section 7 of the Code. In the
							<em>
								 M. Suresh Kumar Reddy (Supra)
							</em>
							 , the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court was of the view that in light of the dismissal of the review petition against Vidarbha case, it was made clear that the same had only been passed in the factual conspectus of the matter before the Court.
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Further rely upon the order passed by the Coordinate Bench, New Delhi,
							<em>
								 IndusInd Bank Limited v. M/s Feedback Energy Distribution Co. Ltd.
							</em>
							 in Company Petition No. (IB)-477(ND)/2023, where the Learned NCLT New Delhi Bench has discussed the ratio of &ldquo;Review Petition of Vidarbha Industries Power Limited
							<em>
								 (2022) 118 SC
							</em>
							 ..
							<strong>
								 (p22)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Hence, Vidarbha case could not be understood as taking a contrary position in law as opposed to
							<em>
								 Innoventive Industries
							</em>
							<em>
								 [2017] 02 SC
							</em>
							 case and also the case law rendered in
							<em>
								 E.S. Krishnamurthy&nbsp;(2021) 173 SC
							</em>
							 by the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court.
							<strong>
								 (p24)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							Therefore, we find that the Vidarbha Judgement of the Hon&rsquo;ble Apex Court will&nbsp;not come to the rescue of the Applicant, herein.
							<strong>
								 (p25)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							In terms of the foregoing discussions, we find no merit in the application being&nbsp;I.A. (IB) No. 1324/(KB)/2022 preferred by the Applicant herein and accordingly, we dismiss the same.
							<strong>
								 (p26)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							This application has been filed with an intent to derail this Tribunal&nbsp;from considering the Application filed by the Respondent under Section 7 of IBC&nbsp;This is in our view is an attempt to abuse the process of Tribunal and therefore&nbsp;levy a penalty of Rs. 1 Lac (Rs. One Lac only) in terms of Rule 11 of NCLT&nbsp;Rules 2016. The amount may be paid to National Defence Fund within 30 days&nbsp;from the date of pronouncement of the Order.
							<strong>
								 (p27)
							</strong>
						</li>
						<li>
							This order is issued in terms of Section 60(5) of the I&amp;B Code, 2016 read with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016.
							<strong>
								 (p28)
							</strong>
						</li>
					</ul>
