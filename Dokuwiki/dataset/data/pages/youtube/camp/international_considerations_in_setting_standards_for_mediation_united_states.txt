## Summary
The American approach to standards for mediators, in contrast to some other countries, does not require specific qualifications or training at the public or state level. Instead, the market is relied upon to set standards and constrain behavior. While there have been discussions about implementing standards, the general consensus in the U.S. is that the market does a good job of sending reputational signals and that centralizing this at a macro level could create more problems than it solves. There are still some people pushing for standards, but the current system largely relies on the market as a behavior and standard constraining mechanism.
