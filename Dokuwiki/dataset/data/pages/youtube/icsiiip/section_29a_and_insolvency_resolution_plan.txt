## Summary
Section 29A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code is a crucial task for a Resolution Professional (RP) during the invitation and verification of resolution plans. It requires utmost due diligence and cannot solely rely on the affidavit or declaration given by the proposed resolution applicant. The RP must check the eligibility of the promoter, even if they are an MSME, at the appropriate stage. The RP must also check the eligibility of connected persons, joint resolution applicants, and related parties of connected persons. The onus is on the RP to verify the eligibility of the resolution applicant before placing it before the Committee of Creditors (COC). The RP must keep a clear vision of the eligibility of the resolution applicant throughout the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) or Liquidation process. A person who is not eligible under 29A will not be considered eligible to put their scheme before the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) or the adjudicating authority under Section 230 of the Companies Act 2013. The myth that being a promoter of the corporate debtor debars them from placing a resolution plan is not true. The RP must check whether the person is disqualified under the law or the Act to become a resolution applicant. The definition of a person who can place a resolution plan includes an individual, Hindu undivided family, a company, trust, partnership firm, Limited Liability Partnership or other entity established under a statute, and includes a person residing outside India. The RP must specify the criteria for eligibility while inviting Expression of Interest (EoI) and must ensure that the resolution applicant is not ineligible under 29A. The first disqualification is undischarged insolvent, which applies to a person or a body corporate with pending insolvency proceedings and no discharge order from the adjudicating authority.
