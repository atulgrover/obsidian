## Summary
Ravi Prakash Ganti, an insolvency professional and engineer, discusses the principle that the commercial wisdom of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) is supreme in matters of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) and intervention of the tribunals should be minimal. However, he raises concerns about the misinterpretation of subsection 4 of section 30 of the IBC, resulting in a minimum distribution to operational creditors. Ganti explains that this is a discretionary power of the CoC, not a mandatory requirement, and it has been observed that the CoC has largely provided for only the minimum payable to operational creditors and unsecured financial creditors. He also points out that the CoC has extraordinary powers with minimal checks and balances, and it appears that the discretionary powers have been used for the benefit of secured financial creditors. Ganti suggests that the community of financial creditors, especially PSU Banks and financial institutions, should take a more balanced and equitable view and look after the interests of those category of creditors who are not represented in the CoC.

## Committee of creditors May approve resolution plan
2:54
Section 30, subsection fourth, the committee of creditors May approve a resolution plan buy a vote of not less than 66 percent of voting share of the financial creditors, after considering its visibility and viability, the manner of distribution proposed, which may take into account the order of priority amongst creditors as laid down in subsection 1 of section 53, including the priority and value of the security interest of a secured creditor, and such are the requirements as may be specified by the work board. 

3:36
