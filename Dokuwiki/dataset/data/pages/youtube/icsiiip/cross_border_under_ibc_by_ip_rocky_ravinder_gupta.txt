## Summary
The speaker discusses the historical roots of insolvency law, tracing it back to Roman law procedures such as cecio banoram, assignment of property, destruction of assets, and remission and dilation (compositions with creditors). These individual debt collection procedures evolved into collective debt procedures, which form the basis of modern insolvency regimes. The speaker notes that bankruptcy legislation has existed in Europe since the 13th century and that the term 'bankruptcy' may have originated from the practice of breaking the bench of bankrupt traders in ancient European markets. The first documented cross-border insolvency case was the Amanti Affair in 1302, and the first Indian case of cross-border insolvency occurred in 1908 with McFadden and Company Limited. The speaker emphasizes that cross-border insolvency cases are complex due to multiple jurisdictions and statutory obligations, and that understanding the procedures and laws involved is crucial. The speaker also highlights the differences between common law and civil law jurisdictions, which impact the approach to cross-border insolvency. The speaker mentions the territorialism and universalism theories and notes that many jurisdictions follow modified universalism, combining elements of both theories.
