## Summary
This summary discusses important concepts and provisions in the IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code) for the benefit of first-time learners. The adjudicating authority for IBC is the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), with appeals going to the NCLAT and Supreme Court. The definition of debt is crucial, as it includes financial and operational debts that are due but unpaid. Financial debt includes loans, deposits, debentures, and lease/hire purchase transactions, while operational debt covers claims for goods/services, employment, and government dues. Financial creditors have higher rights than operational creditors. Default is the trigger for IBC actions, which requires a debt, due payment, and non-repayment. Operational creditors must send a demand notice before initiating action. The corporate debtor, financial creditor, or operational creditor can initiate the process. Disputes can delay operational creditor actions, and pre-existing disputes must be proven before the NCLT. The insolvency commencement date marks the start of the insolvency process, with timelines calculated from this date.
