## Summary
Today's discussion will focus on the treatment of government dues or Crown debts under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The presentation will cover the status prior to IBC, important definitions, decided cases, impact of moratorium, order of priority under section 53, and the treatment of government dues under various legislations and judgments. The speaker has compiled 20 cases, including landmark judgments, to provide a comprehensive understanding of the topic. Prior to IBC, the Companies Act 1956 and 2013, Securitization and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, and the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act 1993 were the major legislations that dealt with government dues. The IBC, introduced in 2016, altered the order of priority of payment of government dues, making it lesser significant compared to other dues. The preamble of the IBC also supports this alteration. The definitions of operational debt and operational creditor are crucial to understanding the treatment of government dues under the IBC. Regulation 7 of the CP regulation and Regulation 17 of the liquidation regulation specify the claim filing process for operational creditors. Section 30 of the IBC deals with the submission of a resolution plan, and section 31 speaks about the approval of a resolution plan. Once approved, the resolution plan is binding on all parties, including the government.
