## Summary
This transcript is about a workshop discussing the interplay of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) and the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA). The session covers three stages of applicability: prior to Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CRP), during CRP, and after CRP. Prior to CRP, if the Enforcement Directorate (ED) has attached assets before the insolvency commencement date, the Resolution Professional (RP) will realize that the assets are attached and intimate the Committee of Creditors (CoC). The RP can file for release of attachments during the CP on the ground of moratorium. During CRP, the assets attached are still attached even when the moratorium is in force. After CRP, if the ED investigation has come to an end and ED realizes that there are certain assets of the corporate data which have to be attached, the new resolution applicant will be bound by the attachment. However, section 32A of the IBC provides criminal immunity to the resolution applicant or the assets of the corporate data once the resolution has been approved by the Adjudicating Authority (AA). The Mumbai High Court in March 2024 held that upon approval of the resolution plan, the assets shall not have any criminal liability, and there cannot be any attachment or such nature of orders passed against such assets.
