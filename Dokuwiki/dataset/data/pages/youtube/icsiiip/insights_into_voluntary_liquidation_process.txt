## Summary
In this webinar, speaker Vishawjeet Gupta discusses the concept of voluntary liquidation as outlined in Article 191g of the Constitution, which grants the right to close a business but with certain restrictions. The primary objective of voluntary liquidation is to terminate operations, sell assets, and distribute them fairly among creditors and shareholders. The voluntary liquidation process was initially outlined in sections 304 to 323 of the Companies Act 2013 but has since been incorporated into the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) under section 59, which was notified on March 30, 2017. The IBBI Voluntary Liquidation Process Regulations 2017 provide the regulations for the voluntary liquidation process. A corporate person, as defined under section 3 subsection 7 of IBC, can be liquidated voluntarily under IBC, except for financial service providers. The conditions for voluntary liquidation include the absence of default and the ability of the company to pay its debts in full from the sale proceeds of its assets. The prerequisites for initiating voluntary liquidation include a declaration by the majority of directors or designated partners that the company has no debt or can pay its debts in full, and that the liquidation is not intended to defraud any person. The declaration must be accompanied by the audited financial statements and record of business operations for the previous two years, and a valuation report of assets if any. The liquidator must comply with all regulations, and if the voluntary liquidation process goes beyond 270 days, the liquidator must file the financial statements with the ROC.
