## Summary
In this webinar, speaker Ajay Kumar Jain discusses the concept of privity of contract in the context of IBC case laws. Privity of contract means that only parties to the contract have the right to enforce the rights and obligations provided by the contract, and strangers to the contract are barred from enforcing any obligation on any party. This doctrine protects parties to a contract from obligations that they never agreed to incur. The speaker illustrates this concept with the example of a contract between A and B, where B agrees to deliver goods to C against consideration from A. If B fails to deliver the goods, C cannot enforce the contract as a stranger to the contract. The speaker then discusses a case between Noida Authority and Roma Unicorn, where the resolution plan was approved without the consent of the Lesser, who was the actual owner of the land. The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) found a violation of law and issued directions for a fresh form G to be published, inviting resolution plans from other applicants. The speaker also discusses a case where the RP of a company filed an appeal before the Supreme Court against the order of NCLT, which was turned down by the court. The speaker emphasizes that the agreed person, which is the COC or other stakeholders, should file an appeal and not the RP. The speaker then discusses a case where employees approached him for their salaries during the CRP period, which is part of the CRP cost if the RP managed the operation of the CD as a going concern. The speaker also refers to a case where the Liquidator failed to pay the dues of the employees during the CRP period, and the matter was referred back to the Liquidator for adjudication. The speaker highlights the mistake committed by the RP in another case, where he refused to pay the salaries of 10 out of 20 employees who worked during the CRP period, discriminating between the employees.
