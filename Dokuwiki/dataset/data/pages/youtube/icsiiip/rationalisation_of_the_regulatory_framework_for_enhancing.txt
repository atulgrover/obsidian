## Summary
The transcript discusses a virtual roundtable discussion on proposed amendments to enhance the effectiveness of Insolvency Professional Entities (IPEs) and their regulatory framework. The moderator, Harmeet Kaur, presents four changes in the discussion paper, including monitoring of IPs, related party definition for IPEs, restrictions on the number of assignments, and minimum fee structure for IPs acting as IPs. The existing framework closely monitors the performance and conduct of IPs, but with the proposed amendments, similar provisions will become applicable to IPEs. The presentation highlights the need for rationalization of the existing framework to meet emerging requirements and to provide clarity and flexibility to IPs in performing their functions. The first issue discussed is the monitoring of IPs, where IPs are jointly and separately liable for all acts or omissions of their partners or directors. The proposal suggests that in case of contravention by the IP acting as IP, the disciplinary proceeding shall be initiated against the IP who is or has been the authorized signatory for that respective assignment, and the IP shall not allow him or her to act as an authorized signatory in any new assignment. The presentation also discusses other laws governing professionals, such as the Chartered Accountants Act, Companies Act, and Advocates Act, and their provisions for taking action against professionals.
