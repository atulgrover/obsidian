## Summary
The webinar, led by speaker Vikram Kumar, discusses recent important orders by the NCLT. One of the orders from March 2023 involves two appeals regarding the classification of applicants as financial or operational creditors in a case of invoice discounting. The primary issue is whether the applicant who has filed the application for admission of CIRP is a financial creditor or an operational creditor. The facts of the case involve the sale of copper products by Ashoka Creations to TTD Copper, with Minion Ventures running a platform for invoice discounting called Creditx. The financial creditors financed the invoice uploaded by the buyer on the platform and paid 1.75 crores to the seller, Ashok Creations. The corporate debtor, TTD Copper, failed to make the payment to the financers on the due date, leading to a joint application to the NCLT for admission of an application under Section 7. The NCLT held that the applicants are not financial creditors but operational creditors, as they stepped into the shoes of the seller, an operational creditor, by assigning the rights to receive the amount from the buyer. The NCLT relied on the Supreme Court judgment in JP Infratech and a similar NCLT judgment in Cooperative Rubber.
