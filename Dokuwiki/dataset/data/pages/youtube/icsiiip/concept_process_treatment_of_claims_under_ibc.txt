## Summary
The topic of the session is the concept, process, and treatment of claims under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The process starts with the filing of an application by a financial creditor, operational creditor, or the insolvent company itself. After admission of the application, the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) is appointed, who invites claims from all creditors. The IRP has three days to publish the invitation for claims in a newspaper, followed by a 11-day window for submission of claims. The IBC defines a claim as a right to payment or a right to remedy for breach of contract. The difference between a claim and debt is that a claim can be matured or unmatured, while debt is an obligation of payment. During the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP), there is no bifurcation between secured and unsecured creditors. However, in liquidation, the bifurcation between secured and unsecured creditors is considered. A secured creditor is one who has a right, title, or interest in a property that secures payment or performance of an obligation. Performance guarantee is not considered a security interest as it is about the performance of a contract and does not involve any underlying assets. The government's dues can be treated as a secured creditor if the assets are attached to it and if the security interest is registered.
