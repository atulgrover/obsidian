## Summary
In this workshop on insolvency, Ajay Garg begins with a story about a golden deer, drawing a parallel to the traps that insolvency professionals may encounter in their practice. He congratulates the attendees for passing the exam and notes that insolvency is the toughest profession, with 75% of the exam based on case studies and laws. He emphasizes the need for an insolvency professional's personality to match the demands of the profession. Insolvency professionals are under regular scrutiny by IBBI, with 85% of complaints resolved against them. They are working in enemy territory and are susceptible to complaints and scrutiny by the Insolvency Professional Agency (IPA) and NCLT. IPEs, with over 100 members, also scrutinize individual insolvency professionals. Enemies in the form of supporters and helpers, such as COC members, CD employees, CD promoters, and operational creditors, are always suspicious of insolvency professionals. Slip of tongue, unethical practices, and delays are punishable offenses. The Punisher is NCLT, which punishes through verbal comments and orders. Government authorities, financial creditors, and COC members also pose challenges in the form of demands and delays. IBBI is the Punisher, and punishment comes in the form of show cause notices and performance pressure. Insolvency professionals must be prepared for regular challenges and punishments.
