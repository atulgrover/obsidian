## Summary
Umesh Chandra Sahoo, an advocate, discussed the importance of understanding the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code and NCLAT judgments in the context of corporate insolvency resolution. He emphasized that the approved resolution plan can be contested if there has been material irregularity in the exercise of power by the resolution professional during the corporate insolvency resolution process. The date of default is not necessarily the NPA date, but rather the date when the debt becomes due and payable and is not paid by the corporate debtor. A default is considered as one instance, but the limitation period for defending the default can be extended if the default continues. The NCLAT judgment in the matter of Lakshmi Surana vs Union Bank of India clarified that the date of default is not the NPA date. The acknowledgement of liability in writing, signed by a party or by any person through whom he derives his title or liability, can start a fresh period of limitation under Section 18 of the Limitation Act. An OTS proposal given by a director on behalf of a company should be supported by a board resolution to be considered valid.
