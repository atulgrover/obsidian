## Summary
Harshul Shah, founder of Corporate Legal Practice, an expert in non-litigation corporate matters, starts a workshop on claim verification in the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) context. He mentions the evolving nature of IBC laws and the various types of creditors, including financial, operational, and home buyers. The session covers the claim submission and verification process in Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CRP) and liquidation processes, discussing various judicial pronouncements and the right to payment. The claim submission process involves reading the IBC code and regulations, making a Public Announcement, and communicating with creditors. The IRP has the responsibility to communicate with the creditors about the Public Announcement and invite claims. The Public Announcement should specify the last date of submission of claims, which is within 14 days of the IRP's appointment, and creditors can submit claims up to 90 days from the insolvency commencement date. The communication process involves approaching creditors with available information, using management details when operative, and giving them the claim form. Claims received after 90 days are examined, verified, and put before the COC for approval. Communication with creditors should be made through post or electronic means, maintaining records of the communication.
