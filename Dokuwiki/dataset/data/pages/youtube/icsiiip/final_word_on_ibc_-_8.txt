## Summary
The webinar discusses the case of MBA Infrastructure, which was incorporated in 1990 and availed loans and credit facilities from a consortium of banks. In 2013, after the failure of the company to act in terms with the repayment plan, personal guarantees were invoked by some of the respondents. The Reserve Bank of India (RBL) filed a petition under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) against the company. The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) appointed an interim resolution professional and imposed a moratorium. Two resolution plans were received, one of which was submitted by the promoter of the company in November 2017. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code Amendment Ordinance 2017 introduced Section 29a, which disqualified certain persons from submitting a resolution plan. The NCLT held that the promoter was eligible to submit a resolution plan. The RBL challenged this order, but it was dismissed as withdrawn after the financial creditors approved the resolution plan with a voting share of 78.50 percent. The NCLT approved the resolution plan in April 2018. The Supreme Court interpreted Section 29a of the IBC in the Bank of Baroda versus MBL infrastructure case and held that a guarantor whose guarantee is invoked by a creditor is not ineligible to submit a resolution plan under Section 29a of the code.
