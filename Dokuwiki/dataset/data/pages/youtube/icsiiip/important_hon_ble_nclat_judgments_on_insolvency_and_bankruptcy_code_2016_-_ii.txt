## Summary
Umesh Chandra Sahoo, an advocate, discussed a case during a webinar about the role of a Resolution Professional (RP) in identifying fraudulent transactions under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The case involved a suspended director of a corporate entity and the question of law was whether an RP can take action for fraudulent transactions beyond the timeline stipulated under CRP regulation 35A. Sahoo explained the importance of the RP's role in identifying defective issues of the corporate data and determining pro transactions in three stages. He also discussed the timeline of 75, 115, and 135 days mentioned in the code and the importance of forming an opinion and determination of transactions. Sahoo shared a judgment where the NCLAT allowed the RP to file an application beyond the time period of 30 days mentioned in the code, stating that regulation 35A is directory and not mandatory. Sahoo concluded by saying that the intent of the legislature and not the language in which it is clothed is what matters in determining whether a statue is mandatory or directory.
