## Summary
In this transcript, Siva Rama Prasad and Puvvala CMA discuss a case related to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The case involves a dispute between a respondent company and a service provider over a breach of a non-disclosure agreement. The respondent company withheld payment due to the breach, leading to the service provider issuing a demand notice under Section 8 of the IBC. However, the respondent company had already issued a notice of dispute, which led to the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) rejecting the service provider's application for initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP). The service provider then approached the Supreme Court, arguing that a dispute to a demand notice does not amount to a valid ground for rejection of the application under Section 9 of the IBC. The Supreme Court will clarify the meaning and existence of a dispute for the purpose of the application under Section 9 of the IBC. The transcript also discusses the process of filing an application under Section 9 of the IBC and the requirements of Section 8, including the serving of a demand notice and the corporate debtor's response within 10 days.
