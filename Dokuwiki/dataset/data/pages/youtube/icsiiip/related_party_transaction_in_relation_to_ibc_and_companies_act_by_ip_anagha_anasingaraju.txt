## Summary
Anagha Anasingaraju, a company secretary and designated partner in Kanjan Co LLP, discusses related party transactions. She compares the definitions of related parties in the IBC, Companies Act, and income tax laws. Key management personnel (KMP) and their relatives are considered related parties if they hold more than 2% of the shares in a private or public company where a director, partner, or manager is a director. Transactions between related parties can lead to asset diversion from the company, which is why the IBC and Companies Act aim to maximize value for creditors and stakeholders by identifying and regularizing such transactions. Under the IBC, related parties include directors, partners, KMP, and their relatives, as well as LLPs or partnership firms where partners or employees act on the instructions of the CD. The definition of related parties is crucial for transaction audits and identifying avoidance transactions in companies undergoing or likely to undergo CRP.
