## Summary
The transcript covers a review of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) regulatory amendments. The key points include an amendment to regulation 6A, which mandates the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) to send a communication along with a copy of the public announcement of his appointment to all creditors via post or electronic mail. The amendment also clarifies that where it is not possible to send a communication to creditors, the public announcement made under regulation 6 shall be deemed to be communicated to such creditors. Additionally, an insertion in regulation 7B subclass 5 requires operational creditors to submit copies of relevant extracts of form gstr1, form gstr 3B, and the copy of e-way Bill to prove their claims. The amendment also exempts those creditors who do not require registration and to whom GST or goods and service tax is not covered under any law relating to the act itself. The timeline of 14 days for the admission or rejection of the application for initiating insolvency proceedings has been observed to be a key reason for delay, primarily due to the verification of the existence of debt and default. The amendment aims to reduce the time taken by the IRPs to verify and decide on the admission of the claim by allowing the use of gstr1, gstr 3B, and e-way Bill as proof of supply of goods and services to the corporate debtor. The amendment also provides for a regulatory fee of 0.25 percent of the realizable value to creditors under the resolution plan, applicable where the resolution plan is approved on or after 31st of October 2022. The amendment also inserts a provision for the appointment of a third registered value to assess the asset class for submitting an estimate of the fair and liquidation value, computed by the two registered valuers, in case the two estimates of a value in an asset class are significantly different. The amendment also provides for the appointment of two registered valuers to determine the fair value and the liquidation value of the corporate data within seven days of the IRP's appointment. The valuation exercise plays a crucial role in the decision-making by the Committee of Creditors (COC) and helps determine the minimum entitlement to operational creditors and dissenting financial creditors. The amendment aims to remove ambiguities and reduce potential litigation by providing a guiding factor in the appointment of the third value. The amendment also requires the RP to forward a copy of the avoidance application to the prospective resolution applicant to enable him to consider the same while submitting the resolution plan within the stipulated time. The amendment also provides for an extension in the timeline for submission of the information memorandum by the RP from 54 days to 95 days from the insolvency commencement date.
