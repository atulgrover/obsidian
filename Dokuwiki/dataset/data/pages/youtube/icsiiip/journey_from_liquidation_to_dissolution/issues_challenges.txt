## Summary
The presentation focuses on involuntary liquidation as an outcome of the IRP process and the role of the Insolvency Professional (IP) in this process. If no resolution plan is received within the prescribed time, the adjudicating authority can pass an order for liquidation. The IP must vet and certify the compliance of the resolution plan with the code and applicable regulations. The COC can decide to liquidate the company before the stipulated period, and if a resolution plan is contravened during implementation, any person can apply to the NCLT for liquidation. Voluntary liquidation is for solvent companies who have not committed any default. The Liquidator, unlike the IP, is a quasi-judicial authority with more power and responsibilities, including the verification of claims, acceptance of claims, and distribution of funds. The Liquidator must decide whether to continue or close the business for beneficial liquidation. The Liquidator has the power to sell movable or immovable property, investigate financial affairs, and institute and defend legal proceedings. The Liquidator can consult any stakeholders, but such consultation is not binding.
