## Summary
The webinar discusses a case related to the filing of a liquidation application under Section 33 of IBC by a workforce union of Jacket Synthetics, a company declared as a sick industrial unit by BIFR in 1998. The union claimed that the company was in continuous breach of a sanctioned Rehabilitation scheme and was unable to meet its obligations towards creditors. The NCLT dismissed the application, stating that the sanctioned Rehabilitation scheme was not a resolution plan under IBC and therefore, there was no question of the respondent company committing a breach of the plan. The appeal was then preferred by the applicant, challenging the NCLT order. The discussion revolves around the validity of the notification of May 2017, which considered a Rehabilitation scheme sanctioned under and under implementation as a resolution plan within the meaning of IBC. The notification was held to be in conflict with the maximum period granted under Section 612 and was not set aside but only observed to be in conflict with the limitation period. The notification was further submitted to be still valid and not affected by the neutral observation of the Supreme Court. The NCLT was also criticized for not giving Liberty to operational creditors to file an application under Section 33 while refusing to entertain an application under Section 9. The respondent refuted the submissions, stating that the approved Rehabilitation scheme was not a resolution plan within the code and the notification dated 2017 was held to be in excess of jurisdiction of the central government and cannot be relied upon. The rehabilitation scheme was fully implemented, and the payment of dues under the scheme was offered and disbursed. The initiation of proceedings under Section 63 was considered fraudulent with a malicious intent. The primary question was whether the approved Rehabilitation scheme was a resolution plan within the meaning of the code, and it was held that it was not, as the notification was beyond the scope of the removal of difficulties provisions.
