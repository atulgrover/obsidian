## Summary
Good afternoon everyone, welcome to the last day of the workshop series on the operationalization of Part III of the IBC code. Today's topic is being delivered by Mr. Vikram Kumar, a chartered accountant and insolvency professional with over 30 years of experience. The session aims to be interactive, with participants encouraged to ask questions. The main focus of the discussion is the operationalization of Part III, which pertains to personal insolvency. Part III contains seven chapters, with the first chapter covering definitions and the application of Part III. The fresh start process, introduced in chapter 2, has not been implemented yet. Chapter 3 pertains to the personal insolvency resolution process, while chapters 4 and 5 cover the bankruptcy process and bankruptcy estate. Chapter 6 notifies the adjudicating authority for individuals and partnership firms, and chapter 7 covers offenses and penalties. Certain sections of Part One and Part Two are critical to understand before starting Part Three, including section 2, which pertains to the application of IBC and has been amended to include personal guarantors, partnership firms, and individuals. The effective date of implementation of Part Three is from 1st December 2019, as notified by the government. The contract act, covered by the Indian contract Act 1872, defines a guarantee as a contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of his default. The liability of a guarantor is co-extensive with the principal debtor, and the guarantor can be discharged from his liability under two circumstances: a variance in the contract of guarantee without his consent, and if the principal debtor is discharged. The guarantor has the right of subrogation, meaning they can step into the shoes of the creditor on honoring the liabilities or paying the liabilities of the creditor.
