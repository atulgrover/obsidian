## Summary
The speaker, Advocate Omega Shetty, is discussing the evolution of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) in India. He mentions that the necessity for the IBC arose due to the absence of a single law dealing with various types of insolvency and the complications that arose from different legislations. The IBC was enacted in 2017 and has seen four amendment acts and several judicial pronouncements that have contributed to its evolution. The speaker will take the audience through important aspects of these amendments and judicial pronouncements. He starts with section 29A, which was incorporated by the First Amendment Act of 2017, and discusses its importance in preventing undeserving persons from taking back the reins of a corporate debtor. Section 29A sets out various ineligibilities for a resolution applicant, including being an undischarged insolvent, a wilful defaulter, or having an account that has become NPA for a period of more than one year. The speaker also mentions the curing provisions and exceptions provided in section 29A. The validity and wisdom of this section were considered in the Salameh Turns case and the Swiss Ribbons Private Limited case, where the Supreme Court upheld its validity.
