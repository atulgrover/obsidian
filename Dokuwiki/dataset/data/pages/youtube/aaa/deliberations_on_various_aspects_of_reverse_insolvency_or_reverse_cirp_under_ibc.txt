## Summary
Today's discussion is on reverse insolvency, which was started in 2019 by Honorable Justice Muralidhar and upheld by the Supreme Court in 2020. This concept is primarily for Real Estate companies and allows promoters to propose completing a project and contributing funds within a reasonable time. During the moratorium, the company receives protection from various cases, and the RP and COC monitor the funds. The purpose of reverse insolvency is to benefit home buyers and provide financial backing for promoters. The first case discussed is Flat Wires Association v. Unitech Realtech Private Limited, where allottees were allowed to sell to third parties and take possession of the property, but refunds were not allowed. The second case is Anand Murti v. Sony Infr Private Limited, where the Supreme Court upheld the benefits of reverse insolvency for real estate projects and timely completion. The RP and COC work together with promoters during the moratorium, and the entire cost of the CIRP is met by promoters. However, there are practical issues and criticisms of this structure, such as the division of the Committee of Creditors and the possibility of promoters gaining from the process. The RP needs to ensure a transparent and strict mechanism for separate accounting of projects and comply with regulations.
