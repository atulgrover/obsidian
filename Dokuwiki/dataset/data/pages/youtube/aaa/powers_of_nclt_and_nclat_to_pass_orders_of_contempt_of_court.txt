## Summary
The webinar discusses the powers of NCLT and NCLAT regarding contempt of court proceedings, particularly in the context of insolvency cases. The speakers analyze the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, and the Companies Act, 2013, to argue that NCLT and NCLAT have the authority to initiate contempt proceedings. The speakers also highlight the importance of these powers for ensuring that orders of the tribunal are complied with and enforced. The discussion also covers the differences between a tribunal and a court, and the legal status of NCLT and NCLAT as tribunals. The speakers emphasize that the principle of effectiveness requires that statutory provisions should be interpreted to make them effective and operative, and that the objectives of the IBC include ensuring that all creditors are treated fairly and that resolution plans comply with the provisions of the code. The speakers conclude that NCLT and NCLAT have the powers of contempt of court, and that these powers are necessary for the effective functioning of the tribunals in insolvency cases.
