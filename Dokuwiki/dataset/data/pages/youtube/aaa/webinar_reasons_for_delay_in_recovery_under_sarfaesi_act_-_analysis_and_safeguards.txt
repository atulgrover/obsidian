## Summary
Advocate Sanjay Gupta, founder director of Tripoli Capital Services Private Limited, discusses the enforcement of security interest in a webinar. The focus of the presentation is on the SARFAESI Act and its procedures for the enforcement of security interest and sale of assets. The SARFAESI Act is expected to perform well in the next few years, especially if the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code is suspended. The Act is applicable to all kinds of entities, unlike the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code which is only applicable to private limited companies. The availability of buyers for the assets is crucial for the success of the recovery process. The recent amendment in the SARFAESI Act has made the registration of charge on the property compulsory, and the priority of interest is given to the lender's charge if registered with the central registry. The webinar had 1400 registrations and 564 attendees. The present situation of the total participants shows that there is no uncertainty about the future of recovery under the SARFAESI Act for banks. The banks need to ensure that all the charges on the property are registered with the central registry before taking any SARFAESI action. The possession of the assets is crucial for enforcing the SARFAESI Act, and it cannot be enforced for pledge transactions. The SARFAESI Act is applicable to all charges, including financial leases, hire-purchase, and conditional sales. The amendment in 2016 has made the surface enforceable for intangible assets, including intellectual property rights, trademarks, and copyrights. The bank can sell the intellectual property rights or intangible properties by invoking the SARFAESI Act if the credit was provided to enable the borrower to acquire the asset. The SARFAESI Act is not applicable for lien on goods, money, or security by any buyer under the Indian contract act, such as bailment, margin money, or less money deposit, or lien on shares for providing loans. Factoring is also not covered under the SARFAESI Act.
