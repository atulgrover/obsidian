## Summary
This webinar, the first in a series, focuses on the Supreme Court judgment in the Sunil Kumar Jain vs Sundresh Bhatt case, delivered on 19th April 2022. The judgment settles various issues related to workmen and employee claims during the insolvency resolution process (CIRP) period and the status of going concern of the company. The importance of the judgment lies in the roles assigned to the IRP, RP, liquidator, and the Committee of Creditors (CoC) regarding workman claims and the going concern status of the company. The case involved ABG Shipyard Limited, where the CIRP started on 1st August 2017 and ended on 25th April 2019. The main issue was whether workmen and employees who were on the role of the company as on the date of commencement of CIRP, and whose services were not terminated, were entitled to their salaries for this period. The NCLT had disposed of the appeal and directed 272 workmen employees to file their individual claims before the liquidator. The Supreme Court discussed the two primary issues: (1) the claim of workmen and employees with respect to wages and salaries during the CIRP period, and (2) the amount due and payable to respective workmen and employees towards pension fund, gratuity fund, and providence fund, in view of Section 36 subsection 4 of the code. The arguments were primarily in favor of the workmen and employees, stating that they were on the payroll of the corporate debtor, their employment contracts were never terminated, and they were instructed to report to the RP. The workmen claimed that their salary and wages for the entire period of CIRP should be paid in full and considered as CIRP cost. The liquidator had accepted this amount as CIRP cost and uploaded the details to the IBBI website. The Supreme Court discussed various sections of the IBC, including the definition of workman, insolvency resolution process cost, CIRP period, and the duties of the IRP, RP, and the personal to attend and cooperate with the IRP. The Court also discussed Section 33 subsection 7 and Section 36 subsection 4, which deal with the notice of discharge to workmen and employees and the non-inclusion of money kept in providence, superannuation, or gratuity funds in the liquidation estate.
