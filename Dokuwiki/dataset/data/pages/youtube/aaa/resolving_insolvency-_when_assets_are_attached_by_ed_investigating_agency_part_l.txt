## Summary
This webinar discusses the resolution of enforcement directorate actions that have caused a backlog of cases due to contradictory judgments. The first part focuses on the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and its provisions, including the definition of money laundering and the powers of the enforcement directorate. The recent judgment from the Bombay High Court in the case of Shiv Ch is also discussed. The second part will cover various judgments of High Courts and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) regarding the attachment of assets under insolvency law. There are three types of cases under litigation: attachment before the commencement of CIRP, during CIRP or liquidation, and after the approval of a resolution plan or sale of assets under liquidation. The PMLA and Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) have contrasting claims, leading to a clash of provisions. The IBC aims to ensure availability of credit, balance the interests of stakeholders, provide a structured framework for recovery, and address insolvency. The PMLA seeks to attach and sell properties acquired through tainted money, with the proceeds going to the central government. The clash between these two laws arises when a secured creditor claims a superior charge on properties and the PMLA asserts its right to attach and sell the properties.
