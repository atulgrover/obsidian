## Summary
The webinar discusses the struggle between secured financial creditors who want to claim the value of their security interest from the resolution plan or liquidation, in light of conflicting judgments from the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), and the Supreme Court. The main issue is whether section 30 subsection 2 Clause B and sub Clause Roman small two of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code entitles dissenting financial creditors to be paid the minimum value of its security interest. The discussion refers to the DBS Bank limited Singapore versus Ruchi soya Industries limited judgment delivered on 3rd of January 2024, and the impact of the Amit metallics limited judgment on various NCLT and NCLAT judgments. The webinar also touches upon the history of this issue, starting with the Committee of creditors of Sr steel India limited versus Sish Kumar Gupta judgment of 2019, and ending with the DBS Bank limited Singapore versus Ruchi soya Industries limited judgment of 2024. The speakers mention that the rights of dissenting secured creditors to get payment equal to the value of the security interest are protected by law, and that the distribution of assets among secured financial creditors should consider the priority of charges and the value of security interest, an issue currently pending before the Supreme Court.
