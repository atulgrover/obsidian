## Summary
In this week's webinar, conducted from Singapore as part of a historical event by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, the speaker discussed recent judgments related to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. One of the judgments discussed was from the Supreme Court of India, involving Rainbow Paper Private Limited. The speaker, along with their partner Parveen, had secured operational creditor status in a case in 2017, which was upheld by the Supreme Court. The judgment also diluted the earlier Rainbow Papers judgment, clarifying that statutory authorities' dues do not have a separate line of priority. The case involved electricity connection dues from 2010, with a charge on the assets of the corporate debtor. The electricity Distribution Company had attached the properties of the company and created a charge on the property before the insolvency proceedings started. The Liquidator argued that the electricity dues did not enjoy any priority and were not registered as a charge under the company's act. The Supreme Court ultimately ruled that the hierarchy of the order of priority under section 53 of the IBC accords government dues lower priority than unsecured and secured financial creditors, and that electricity dues are not considered government dues but operational debts. The speaker also discussed the relationship between the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code and the Electricity Act of 2003, stating that the IBC prevails over the earlier act.
