## Summary
In this webinar episode III, the speakers discuss the basics of bankruptcy for individuals, focusing on the moratorium and its implications for creditors and debtors. The interim moratorium under section 124 of the IBC sets in when the bankruptcy application is filed, preventing any legal action or proceedings against the debtor or their property. The bankruptcy order is passed within 14 days of the application, and the estate of the bankrupt vests with the bankruptcy trustee. The bankrupt is disqualified from holding public office, acting as a public servant, or being appointed as a public servant. They cannot create charges on their assets or take further debt without the trustee's special approval. They must inform all parties of their bankruptcy status before entering into financial or commercial transactions of over INR 1 lakh. They cannot maintain legal actions or proceedings without the adjudicating authority's specific approval and cannot travel overseas without such approval. These restrictions and disqualifications are in effect until the bankruptcy order is recalled, modified, or a discharge order is passed.
