## Summary
This summary covers a webinar discussing the Supreme Court judgment in the case of Kotak Mahindra Bank vs A Balakrishnan & Others, dated 30th May 2022. The main issues addressed in this judgment are whether a decree or order of a court can become a financial creditor or an operational creditor, and what kind of limitation law is applicable to such orders. The case involves Kotak Mahindra Bank, which became a creditor after acquiring loans from End Bank Housing Limited, assigned to them by the original lender. The borrowers, including Prasad Properties and Investment Private Limited, defaulted on the loans, which led to civil suits and a compromise arrangement. However, the compromise was not honored, and Kotak Mahindra Bank proceeded with recovery measures, including filing applications under the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act 1993. The bank later filed an application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016 against the corporate guarantor, Prasad Properties and Investment Private Limited, claiming a total amount of approximately 835 crores. The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) held the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) to be invalid due to the limitation and the recovery certificate not being considered a debt. The Supreme Court is now hearing the appeal against this impugned order. The key arguments revolve around the applicability of the limitation law, the merger of cause of action into the order of issuance of the recovery certificate, and the status of the recovery certificate holder as a financial creditor. The webinar also explains various legal doctrines, such as res judicata, merged cause of action, and the difference between a decree and a recovery certificate.
