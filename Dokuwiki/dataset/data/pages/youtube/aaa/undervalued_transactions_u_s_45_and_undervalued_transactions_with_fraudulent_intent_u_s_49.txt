## Summary
The discussion revolves around undervalued transactions in the context of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. Undervalued transactions were also present in the Companies Act of 1956 and are in the Companies Act of 2013 in sections 328 and 331. The Resolution Professional (RP) or the Liquidator can file an application for undervalued transactions under section 45 subsection 1. However, in the case of undervalued transactions, a creditor, a member of the company, or a partner of a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) can also file this application. The lookback period for undervalued transactions is two years from the insolvency commencement date for related parties and one year for others. A gift or a transaction involving the transfer of assets of the company, with consideration significantly less than the market value, can be considered undervalued transactions. The adjudicating authority has the power to get an independent expert assessment of the transaction's value. The adjudicating authority can pass orders to declare the transaction void, require any property transferred as part of the transaction to be vested in the corporate data, release or discharge any security interest created on the assets of the corporate data, and direct the other person who has got the benefit to give back that kind of sum to the Liquidator or the RP. If the application is not filed by the RP or the Liquidator and is filed by a creditor or a member or a partner of an LLP, the adjudicating authority can pass the order to declare such transaction as void and initiate disciplinary proceedings against the Liquidator or the RP. Protections are provided to those who have acquired the interest in the property in good faith. The adjudicating authority cannot pass an order that the asset be vested back to the corporate data if the person has purchased the asset from someone other than the corporate data and was acting in good faith.
