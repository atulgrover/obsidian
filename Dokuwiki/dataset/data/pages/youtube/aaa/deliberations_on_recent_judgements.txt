## Summary
The webinar discusses significant judgments related to the SARFAESI Act and their impact on debt recovery, particularly in comparison to the IBC. One important judgment is Ham Incorporation Private Limited vs. Amit Hatcher's Private Limited (decided on 6th Dec 2023) by the Honorable Supreme Court of India, which clarified that the completion of sale under the SARFAESI Act prior to the Declaration of moratorium under IBC prevents treated properties from being considered part of the liquidation estate in subsequent liquidation proceedings. The Supreme Court referred to SJP Impacts Private Limited vs. Assistant General Manager and Authorized Officer of Canara Bank (decided on 2021) and Inspector General of Registration and Others vs. G Maduram Madul and Others (decided on 11th Nov 2022) to support its decision. These judgments emphasize that when a sales certificate is executed, the seller has the responsibility to forward a copy of the sales certificate to the registration authority for recording in book one under the Registration Act. If the registration is done as per law, stamp duty is not required. This applies to sales certificates issued by authorized officers, including those appointed by the NCLT for liquidation sales. The sales certificate should include the description of the property, the power of the seller, the process conducted, and the eligibility of the buyer, among other details. The authorized officer can refuse to get the sale deed registered, as the obligation to register lies with the registration authority.
