## Summary
This part III of the webinar focuses on the insolvency resolution process for individual guarantors, specifically addressing avoidance transactions and the bankruptcy process. The bankruptcy process involves understanding the creditors, their rights, and the restrictions during bankruptcy. The discharge, which releases the debtor from past liabilities, is a crucial aspect of individual insolvency. The repayment plan for secured creditors has two options: forfeiting the right to enforce security interest and participating in the meeting of creditors, or submitting an affidavit to participate and vote only for the unsecured part of the debt. The repayment plan must consider secured and unsecured parts separately. The RP prepares a report on the meeting of creditors, including the approval or rejection of the repayment plan, modifications, voting records, and other relevant information. The approval of the repayment plan by the adjudicating authority is based on the report of the meeting of creditors and can be approved or rejected. If there is no meeting of creditors, the adjudicating authority can pass an order based on the report of the RP. The repayment plan is binding only on the debtor and the creditors mentioned in the plan, unlike the resolution plan in CIRP which is binding on all creditors of the corporate debtor. If the repayment plan is rejected, the debtor and creditor can file a bankruptcy application.
