## Summary
The webinar discusses the recent judicial pronouncement on Section 66 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) and its implications for resolution professionals and liquidators. The speakers explain the provisions of Section 66, which deals with transactions carried on with the intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purposes. They also highlight the differences between Section 66 and Section 49 of the IBC, which deals with avoidance transactions. The speakers emphasize that the interpretation of Section 66 depends on how the transaction is understood by the transactional auditor, reported by the RP, pleaded before the adjudicating authority, and interpreted by the bench. They also discuss various judgments from the NCLAT, NT, and Supreme Court that have clarified the provisions of Section 66. The speakers highlight that the Supreme Court has not yet taken a big call on this issue, and there is only one judgment where the 66 issue has been considered. They also explain the concept of the Twilight Zone in Section 66 subsection 2 and the conditions for passing an order against directors or partners in LLPs. The speakers suggest that combined applications should be filed under Section 66 subsection 1 and 2, and Section 49, if there is an overlap. They also discuss the provisions of Section 67, which deals with the claim of contributories in liquidation. The webinar concludes with a discussion on various judgments related to Section 66, including the mismatch of stock and inventory, and loans and advances given to other entities.
