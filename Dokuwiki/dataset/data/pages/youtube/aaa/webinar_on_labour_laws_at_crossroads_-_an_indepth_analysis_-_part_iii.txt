## Summary
Welcome to the third and final part of the webinar series on labor laws for workmen employees. Today, we will discuss the position of various judgments on handling labor in the IBC and liquidation process, focusing on welfare claims such as gratuity, pension fund, and provident fund. The latest Supreme Court judgment in Sunil Kumar Jain vs. Bhatt clarifies that during CIRP, only workmen and employees working with the RP and whose operations are continued by the RP and COC are entitled to salary or wages. The judgment also discusses the treatment of service claims of employees and workmen during CIRP, stating that claims may be verified to determine if the corporate debtor was a going concern and if the workman employee actually worked during CIRP. The liquidator is responsible for determining how many people worked and whether the company was kept as a going concern. The wages and salaries of workmen who have not worked during CIRP cannot be included in the CIRP cost. The second issue discussed in the judgment is the use of workman employees' provident fund, gratuity, and pension, which shall be governed by section 36 4 of the IBC and are not to be treated as liquidation estate assets. Workmen are entitled to receive their dues from these funds, as per section 36 4 of the IBC.
