## Summary
The webinar focuses on the Mediation Act of 2023 and its importance in the future. The Act, consisting of 11 chapters, 65 sections, and 10 schedules, aims to promote and facilitate mediation, especially institutional mediation, to reduce the burden on courts and enforce mediated settlement agreements. The Act applies to commercial disputes, which include transactions of merchants, buy and selling, bankers, financers, traders, Mercantile documents, export or import of merchandise or services, admiralty and maritime law, transactions related to aircraft engines, carriage of goods, construction and infrastructure contracts, tenders agreements, immovable property, franchising agreements, distribution and licensing agreements, management and consultancy agreements, joint venture agreements, shareholders agreement, subscription and investment agreements, pertaining to the services in Services industry, Mercantile agency, Mercantile usage, partnership agreements, technology development agreements, intellectual property rights, commercial disputes notified by the central government, and other commercial disputes. The Act mandates pre-litigation mediation for commercial disputes before filing a suit, with the exception of cases requiring urgent interim relief. The mediation period is 120 days, extendable by 60 days with the consent of both parties. The mediated settlement agreement will have a judicial stem and will be binding on both parties. The Act also amends the Arbitration and Conciliation Act of 1996, replacing the term 'conciliation' with 'mediation' and construing references to conciliation in other enactments as mediation. The mediation process will be a regulated profession.
