## Summary
This webinar discusses fraudulent and wrongful trading transactions under Section 66 of the IBC. The term 'avoidance transactions' is used to refer to transactions that can be avoided or are avoidable. These transactions are based on the UK solvency act and are covered under sections 43, 45, 49, 50, and 66 of the IBC. The intent to defraud creditors or any fraudulent purposes is important in these transactions. The adjudicating authority can direct any person who was knowingly party to such transactions to contribute to the assets of the corporate debtor, which will go to the creditors in most cases. Section 66 has two parts: subsection one deals with the business of the corporate debtor being carried on with the intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purposes, and subsection two deals with the liability of directors or partners during the insolvency process. The Twilight Zone, a period of trading when the company has insufficient funds to pay its debt and is on the verge of insolvency, is an important concept in Section 66. The webinar also discusses the differences between Section 661 and 662.
