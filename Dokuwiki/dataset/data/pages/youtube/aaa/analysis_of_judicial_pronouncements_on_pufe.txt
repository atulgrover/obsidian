## Summary
In this webinar, the speaker discusses the judicial pronouncements on avoidance transactions, specifically focusing on Section 43 (preferential transactions) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The speaker highlights a Supreme Court judgment, Anu Jan vs. Axis Bank, which outlines five parameters for a transaction to be considered preferential: (1) transfer of property to a creditor, surety, or guarantor, (2) on account of an antecedent financial debt, (3) resulting in a beneficial position for the creditor, (4) not excluded by Section 43's ordinary course of business clause, and (5) within the specified look-back period (two years for related parties, one year for unrelated parties). The speaker also delves into the concept of 'ordinary course of business' and the look-back period's relevance, citing the Downs Distributing Company vs. Associated Blue Star Stones case. Additionally, the speaker touches upon the intent of the corporate debtor, emphasizing that the transaction's voluntary nature or duress is irrelevant once it has occurred. The webinar also references the Din Rayan Udyog Private Limited vs. Puja Bari case, which upheld the notion that the dominant motive of the corporate debtor is not significant in Section 43 cases. The speaker further illustrates various transactions held as Section 43 transactions, such as receiving money from a debtor in a director's personal account or selling a company car at an undervalued price during the insolvency process.
