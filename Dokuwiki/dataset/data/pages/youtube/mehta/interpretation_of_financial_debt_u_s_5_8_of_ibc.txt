## Summary
The webinar discusses the Supreme Court judgment on NOIDA (New Okhla Industrial Development Authority) vs Anand Sanbhadra, delivered on 17th May 2022 by Honorable Justices KM Joseph and Rishikesh Roy. The judgment is a common one for two appeals (Civil Appeal Number 222 of 2021 and Civil Appeal Number 2367-2369 of 2021) that deal with the question of whether NOIDA can be treated as a financial creditor within the meaning of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). The first appeal involves NOIDA's claim as an operational creditor that was later revised to a financial creditor during the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP). The second appeal involves NOIDA's appeal against the NCLAT's stay order on its being treated as a financial creditor by the NCLT. The judgment goes into great detail about the definitions of financial debt, debt, claim, and transaction as per the IBC. The brief facts of the case involve a land parcel owned by NOIDA, which was leased to the corporate debtor for the construction of residential flats. The contention of NOIDA is that they should be treated as a financial creditor, which would give them certain rights and powers under the IBC. The Supreme Court examines the definition of financial debt and holds that disbursement is an indispensable requirement for a debt to be considered a financial debt. In this case, the initial down payment made by the corporate debtor to NOIDA does not constitute a disbursement since it flows from the debtor to the creditor. Therefore, NOIDA cannot be treated as a financial creditor.
