## Summary
The video discusses the liquidation process in terms of insolvency law, focusing on the appointment of liquidators, their fees, powers, and functions. The process begins with an attempt at resolution, where the entity is given a chance to resolve its financial distress. If resolution is not possible, the terminal phase of liquidation is considered. The liquidation process involves the submission of claims by creditors, the admission of claims by the liquidator, and the formation of a liquidation estate. The liquidator then starts liquidating assets, collecting the money, and distributing the proceeds in a waterfall order. The video also discusses the exclusions from the liquidation estate, such as assets of third parties and assets of subsidiaries. The appointment of the liquidator is made by the adjudicating body but is practically decided by the committee of creditors. The fees of the liquidator are settled by the committee of creditors and are based on the timing and magnitude of the realization and distribution of assets.
