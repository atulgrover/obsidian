## Summary
Anil Kohli, an insolvency professional, discusses the contents of a resolution plan and its approval in the context of insolvency. A resolution plan is a document that outlines the diagnosis, treatment, and process for implementing the treatment of a corporate debtor's default. It may include restructuring, management changes, mergers, or cancellations of shares. The resolution plan should aim to maximize the value of the corporate debtor's assets and address the cause of the default. Regulation 37 provides the basics of a resolution plan, including the payment of operational creditors' dues and a statement of how the plan deals with the interests of all stakeholders. The resolution plan should also provide the terms of implementation and a timeline for payments. The management and control of the corporate debtor's business during the implementation term is overseen by a monitoring committee. Regulation 38 provides the mandatory contents of a resolution plan, including feasibility, viability, and effective implementation. Section 32 outlines the submission of the resolution plan, including the requirement for an affidavit and information memorandum. The resolution professional must ensure that the plan provides for the payment of insolvency resolution process costs and operational creditor dues before other debts. The waterfall mechanism in section 53 is incorporated into the resolution process to maintain the priority of creditors.
