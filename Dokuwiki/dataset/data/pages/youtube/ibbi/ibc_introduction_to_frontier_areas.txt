## Summary
Welcome to this session on the frontier areas of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) in India. The session covers three major parts: individual insolvency, cross-border insolvency, and group insolvency. The first part focuses on individual insolvency, which is a frontier area as the IBC is a comprehensive code dealing with insolvency resolution for all kinds of persons, including individuals. The current laws dealing with individual insolvency are the Presidency Towns Insolvency Act, 1909, and the Provincial Insolvency Act, 1920. The IBC provides a modern regime for insolvency resolution of individuals, with an objective trigger for insolvency, a moratorium period, use of qualified resolution professionals, automatic discharge, and a fresh start process. The implementation of individual insolvency will be in phases, with the first phase being the insolvency and bankruptcy of personal guarantors to corporate debtors, which has been implemented from December 1, 2019. The adjudicating authority for individuals can be either the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) or the Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT), depending on the circumstances. Cross-border insolvency is another frontier area, with the IBC providing for bilateral agreements with foreign countries and the issuance of letters of request. However, there are challenges with these existing options, and various committees have recommended adopting a comprehensive cross-border insolvency framework based on the UNCITRAL Model Law.
