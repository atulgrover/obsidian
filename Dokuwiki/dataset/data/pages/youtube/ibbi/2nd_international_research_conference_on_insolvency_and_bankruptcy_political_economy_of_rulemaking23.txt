## Summary
Professor Vikrant is discussing the political economy of financial regulation, spanning research on the complexity of the financial system, the challenge of globalization and organization of large multinational banks, and the externalities of bank failures. He highlights the increasing complexity of financial regulation and the debate over whether complex regulations are driven by the need for stronger policing of complex systems or by incumbents wanting to create barriers to entry. He also discusses the potential gains from competitive federalism and the challenges of harmonizing rules across different jurisdictions. He ends by talking about the political economy of rulemaking and how changing the rules of the game can shift the distribution of surplus.
