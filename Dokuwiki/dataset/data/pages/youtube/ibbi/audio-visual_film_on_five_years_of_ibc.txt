## Summary
The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) of India, celebrating its 5th year of operation, has proven to be a dynamic and efficient law for time-bound insolvency resolution of corporations, partnership firms, and individuals. It aims to maximize asset value, promote entrepreneurship, increase credit availability, and balance stakeholder interests. Despite facing implementation issues and legal challenges, the IBC has protected creditor rights, increased credit flow, and created a distressed asset market. The IBC has realigned the creditor-debtor relationship, enabling market choices, and has been a cornerstone in resolving stressed assets. It has significantly improved India's business environment, recovery of non-performing assets, and job protection. The IBC has been recognized globally for its success, with India's rank improving in the World Bank's ease of doing business and global innovation indices.
