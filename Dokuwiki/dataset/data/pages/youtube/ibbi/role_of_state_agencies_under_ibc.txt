## Summary
The video discusses the role of state agencies under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016. The session is divided into two parts, with Shri Deepak Rao covering the role of state agencies during the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) and the speaker covering the role during the liquidation process. The IBC was enacted in 2016 to resolve insolvency matters in a time-bound manner and maximize the value of assets. The code has primacy over all other laws, including the Income Tax Act of 1961. State agencies are operational creditors under the IBC and need to cooperate with the IRP or RP to resolve the case. The moratorium period starts with the commencement of the CIRP, during which no suits or proceedings can be initiated or continued against the corporate debtor. Government agencies cannot terminate licenses, quotas, permits, or registrations granted to the corporate debtor solely on the grounds of insolvency. Claim submission is important, and non-submission can lead to a loss to the exchequer. Once the resolution plan is approved by NCLT, it is binding on all departments, including central and state government departments and local authorities. During the liquidation process, the submission of claims has to be done, and the liquidator takes control and custody of the assets of the corporate debtor. The duties and powers of the liquidator include inviting claims, verifying them, auctioning the assets, and distributing the sale proceeds in accordance with section 53 of the code. The government has reduced its priority even below the unsecured financial creditors in the waterfall mechanism to give the market a boost. The moratorium period also applies during the liquidation process. The key takeaways for state governments, central governments, and other local authorities include submitting claims in the prescribed time limit, not instituting or continuing any proceedings against the corporate debtor during the resolution or liquidation process, not discontinuing essential services to the corporate debtor, and supporting the IRP, RP, or liquidator in fulfilling their statutory duties.
