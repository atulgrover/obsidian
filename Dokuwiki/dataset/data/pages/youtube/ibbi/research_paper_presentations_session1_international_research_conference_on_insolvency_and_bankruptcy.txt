## Summary
The first paper presentation at the international research conference on insolvency and bankruptcy focuses on assessing behavioral change of creditors under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) in India. The paper argues that IBC should be amended through broader frameworks such as behavioral economics. The speakers establish that IBC is a behavioral policy, and when designed appropriately and enforced efficiently, it can incentivize self-interested economic actors to choose the most efficient outcome. The code has achieved this by subverting the behavioral science principle that loss aversion is twice as powerful as gaining something, and by providing a choice architecture that influences choice by organizing the context in which people make decisions. The speakers also discuss how the change in default threshold led to a significant change in the behavior of creditors and further led to a reduction in filing of petty claims. The presentation also mentions the impact of the Covid-19 pandemic and the subsequent amendment to the IBC that suspended the rights of creditors to initiate CIRP against corporate debtors under section 7, 9, and 10 of the IBC with respect to defaults arising on or after 25th March 2020 for a period of six months or such further notice not exceeding one year from 25th March 2020. The speakers conclude that the inspiration for the work came from the suggestion that a code of conduct for creditors could incentivize insolvency, and the need to measure the behavioral change brought about by IBC.
