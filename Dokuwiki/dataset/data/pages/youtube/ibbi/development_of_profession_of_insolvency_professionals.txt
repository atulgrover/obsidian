## Summary
Rajesh Kumar Gupta, CGM of IBBI, and Namisha Singh, Manager at IBBI, discuss the development of the profession of Insolvency Professionals in a lecture series on Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). Insolvency Professionals are a new kind of profession that emerged with the enactment of the IBC in 2016. They act as the board of directors of a firm under distress and make all decisions on behalf of the firm while ensuring compliance with all laws and regulations. The IBBI has been continuously enhancing the capacity of Insolvency Professionals through capacity building initiatives and continuous professional education programs. Insolvency Professionals are expected to balance the interests of all stakeholders, including creditors, financial creators, operational creditors, employees, tax authorities, and government authorities. They are governed by a two-tier regulatory structure, with IBBI as the principal regulator and Insolvency Professional Agencies as the frontline regulators. To become an Insolvency Professional, one needs to pass a limited insolvency examination conducted by the IBBI and have at least 10 years of experience as a CA, CS, or advocate or 10 years of experience in management with a master's degree or 10 years of experience with a bachelor's law degree or 50 years of experience in a bachelor's degree of management. Insolvency Professionals are required to follow a code of conduct that includes acting with integrity, objectivity, and independence, maintaining confidentiality of information, and charging reasonable remuneration for services provided.
