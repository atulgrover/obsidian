## Summary
Asit Behera, a representative of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India, discusses the Clean Slate principle under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016. He explains how the code aims to restructure loans and reset the liabilities of a company, making it competitive again, similar to the process in the United States. The code addresses the fragmented and delay-prone legal regime for insolvency and debt resolution in India, particularly in the banking sector which has been plagued by non-performing accounts and bad loans. Section 32A, which was introduced in 2019, is discussed in detail. It states that the liability of a corporate debtor for offenses committed prior to the commencement of the corporate insolvency resolution process shall cease, and the corporate debtor shall not be prosecuted from the date the resolution plan is approved by the adjudicating authority. However, certain provisions where this section shall not apply are mentioned. The section provides immunity to the corporate entity, but not to the persons who were designated partners, officers in default, or any other person who is in charge of or responsible for the conduct of its business or associated with the corporate debtor. These persons shall continue to be liable for prosecution and punishment for such offenses committed by the corporate debtor. The section aims to provide incentives to investors, instill a sense of confidence in them, and prevent genuine, honest resolution applicants from being afraid of persecution for offenses committed prior to the corporate insolvency resolution process.
