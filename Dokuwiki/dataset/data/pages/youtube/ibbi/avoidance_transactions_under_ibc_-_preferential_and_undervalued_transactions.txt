## Summary
In this tele lecture, Ashish Makhija discusses avoidance transactions under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), focusing on undervalued and preferential transactions in a Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP). He explains the duties of the Insolvency Resolution Professional (IRP) or Resolution Professional (RP) in identifying and determining such transactions within specific time limits. Avoidance transactions, also known as vulnerable or look-back transactions, occur during a vulnerable period when the company is in financial strain. The genesis of IBC's avoidance transactions can be traced back to the Bankruptcy Law Reforms Committee and the Companies Act of 1956. Preferential transactions, covered under sections 43 and 45, are the responsibility of the RP or liquidator to file an application for avoidance. Undervalued transactions, from sections 45 to 48, can also be reported by creditors, members, or partners if the RP or liquidator fails to do so. The meaning of preferential transactions involves a transfer of property or interest for the benefit of a creditor or guarantor while anticipating financial or operational debt. The effect of such a transfer should put the creditor in a more beneficial position than they would have been in distribution of assets under section 53. Defenses against preferential transactions include ordinary course of business, subsequent new value, and contemporaneous exchange.
