# IU and BlockChain

The source explores the use of blockchain technology in the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) as a means to improve the functioning of Information Utilities (IUs). IUs are intended to provide a centralized repository of financial data to improve transparency and reduce information asymmetry during insolvency proceedings. The author argues that blockchain technology's transparency, immutability, and efficiency make it an ideal foundation for IUs, citing the potential for increased trust and reduced disputes. The author analyzes the current state of IUs in India, highlighting their lack of success, and explores various challenges and potential solutions, including the adoption of blockchain technology. The source also provides an overview of blockchain's global adoption and its potential to revolutionize various industries, including finance, healthcare, and government services.

## **IU and Blockchain: An Unlikely but Holy Partnership**

This briefing document reviews the main themes and important ideas from "IU and Blockchain.pdf," a research paper exploring the potential of blockchain technology to enhance the functioning of Information Utilities (IUs) in India.  
**Key Themes**

* **Information Asymmetry in the Indian Credit Ecosystem**: The paper identifies a significant information asymmetry within the Indian credit ecosystem, hindering the efficient resolution of insolvencies. It highlights that both creditors and debtors often lack access to reliable and verifiable financial data, leading to mistrust and inefficient decision-making.  
* **Information Utilities as a Solution**: The research paper posits that IUs, introduced by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, offer a potential solution. IUs are envisioned as central repositories of financial information about debtors, facilitating information symmetry and transparency.  
* **Challenges Facing Existing IUs**: Despite their potential, the paper argues that existing IUs, like NeSL (National E-Governance Services Limited), face challenges in gaining trust and widespread adoption. Stakeholders, including financial and operational creditors, remain apprehensive about sharing sensitive financial information with an institution largely controlled by banks.  
* **Blockchain as a Potential Enabler**: The research proposes that blockchain technology, with its inherent transparency, immutability, and security, can address the trust deficit and enhance the effectiveness of IUs.

**Important Ideas and Facts**

* **NeSL's Limited Success**: Despite efforts to integrate NeSL within the corporate economy, the paper highlights that it has yet to gain widespread acceptance. The author cites data indicating a limited number of debtor registrations and loan records authenticated on the platform.  
* **Stakeholder Concerns**: Interviews with insolvency professionals reveal a lack of confidence in the current IU system. Concerns about data security, transparency, and potential misuse of information by stakeholders were prevalent.  
* **Blockchain's Advantages**: The paper elaborates on blockchain's key features, including:  
* **Transparency:** All transactions are recorded on a public ledger, accessible to all participants.  
* **Immutability:** Once recorded, data cannot be altered, ensuring data integrity.  
* **Security:** Cryptographic techniques protect the blockchain from unauthorized access and manipulation.  
* **Potential of Blockchain for IUs**: The research argues that implementing blockchain within IUs would create a more trustworthy and efficient system. It would allow:  
* Real-time verification of financial information by counterparties, reducing delays and disputes.  
* Enhanced data security and integrity, addressing stakeholder concerns.  
* Greater transparency, enabling all participants to monitor data storage and usage.

**Supporting Quotes**

* **Information Asymmetry**: "Availability of adequate and reliable information from the prospective borrower is vital for taking decisions in relation to sanctioning of credit."  
* **Blockchain's Impact**: "Blockchain simply put is an accounting book which records transactions after transactions in a manner that no alteration in the original information is possible."  
* **Trust Deficit**: "It is the case of the researcher that the Information Utility though a good concept has failed to generate the requisite trust, confidence and support from the very stakeholders it is meant to cater to thereby threatening the very survival of the Information Utility."  
* **Blockchain as a Solution**: "It is thus the need of the hour to recalibrate the foundational and technological parameters and introduce a more transparent and immutable technique which can restore the balance of trust amongst the stakeholders. It is in this perspective that blockchain technology comes into the picture."

**Call to Action**  
The research strongly advocates for exploring the implementation of blockchain technology within IUs. It suggests conducting pilot studies and addressing potential challenges related to data sovereignty and scalability before full-fledged adoption.  
**Conclusion**  
The paper presents a compelling case for leveraging blockchain technology to address the limitations of existing IUs in India. By enhancing trust, transparency, and efficiency, blockchain can empower IUs to achieve their full potential, contributing significantly to a more robust and equitable credit ecosystem.

## **Timeline of Main Events**

**1962:** First attempt to create a credit registry in India by amending the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934\.  
**1970s:** The Rand Corporation develops the concept of Information Utilities.  
**1972:** The Banking Regulation Act, 1949, is amended to include credit information dissemination.  
**1999:** A working group under the chairmanship of Mr. N. H. Siddiqui explores the possibility of establishing a Credit Information Bureau in India.  
**2000:** CIBIL (Credit Information Bureau of India Limited) is established.  
**2004:** CIBIL begins operations.  
**2005:** Credit Information Companies (CICs) are formally introduced through the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005\.  
**2006:** CIBIL starts commercial operations.  
**2015:** RBI mandates all credit institutions to become members of all CICs and submit current and historical borrower data. The Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest (CERSAI) is established.  
**2016:**

* The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, introduces the concept of Information Utilities (IUs) for the first time in practice.  
* The National E-Governance Services Limited (NeSL) is incorporated, becoming the first IU in India.

**2017:**

* RBI amends the CIC Regulation, 2005, to allow CICs to access data from IUs.  
* NeSL receives backing from the Government of India and state-owned agencies, including RBI, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI).  
* NeSL launches initiatives like the Platform of Distressed Assets (PDA) and executes information sharing agreements and smart contracts with major banks and financial institutions.  
* RBI mandates all financial creditors to submit financial information to an IU.

**2018:** The High Level Task Force Committee headed by Y.M. Deosthalee suggests establishing a Public Credit Registry (PRC).  
**April 2021:** Mr. R Gandhi takes over as head of NeSL.  
**August 20, 2020:** The High Court of Calcutta overturns an NCLT order mandating the filing of IU certificates as proof of debt, revealing a lack of trust in the IU concept.

## **Information Utilities and Blockchain Technology: A Symbiotic Partnership for Financial Transparency**

### **Glossary of Key Terms**

* **Information Utility (IU):** A specialized institution established under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 in India. It serves as a central repository of financial information, primarily concerning credit facilities extended and sought by creditors and debtors respectively.  
* **Blockchain Technology:** A decentralized and distributed digital ledger technology that records transactions across multiple computers in a secure and transparent manner. Each transaction is grouped into a "block" and added to the chain, making it tamper-proof and auditable.  
* **Information Symmetry:** A state where all parties involved in a transaction have equal access to relevant information, enabling informed decision-making.  
* **National E-Governance Services Limited (NeSL):** The first and currently only registered IU in India. It is responsible for collecting, collating, verifying, and disseminating financial information to stakeholders in the insolvency and bankruptcy ecosystem.  
* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016:** A comprehensive legal framework in India governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings for individuals, companies, and partnerships.  
* **Credit Information Companies (CICs):** Entities providing credit information services, similar to credit bureaus, collecting and disseminating credit data on individuals and businesses.  
* **Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest (CERSAI):** A central registry in India responsible for registering security interests created on tangible assets, including equitable mortgages.  
* **Non-Performing Assets (NPAs):** Loans or advances that are in default or are in arrears for a specific period, posing a financial risk to lending institutions.  
* **Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT):** The underlying technology behind blockchain, enabling the decentralized and secure recording of transactions across multiple computers.  
* **Hash Function:** A cryptographic algorithm that converts any input data into a fixed-size string of characters. In blockchain, it is used to create a unique fingerprint for each block, ensuring data integrity.  
* **Node:** A participant in a blockchain network that maintains a copy of the distributed ledger and contributes to the verification and validation of transactions.  
* **Cryptocurrency:** A digital or virtual currency that utilizes cryptography for security and operates independently of a central bank. Bitcoin is the most well-known example.

### **Answer Key**

* An Information Utility acts as a central repository of financial data, primarily concerning credit facilities, with the aim of promoting transparency and facilitating efficient insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings.  
* NeSL faces challenges in gaining stakeholder trust due to concerns about data security, potential bias from bank-dominated shareholding, and the perceived lack of transparency in its operations.  
* Information asymmetry leads to an uneven playing field, where one party holds more information than the other, potentially leading to unfair lending practices, mispricing of risk, and financial instability.  
* The dominance of banks as shareholders in NeSL raises concerns about potential bias in favor of financial creditors, potentially deterring corporate debtors and operational creditors from utilizing the platform.  
* Data integrity in NeSL encompasses system security, information security, and secure data storage mechanisms, ensuring the accuracy, confidentiality, and protection of sensitive financial information.  
* A block in a blockchain is a collection of validated transactions added to the chain in a chronological order. Each block is cryptographically linked to the previous one, creating a tamper-proof and auditable record.  
* Public blockchains are open and permissionless, allowing anyone to participate, while private blockchains restrict access to a specific group of authorized participants, often used for enterprise solutions.  
* A hash function is a one-way algorithm that transforms input data into a unique and fixed-size string of characters. Any alteration in the input data will result in a different hash value, ensuring data integrity and immutability.  
* Blockchain's decentralized and tamper-proof nature, combined with transparency and auditability features, can address the trust deficit by providing all participants with equal access to verifiable information.  
* Blockchain integration can enhance transparency by providing a shared and auditable ledger, increase efficiency through automated verification processes, and bolster data security through cryptographic protection.

### **FAQ: Information Utilities and Blockchain in India**

**1\. What are Information Utilities (IUs) in the context of the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016?**  
Information Utilities (IUs) are institutions registered under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 in India. They serve as central repositories of financial information related to debtors, especially concerning credit facilities extended and sought. This information is crucial for stakeholders involved in insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings, fostering transparency and informed decision-making.  
**2\. What is the significance of information symmetry, and how do IUs promote it?**  
Information symmetry refers to a situation where all parties involved in a transaction or process have equal access to relevant information. IUs play a crucial role in achieving information symmetry in the credit ecosystem. By providing a platform where creditors and debtors can submit and verify financial data, IUs reduce information gaps and asymmetries. This fosters trust and fairness, leading to more efficient insolvency proceedings.  
**3\. What is NeSL, and what role does it play in India's insolvency framework?**  
NeSL (National E-Governance Services Limited) is currently the only registered IU in India. Established in 2016, it functions as a digital repository of financial information and plays a crucial role in facilitating insolvency resolution processes. NeSL collaborates with various stakeholders, including financial institutions, operational creditors, and insolvency professionals, to maintain a comprehensive and reliable database.  
**4\. What are some of the challenges faced by NeSL in its operation?**  
Despite its potential, NeSL faces challenges in gaining widespread acceptance and trust among stakeholders. Some concerns include the dominant shareholding of banks in NeSL, leading to apprehension among corporate debtors. Additionally, the perceived lack of transparency in data access and limited user engagement pose hurdles to NeSL's effectiveness.  
**5\. How can blockchain technology address the challenges encountered by IUs like NeSL?**  
Blockchain technology can potentially revolutionize the functioning of IUs by enhancing transparency, security, and trust. Its decentralized nature, immutability, and cryptographic security features can address concerns related to data manipulation, unauthorized access, and information asymmetry.  
**6\. What are the specific benefits of integrating blockchain into IU operations?**  
Integrating blockchain can streamline various IU processes, such as:

* **Faster Verification**: Real-time data updates and automated verification can significantly expedite the debt verification process, reducing delays in insolvency proceedings.  
* **Enhanced Transparency**: All stakeholders can access a shared, immutable ledger, promoting trust and accountability.  
* **Improved Data Security**: Blockchain's cryptographic security measures safeguard against data breaches and manipulation.

**7\. What are some global perspectives and initiatives concerning the use of blockchain in governance and finance?**  
Organizations like the OECD, World Bank, and Niti Aayog (India's policy think tank) are actively exploring the potential of blockchain technology in governance and finance. Pilot projects are being conducted globally to assess the feasibility and impact of blockchain in areas like land registry, supply chain management, and aid disbursement.  
**8\. How can the implementation of blockchain in IUs benefit India's corporate economy?**  
By leveraging blockchain's capabilities, IUs can contribute to a more robust and efficient corporate credit ecosystem in India. This can lead to:

* **Reduced Trust Deficit**: Enhanced transparency and security can foster greater trust among stakeholders.  
* **Efficient Insolvency Resolution**: Streamlined processes and improved data accuracy can expedite insolvency proceedings, minimizing losses and facilitating faster economic recovery.  
* **Strengthened Financial Discipline**: Increased accountability and transparency can encourage both lenders and borrowers to exercise greater financial prudence.
