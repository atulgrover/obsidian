# Resolving Airline Insolvencies

This research paper examines the impact of the Cape Town Convention (CTC) and its Aircraft Protocol on airline insolvencies in India, particularly concerning the right of lessors to repossess aircraft during insolvency proceedings. The paper argues that granting lessors unrestricted repossession rights could hinder airline revival efforts, potentially harming other stakeholders like banks, employees, and passengers. The authors propose modifications to the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) to create a more balanced approach, ensuring that lessors are protected while allowing distressed airlines a chance to recover. They suggest that lessors should be given representation and voting rights in the Committee of Creditors, have their claims prioritized in liquidation, and be subject to an interim moratorium. Additionally, the authors advocate for extending the pre-packaged insolvency resolution process to airlines and establishing a cross-border insolvency framework to address the complexities of global airline operations.

**Glossary of Key Terms**

* **IBC:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 \- India's primary legislation for resolving insolvencies of corporations and individuals.  
* **CIRP:** Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process \- A formal process under the IBC for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor.  
* **Moratorium:** A legal stay imposed by the court during the CIRP, preventing creditors from taking any action to recover their dues from the corporate debtor.  
* **IDERA:** Irrevocable De-registration and Export Request Authorisation \- A legal instrument granting the holder the right to deregister and export an aircraft upon default by the lessee.  
* **Aircraft Protocol:** Protocol to the Convention on International Interests in Mobile Equipment on Matters Specific to Aircraft Equipment \- A treaty facilitating the financing and leasing of aircraft by providing a standardized international legal framework for enforcing creditor rights.  
* **CTC:** Convention on International Interests in Mobile Equipment \- A treaty establishing a legal framework for secured transactions involving high-value mobile equipment, including aircraft.  
* **DGCA:** Directorate General of Civil Aviation \- India's regulatory body for civil aviation, responsible for enforcing aircraft safety regulations and managing aircraft registration.  
* **NCLT:** National Company Law Tribunal \- A quasi-judicial body in India that adjudicates matters relating to company law, including insolvency cases.  
* **Resolution Professional (RP):** An insolvency professional appointed by the NCLT to manage the CIRP of a corporate debtor.  
* **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee formed by the financial creditors of a corporate debtor to make key decisions during the CIRP, including approving the resolution plan.  
* **Resolution Plan:** A plan submitted by a prospective resolution applicant outlining how they will restructure the corporate debtor and repay its creditors.  
* **Pre-packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (Pre-pack):** A hybrid insolvency resolution mechanism that allows a corporate debtor to negotiate a resolution plan with its creditors before initiating formal insolvency proceedings.  
* **Aviation Bill:** Protection and Enforcement of Interests in Aircraft Objects Bill, 2022 \- A proposed legislation in India aimed at implementing the CTC and Aircraft Protocol and creating a dedicated legal framework for aircraft financing and leasing.  
* **"Sector Agnostic":** A term referring to legislation that applies universally to all sectors of the economy, without any specific exemptions or carve-outs for particular industries.  
* **Sui Generis:** A Latin term meaning "of its own kind," often used to describe something unique or exceptional. In the context of insolvency law, it refers to a legal framework specifically tailored to the particular characteristics of a particular industry.  
* **Retention Obligations:** Obligations imposed on the resolution professional or the corporate debtor to maintain and preserve the leased aircraft during the CIRP.

# **FAQ: Resolving Airline Insolvencies in India**

## **1\. What is the significance of the airline sector to the Indian economy?**

The airline sector plays a crucial role in India's economy, supporting millions of jobs and contributing significantly to the country's GDP. With a rapidly growing passenger base, the industry's potential for expansion is immense. The government has recognized this importance and has been actively investing in infrastructure development and policy reforms to facilitate growth.

## **2\. How does aircraft leasing impact the Indian aviation sector?**

Aircraft leasing has become a prevalent method of financing aircraft acquisition in India due to its advantages, such as reduced upfront costs and flexibility for airlines. However, the heavy reliance on foreign lessors raises concerns about potential vulnerabilities, especially during insolvency proceedings.

## **3\. What is the Cape Town Convention (CTC), and how does it affect aircraft repossession?**

The CTC is an international treaty aimed at harmonizing laws related to secured transactions involving high-value mobile equipment, including aircraft. It provides a legal framework for creditors, including lessors, to repossess their assets in case of debtor default, including during insolvency.

## **4\. How has India incorporated the CTC into its legal framework?**

India has ratified the CTC and its Aircraft Protocol and has taken steps to align its domestic laws with the treaty's provisions. Notably, the Aircraft Rules were amended to facilitate the de-registration and export of aircraft by lessors holding an Irrevocable De-registration and Export Request Authorization (IDERA).

## **5\. What conflicts arise between the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) and Aircraft Laws during airline insolvency?**

The IBC, India's primary insolvency law, imposes a moratorium upon commencement of insolvency proceedings, prohibiting creditors from recovering assets in the debtor's possession. This conflicts with the rights granted to aircraft lessors under the CTC to repossess their aircraft using an IDERA.

## **6\. How has the Indian government addressed this conflict?**

The government issued a notification exempting aircraft lease agreements from the IBC's moratorium, allowing lessors to repossess aircraft during insolvency. Additionally, the proposed Aviation Bill reinforces the primacy of Aircraft Laws over the IBC in such situations.

## **7\. What are the implications of prioritizing Aircraft Laws over the IBC for the airline sector?**

Prioritizing Aircraft Laws may lead to quicker aircraft repossession, potentially impacting the chances of a successful resolution for distressed airlines. It raises concerns about balancing the interests of lessors with those of other stakeholders, including employees, passengers, and the overall aviation industry.

## **8\. What potential solutions can help achieve a balanced approach?**

Several solutions can be considered, such as:

* **Granting airlines interim moratorium benefits:** Protect airline assets from repossession immediately upon filing for insolvency.  
* **Utilizing pre-packaged insolvency resolution:** Facilitate quicker and more amicable resolutions with creditor involvement.  
* **Preserving aircraft and fleet:** Explore options to retain aircraft with the airline during insolvency proceedings.  
* **Enacting a cross-border insolvency framework:** Address the complexities of international airline operations during insolvency.  
* **Giving lessors greater say in the resolution process:** Offer lessors a more prominent role in exchange for delaying repossession, aiming for a collaborative approach to airline revival.
