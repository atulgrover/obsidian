# Increasing Resolutions

The provided text argues that the primary objective of India's Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) should be **resolution**—reviving failing companies to maintain their operations—rather than solely focusing on **recovery** for creditors. The author critiques the current approach, which prioritizes high upfront payments and leads to excessive litigation, hindering timely resolution. He emphasizes the need for a more balanced approach that considers the interests of all stakeholders, including employees and the public, while recognizing the crucial role of **speed** in maximizing value and achieving successful outcomes. The author also highlights the challenges posed by judicial delays and advocates for strengthening the infrastructure and capacity of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) to facilitate swifter and more efficient resolution processes.

* **Focus on time-bound resolutions:** Quoting Dr. Sahoo again, the article notes that the enterprise value of a firm diminishes rapidly during prolonged insolvency proceedings. Timely resolution is crucial to preserve value and attract potential resolution applicants.  
* **Balancing interests:** While the CoC (Committee of Creditors) plays a crucial role, the article emphasizes the need to consider all stakeholders, including employees and workmen, whose livelihoods depend on the outcome of CIRP.  
* **Avoiding the perfect deal fallacy:** The author acknowledges the need for procedural integrity and safeguards against fraudulent resolution plans. However, he cautions against seeking the "perfect deal" and suggests that a timely compromise is often preferable to protracted negotiations that could lead to liquidation.

The article advocates for a paradigm shift in the way IBC is perceived and implemented. **Glossary of Key Terms**

* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC):** A comprehensive legislation in India that governs the resolution of insolvency for corporations, partnerships, and individuals.  
* **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A time-bound process under the IBC aimed at reviving financially distressed companies by restructuring their debts and operations.  
* **Resolution Plan:** A proposal submitted by a potential investor or existing management to revive a financially distressed company under the CIRP.  
* **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A group of financial creditors who have a significant stake in the insolvent company and hold the decision-making power during the CIRP.  
* **Liquidation Value:** The estimated value of a company's assets if they were to be sold off in a liquidation scenario.  
* **Resolution Applicant:** An entity that submits a resolution plan to the CoC proposing to take over the management and operations of the insolvent company.  
* **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT):** A specialized tribunal in India that adjudicates matters related to company law, including insolvency cases.  
* **National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT):** An appellate tribunal that hears appeals against orders passed by the NCLT.  
* **Haircut:** The reduction in the amount owed to creditors as part of a debt restructuring or resolution plan.  
* **Going Concern:** A business that is assumed to be operating in the foreseeable future.

## **Timeline of Events**

This timeline focuses on the evolution of the IBC and significant legal cases that shaped its interpretation:  
**2009:**

* **Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy:** This event, detailed in Andrew Ross Sorkin's book "Too Big To Fail", highlights the importance of swift resolutions in insolvency cases, even if they involve compromises.

**2016:**

* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) Operationalized:** This new legal regime in India aims to facilitate corporate insolvency resolution and prioritize rescuing businesses over liquidation.

**2018:**

* **Binani Industries Case:** The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) emphasizes the 'resolution' goal of IBC, prioritizing it over maximizing value for any specific stakeholder group.

**2019:**

* **Swiss Ribbons Case:** The Supreme Court underscores that the IBC aims to revive and continue struggling businesses, preventing "corporate death by liquidation."  
* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act:** Amendments clarify the binding effect of approved resolution plans on government authorities, including for tax dues.

**2021:**

* **Ghanashyam Mishra v. Edelweiss:** The Supreme Court rules that government dues, including taxes, not included in the resolution plan are extinguished after the resolution process concludes.  
* **Report of the Working Group on Tracking Outcomes under the IBC:** This report highlights the positive economic impact of the IBC, including the efficient reallocation of resources and the rescue of viable businesses.

**2022:**

* **Invent Asset v. GirnarFibres:** The Supreme Court reiterates that the IBC is not primarily a debt recovery mechanism but a law designed to revive struggling companies.  
* **Asset Reconstruction Company (India) Limited v. Tulip Star Hotels Limited & Ors:** The Supreme Court reaffirms that the IBC aims to revive companies unable to pay their debts, not simply liquidate them.

**August 2023:**

* **"Report of Study on Effectiveness of the Resolution Process" by IIM Ahmedabad Professors:** The study concludes that companies resolved under IBC show improved performance, contributing to job creation, capital investments, and efficient resource utilization.

**2024:**

* **Standing Committee on Finance Report on IBC Implementation:** The report expresses concern over the significant backlog of IBC cases pending with the NCLT, attributing 71% of the delays to cases pending for over 180 days.  
* **IBBI Newsletter, Jan-Mar 2024:** Data presented shows a direct correlation between the length of the CIRP and the recovery rate for creditors, with faster resolutions leading to higher recovery.  
* **IBBI Issues New Guidelines for Committee of Creditors:** These guidelines aim to enhance transparency, coordination, and time-bound decision-making by the CoC during the CIRP, contributing to more successful resolutions.

# **FAQ: Increasing Resolutions under the IBC**

**1\. What is the primary goal of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**  
The primary goal of the IBC is the **resolution** of insolvency, not simply the recovery of debts. This means focusing on reviving and continuing the operations of the corporate debtor (CD), preventing its liquidation and maximizing the value of its assets. Resolution aims to balance the interests of all stakeholders, including creditors, employees, and the economy.  
**2\. Why is focusing solely on recovery rates a flawed approach when evaluating the IBC's effectiveness?**  
A recovery-centric approach overlooks the broader objective of preserving viable businesses. While maximizing recovery for creditors is important, the emphasis should be on finding resolutions that allow the CD to continue operating. This benefits employees, preserves economic activity, and often leads to better long-term outcomes for all stakeholders.  
**3\. What are some of the main causes of delays in the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**  
Key factors contributing to delays include:

* Inadequate capacity of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).  
* Difficulties in marketing distressed assets.  
* Non-cooperation from corporate debtors.  
* Poor documentation practices within companies.

**4\. How do delays in CIRP negatively impact the resolution process?**  
Delays erode the value of the distressed CD, making it less attractive to potential resolution applicants. This can lead to:

* Reduced chances of successful resolution.  
* Lower recovery rates for creditors.  
* Increased likelihood of liquidation.  
* Job losses and negative economic consequences.

**5\. What role do creditors play in achieving successful resolutions under the IBC?**  
Creditors play a crucial role in CIRP as they form the Committee of Creditors (CoC) which has the power to approve or reject resolution plans. Their focus should be on assessing the viability and long-term sustainability of the resolution plan, not just the upfront payment offered.  
**6\. How can the IBC be strengthened to further promote resolutions?**  
Several measures can be taken to enhance resolution outcomes:

* Addressing the capacity constraints at the NCLT and NCLAT to expedite the judicial process.  
* Streamlining the CIRP process to minimize procedural delays.  
* Encouraging greater creditor participation and fostering a resolution-oriented mindset.

**7\. Is it ever preferable to liquidate a company rather than pursue resolution?**  
Yes, liquidation can be a better outcome in certain situations. If the CD's business model is fundamentally flawed, if its assets hold little value, or if there is no realistic prospect of revival, liquidation may be the most efficient and pragmatic option.  
**8\. How does the IBC benefit the overall economy?**  
By providing a robust framework for resolving insolvency, the IBC promotes:

* Efficient allocation of capital by freeing up resources trapped in non-viable businesses.  
* Entrepreneurship by allowing businesses to exit and restart.  
* Availability of credit by instilling greater confidence in lenders.  
* Job creation and economic growth through the revival of viable companies.
