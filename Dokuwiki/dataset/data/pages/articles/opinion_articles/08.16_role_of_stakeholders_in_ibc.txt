**Source:** "A Reflection on the Role & Effectiveness of Stakeholders Under the IBC Framework in India" by Sunil Mehta  
**Introduction:**  
This briefing doc analyzes the roles and responsibilities of key stakeholders within the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) framework, highlighting their contributions and areas for improvement.  
**Main Themes:**

* **Maximizing Asset Value:** The core objective of the IBC is to maximize the value of assets of the Corporate Debtor (CD) while balancing stakeholder interests. This involves timely resolution, efficient asset utilization, and equitable distribution of proceeds.  
* **Collaborative Ecosystem:** The IBC fosters a collaborative ecosystem with various stakeholders – Corporate Debtors, Financial Creditors (FCs), Operational Creditors (OCs), Resolution Professionals (RPs), investors, and regulatory bodies – working together to achieve resolution.  
* **Transparency and Accountability:** The IBC emphasizes transparency throughout the resolution process, requiring timely information disclosure, adherence to timelines, and standardized procedures for claim verification and approval.

**Key Stakeholder Roles and Responsibilities:**  
**1\. Corporate Debtor (CD):**

* Cooperate with the RP and provide necessary information.  
* Comply with NCLT decisions.  
* Maintain financial transparency.  
* Participate in Committee of Creditors (CoC) meetings.  
* Submit viable resolution plans.  
* **Quote:** "By fulfilling their responsibilities diligently and proactively participating in the resolution process, CDs contribute to the effectiveness and success of insolvency proceedings, ultimately facilitating the revival and rehabilitation of distressed businesses."

**2\. Financial Creditors (FCs):**

* Initiate insolvency proceedings against defaulting CDs.  
* Form the CoC and make critical decisions.  
* Vote on resolution plans.  
* Oversee insolvency proceedings.  
* Determine the distribution of proceeds.  
* **Quote:** "The Code casts on FC a responsibility to facilitate the resolution of the stress rather than merely looking at the Code for recovery of its dues."

**3\. Operational Creditors (OCs):**

* File claims and participate in CoC meetings.  
* Advocate for fair treatment and equitable distribution.  
* Ensure operational continuity of the CD.  
* **Quote:** "OCs also have a responsibility of striving towards resolution of the stress in CD in the interest of all other stakeholders thus rising above the narrow concerns limited to them."

**4\. Resolution Professional (RP):**

* Manage the affairs of the CD.  
* Verify and admit claims.  
* Facilitate the insolvency resolution process.  
* Comply with IBC timelines and requirements.  
* Negotiate and implement resolution plans.  
* **Quote:** "RPs serve as catalysts for change and transformation in the insolvency resolution landscape, leveraging their expertise, impartiality, and commitment to drive the resolution of financial distress and promote the rehabilitation of distressed businesses."

**5\. Committee of Creditors (CoC):**

* Approve resolution plans.  
* Protect creditor interests.  
* Negotiate and build consensus.  
* Oversee and monitor the insolvency resolution process.  
* Determine the distribution of proceeds.  
* **Quote:** "The CoC stands as a cornerstone of the insolvency resolution architecture under the IBC, embodying collaboration, accountability, and economic prudence in driving the resolution of financial distress."

**6\. Investors and Resolution Applicants:**

* Inject capital, expertise, and resources to revive distressed companies.  
* Develop and implement turnaround strategies.  
* Maximize value and creditor recovery.  
* **Quote:** "By leveraging their resources, knowledge, and commitment to value creation, these stakeholders can keep the entity as a successful going concern."

**7\. Adjudicating Authority/Appellate Authority (NCLT/NCLAT):**

* Adjudicate insolvency petitions.  
* Supervise insolvency proceedings.  
* Approve resolution plans.  
* Resolve disputes and adjudicate legal challenges.  
* Monitor and enforce resolution plans.  
* **Quote:** "By strict adherence to the law of the land, ensuring procedural fairness, and promoting the effective administration of insolvency resolution processes, the NCLT / NCLAT contributes to the integrity, efficiency, and fairness of the insolvency resolution framework."

**8\. Regulatory Authorities (IBBI, RBI):**

* Formulate policies and guidelines for insolvency resolution.  
* Supervise and monitor insolvency proceedings.  
* Enforce compliance and take disciplinary action.  
* Educate stakeholders and raise awareness.  
* Provide guidance and support to other stakeholders.  
* **Quote:** "By setting standards, monitoring compliance, and providing guidance to stakeholders, regulatory authorities contribute to the stability, transparency, and credibility of insolvency proceedings."

**9\. Information Utilities (IUs):**

* Facilitate information sharing among stakeholders.  
* Ensure efficient management of bankruptcy procedures.  
* Enhance transparency and accountability.  
* **Quote:** "IUs play a very significant role in enhancing transparency, accountability, and effectiveness in insolvency procedures governed by the IBC."

**10\. Indian Banks' Association (IBA):**

* Advocate for policy changes and industry engagement.  
* Conduct capacity building and training initiatives.  
* Facilitate information sharing and best practice dissemination.  
* Drive technology adoption and digital transformation.  
* **Quote:** "The IBA plays a significant role in fortifying the insolvency resolution framework under the Code, leveraging its expertise, influence, and collaborative efforts to enhance the insolvency regime’s effectiveness, efficiency, and resilience."

**Conclusion:**  
The IBC has significantly impacted the insolvency landscape in India. Its success depends on the active participation and collaboration of all stakeholders, each playing a crucial role in maximizing value for all parties involved. By understanding and fulfilling their responsibilities effectively, stakeholders contribute to a more efficient, transparent, and robust insolvency resolution framework in India, ultimately fostering economic revival and financial stability.  
**Areas for Improvement:**

* Addressing concerns regarding inadequate manpower and infrastructure at NCLT/NCLAT to reduce delays.  
* Encouraging specialization of RPs to handle sector-specific IBC cases.  
* Promoting voluntary adoption of the Code of Conduct for the CoC.  
* Encouraging the establishment of multiple IUs to handle the volume of stressed assets in India.

## **Timeline of the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)**

**2016:**

* **May 2016:** The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) is enacted.

**Subsequent Years:**

* **Introduction of Section 29A:** This amendment bars defaulting promoters from re-acquiring their companies during the resolution process.  
* **Homebuyers Recognized as Financial Creditors:** Homebuyers gain enhanced rights in insolvency proceedings, improving their chances of recovering investments.  
* **Insolvency Resolution for Financial Service Providers:** The IBC expands to cover financial service providers, further stabilizing the financial sector.  
* **Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process:** This streamlined process offers a quicker and more efficient path for certain types of insolvency cases.  
* **May 2023:** Over 25,565 corporate insolvency resolution processes, involving a default amount of ₹8.23 lakh crore, are resolved before formal admission to the IBC process, demonstrating the code's influence on proactive financial management.

**Ongoing:**

* **Continuous Evolution of the IBC:** The government and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) work to adapt the code to new challenges and evolving financial landscapes.  
* **Focus on Stakeholder Effectiveness:** The emphasis shifts towards understanding and enhancing the roles of various stakeholders to ensure successful outcomes aligned with the IBC's objectives.

# **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) in India: Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1\. What is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), enacted in 2016, is a comprehensive legal framework in India designed to resolve insolvencies in a time-bound and efficient manner. The IBC aims to maximize the value of the assets of the distressed company (Corporate Debtor or CD) while balancing the interests of all stakeholders involved.

### **2\. What are the key objectives of the IBC?**

The primary objectives of the IBC are:

* **Rehabilitation of the Corporate Debtor (CD):** The IBC prioritizes the revival and continuation of the CD's business operations as a going concern whenever possible.  
* **Maximization of Value:** The Code aims to ensure that creditors recover the maximum possible value from the CD's assets through a transparent and fair resolution process.  
* **Time-Bound Resolution:** The IBC mandates a strict timeline for resolving insolvency cases to prevent protracted legal battles and minimize value erosion.  
* **Promoting Entrepreneurship:** By providing a predictable and efficient framework for dealing with business failures, the IBC encourages entrepreneurship and risk-taking.

### **3\. Who are the key stakeholders in the IBC ecosystem?**

The IBC ecosystem involves a wide range of stakeholders, each playing a distinct role:

* **Corporate Debtor (CD):** The company undergoing insolvency proceedings.  
* **Financial Creditors (FCs):** Entities to whom the CD owes financial debts, such as banks and financial institutions.  
* **Operational Creditors (OCs):** Entities that provide goods or services to the CD.  
* **Resolution Professional (RP):** A licensed insolvency practitioner appointed to manage the CD's affairs during insolvency proceedings.  
* **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee formed by FCs to make key decisions regarding the insolvency resolution process.  
* **Investors and Resolution Applicants:** Entities that submit resolution plans to revive the CD.  
* **Adjudicating Authority (AA):** The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) that oversees and adjudicates insolvency proceedings.  
* **Appellate Authority:** The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) that handles appeals arising from NCLT decisions.  
* **Regulatory Authorities:** Bodies like the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), which set guidelines and oversee the IBC framework.  
* **Information Utilities (IUs):** Entities that maintain and share financial information about debtors, promoting transparency in insolvency proceedings.

### **4\. What is the role of the Committee of Creditors (CoC)?**

The CoC plays a central role in the IBC process:

* **Decision-Making Authority:** The CoC holds the ultimate decision-making power regarding the insolvency resolution process.  
* **Appointment of RP:** The CoC appoints the RP to manage the CD's affairs.  
* **Evaluation of Resolution Plans:** The CoC evaluates and votes on resolution plans submitted by potential investors.  
* **Approval of Resolution Plan:** The CoC's approval is essential for the implementation of a resolution plan.

### **5\. What is a Resolution Plan, and how is it approved?**

A resolution plan is a proposal outlining the strategy to revive the distressed company (CD). It includes details on debt restructuring, operational changes, and potential investments. A resolution plan is approved through the following steps:

* **Submission:** Resolution applicants submit their plans to the RP.  
* **Evaluation:** The RP examines the plans for feasibility and compliance.  
* **CoC Voting:** The CoC votes on the proposed plans.  
* **NCLT Approval:** If the CoC approves a plan, it is submitted to the NCLT for final approval.

### **6\. What is the role of Information Utilities (IUs) in the IBC process?**

Information Utilities (IUs) address information asymmetry in insolvency proceedings:

* **Centralized Information Repository:** IUs maintain a database of financial information about debtors.  
* **Facilitate Transparency:** This information is accessible to stakeholders, promoting transparency in insolvency proceedings.  
* **Expedite Resolution:** The readily available information helps expedite the resolution process.

### **7\. How does the Indian Banks' Association (IBA) contribute to the IBC framework?**

The IBA, as the representative body of banks, plays a crucial role in strengthening the IBC:

* **Policy Advocacy:** The IBA advocates for policy changes and improvements to the IBC framework.  
* **Capacity Building:** The IBA conducts training programs to enhance banks' understanding and handling of IBC cases.  
* **Information Sharing:** The IBA facilitates the sharing of best practices and knowledge among its member banks.

### **8\. What are some of the key challenges and areas for improvement in the IBC framework?**

While the IBC has significantly improved insolvency resolution in India, challenges remain:

* **Timely Resolution:** While the IBC mandates a time-bound process, delays can still occur, impacting value maximization.  
* **Infrastructure and Capacity:** The NCLT and NCLAT face a large volume of cases, leading to delays. Strengthening the infrastructure and capacity of these institutions is essential.  
* **Awareness and Understanding:** There is a need for continuous awareness campaigns to enhance the understanding of the IBC among all stakeholders.  
* **Balancing Stakeholder Interests:** The IBC aims to balance the interests of all stakeholders. Continuous refinements to the framework are needed to ensure fairness and effectiveness.
