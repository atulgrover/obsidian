# **Frequently Asked Questions: Valuation Under the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code**

## **1\. What is the role of valuation in the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**

Valuation plays a pivotal role in the IBC, driving crucial decisions throughout the insolvency resolution process. It aids the Committee of Creditors (CoC) in:

* Assessing the viability of the distressed company as a going concern.  
* Evaluating the feasibility of proposed resolution plans.  
* Determining whether to pursue resolution or liquidation.

Valuation also ensures transparency, fairness, and equitable distribution among stakeholders, especially for operational creditors and dissenting creditors. It helps resolution applicants assess the distressed company’s worth and determine appropriate bid amounts. During liquidation, valuation provides a benchmark for asset sale.

## **2\. How is valuation conducted under the IBC?**

Valuation under the IBC must be performed by Registered Valuers (RVs) regulated under the Companies Act, 2013\. Key aspects of the valuation framework include:

* **Dual Valuation:** Two RVs are appointed to determine both the Fair Value (FV) and Liquidation Value (LV).  
* **Third Valuer:** If the difference between the two valuations exceeds 25%, a third valuer is appointed, and the final value is determined by averaging the two closest estimates.  
* **Physical Verification:** RVs must physically verify the distressed company's assets.  
* **Adherence to Standards:** RVs must adhere to internationally accepted valuation standards and a strict code of conduct.

## **3\. What are the key challenges in the IBC valuation landscape?**

Despite the robust framework, challenges in IBC valuation exist, including:

* **Information Asymmetry:** Valuers often face difficulty accessing complete and accurate information about the distressed company, relying on potentially incomplete data from Insolvency Professionals.  
* **Industry and Asset Specific Nuances:** The current framework may not fully account for unique industry and asset-specific characteristics, potentially leading to inaccurate valuations.  
* **Inconsistencies in Valuation Methodologies:** The absence of specific valuation standards for the Indian IBC framework allows RVs to choose from various international standards, leading to potential discrepancies in outcomes.  
* **Independence and Objectivity of Valuers:** Concerns regarding potential conflicts of interest when valuers have prior associations with stakeholders may compromise the objectivity of valuations.

## **4\. What are some emerging trends influencing IBC valuations?**

Several emerging trends are anticipated to impact IBC valuations:

* **Technology Adoption:** Integrating technologies like data analytics, artificial intelligence, and blockchain can enhance valuation accuracy, transparency, and efficiency.  
* **Cross-Border Insolvency:** As India adopts cross-border insolvency provisions, valuations will require navigating different accounting standards, valuation practices, and country-specific risks across multiple jurisdictions.  
* **Group Insolvency:** Valuations in group insolvencies require careful consideration of inter-connected transactions and asset ownership among group entities.  
* **ESG Considerations:** Growing awareness of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) factors necessitates their incorporation into valuations, including assessing environmental liabilities and the sustainability of business models.

## **5\. What lessons can be learned from international valuation practices?**

Examining international best practices offers valuable insights for improving India’s IBC valuation framework. Key takeaways include:

* **Uniform Valuation Standards:** Adopting uniform standards, as seen in the UK, Singapore, the US, and Australia, reduces discrepancies and enhances reliability.  
* **Technology Integration:** Utilizing technology like automated valuation models (AVMs) and data analytics can significantly improve accuracy, speed, and transparency.  
* **Stakeholder Engagement:** Actively engaging stakeholders ensures transparency and incorporates all relevant information into the valuation process.

## **6\. What recent legal and regulatory developments have strengthened the IBC valuation framework?**

Recent developments have enhanced the IBC valuation framework, including:

* **Disclosure of Fair Value:** The CIRP Regulations now allow for disclosing FV in the Information Memorandum (IM), enhancing transparency for prospective resolution applicants.  
* **Discussion of Valuation Methodology:** RVs are now required to explain their methodologies to the CoC before computing valuations, facilitating early understanding and reducing potential disputes.  
* **Submission of Valuation Report to CoC:** The RP must provide the valuation report, along with FV and LV, to each CoC member, promoting transparency and informed decision-making.  
* **Valuation Report Identification Number (VRIN):** The introduction of VRIN enhances authenticity and traceability of reports, boosting stakeholder confidence.  
* **Simplified Approach for Smaller Companies:** A proposal to use a single valuation for smaller companies and MSMEs aims to reduce costs and streamline the resolution process.

## **7\. What are potential areas for reform and capacity building in the Indian valuation framework?**

Several measures can further strengthen the IBC valuation ecosystem:

* **Standardisation and Best Practices:** Implementing uniform methodologies and templates for valuation reports and developing industry-specific guidelines enhance consistency and accuracy.  
* **Legal and Regulatory Reforms:** Introducing a tiered system of valuation based on case complexity and size can ensure proportionate valuation efforts.  
* **Institutional Capacity Building:** Continuous professional development programs for RVs and promoting research in valuation methodologies can enhance the skillset and expertise of professionals.

## **8\. Why is continuous improvement of the IBC valuation framework crucial?**

The success of the IBC hinges on the robustness of its valuation framework. A strong valuation framework ensures:

* **Maximized Asset Value:** Accurate valuations help realize the full potential value of distressed assets.  
* **Balanced Stakeholder Interests:** Transparent and fair valuations ensure equitable treatment of all stakeholders involved.  
* **Efficient Resolution Process:** A robust framework facilitates a timely and effective resolution process, minimizing delays and costs.

Continuous improvement of the valuation framework is vital for achieving the IBC's objectives and strengthening India’s insolvency resolution regime.

### **Glossary of Key Terms**

**Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016:** Landmark legislation in India aimed at consolidating and amending laws relating to reorganization and insolvency resolution of corporate persons, partnership firms, and individuals.  
**Fair Value (FV):** The estimated realizable value of an asset if exchanged in an arm's-length transaction between a willing buyer and seller on the insolvency commencement date.  
**Liquidation Value (LV):** The estimated realizable value of an asset if the company were liquidated on the insolvency commencement date.  
**Registered Valuer (RV):** A professional registered with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) who is authorized to conduct valuations under the IBC.  
**Insolvency Professional (IP):** A licensed professional responsible for managing the insolvency resolution process, including the appointment of valuers and overseeing the valuation process.  
**Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A committee consisting of financial creditors of the distressed company who play a key role in decision-making during the insolvency resolution process, including the approval of resolution plans.  
**Resolution Plan:** A plan proposed by a resolution applicant outlining the proposed measures for reviving the distressed company. The plan includes details of financial restructuring, operational changes, and the distribution of proceeds to creditors.  
**Information Asymmetry:** A situation where one party in a transaction has more or better information than the other party, potentially leading to an unfair advantage.  
**International Valuation Standards (IVS):** A set of globally recognized standards for valuation, providing guidance on methodologies, ethics, and reporting.  
**Stakeholder Engagement:** The process of actively involving all parties who have an interest in the outcome of a process, ensuring their perspectives and concerns are considered.  
**Institutional Capacity Building:** Efforts aimed at strengthening the skills, knowledge, and resources of institutions to effectively perform their functions. This includes training programs, research initiatives, and the development of infrastructure.

## **Timeline of Main Events in India's Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) Valuation Framework**

**2016:**

* **Enactment of the IBC:** The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) is enacted, marking a significant shift in India's approach to insolvency resolution. The IBC introduces the core objectives of timely resolution and maximizing the value of debtor assets, with valuation playing a crucial role in achieving these objectives.

**2017:**

* **Introduction of Registered Valuers (RVs):** The Companies Act, 2013 is amended to include the concept of ‘Registered Valuers’ (RVs) under section 247 of Chapter XVII. The amendment aims to regulate and formalize the valuation profession, ensuring transparency, accountability, and better governance in valuation exercises.  
* **Designation of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) as the Regulatory Authority:** The Central Government designates the IBBI as the ‘Authority’ under section 458 of the Companies Act, 2013\.  
* **Notification of the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017:** The Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 (Valuation Rules) are notified, establishing a comprehensive framework for the development and regulation of the valuer profession through a two-tier structure involving Registered Valuer Organizations (RVOs) as front-line regulators.

**2018:**

* **Introduction of Fair Value (FV) and Liquidation Value (LV):** The CIRP Regulations are amended to introduce the concepts of FV and LV, providing clear definitions for both. FV is defined as the estimated realizable value of the corporate debtor's assets if exchanged on the insolvency commencement date in an arm's-length transaction between a willing buyer and seller. LV is defined as the estimated realizable value of the assets if the corporate debtor were liquidated on the insolvency commencement date.  
* **Amendments to the Liquidation Regulations:** The requirement for appointing two RVs to value assets is eliminated in the Liquidation Regulations, allowing consideration of valuations conducted during the insolvency resolution process. This change aims to reduce costs and expedite liquidation proceedings.

**2019:**

* **Clarification of Operational Creditors' (OCs) Position:** The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2019 clarifies the position of OCs in the distribution waterfall, leading to more nuanced valuation approaches.

**2020:**

* **Guidelines on Caveats, Limitations, and Disclaimers:** The IBBI issues guidelines providing clear instructions on the use of caveats, limitations, and disclaimers in valuation reports. This aims to address inconsistencies in their application and enhance the credibility of valuation reports.

**2024:**

* **Disclosure of FV in Information Memoranda (IMs):** An amendment to the CIRP Regulations allows for the disclosure of FV in IMs at the discretion of the CoC. This aims to enhance transparency and attract more serious bidders by providing them with equal access to critical information.  
* **Mandatory Discussion of Valuation Methodology with CoC:** An amendment mandates the RP to facilitate a meeting between the RVs and the CoC before the computation of valuation estimates. This allows for early clarification of methodologies, promotes transparency, and reduces potential disputes.  
* **Submission of Valuation Report to CoC:** A new requirement mandates the RP to provide the complete valuation report, along with FV and LV, to each CoC member after receiving resolution plans. This ensures uniform access to valuation data, supports informed decision-making, and helps prevent misuse of information.  
* **Introduction of Valuation Report Identification Number (VRIN):** The IBBI introduces the VRIN, a unique identifier generated by RVs for each valuation report conducted under the IBC. This enhances the authenticity and traceability of reports, standardizes identification, and boosts stakeholder confidence.  
* **Consultation Paper on a Simpler Valuation Approach:** The IBBI issues a consultation paper suggesting a single valuation estimate for smaller corporate debtors and MSMEs. This aims to reduce costs and make the resolution process more accessible and efficient for these entities.
