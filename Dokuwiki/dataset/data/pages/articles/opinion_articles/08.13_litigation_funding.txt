# Litigation Funding

This article discusses the role of **third-party litigation funding (TPLF)** in **insolvency proceedings** in India. The authors argue that TPLF is a valuable tool to maximize returns for creditors by enabling them to pursue **avoidance transactions**, which are legal actions to recover assets transferred by insolvent companies before insolvency proceedings begin. The article examines the legal framework for TPLF in India, highlighting its recognition by courts and the need for legislation to regulate the practice. The authors also provide a global overview of TPLF, emphasizing its acceptance in developed jurisdictions and its potential benefits for both creditors and insolvent entities.

## **Briefing Doc: Litigation Funding in India's Insolvency Regime**

**Main Themes:**

* **Unlocking Value of Unattended Assets:** Insolvent entities often hold significant value trapped in disputed claims and avoidance transactions, which creditors are hesitant to fund for recovery.  
* **Litigation Funding as a Solution:** Third-Party Litigation Funding (TPLF) emerges as a viable solution to bridge the funding gap and maximize creditor returns.  
* **Global Context & Indian Scenario:** While TPLF is well-established in developed economies, India is witnessing its nascent adoption.  
* **Need for Framework & Stakeholder Alignment:** There's a pressing need for a robust framework and stakeholder alignment to facilitate TPLF's effective integration within the Indian insolvency ecosystem.

**Most Important Ideas/Facts:**

* **Unrealised Assets in Insolvency Estates:**  
* Insolvent entities often possess contingent assets like sundry debts, disputed assets, and claims arising from avoidance transactions.  
* These assets are often treated as nil due to uncertainty and lack of funding for recovery, leading to diminished value of the insolvent estate.  
* **Avoidance Transactions & Their Significance:**  
* Avoidance transactions refer to procedures challenging pre-insolvency transactions by a Corporate Debtor (CD) to recover assets and ensure equitable treatment of creditors.  
* They are crucial for maximizing the value of the insolvent estate and discouraging opportunistic behavior by debtors nearing insolvency.  
* However, these applications are complex, time-consuming, and often remain pending even after the resolution or liquidation process concludes.  
* "As per IBBI quarterly newsletter for January-March, 2024, claims of more than 3.1 lakh crore of rupees (₹3.1 trillion) filed as avoidance applications are pending at various National Company Law Tribunals (NCLTs) under the IBC in India."  
* **Litigation Funding: How it Works:**  
* TPLF involves a third-party funder financing litigation in exchange for a share of the proceeds if the case is successful.  
* It operates on a "non-recourse basis," meaning the funder is only repaid from the proceeds of a successful litigation and bears the risk of loss.  
* **Need for Litigation Funding in India:**  
* Due to the complexities and uncertainty surrounding avoidance transactions, creditors are reluctant to fund their pursuit.  
* TPLF can provide the necessary capital and expertise to pursue these applications, increasing the likelihood of successful recovery and maximizing creditor returns.  
* **NRRA as a Glimpse into TPLF:**  
* The concept of Not Readily Realisable Assets (NRRA) in the liquidation process under IBC allows for the assignment of contingent assets, including those underlying avoidance proceedings.  
* However, the uncertainty around NRRA assignment highlights the need for a dedicated framework for litigation funding under IBC.  
* **Challenges and Learnings:**  
* **Information Asymmetry:** Litigation funders require detailed information about the case merits, quantum, recoverability, and litigation costs to assess the viability of funding.  
* **Balancing Stakeholder Interest:** Aligning the interests of creditors, insolvency professionals (IPs), and funders is crucial for TPLF's success.  
* **Capacity Building:** IPs need to be equipped with the knowledge and expertise to navigate TPLF arrangements and make informed decisions in the best interest of creditors.  
* **Key Takeaways & Recommendations:**  
* Standardize information sharing processes and develop templates for avoidance applications to reduce uncertainty and facilitate efficient evaluation by funders.  
* Explore innovative mechanisms like litigation trusts and dedicated RPs for avoidance transactions.  
* Strengthen the role of Adjudicating Authorities (AAs) in reviewing TPLF arrangements to ensure proper use of authority and prevent abuse.  
* Enhance IP capacity through training and guidance on various funding options.  
* Establish dedicated benches to deal with avoidance applications efficiently.

**Conclusion:**  
"Litigation funding in India is not a common practice. However, there exist claims during insolvency which have the potential to maximize the value of the CD. Litigation funding in the IBC framework can play a pivotal role in facilitating the resolution and liquidation of insolvency cases in a time bound manner..."  
By addressing the identified challenges and implementing the recommendations, India can unlock the potential of TPLF to revitalize its insolvency regime and ensure fairer outcomes for all stakeholders.

# **FAQ: Litigation Funding in India's Insolvency Landscape**

## **1\. What is Third-Party Litigation Funding (TPLF) and how does it work?**

TPLF is a financial arrangement where an external entity provides funds to a party involved in a legal dispute in exchange for a share of the potential proceeds if the case is successful. The funder typically covers legal expenses and receives no compensation if the case fails (non-recourse basis).  
**Here's how it works:**

* **Seeking a Funder:** A party involved in a lawsuit approaches a litigation funder.  
* **Due Diligence:** The funder conducts comprehensive due diligence, evaluating the merits of the case, potential recovery, and the litigant's chances of success.  
* **Financing Agreement:** If the case is deemed viable, the funder presents a financing option to the litigant. This may involve funding a single case or a portfolio of cases.  
* **Funding Disbursement:** The funder provides the necessary financial support to cover legal expenses.  
* **Case Outcome:** If the case is successful, the funder receives a pre-agreed share of the proceeds. If the case fails, the funder typically receives no compensation.

## **2\. Why is TPLF relevant to insolvency proceedings in India?**

Insolvent entities often lack the financial resources to pursue legal actions that could potentially recover significant assets, including claims related to avoidance transactions (claw-back actions) aimed at recovering assets transferred prior to insolvency. TPLF can bridge this funding gap and help maximize the value of the insolvent estate for the benefit of creditors.

## **3\. What are the key benefits of implementing TPLF in Indian insolvency cases?**

* **Unlocking Value:** TPLF enables the pursuit of potentially valuable claims that would otherwise remain dormant due to lack of funding.  
* **Enhanced Recovery for Creditors:** By facilitating the recovery of hidden or undervalued assets, TPLF can increase the overall recovery for creditors.  
* **Timely Resolution:** TPLF can help expedite the resolution process by providing the financial resources needed for timely legal action.  
* **Deterrence of Fraudulent Activities:** The prospect of TPLF-backed litigation can act as a deterrent against fraudulent transactions prior to insolvency.

## **4\. What are Not Readily Realizable Assets (NRRA) and how are they connected to TPLF?**

NRRAs are assets that are not easily converted into cash, typically including contingent or disputed assets and those underlying avoidance proceedings. Regulation 37A of the Liquidation Process Regulations allows liquidators to assign or transfer NRRAs, potentially to third-party funders, to realize their value. This provision opens the door for TPLF involvement in insolvency proceedings.

## **5\. What are the key challenges in implementing TPLF within the Indian insolvency framework?**

* **Information Asymmetry:** Litigation funders require comprehensive information about the potential claims to make informed investment decisions. Ensuring transparency and access to relevant data is crucial.  
* **Balancing Stakeholder Interests:** The interests of various stakeholders, including creditors, debtors, and the insolvency professional, need to be carefully considered.  
* **Regulatory Clarity:** Clear guidelines and regulations are needed to govern TPLF arrangements in insolvency proceedings.  
* **Capacity Building:** Insolvency professionals need to be equipped with the knowledge and skills to effectively engage with litigation funders and manage TPLF arrangements.

## **6\. What steps can be taken to facilitate the effective adoption of TPLF in Indian insolvency cases?**

* **Standardized Information Sharing:** Develop standardized formats for capturing and presenting information related to potential claims to litigation funders.  
* **Dedicated Benches:** Establish specialized benches within the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) to handle avoidance transaction applications efficiently.  
* **Transparency and Oversight:** Implement robust mechanisms for transparency and oversight of TPLF arrangements to ensure fairness and accountability.  
* **Capacity Building Initiatives:** Conduct training programs for insolvency professionals on TPLF best practices and risk management.

## **7\. What are the potential roles of different stakeholders in promoting TPLF in India?**

* **Insolvency Professionals:** Play a crucial role in identifying potential claims, conducting due diligence, and engaging with litigation funders.  
* **Creditors:** Can play an active role in supporting TPLF arrangements that align with their interests and enhance recovery prospects.  
* **Regulators:** Should establish clear guidelines and regulations to govern TPLF in insolvency proceedings, ensuring transparency and fairness.  
* **Government:** Can consider establishing an insolvency and bankruptcy fund to support litigation in certain cases.

## **8\. How can TPLF contribute to the overall effectiveness of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**

By unlocking the value of unrealized assets and promoting timely resolution, TPLF can significantly enhance the effectiveness of the IBC. It can contribute to achieving the objectives of maximizing asset recovery, balancing stakeholder interests, and fostering a robust insolvency regime.

## **Timeline of Events**

This timeline focuses on the evolution of Third-Party Litigation Funding (TPLF) with a particular emphasis on its application within insolvency proceedings in India.  
**Mid-1990s:**

* **Australia pioneers TPLF:** The concept of TPLF emerges in Australia, marking the beginning of a global trend.

**1980-2014:**

* **India acknowledges TPLF:** Indian courts recognize the validity of TPLF, setting the stage for its potential integration into insolvency proceedings.  
* **Indian states formally embrace TPLF:** Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, and Uttar Pradesh incorporate TPLF into their legal frameworks through amendments to the Code of Civil Procedure.  
* **Bombay High Court empowers cost recovery:** The court enables the recovery of litigation expenses from third-party financiers, further strengthening the legal foundation for TPLF in India.

**2015:**

* **UK expands TPLF in insolvency:** The Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act broadens the scope of TPLF within insolvency proceedings, specifically allowing the assignment of office holder actions.

**2016:**

* **India enacts the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC):** This landmark legislation aims to streamline insolvency resolution and liquidation processes, with an emphasis on maximizing asset value.

**2016-2023:**

* **India grapples with avoidance transaction challenges:** Delays and uncertainties in adjudicating avoidance transactions hinder creditor recoveries and discourage investment in pursuing these claims.  
* **Discussion arises on funding avoidance transactions:** The Bankruptcy Law Reforms Committee (BLRC) and the Insolvency Law Committee (ILC) highlight the need for funding mechanisms to support the pursuit of avoidance transactions.  
* **Introduction of Not Readily Realisable Assets (NRRA):** Regulation 37A of the Liquidation Process Regulations allows liquidators to assign or transfer NRRA, including assets tied to avoidance proceedings, potentially paving the way for TPLF involvement.

**2022:**

* **ILC recommendations and regulatory updates:** The ILC suggests market-based funding for avoidance transactions and proposes the use of debtor's assets or contributions from creditors. Regulation 38(2)(d) is added to the CIRP Regulations, addressing the handling of PUFE (Preferential, Undervalued, Fraudulent and Extortionate) transaction proceedings post-resolution.  
* **Delhi High Court clarifies the scope of avoidance applications:** The court affirms that proceedings related to PUFE transactions are independent of the CIRP and can continue afterward, reinforcing their importance.

**2023:**

* **Supreme Court limits remedy scope under Section 66 of IBC:** The ruling restricts the availability of remedies against third parties involved in fraudulent trading, directing stakeholders to pursue civil remedies instead.  
* **UK Court of Appeal expands fraudulent transaction claims:** The court broadens the scope of claims under the Insolvency Act 1986, enabling liquidators to pursue third parties involved in fraudulent business activities.  
* **IBBI discussion paper on Regulation 37A:** This paper, released on October 20, 2023, proposes that NRRA can be assigned before the adjudication of avoidance proceedings, potentially facilitating earlier TPLF involvement.  
* **Global Litigation Funding Market Growth:** The market reaches a value of US$ 10,916.3 million in 2018 and is projected to reach US$ 22,373.3 million by 2027, showcasing the increasing global acceptance and adoption of TPLF.

**2024:**

* **Pending avoidance applications represent significant value:** As of January-March 2024, over ₹3.1 trillion worth of claims filed as avoidance applications are pending in Indian NCLTs, emphasizing the substantial potential for recovery through TPLF.

**Present:**

* **India explores TPLF integration within IBC:** Ongoing discussions and analysis focus on leveraging TPLF to address the funding gap for pursuing avoidance transactions, potentially unlocking significant value for creditors.
