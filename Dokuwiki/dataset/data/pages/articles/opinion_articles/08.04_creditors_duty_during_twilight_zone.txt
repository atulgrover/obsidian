# Creditor's duty During Twilight period

## **Briefing Doc: Creditor Duty During the "Twilight Zone"**

**Main Themes:**

* **Shifting Focus from Shareholders to Creditors:** As a company approaches insolvency, the economic interest and risk shift from shareholders to creditors. Consequently, the directors' duty must also shift to prioritize the interests of creditors.  
* **Defining the "Twilight Zone":** This imprecise term refers to the period when a company is on the verge of insolvency but not yet insolvent. Determining when a company enters this zone is crucial for establishing when creditor duty becomes paramount.  
* **Balancing Director Duties:** Directors face a delicate balancing act between their fiduciary duty to the company and its shareholders and their duty to creditors when insolvency looms.  
* **Jurisdictional Approaches:** This document explores the emerging jurisprudence of creditor duty in the UK, Singapore, and India, highlighting similarities and differences in their legal frameworks.

**Key Ideas and Facts:**  
**UK:**

* **Statutory Basis:** The Companies Act 2006 indirectly recognizes creditor duty by requiring directors to consider creditor interests in certain circumstances (Section 172).  
* **Triggering Point:** The Supreme Court in *BTI 2014 LLC v. Sequana SA* clarified that creditor duty is triggered when a company is actually insolvent, nearing insolvency, or when there is a real risk of insolvency.  
* **"The more parlous the state of the company, the more the interests of the creditors will predominate."** (*Bilta (UK) Ltd. v. Nazir*)  
* **Personal Liability:** The Insolvency Act 1986 (Section 213\) imposes personal liability on directors for fraudulent transactions against creditor interests.

**Singapore:**

* **Creditor Duty as Fiduciary Duty:** The Court of Appeal in *Foo Kian Beng v. OP3 International Pte Ltd.* explicitly recognized creditor duty as a fiduciary duty owed to the company.  
* **"Intermediate Zone" and Good Faith:** The court highlighted an "intermediate zone" where directors must reasonably apprehend whether a transaction could lead to imminent insolvency. Acting in good faith is critical in this zone.  
* **Factors to Consider:** The court outlined factors like financial performance, industry prospects, and external developments to assess a company's financial state and potential insolvency.  
* **Fraudulent Trading:** The Insolvency, Restructuring and Dissolution Act 2018 (Section 238\) addresses fraudulent trading and the personal liability of directors involved.

**India:**

* **Indirect Recognition:** While no explicit "creditor duty" exists, the Companies Act 2013 (Section 166\) emphasizes directors acting in good faith for the benefit of all stakeholders, including creditors.  
* **Trust Fund Doctrine:** Indian courts recognize the "trust fund doctrine," prohibiting directors from misusing assets that could prejudice creditor interests.  
* **Look-back Period and Fraudulent Trading:** The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016 (Sections 45 and 66\) allows for scrutiny of past transactions and holds directors liable for fraudulent trading during the twilight period.  
* **Limited Jurisprudence:** Unlike the UK and Singapore, India lacks extensive judicial interpretation on the scope and remedies for breaching creditor duty.

**Conclusion:**  
The concept of creditor duty is gaining recognition globally as a critical safeguard for creditors when a company faces financial distress. While legal frameworks and judicial pronouncements vary across jurisdictions, the underlying principle remains consistent: as insolvency becomes a real threat, directorial decisions must prioritize the interests of creditors to ensure fair treatment and minimize potential losses. Further development of clear legal guidelines and judicial precedents will be essential to solidify the application and enforcement of creditor duty in the twilight zone.

* The "twilight zone" refers to the period when a company is on the verge of insolvency but not yet formally declared insolvent. It's significant because during this time, directors must navigate a complex legal landscape, balancing their duties to shareholders with the emerging paramount duty to protect creditors.  
* Traditionally, directors' duty to the "interest of the company" has been interpreted as primarily serving shareholders. This can be problematic as a company nears insolvency because it prioritizes shareholder interests over the financial well-being of creditors who are at risk of losing their investments.  
* "Creditor duty" arises as a company approaches insolvency, shifting the focus of a director's responsibility from shareholders to creditors. This duty obligates directors to prioritize the interests of creditors to prevent further financial losses and potential fraudulent activity.  
* The UK Supreme Court outlined three situations triggering creditor duty: (i) actual insolvency, both cash flow or balance sheet, (ii) imminent insolvency, and (iii) a real and not remote risk of insolvency.  
* Section 213 of the UK Insolvency Act, 1986 imposes personal liability on directors for fraudulent conduct or transactions that harm creditors. This provision acts as a deterrent against actions that prioritize shareholder interests at the expense of creditors.  
* The Singapore Court of Appeal identified three financial states: (i) solvency, where creditor duty doesn't arise; (ii) the "intermediate zone" of imminent insolvency, where creditor duty emerges and requires careful consideration; and (iii) inevitable insolvency, where creditor duty takes precedence to prevent further harm.  
* The "trust fund doctrine" in India views corporate assets as being held in trust by directors for the benefit of all stakeholders, including creditors. This doctrine reinforces the fiduciary duty of directors to avoid actions that would misappropriate assets or prejudice the interests of creditors.  
* "Fraudulent trading" under Section 66 of the IBC refers to conducting business with the intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purpose. This section allows for holding directors personally liable for transactions during the twilight zone that aim to deceive or harm creditors.  
* The "look-back period" under Section 45 of the IBC allows scrutiny of past transactions conducted by directors. This provision enables holding directors accountable for wrongful actions taken before formal insolvency proceedings, providing recourse for creditors who suffered losses due to such actions.  
* A conflict can arise between a director's duty of care towards creditors and their fiduciary duty to shareholders. In the twilight zone, as a company nears insolvency, creditor duty becomes paramount, requiring directors to prioritize actions that protect creditors even if it might disadvantage shareholders. This legal tightrope necessitates a careful balancing act to ensure responsible and ethical decision-making.

**Glossary of Key Terms:**

* **Twilight Zone:** The period when a company is on the verge of insolvency but not yet formally declared insolvent.  
* **Creditor Duty:** The legal obligation of directors to prioritize the interests of creditors when a company is approaching or in a state of insolvency.  
* **Fiduciary Duty:** The legal and ethical obligation of directors to act in the best interests of the company and its shareholders.  
* **Insolvency:** A state where a company is unable to pay its debts as they become due or where its liabilities exceed its assets.  
* **Fraudulent Trading:** Conducting business with the intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purpose.  
* **Look-Back Period:** A period before the commencement of insolvency proceedings during which past transactions can be scrutinized for potential avoidance.  
* **Trust Fund Doctrine:** The principle that corporate assets are held in trust by directors for the benefit of all stakeholders, including creditors.  
* **Going Concern:** The assumption that a company will continue to operate in the foreseeable future.  
* **Balance Sheet Insolvency:** A situation where the value of a company's assets is less than the total of its liabilities.  
* **Cash Flow Insolvency:** A situation where a company is unable to pay its debts as they become due, even if its assets exceed its liabilities.

# **What is the "twilight zone" in corporate insolvency?**

The "twilight zone" refers to the period when a company is on the verge of insolvency but not yet officially insolvent. This period is characterized by deteriorating financial stability and the imminent threat of insolvency proceedings.

# **What is "creditor duty" and when does it become paramount?**

Creditor duty is the legal obligation of company directors to prioritize the interests of creditors when a company is approaching insolvency. It becomes paramount during the twilight zone when the risk of loss shifts from shareholders to creditors.

# **How does UK law define the triggering point of creditor duty?**

The UK Supreme Court, in the *BTI 2014 LLC v. Sequana SA* case, outlined three situations that trigger creditor duty:

* **Actual insolvency**: When the company can't pay its debts or its liabilities exceed its assets.  
* **Imminent insolvency**: When insolvency is unavoidable in the near future.  
* **Likely insolvency**: When there's a real and not remote risk of insolvency.

# **What is the nature of creditor duty in Singapore?**

The Singapore Court of Appeal, in *Foo Kian Beng v. OP3 International Pte Ltd.*, clarified that creditor duty is a fiduciary duty owed to the company itself. It arises when the company is in a "financially parlous" state, requiring directors to consider the interests of creditors distinctly.

# **How does the concept of "fraudulent trading" relate to creditor duty?**

Fraudulent trading occurs when a company's business is conducted with the intent to defraud creditors. Both UK and Singapore law impose personal liability on directors who engage in fraudulent trading, particularly during the twilight zone.

# **What legal provisions in India address creditor duty?**

While India doesn't explicitly define "creditor duty", relevant provisions like Section 166 of the Companies Act, 2013, emphasize directors' responsibility to act in the best interests of all stakeholders, including creditors. Furthermore, Section 66 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, deals with "fraudulent trading" and holds directors accountable for actions that harm creditors during the twilight zone.

# **What is the "trust fund doctrine" in the context of creditor duty?**

The trust fund doctrine views company assets as being held in trust by directors for the benefit of all stakeholders, including creditors. This reinforces directors' fiduciary duty and prevents them from misusing assets to the detriment of creditors, particularly during financial distress.

# **What are some key challenges in the application of creditor duty?**

One challenge is the potential conflict between creditor duty and the traditional fiduciary duty directors owe to shareholders. Another challenge is the lack of clear, uniform legal frameworks defining the scope and application of creditor duty across different jurisdictions. This ambiguity can lead to uncertainty and inconsistencies in legal outcomes.
