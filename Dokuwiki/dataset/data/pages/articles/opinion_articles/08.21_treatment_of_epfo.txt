# Treatment of EPFO

This paper examines the treatment of employee and worker dues from provident funds, pension funds, and gratuity funds under India’s Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). It analyzes the Code’s legislative intent regarding the exclusion of third-party assets in possession of a corporate debtor from the insolvency resolution process, specifically the provisions related to employee and worker funds. The paper also scrutinizes judicial interpretations on the repayment of these funds, exploring whether these interpretations align with the Code’s objectives. The paper argues that the Code intends to differentiate between dues owed to employees and those owed by employers to statutory authorities like the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), highlighting the potential for conflicting interpretations in practice.

## **Briefing Doc: Treatment of Employee Provident Fund Organization (EPFO) Dues Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)**

**Source:** Garg, S., Agrawal, A., & Rajpurohit, V. (2024). *Treatment of dues to any workman and employee from the provident fund, pension fund and gratuity fund under IBC.*  
**Main Themes:**

* **Exclusion of Third-Party Assets:** The IBC excludes assets not belonging to the Corporate Debtor (CD) from the insolvency process. This includes employee-related funds like provident fund, pension fund, and gratuity fund held by the CD but owned by a third party.  
* **Priority of Workmen and Employee Dues:** While excluded from the liquidation estate, the IBC prioritizes these dues in the waterfall mechanism under Section 53\.  
* **Interpretation of Relevant Sections:** The paper analyzes the interplay and interpretation of Sections 18(1)(f), 36(4)(a)(iii), and 53 of the IBC and their application in judicial precedents.  
* **Distinction Between Employee Dues and Employer Obligations:** The paper emphasizes the difference between dues payable to employees and the employer's liabilities towards statutory authorities like EPFO.

**Most Important Ideas/Facts:**

* **Third-Party Assets Exclusion:** Section 36(4)(a)(iii) excludes "all sums due to any workman or employee from the provident fund, the pension fund and the gratuity fund" from the liquidation estate. However, this exclusion is contingent upon the funds being "assets owned by a third party which are in possession of the corporate debtor."  
* This clarifies that only funds held by the CD but belonging to a third party are excluded.  
* Government-managed funds are not covered as they are not in the CD's possession.  
* **Waterfall Mechanism:** Section 53 outlines the priority of claims in liquidation.  
* Workmen's dues for 24 months prior to liquidation commencement rank equally with secured creditors.  
* Dues of employees (other than workmen) for 12 months preceding liquidation are paid subsequently.  
* Government dues, including those to EPFO (excluding employee/workmen dues) for two years prior, are placed lower.  
* **Judicial Interpretations:** Several cases, including *State Bank of India v. Moser Baer Karamchari Union & Anr.* and *Sunil Kumar Jain v. Sundaresh Bhatt*, have affirmed the exclusion of these funds from the liquidation estate. However, there are inconsistencies regarding whether full payment is required even when funds are insufficient.  
* NCLAT, in *Moser Baer*, stated that even when funds are insufficient, the liquidator must ensure their availability. This has been upheld in subsequent cases.  
* The Supreme Court in *Sundaresh Bhatt* clarified that payment is contingent on fund availability: "workmen/employees...shall be entitled to provident fund, gratuity fund and pension fund from such funds which are…available."  
* **"Clean Slate" Principle:** Requiring the Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA) to pay all past employee dues, even when funds are unavailable, contradicts the "clean slate" principle, where the SRA inherits the business free from past liabilities.  
* **Employer Dues vs. Employee Dues:** The IBC prioritizes dues **owed to employees**. Amounts owed by the employer to EPFO, like penal interest or damages, are treated as government dues under Section 53\. This distinction is crucial.  
* The NCLAT in *Anuj Bajpai* controversially ruled that damages levied on the employer under the EPF Act should be prioritized like employee dues.  
* This decision blurs the distinction between employee and employer dues, potentially elevating all government dues to a higher priority.

**Key Quotes:**

* **Section 36(4)(a):** "The following shall not be included in the liquidation estate assets... (a) assets owned by a third party which are in possession of the corporate debtor, including –... (iii) all sums due to any workman or employee from the provident fund, the pension fund and the gratuity fund;"  
* **Section 53(1)(b)(i):** "the following debts which shall rank equally between and among the following: (i) workmen's dues for the period of twenty-four months preceding the liquidation commencement date; and (ii) debts owed to a secured creditor in the event..."  
* **Sunil Kumar Jain v. Sundaresh Bhatt:** "Therefore, the concerned workmen/employees shall be entitled to provident fund, gratuity fund and pension fund from such funds which are…available... the Liquidator shall not have any claim over such funds."

**Concluding Remarks:**  
The paper argues for a balanced approach that considers the objective of IBC and the legislative intent behind the relevant sections. While prioritizing employee dues, it advocates for a clear distinction between employee dues and employer obligations towards EPFO. The interpretation of Section 36(4)(a)(iii) in recent NCLAT judgments raises concerns, potentially upsetting the priority framework and undermining the "clean slate" principle. Further clarity from higher courts is needed to ensure consistent and equitable application of the IBC in cases involving EPFO dues.

# **FAQ: Treatment of Employee Provident, Pension, and Gratuity Funds under the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code**

## **1\. What is the purpose of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)?**

The IBC aims to consolidate and amend laws related to insolvency and bankruptcy resolution for corporate entities, partnerships, and individuals. Its goals are:

* **Timely Resolution:** To resolve insolvencies in a timely manner.  
* **Value Maximization:** To maximize the value of the debtor's assets.  
* **Promote Entrepreneurship:** To foster entrepreneurship and improve the availability of credit.  
* **Stakeholder Balance:** To balance the interests of all stakeholders involved, including creditors, debtors, and employees.

## **2\. How does the IBC define the "estate" of a corporate debtor (CD)?**

The estate of a CD under the IBC comprises the assets owned by the debtor that are available for distribution to creditors during insolvency proceedings. Importantly, assets owned by third parties but in the CD's possession are not included in the estate. This principle is crucial when considering employee-related funds.

## **3\. Are employee provident funds, pension funds, and gratuity funds included in the liquidation estate of a CD?**

This depends on who controls the funds.

* **Funds controlled by the CD:** If the CD maintains and controls these funds, they are excluded from the liquidation estate under Section 36(4)(a)(iii) of the IBC.  
* **Funds controlled by a third party (e.g., EPFO):** If a statutory authority like the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) manages the funds, they are not considered part of the CD's estate because the CD does not possess them.

## **4\. How are employee dues from these funds treated during insolvency proceedings?**

* **Funds Exist:** If sufficient funds are available in the CD-controlled provident, pension, or gratuity funds, employees are entitled to receive their dues from these funds.  
* **Funds Insufficient or Non-Existent:** If funds are insufficient or do not exist, employee dues fall under the waterfall mechanism of Section 53 of the IBC.

## **5\. What is the "waterfall mechanism" under Section 53 of the IBC?**

Section 53 establishes a priority order for the payment of debts during liquidation. Workmen's dues for 24 months preceding liquidation and dues owed to secured creditors have equal priority. Dues owed to employees (other than workmen) for 12 months preceding liquidation are given a lower priority.

## **6\. How do court interpretations affect the treatment of employee dues from these funds?**

There have been varying interpretations, leading to some confusion.

* **Some courts:** Have ruled that employee dues from these funds are completely outside the liquidation estate, even if funds are unavailable, potentially giving them super-priority over other creditors.  
* **Other courts:** Have maintained that payment is contingent on the availability of funds within these specific accounts, adhering to the principle that third-party assets should not be used to settle the CD's debts.

## **7\. What is the distinction between dues owed to employees and dues owed by the employer to EPFO?**

* **Dues to Employees:** The IBC prioritizes dues owed directly to workmen and employees from the provident fund, gratuity fund, and pension fund.  
* **Dues owed by the Employer to EPFO:** These are considered government dues and have a different priority under the IBC. They include penalties, damages, and interest payable by the employer to the EPFO, and are not considered direct employee dues.

## **8\. What are the potential issues with giving full priority to all employee dues?**

* **Discourages Resolution:** Requiring full payment of all past employee dues, even when funds are unavailable, might discourage potential resolution applicants and lead to more liquidations, ultimately harming employees.  
* **Conflicts with IBC Objectives:** This approach conflicts with the IBC's objectives of balancing stakeholder interests and maximizing the value of assets.  
* **Clean Slate Principle:** It contradicts the 'clean slate' principle by burdening the successful resolution applicant (SRA) with the CD's past liabilities, potentially hindering business revival.

**Glossary of Key Terms**

* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC):** An Indian law that consolidates and amends the laws relating to reorganisation and insolvency resolution of corporate persons, partnership firms, and individuals.  
* **Corporate Debtor (CD):** A company or individual undergoing insolvency resolution or liquidation under the IBC.  
* **Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO):** A statutory body responsible for administering the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952\.  
* **Employees’ Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 (EPF Act):** Indian legislation that provides for the establishment and regulation of provident funds, pension funds, and gratuity funds for employees.  
* **Provident Fund (PF):** A retirement savings scheme where both employee and employer contribute a certain percentage of the employee's salary.  
* **Gratuity Fund:** A lump sum payment made by an employer to an employee upon leaving employment, subject to certain conditions.  
* **Pension Fund:** A fund that provides a regular income to employees after their retirement.  
* **Workmen's Dues:** Monetary benefits owed to workmen, including wages, salaries, accrued holiday remuneration, and dues from provident funds, gratuity funds, and other welfare funds.  
* **Employee Dues:** Monetary benefits owed to employees, similar to workmen's dues but may include different categories and timeframes.  
* **Liquidation Estate:** The assets of the Corporate Debtor available for distribution to creditors during liquidation proceedings.  
* **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A process under the IBC aimed at reviving a financially distressed company through restructuring or finding a buyer.  
* **Liquidation Process:** The process of selling off the assets of a company to pay off creditors when the company cannot be revived under CIRP.  
* **Resolution Applicant (RA):** A person or entity that submits a resolution plan to acquire or restructure a distressed company under the IBC.  
* **Successful Resolution Applicant (SRA):** The Resolution Applicant whose plan is approved by the Committee of Creditors and the Adjudicating Authority.  
* **Waterfall Mechanism:** The hierarchical order of distribution of assets in a liquidation process as per Section 53 of the IBC.  
* **Clean Slate Principle:** The concept that a company emerging from insolvency resolution is relieved of its past liabilities, allowing for a fresh start.  
* **Section 36(4)(a)(iii):** The provision in the IBC that excludes "all sums due to any workman or employee from the provident fund, the pension fund and the gratuity fund" from the liquidation estate.  
* **Section 53:** The provision in the IBC that outlines the waterfall mechanism, prioritizing different categories of debts during liquidation.
