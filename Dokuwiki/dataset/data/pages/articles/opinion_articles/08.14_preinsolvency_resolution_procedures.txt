# Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process

This research paper examines the Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP) in India, a new legal framework introduced in 2021 to address the financial distress of Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). The authors compare the PPIRP to traditional corporate insolvency resolution processes, exploring its potential benefits and limitations. They discuss the challenges hindering wider adoption of the PPIRP, such as creditor apprehension and the lack of a robust adjudicating infrastructure. The paper concludes by analyzing global best practices and suggesting possible reforms to enhance the PPIRP's effectiveness and promote broader adoption.

# **PPIRP FAQ: India's Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process**

## **1\. What is the Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP)?**

PPIRP is a hybrid insolvency resolution mechanism introduced in India's Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC), specifically for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). It combines the speed and flexibility of out-of-court settlements with the legal framework of formal insolvency proceedings. In PPIRP, the debtor and creditors negotiate a resolution plan *before* formally initiating insolvency proceedings.

## **2\. How does PPIRP differ from the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?**

Here are some key differences:  
FeatureCIRPPPIRPEligibilityAll companies and LLPsOnly MSMEsMinimum Default₹1 Crore₹10 LakhTimelineUp to 330 days (180+90 extension)120 days (no extension)ManagementVested with Resolution Professional (RP)Remains with existing management, monitored by RPBase Resolution PlanNot applicableSubmitted by the debtor to creditors before initiationPPIRP is designed to be faster, less disruptive to business operations, and more cost-effective than CIRP.

## **3\. What are the benefits of using PPIRP?**

PPIRP offers several advantages:

* **Speed and Efficiency:** Resolutions are typically achieved within 120 days, significantly faster than CIRP.  
* **Business Continuity:** Existing management retains control, minimizing disruption to operations.  
* **Cost Savings:** Reduced legal and administrative expenses compared to CIRP.  
* **Value Preservation:** By enabling faster resolution, PPIRP helps preserve the value of the business and its assets.  
* **Enhanced Creditor Confidence:** The pre-negotiated nature of the resolution plan instills greater confidence in creditors.

## **4\. Why has the adoption of PPIRP been limited?**

Despite its benefits, PPIRP adoption has been slow due to several challenges:

* **Reluctance of Financial Creditors:** Many financial creditors are hesitant to consent to PPIRP due to concerns about risk and lack of familiarity with the process.  
* **Difficulty in Convincing Stakeholders:** Educating stakeholders about the benefits of PPIRP and addressing their concerns remains a challenge.  
* **Delays in Decision-Making:** Delays in obtaining approvals and arranging funds can hinder the effectiveness of PPIRP.  
* **Lack of Awareness among MSMEs:** Many MSMEs are unaware of PPIRP or perceive it as complex and risky.

## **5\. How can the adoption of PPIRP be improved?**

Several measures can be taken to enhance PPIRP adoption:

* **Increased Awareness and Education:** Conducting outreach programs and workshops to educate MSMEs and creditors about PPIRP.  
* **Streamlining the Process:** Simplifying procedural requirements and addressing bottlenecks to expedite decision-making.  
* **Addressing Creditor Concerns:** Providing clear guidelines and safeguards to mitigate risks and build creditor confidence.  
* **Government Support:** Offering incentives and support mechanisms to encourage participation in PPIRP.

## **6\. Are there any global best practices that India can learn from?**

Yes, studying pre-pack frameworks in countries like the UK, US, and Singapore can provide valuable insights. Learning from their experiences in areas like:

* **Eligibility Criteria:** Expanding PPIRP eligibility beyond MSMEs to include other financially distressed companies.  
* **Regulation and Oversight:** Establishing clear regulatory guidelines and oversight mechanisms to ensure transparency and fairness.  
* **Creditor Involvement:** Balancing creditor rights with the need for swift resolution.

## **7\. What are the future prospects for PPIRP in India?**

The Indian government is actively considering expanding the scope of PPIRP and refining its framework based on stakeholder feedback and global best practices. If implemented effectively, PPIRP has the potential to become a valuable tool for resolving insolvency and supporting the revival of distressed businesses in India.

## **8\. What are the key takeaways for stakeholders?**

* PPIRP offers a faster, more flexible, and cost-effective alternative to traditional insolvency resolution for MSMEs.  
* Overcoming challenges related to creditor reluctance, awareness, and procedural complexities is crucial for wider adoption.  
* Learning from global best practices and continuously refining the framework can enhance the effectiveness of PPIRP in the Indian context.

### **Glossary of Key Terms**

TermDefinition**Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)**The primary legal framework governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings in India, aimed at facilitating timely and efficient resolution of financial distress in companies and individuals.**Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP)**An alternative insolvency resolution mechanism introduced in India under the IBC, specifically designed for MSMEs, characterized by a pre-negotiated resolution plan agreed upon by the debtor and creditors before formal proceedings.**Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)**The standard insolvency resolution process under the IBC for companies other than MSMEs, typically initiated by creditors and involving the appointment of an insolvency professional to manage the company during the resolution process.**Resolution Professional (RP)**A licensed professional appointed by the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal) to oversee the insolvency resolution process, manage the company's affairs, and facilitate negotiations between debtors and creditors.**Base Resolution Plan**A pre-drafted plan outlining the proposed restructuring measures, financial arrangements, and terms of settlement agreed upon by the debtor and financial creditors before initiating the PPIRP.**Financial Creditor (FC)**An entity or individual to whom a financial debt is owed by the debtor company.
