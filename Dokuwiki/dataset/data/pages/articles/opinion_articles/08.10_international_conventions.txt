### **Glossary of Key Terms**

**Bilateral Investment Promotion Agreements (BIPAs):** Treaties between two countries aimed at promoting and protecting investments made by investors from one country in the territory of the other country.  
**Cape Town Convention:** Formally known as the Convention on International Interests in Mobile Equipment, it creates an international legal framework for the financing of high-value mobile equipment, including aircraft, by providing standardized rules for the creation, registration, and enforcement of security interests.  
**Dualist Theory:** A theory of legal systems that views international law and domestic law as separate and distinct systems. In this view, international law norms require specific adoption or transformation into domestic law to have legal effect within a country.  
**Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016:** A comprehensive legislation in India that governs insolvency resolution and bankruptcy proceedings for companies, partnerships, and individuals. It aims to streamline and expedite the process of resolving insolvency and maximizing the value of assets.  
**International Conventions:** Formal agreements between states that establish rules or standards on specific matters, often with the goal of harmonizing laws and practices across different jurisdictions. They are a primary source of international law.  
**Monist Theory:** A theory of legal systems that views international law and domestic law as part of a single, integrated legal system. Monism generally holds that international law is superior to domestic law, meaning that in case of conflict, international law prevails.  
**Sector-Neutral Approach:** A legislative or regulatory approach that applies the same rules and principles to all sectors of the economy, regardless of their specific characteristics or needs. The IBC adopts this approach, aiming for fairness and efficiency in resource allocation.  
**Sovereignty:** The supreme authority within a territory. In international law, it refers to the right of a state to govern itself independently and without interference from other states.  
**UNCITRAL:** The United Nations Commission on International Trade Law, a UN body dedicated to harmonizing and modernizing the law of international trade by preparing legal texts, model laws, and conventions.  
**UNIDROIT Principles:** Principles on Digital Assets and Private Law developed by the International Institute for the Unification of Private Law (UNIDROIT) to address the legal challenges posed by digital assets, aiming to provide a harmonized legal framework for dealing with digital assets in private transactions.  
**United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG):** A treaty that provides uniform rules for the formation and performance of contracts for the international sale of goods, aiming to facilitate international trade by reducing legal uncertainties.  
**United Nations Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (New York Convention):** A widely adopted treaty that requires courts in contracting states to recognize and enforce arbitral awards made in other contracting states, promoting the use of arbitration as a means of settling international commercial disputes.

# **FAQ: India's Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code in a Globalized World**

## **How does India balance its domestic legal system with its international legal commitments?**

India's legal system prioritizes its Constitution. When engaging with the international community, India carefully balances domestic priorities with international commitments. This approach aligns with the dualist theory of law, where international law needs to be adopted into domestic law to take effect. India's participation in international legal agreements is a conscious choice, reflecting its engagement with the global community while upholding its Constitution.

## **How does the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) interact with sector-specific conventions, particularly in the aviation sector?**

The IBC is a comprehensive law designed to be applicable across all sectors, ensuring fairness and a level playing field. However, sector-specific conventions like the Cape Town Convention, which deals with aircraft assets, can create complexities. India's ratification of the Cape Town Convention and a recent notification exempting certain aviation assets from IBC's moratorium provisions show India's commitment to aligning its domestic laws with international standards. This exemption gives creditors and lessors specific rights in insolvency, highlighting the delicate balance between sector-specific needs and the broader principles of the IBC.

## **What is India's approach to digital assets in the context of insolvency, considering ongoing international developments?**

India is closely following international developments regarding the treatment of digital assets in insolvency. The UNCITRAL is developing rules on this subject, and the UNIDROIT has formulated Principles on Digital Assets and Private Law, both of which could significantly influence India's approach. India is awaiting a global consensus on these matters, as evidenced by the G20 New Delhi Leaders' Declaration 2023, before adapting and aligning its insolvency framework with international best practices.

## **How does the IBC consider environmental obligations, especially given India's commitment to international environmental agreements?**

The IBC mandates compliance with all applicable laws, including environmental laws. However, specific considerations regarding environmental liabilities in insolvency are still evolving. Key considerations include the treatment of clean-up costs, priority of environmental claims, responsibility for ongoing compliance during proceedings, and balancing environmental protection with business rescue and creditor satisfaction. India's commitment to agreements like the UNFCCC and the Paris Agreement suggests that its insolvency regime will continue to evolve to address environmental concerns effectively.

## **How does India reconcile its obligations under maritime conventions with the IBC when handling insolvency cases involving shipping companies?**

India recognizes several international maritime conventions, including those on maritime liens and mortgages, arrest of ships, and wreck removal. These conventions can impact insolvency proceedings involving shipping companies and maritime assets. The IBC needs to be applied while respecting these conventions, ensuring a consistent approach that aligns with global standards. Balancing the objectives of insolvency law with the specialized regime of maritime claims presents a continuing challenge.

## **What is the significance of the New York Convention in the context of the IBC?**

The New York Convention, which deals with the recognition and enforcement of foreign arbitral awards, is crucial for resolving cross-border disputes in insolvency cases. India, as a signatory, strives to uphold this convention. However, its provisions may sometimes conflict with the IBC's moratorium, necessitating careful consideration to balance international commitments with domestic insolvency processes.

## **How do Bilateral Investment Promotion Agreements (BIPAs) interact with the IBC, particularly in cases involving foreign investments and insolvency?**

BIPAs often contain investor protection provisions that can intersect with insolvency proceedings involving foreign investments. Reconciling investor protection under BIPAs with the collective nature of insolvency proceedings, particularly concerning foreign creditors and potential investor-state disputes, is a key challenge.

## **What challenges and opportunities does the interplay between the IBC and international conventions present for India's insolvency regime?**

The interaction between the IBC and international conventions presents both challenges and opportunities. Challenges include balancing domestic priorities with global best practices, navigating conflicting provisions, and addressing issues like forum shopping and regulatory arbitrage. Opportunities include strengthening India's insolvency framework, attracting foreign investment, and contributing to the development of international insolvency law. As India continues to integrate into the global economy, continuous monitoring of international developments and updates to the IBC will be crucial for maintaining a robust and internationally aligned insolvency regime.
