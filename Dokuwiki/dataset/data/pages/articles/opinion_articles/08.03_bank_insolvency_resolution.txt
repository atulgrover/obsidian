## **Timeline of Main Events:**

**2008:** Global Financial Crisis (GFC) prompts countries to develop frameworks for resolving large financial institutions, but a gap remains for smaller banks.  
**June 7-8, 2021:** UNIDROIT holds a virtual exploratory workshop on "Bank Liquidation" to discuss the need for a global instrument on bank insolvency.  
**December 2021:** First session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on Bank Insolvency takes place in Rome. The group focuses on definitions, objectives of a bank insolvency regime, institutional models, and procedural aspects.  
**April 2022:** Second session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on Bank Insolvency focuses on the ranking of claims and safeguarding procedures.  
**October 2022:** Third session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on Bank Insolvency in Brussels addresses cross-border aspects and proposes a structure for the Guide on Bank Insolvency.  
**March 29-31, 2023:** Fourth session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on Bank Insolvency held in Basel, Switzerland, delves into specific chapters of the draft guide and discusses various administrative models for bank liquidation.  
**October 2023:** Fifth session of the UNIDROIT Working Group on Bank Insolvency in Rome. The group reviews the consolidated draft Guide on Bank Insolvency, with discussions focusing on deposit insurance systems and their role in financial stability.  
**2024:** The UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency is scheduled to be adopted.

* **Bail-out:** A resolution mechanism where external parties, typically the government, provide financial assistance to a failing bank to prevent its collapse.  
* **Deposit Insurance:** A system where depositors are protected from losing their savings in the event of a bank failure.

**Main Themes:**

* **Addressing Gaps in International Bank Insolvency Resolution:** The absence of a global framework for resolving small and medium-sized banks (SMBs) poses a significant challenge. Domestic legislation alone governs bank liquidation, leading to inconsistencies across jurisdictions. UNIDROIT aims to bridge this gap with its Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency, providing a soft law instrument with fundamental elements for effective resolution processes.  
* **Bail-in as a Resolution Tool:** Bail-in, an internal recapitalization mechanism, is gaining prominence in resolving distressed financial institutions. It promotes market discipline by involving shareholders and creditors in absorbing losses. The paper explores bail-in's principles, including loss absorption, hierarchy of claims, and continuity of essential banking functions. It also analyzes the potential impacts on banks' liability structures and funding costs.  
* **Deposit Insurance Agencies (DIAs) and Resolution Financing:** The role of DIAs in resolution financing is explored, highlighting the variability in countries' approaches. The paper outlines risks associated with using DIA funds, such as acquiring stakes in troubled institutions and potential depletion of funds. It suggests measures to mitigate these risks, including transparent assessment methodologies and strengthening institutional frameworks.  
* **UNIDROIT Working Group Discussions:** The paper summarizes key discussions from the UNIDROIT Working Group sessions, focusing on:  
* Defining "bank" in a technologically evolving landscape.  
* Objectives of a bank insolvency regime, including value maximization, financial stability, and depositor protection.  
* Institutional models and procedural aspects, considering administrative vs. court-based systems and creditor involvement.  
* Safeguards and tools, exploring cross-border cooperation, netting provisions, and asset transfer mechanisms.  
* Addressing specific issues like stablecoins, secured creditors, related party claims, and treatment of interbank deposits.

**Important Ideas & Facts:**

* **Financial Stability Board’s "Key Attributes" as a benchmark:** The FSB's "Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions" serves as an international standard, influencing the development of resolution laws globally.  
* **Bridge Service Providers/Bridge Banks (BSP/BBs) for continuity:** BSP/BBs play a crucial role in preventing market disruption by temporarily taking over critical functions of failing institutions, allowing time for resolution strategies like acquisition or liquidation.  
* **Challenges with Bailouts:** While bailouts offer immediate relief, they can create moral hazard and incentivize excessive risk-taking. The paper analyzes the drawbacks observed in past crises, emphasizing the need for balanced approaches.  
* **Cyprus Bank Failure as a Case Study:** The 2013 Bank of Cyprus failure highlights the challenges and complexities associated with bail-in implementation. The paper dissects issues like data integrity, operational hurdles, and impact on depositor confidence.  
* **Key Considerations for Statutory Bail-in Frameworks:** The paper emphasizes the importance of clear legal frameworks, jurisdictional cooperation, comprehensive scope, and entity-specific approaches to address group and cross-border complexities.  
* **Importance of Collaboration and Recognition in Cross-Border Insolvency:** The IMF's framework for enhanced collaboration encourages recognition of bank-resolution measures across jurisdictions, provided they meet core coordination standards, ensuring smoother cross-border resolution.  
* **Structured Approach to the Legislative Guide:** The UNIDROIT Working Group proposes a structured approach for the Guide, covering chapters on introduction, institutional models, objectives, definitions, banking groups, cross-border aspects, safeguards, tools, and miscellaneous provisions.

**Answer Key**

* The lack of a global framework for resolving small and medium-sized banks, alongside the inconsistencies in bank liquidation regulations across different jurisdictions, led to the development of the UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency.  
* The FRDI Bill aimed to establish a centralized system for monitoring financial institutions, assisting with bankruptcy proceedings, and proactively mitigating risks to their stability, ultimately ensuring the fulfillment of obligations like depositor repayment. However, concerns about the implications of its provisions, particularly the bail-in clause, led to its withdrawal.  
* Bail-in involves internal recapitalization of a failing bank by writing down or converting debt into equity, imposing losses on shareholders and creditors. In contrast, a bail-out involves external recapitalization using public funds, often leading to taxpayer burden and moral hazard.  
* BSP/BB are temporary entities that take over ownership/control and critical functions of a failing bank to prevent immediate market disruption and facilitate an eventual resolution. They provide continuity of essential banking services while a long-term solution is sought.

* The hierarchical structure ensures that losses are absorbed in a predetermined order, typically starting with shareholders, then moving to unsecured creditors, and finally, if necessary, to secured creditors. This minimizes disruptions and provides clarity for stakeholders involved.

It also revealed challenges in data integrity for valuation, potential disruptions to banking systems, and difficulties in protecting specific depositor classes.  
* DIAs face risks of being drawn into resolution financing, potentially acquiring stakes in failing institutions, and depleting their funds. Inaccurate asset valuations can further expose DIAs to multiple rounds of funding appeals, increasing their financial burden.  
* The UNIDROIT Working Group comprises experts in insolvency law, deposit insurance, and banking crisis management. It aims to develop a soft law instrument to guide lawmakers and policymakers in improving their bank liquidation systems. The group actively debates different models, principles, and safeguards for a comprehensive framework.  
* The three key objectives are maximizing value from the liquidation process, ensuring financial stability by containing systemic risks and maintaining public confidence, and safeguarding the interests of depositors, who are considered particularly vulnerable stakeholders.

**Glossary of Key Terms**

* **Bail-in:** A resolution mechanism where shareholders and creditors of a failing bank absorb losses by having their debt written down or converted into equity, thereby recapitalizing the bank internally.  
* **Bail-out:** The use of public funds to rescue a failing bank, often through direct financial assistance or government guarantees.  
* **Bridge Service Provider/Bridge Bank (BSP/BB):** A temporary entity established to assume ownership/control and maintain essential functions of a failing bank, providing continuity during the resolution process.  
* **Deposit Insurance Agency (DIA):** An institution that provides insurance coverage to depositors, protecting their funds up to a specified limit in case of bank failure.  
* **Financial Resolution and Deposit Insurance Bill (FRDI Bill):** A proposed legislation in India aimed at creating a comprehensive framework for resolving distressed financial institutions, including banks.  
* **Financial Stability Board (FSB):** An international body that monitors and makes recommendations about the global financial system.  
* **Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions:** A set of principles developed by the FSB outlining essential features of robust resolution frameworks for financial institutions.  
* **Liquidation:** The process of winding down a failing bank, selling its assets, and distributing proceeds to creditors according to a predetermined hierarchy.  
* **Resolution:** A set of actions taken to stabilize and restructure a failing bank, aiming to preserve its critical functions and minimize systemic risk.  
* **Systemically Important Financial Institution (SIFI):** A financial institution whose failure could pose a significant threat to the stability of the financial system.  
* **UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency:** A soft law instrument developed by UNIDROIT to provide guidance on key aspects of bank liquidation proceedings, promoting consistency and best practices across jurisdictions.

# **FAQ: Bank Insolvency Resolution**

## **What is the UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency?**

The UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency is a soft law instrument being developed to address the lack of a global framework for resolving small and medium-sized bank insolvencies. It aims to provide guidance to lawmakers and policymakers on creating effective bank liquidation systems, covering various aspects, including institutional models, objectives, definitions, banking groups, cross-border aspects, safeguards, tools, and treatment of liabilities.

## **Why is a specific framework for bank insolvency resolution necessary?**

Banks play a crucial role in the financial system, and their failure can have a significant impact on the economy. Traditional insolvency frameworks may not be well-suited to address bank insolvencies due to their unique characteristics, such as the importance of maintaining financial stability and protecting depositors. A specific framework for bank insolvency resolution allows for specialized tools and procedures that can effectively address the complexities of bank failures.

## **What are the key objectives of a bank insolvency regime?**

The UNIDROIT Working Group has identified three primary objectives for a bank insolvency regime:

* **Value Maximization**: Aiming to recover as much value as possible from the failed bank's assets to satisfy creditors' claims.  
* **Financial Stability**: Ensuring the smooth functioning of the financial system and preventing the spread of contagion.  
* **Depositor Protection**: Safeguarding the interests of depositors, who are often considered the most vulnerable creditors.

## **What are the main challenges in resolving cross-border bank insolvencies?**

Cross-border bank insolvencies pose significant challenges due to the involvement of multiple jurisdictions with potentially conflicting legal frameworks and regulatory authorities. These challenges include:

* **Coordination and Cooperation**: Ensuring effective communication and collaboration among different countries' authorities.  
* **Recognition of Insolvency Proceedings**: Ensuring that insolvency proceedings initiated in one jurisdiction are recognized and enforced in others.  
* **Differences in Legal Frameworks**: Harmonizing the application of different countries' insolvency laws.

## **How can the statutory bail-in framework address group issues and cross-border challenges?**

A statutory bail-in framework can address group issues and cross-border challenges by incorporating several key elements:

* **Jurisdictional Cooperation**: Promoting collaboration and information sharing among relevant jurisdictions.  
* **Comprehensive Legal Framework**: Establishing clear legal grounds for the bail-in process and the authority of home country regulators.  
* **Broad Scope of Application**: Covering all liabilities, including those held abroad and governed by foreign laws.  
* **Entity-Specific Approach**: Targeting only the failing member of a banking group without harming viable entities.  
* **Cross-Border Recognition**: Facilitating the recognition and enforcement of bail-in actions across different jurisdictions.

## **What is the difference between bail-in and bail-out?**

* **Bail-in**: Involves imposing losses on the failed bank's shareholders and creditors by writing down or converting their claims into equity to recapitalize the bank internally.  
* **Bail-out**: Involves using public funds to rescue the failed bank, often by injecting capital or providing guarantees.

## **What are the roles of Deposit Insurance Agencies (DIAs) in bank insolvency resolution?**

DIAs play a vital role in protecting depositors and maintaining confidence in the banking system. Their role in bank insolvency resolution can include:

* **Providing Deposit Insurance Payouts**: Compensating insured depositors up to the coverage limit.  
* **Contributing to Resolution Financing**: Using DIA funds to support the resolution of a failed bank.  
* **Participating in the Resolution Process**: Working with resolution authorities to ensure the smooth implementation of resolution measures.

## **What are the main topics covered in the draft chapters of the UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency?**

The draft chapters of the UNIDROIT Legislative Guide on Bank Insolvency cover a comprehensive range of topics related to bank liquidation, including:

* **Institutional Models**: Discussing administrative and court-based systems for handling bank insolvencies.  
* **Objectives**: Defining the key objectives of a bank liquidation regime.  
* **Definitions**: Clarifying key terms related to bank insolvency.  
* **Banking Groups**: Addressing the complexities of resolving insolvencies involving banking groups.
