### **Short Answer Quiz Answer Key**

* Before the 2018 amendment, homebuyers were not explicitly categorized as either financial creditors (FCs) or operational creditors (OCs) under the IBC. This ambiguity left their rights and interests uncertain and made it difficult for them to participate effectively in insolvency proceedings.  
* The "assured return" scheme, where sellers promise regular payments to homebuyers until possession, led some courts to classify these transactions as financial debts. This is because the payments were seen as akin to loans with an expectation of returns, aligning with the concept of financial debt under the IBC.  
* The 2018 amendment classified amounts raised from homebuyers for real estate projects as "deemed commercial borrowing," effectively recognizing them as financial debt. This granted them similar rights to secured FCs, allowing for greater participation in the insolvency resolution process.  
* Although granted similar rights to secured FCs, homebuyers are not considered fully secured because their claims are not backed by specific, identifiable assets like a mortgage. Their interest lies in the project's completion, making their security dependent on the project's success.  
* The 2020 Amendment Act requires at least 100 allottees or 10% of the total allottees, whichever is less, to jointly file for CIRP against real estate companies. This aimed to prevent frivolous or individual petitions and encourage collective action to avoid abrupt disruptions to projects.  
* The Amitabh Kant Committee recommended project-wise CIRP, aligning with RERA registration, and allowing for the transfer of ownership/possession to allottees during the resolution process. These recommendations focus on smoother resolution processes and safeguarding homebuyers' interests.  
* The Transfer of Property Act, 1882, grants the buyer a charge on the property for the purchase money paid, creating a financial interest in the property. This supports their classification as creditors, as they have a legal claim on the asset in case of the seller's default.  
* A registered sale deed is essential for transferring ownership rights in Indian real estate. Without it, the buyer does not legally own the property, even if possession has been granted. This underscores the difference between contractual rights and actual ownership.  
* The *M/s. Samruddhi Realty Ltd.* case highlights the importance of a registered sale deed in securing homebuyers' rights. Despite full payment and possession, the court denied relief due to the lack of a registered deed, emphasizing the need for proper legal documentation.  
* The study concludes that homebuyers' rights in insolvency remain unclear and often categorized as "unsecured." It recommends formalizing the transfer of ownership through registered sale deeds during the resolution process to strengthen their position and ensure clearer legal recognition.

### **Glossary of Key Terms**

* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC):** The primary legal framework in India governing insolvency and bankruptcy proceedings for companies and individuals.  
* **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):** A time-bound process under the IBC to revive financially distressed companies or, if unsuccessful, to liquidate them in a structured manner.  
* **Financial Creditor (FC):** An entity whose primary claim arises from a financial debt owed by the corporate debtor.  
* **Operational Creditor (OC):** An entity whose claim arises from the provision of goods or services to the corporate debtor.  
* **Secured Creditor:** A creditor whose claim is backed by a specific asset of the debtor, providing them with priority in recovering their dues.  
* **Unsecured Creditor:** A creditor whose claim is not backed by a specific asset, making their recovery dependent on the remaining assets after secured creditors are paid.  
* **Committee of Creditors (CoC):** A body formed by FCs in a CIRP to make decisions regarding the resolution plan for the distressed company.  
* **Resolution Professional (RP):** An insolvency professional appointed to manage the affairs of the corporate debtor during the CIRP.  
* **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA):** Legislation aimed at regulating the real estate sector, protecting homebuyers' interests, and promoting transparency.  
* **Transfer of Property Act, 1882:** A fundamental law governing the transfer of property in India, including sales, mortgages, leases, and gifts.  
* **Agreement to Sell:** A contract outlining the terms and conditions for the future sale of a property. It does not transfer ownership but creates certain rights for the buyer.  
* **Sale Deed:** A legal document that officially transfers the ownership of a property from the seller to the buyer. It must be registered to be legally valid.  
* **Charge:** A legal claim on a property used as security for a debt.  
* **Liquidation:** The process of selling off the assets of a company to repay its creditors when the company cannot be revived.  
* **Amitabh Kant Committee:** A committee formed to address the issue of stalled real estate projects in India and recommend solutions.  
* **Nemo dat quod non habet:** A legal maxim meaning "no one can give what they do not have," implying that a seller cannot transfer ownership rights they do not possess.

## **Timeline of Main Events:**

**2016:**

* **Enactment of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC):** This legislation brings hope for distressed companies but leaves the rights of homebuyers unclear. Initial judgments find that homebuyers don't fit into the existing categories of financial creditors (FCs) or operational creditors (OCs).  
* **Nikhil Mehta v. AMR Infrastructure:** In this case, homebuyers are considered FCs due to an "assured return" clause in their contracts, making the transaction akin to a loan.  
* **Anil Mahindroo & anr v. Earth Organics Infrastructure:** This case also views homebuyers as FCs based on specific contractual terms, creating uncertainty about a general legal status for homebuyers.

**2017:**

* **Introduction of Form F by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI):** This form allows for claims from "creditors other than FCs or OCs," suggesting homebuyers fall into a separate category. While they can file claims, they cannot initiate insolvency proceedings.

**2018:**

* **Insolvency Law Committee (ILC) Report:** The report recommends classifying funds raised from homebuyers as financial debt under Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC.  
* **Amendment to the IBC on June 6th:** The amendment clarifies that funds from homebuyers in real estate projects are "deemed commercial borrowing," effectively treating them as FCs.  
* **Chitra Sharma v. Union of India:** This Supreme Court judgment grants homebuyers rights similar to secured FCs, recognizing the amounts owed to them as financial debt. However, they are not explicitly classified as secured FCs.

**2019:**

* **Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited and Anr. v. Union of India and Ors.:** The Supreme Court upholds the 2018 amendment's validity, classifying funds from homebuyers as financial debt and recognizing them as individual FCs. However, the court clarifies that they remain unsecured creditors despite their vital stake in the project.

**2020:**

* **Flat Buyers Association v. Umang Realtech Pvt. Ltd.:** The NCLAT affirms that unsecured creditors, including homebuyers, hold rights over the corporate debtor's assets. The homeowner gains immediate interest in the property upon signing the builder-buyer agreement, even if the property is not yet constructed.  
* **Anuj Jain IRP for Jaypee Infratech Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd.:** The Supreme Court reinforces the "time value of money" as a key characteristic of financial debt.  
* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act:** This amendment introduces new requirements for initiating CIRP against real estate companies, demanding a joint petition from at least 100 allottees or 10% of the total allottees under the same project. Individual homebuyer cases filed before the amendment face dismissal if they don't meet the threshold.  
* **Manish Kumar v. Union of India:** The Supreme Court upholds the constitutional validity of the 2020 Amendment Act.

**2021:**

* **Flat Buyers Association Winter Hills v. Umang Realtech:** The NCLAT establishes the concept of "Reverse CIRP," where the corporate debtor can act as a financial lender to resolve debt burden, potentially benefiting homebuyers by securing their property rights.

**2023:**

* **Logix Developers Ltd. case:** The NCLT directs the Resolution Professional (RP) to hand over possession of flats to homebuyers, ensuring their lawful and peaceful occupancy.  
* **AU Small Finance Bank Ltd. v. Coral Infragold Pvt. Ltd.:** The NCLT permits the RP to hand over flats and allows sale/registration for homebuyers after compliance with statutory provisions.  
* **M/s. Samruddhi Realty Ltd. case:** The NCLT rules that, despite full payment and possession, property cannot be excluded from the liquidation estate without a registered sale deed. The NCLAT upholds this decision, denying relief to the homebuyers and emphasizing the importance of a registered deed for ownership rights.  
* **Naresh Sundarlal Jain v. Udaipur Entertainment World:** The Bombay High Court distinguishes between lenders and homebuyers, emphasizing that agreements/allotments create security interest for homebuyers, potentially classifying them as secured creditors.  
* **Amitabh Kant Committee Report:** This report recommends reforms to the IBC for better handling of real estate sector complexities, including project-wise CIRP, transfer of ownership/possession to allottees during the resolution process, and formal registration of already transferred properties.  
* **Pradip Kumar Chaudhuri v. Dagcon (India) (P) Ltd.:** This case affirms that there's no barrier to executing sale deeds in favor of allottees during the moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC.

## **Briefing Doc: Status Quo of Homebuyers in India**

**Main Themes:**

* The legal status of homebuyers in insolvency proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) in India.  
* The interplay between the IBC, the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA), and the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 in determining homebuyer rights.  
* Challenges and uncertainties faced by homebuyers in recovering their investments in stalled real estate projects.

**Most Important Ideas/Facts:**

* **Homebuyers as Financial Creditors:** The 2018 amendment to the IBC recognized homebuyers as financial creditors (FCs), affording them rights similar to secured FCs. This means they have a voice in the Committee of Creditors (CoC) and are entitled to a share in the liquidation waterfall. However, the Supreme Court has clarified that they are still considered **unsecured creditors**.  
* “True, allottees are unsecured creditors, but they have a vital interest in amounts that are advanced for completion of the project, maybe to the extent of 100% of the project being funded by them alone.” (Pioneer case)  
* **Ambiguity and Judicial Precedents:** Despite the amendment, judicial pronouncements on homebuyer rights have been mixed, leading to continued uncertainty. Some courts have recognized homebuyers as secured creditors based on specific contractual terms, while others have maintained their unsecured status.  
* “……..It is an admitted fact that the role of the Petitioner was no more than that of a lender of some money to Respondent no.1 and that there was neither any allotment/agreement whatsoever made in favour of the Petitioner unlike the home buyers in whose favour there were agreements/allotments made for sale of flats in their favour creating in them a security interest as defined under Section 2(31) of the IBC..." (Naresh Sundarlal Jain v. Udaipur Entertainment World)  
* **Importance of Registration:** The Transfer of Property Act, 1882, mandates the registration of any agreement transferring interest in immovable property. Therefore, a registered sale deed is crucial for homebuyers to acquire ownership rights. Without it, their rights are limited, even if they have made full payment and taken possession.  
* "In essence, the transfer of title in immovable property can only be accomplished through a registered sale deed. Without a properly stamped and registered sale deed, the buyer does not gain any rights, title, or interests in the property."  
* **Intricacies of IBC and RERA:** RERA aims to protect homebuyer interests by ensuring project completion, while the IBC focuses on resolving insolvency, potentially leading to project modifications or liquidation. This potential conflict poses challenges for balancing the objectives of both laws.  
* **Recommendations for Strengthening Homebuyer Rights:**  
* **Project-wise CIRP:** Implementing a project-wise CIRP under the IBC, as recommended by the Amitabh Kant Committee, could offer better protection for homebuyers by considering the specific details of each project.  
* **Formalizing Possession Transfers:** Ensuring the registration of sale deeds for units already occupied by homebuyers during CIRP would strengthen their legal standing and provide them with ownership rights.

**Conclusion:**  
While the IBC amendments have taken positive steps toward recognizing homebuyer rights, further clarity and legal frameworks are needed to ensure their effective protection. Harmonizing the provisions of the IBC, RERA, and the Transfer of Property Act, along with consistent judicial interpretation, is crucial to address the existing ambiguities and provide homebuyers with the security and confidence they deserve.

### **FAQ: Homebuyers and Insolvency Proceedings in India**

**1\. What is the current status of homebuyers in insolvency proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)?**  
While the 2018 amendment to the IBC aimed to classify homebuyers as financial creditors (FCs), their position in insolvency proceedings remains ambiguous. The Supreme Court in the Pioneer case clarified that homebuyers are technically unsecured creditors. This means they do not hold a direct security interest in the property until a registered sale deed is executed. However, homebuyers are granted rights similar to secured FCs during the resolution process, recognizing their significant financial stake in the project.  
**2\. How has the IBC evolved to address the concerns of homebuyers in real estate projects?**  
The IBC has undergone several amendments to better protect homebuyers. Key changes include:

* **2018 Amendment:** Recognized funds raised from homebuyers as "financial debt," giving them a stronger position as creditors.  
* **2020 Amendment:** Requires a minimum threshold of 100 allottees or 10% of the total allottees to jointly file for CIRP against real estate companies, preventing individual actions. This amendment aims to prevent frivolous lawsuits and protect ongoing projects.

**3\. What is the significance of the Amitabh Kant Committee Report for homebuyers facing stalled real estate projects?**  
The Amitabh Kant Committee, formed to address legacy stalled projects, recommended significant reforms to the IBC, including:

* **Project-wise CIRP:** Mandating project-specific resolution processes to cater to the unique complexities of each real estate project.  
* **Transfer of Ownership/Possession:** Empowering Resolution Professionals (RPs) to transfer ownership and possession of completed units to homebuyers during CIRP.

**4\. How do the IBC, RERA, and the Transfer of Property Act interact to define homebuyer rights?**

* **IBC:** Defines the framework for insolvency proceedings, including the classification of creditors and the resolution process.  
* **RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Authority Act):** Focuses on regulating the real estate sector and protecting homebuyers' interests, emphasizing timely project completion.  
* **Transfer of Property Act:** Governs property transfers and outlines the concept of "charge" on the property, granting the buyer a limited right until the sale deed is registered.

**5\. Why is the registration of a sale deed crucial for homebuyers?**  
Under Indian law, only a registered sale deed legally transfers property ownership to the buyer. An agreement for sale without a subsequent registered sale deed does not confer ownership rights, leaving the buyer vulnerable in case of insolvency.  
**6\. What are the key challenges and uncertainties that homebuyers still face in insolvency cases?**

* **Unsecured Creditor Status:** The lack of a direct security interest creates uncertainty for homebuyers in the event of liquidation, potentially leading to delays in receiving completed units or financial recoveries.  
* **Conflicting Objectives:** RERA's focus on project completion and the IBC's emphasis on debt resolution may clash, creating complexities in balancing stakeholder interests.  
* **Judicial Interpretations:** The legal landscape is evolving, and differing judicial interpretations of homebuyer rights under the IBC continue to create ambiguity.

**7\. How does the concept of "Reverse CIRP" potentially benefit homebuyers?**  
Reverse CIRP allows the Corporate Debtor (the builder in this case) to proactively propose a resolution plan even before CIRP formally commences. This can benefit homebuyers by enabling faster project completion and the transfer of ownership, provided the plan safeguards the rights of all stakeholders involved.  
**8\. What recommendations are being made to strengthen the rights of homebuyers?**  
Experts suggest prioritizing the formalization of property transfers during the resolution process, even if possession has already been handed over to the buyer. This involves the execution and registration of sale deeds with the approval of the Committee of Creditors (CoC), solidifying the homebuyer's legal ownership and mitigating risks in insolvency scenarios.
