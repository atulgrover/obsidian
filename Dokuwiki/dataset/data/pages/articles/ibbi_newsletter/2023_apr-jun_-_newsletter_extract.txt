<h1>Clicbrics Technologies Prt: Ltd. Vs Ansal Housing Ltd [CA (ATIns)No: 12680/2022]</h1>
<ul>
<li>In this case, the operational creditor (OC) being real estate agent filed application under section 9 seeking initiation of CIRP against CD-Ms: Ansal Housing Limited. The application was dismissed by AA holding that the OC had approached it with malafide intention and not for genuine resolution. The NCLAT while disposing the appeal observed that intent and motive behind the transaction is the pivotal factor in deciding the preferential transaction and whether the sums arranged from relatives of the CD can be held to be preferential transaction: The NCLAT while allowing appeal of OC observed that no material has been placed on record by CD to establish that the invoices were disputed by the CD prior to demand notice. The submission by CD that the OC by refusing to receive payment tried to coerce and intimidate the CD to succumb to his illegitimate demands cannot be accepted. It held that the operational debt which had admittedly become due and payable; having not been disputed prior to issue of demand notice and not been discharged by the CD, is a fit case for admission of CIRP.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Westcoast Infraprojects Prt: Ltd: Vs: Mr: Ram Chandra Dallram Logisdcs Pvt: Ltd: [CA (AT) (Ins) No.361 012023]</h1>
<ul>
<li>The question for consideration before the NCLAT was whether liquidator could forfeit the EMD amount deposited by highest bidder for want of payment of full consideration within time. Before the NCLAT, the bidder came up with fresh argument that liquidator had no jurisdiction to forfeit the EMD. As per section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, liquidator ought to have filed a suit for recovery. <h1>Mr: Arun Chadha Liquidator of Pawan Buildwell Pvt: Ltd: Vs: Ramesh Kumar Suneja [CA(AT) (Ins)No.747 &amp; 7480f2021]</h1>
<ul>
<li>The Issue before NCLAT was whether the creation of security interest by way of mortgage of the property is a preferential transaction under section 43 or is covered under section 66 (transaction to defraud the creditors). NCLAT observed that while CD was in default; it mortgaged its property for the benefit of its related parties. Moreover, CD uploaded Form CHG-1 on the website of MCA to create "first and exclusive charge" over the mortgaged property. Further, NCLAT also took note of the independent auditor's report indicating that CD gave advances and guarantees to its associates/related persons in excess of 60% of its share capital and reserves &amp; surplus and the CD has not received counter-guarantees of the equivalent amount from the respective related parties: NCLAT cancelled the mortgage and held that security interest created on mortgaged property in favour of related party infringes section 43.</li>
</ul>
