<h1>EDAC Engineering Ltd: Vs: [Application No: 2080 and 4609 of 2021] Industrial Fans (India) Pvt Ltd: &amp; Ors</h1>
<ul>
<li>In this case, the arbitrator exercised his statutory lien for non-payment of his fees/costs by the CD. Aggrieved by action of arbitrator; CD filed an application before Madras HC seeking the direction to the arbitrator to release the lien on the arbitral award dated April 30, 2021 in view of the moratorium under section 14 of the Code. HC while dismissing the application of CD noted that arbitral award was passed on April 30, 2021, which was prior to the moratorium order dated August 8, 2023. HC held that the appointment of arbitrator having been done by the Court much prior to the initiation of CIRP, the fee payable has to be treated as part of CIRP costs. It observed that moratorium under section 14 of the Code does not affect the payment of fees to the arbitrator for the award passed before the moratorium was declared.</li>

<h1>KRBL Limited Vs. State of Gujarat [RSpecial Civil Application No: [9804 of 2072]</h1>
<ul>
<li>The petitioner is the successful bidder for CD's property in the auction conducted by the liquidator. Post auction sale, the State Goods and Services Tax (SGST) department had issued an order creating charge in the revenue records on the auctioned property towards the outstanding dues of GST. Successful bidder challenged the order of SGST department before Gujarat HC. While allowing the petition of the successful bidder; HC noted that SGST department failed to lodge its claim during the stage of liquidation. Relying on the judgments of SC in the cases of Nizam Limited and Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited, it held that once the resolution plan is approved, it becomes binding on the stakeholders including government OCs. It further observed that the department having relinquished its interest under section 52, cannot continue insistence of maintaining the charge in the revenue records in order of priority under section 53. It further observed that charge in terms of section 100 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 cannot be enforced against any person in whose name the property has been transferred for consideration without notice.</li>

<h1>Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd Vs Anuj Jain RP of Ballarpur&nbsp;Industries Ltd &amp; Ors [CA(AT)(Ins-) No 5178518012073]</h1>
<ul>
<li>Yes Bank Ltd granted two term loans to BILT Graphic Paper Products Ltd (BGPPL) Principal Borrower, an affiliate company of Ballarpur Industries Limited (CD): Security for the loans was created in favour of the Security Trustee: To secure the loan facility availed by BGPPL, the CD also executed a guarantee to secure Term Loan-II of Yes Bank Ltd. Yes Bank Ltd assigned its debt and underlying securities in respect of the BGPPL in favour of Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd (ARC). BGPPL had not committed any default in repayment of its loan to its creditor/assignee. Later, the CIRP was initiated by the Finquest Financial Solutions Pvt Ltd (FC) in respect of the CD. During the CIRP, the ARC filed its claim as secured creditor with respect to the Term Loan II. RP rejected the claim of the ARC, stating that the default was not committed by the principal borrower and therefore; the ARC is placed in the category of other creditors. ARC has not challenged the rejection of claim and the notional value of ₹1/- allotted to its claim.</li>
<li>While the IA filed by RP before AA for the approval of resolution plan; the ARC filed an IA praying for rejection of the resolution plan. However; the AA rejected the IA filed by the ARC and approved the resolution plan. Aggrieved by the aforesaid two orders, these appeals have been filed.</li>
<li>In this appeal filed by ARC, the Appellate Tribunal, relying upon SC judgement in Anuj Jain Vs Jaypee Infratech Ltd, reiterated the distinction provided in Code with regard to secured creditor who has only security interest. It observed that after moratorium is declared, there is prohibition on enforcement of any security interest by one or other creditor including secured FC or third party secured creditor for the purpose and object. FC who is part of the CoC is prohibited from enforcing any security interest. A third-party secured interest like that of the Appellant is equally bound by the provision of section 14(1)(c) and cannot claim any enforcement of security interest in the CIRP.</li>
<li>In Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Ltd. Vs Satish Kumar Gupta &amp; Ors, SC while considering the provisions of section 30, 50, 52 and 53 of the Code has held that provision of sections 52 and 53 are not applicable in the insolvency resolution process as they are applicable only during liquidation. Reference of section 53 under section 30(2) is for the purpose of computing the payment to OCs and dissenting FCs to which they may be entitled under section 53.</li>
<li>Regulation 37 of CIRP Regulations thus, is provision of the Code which is consistent to the Code and to carry out the provisions of the Code. Regulation 37(6) indicates that resolution plan shall provide for sale of all or part of the assets whether subject to any security interest or not. The use of expression "subject to any security interest or not" makes it clear that the assets of the CD can be dealt with in the resolution plan whether it is subject to any security interest or not. Further, sub-clause (d) permits the resolution plan to contain provision for satisfaction or modification of any security interest. Thus, as per scheme regulation 37, security interest in assets of the CD can be dealt with, modified, satisfied and there is no exclusion to the resolution plan regarding dealing of the security interest.</li>
<li>CIRP and liquidation are two different concepts with two different consequences. While in CIRP the claim of FCs is dealt with; there is no cap to debt which is owed by the CD. Despite FC having a security interest in the assets of the CD, they can be dealt with in the resolution plan in any manner as per the commercial wisdom of the CoC. When the security interest of FC or third-party having security interest in the assets of the CD cannot claim any higher status or status different from the FC.</li>
<li>When any asset including security interest is part of the CIRP, there is no constraint or prohibition in the Code or Regulations to deal with the said asset including a security interest. The claim was filed by the ARC, and it was part of the CIRP hence; its security interest can very well be dealt with in the resolution plan. The scheme delineated by regulation 37 of CIRP Regulations allows this.</li>
<li>The NCLAT concurred with AA's observations that for extinguishment of the security interest of the Appellant; no prior consent of the Appellant was required. It further held that when there is no default by the principal borrower as in the present case and there is no actual loss to the ARC, and more so when the principal borrower is to furnish additional security for better protection, its security interest has been extinguished by the RF; it could demand the security interest.</li>
<li>The Appellant at no point of time challenged the admission of its claim by RP of Jaypee Kensington Boulevard Apartments Welfare Association &amp; Ors; as 'other creditor'. The main distinguishing feature of present case with that of NBCC (India) Ltd &amp; Ors is that in Jaypee Kensington security interest of the lender of that case was not part of the CIRP but in the present case same was part of the CIRP.</li>
</ul>
