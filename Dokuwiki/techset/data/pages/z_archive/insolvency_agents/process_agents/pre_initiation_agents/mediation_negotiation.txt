The sources strongly suggest that while Artificial Intelligence (AI) can provide powerful support for the data-intensive and analytical aspects of **Mediation and Negotiation**—including supporting voluntary settlements—it cannot replace the core human skills required for this phase, particularly the emotional intelligence and strategic thinking necessary for dispute resolution.

### 1. Negotiation Support Agent

This agent would focus on providing real-time data and analytical insights to inform negotiation strategy and support decision-making during settlement discussions.

*   **Data-Driven Insights:** AI can assist professionals in making informed decisions by providing **data-driven insights and recommendations** based on the analysis of complex financial and legal information. This is the fundamental way AI acts as a **tool or an assistant**.
*   **Benchmarking and Scenario Modeling:** AI can model and compare **multiple debt restructuring scenarios**, which is essential for determining a viable settlement range. It can also perform **sensitivity analysis** on proposed offers. For instance, it can benchmark a proposed settlement against industry data or liquidation value to ensure the professional enters negotiations with the strongest possible position.
*   **Customer Interaction (Pre-Negotiation):** AI chatbots and virtual assistants can **facilitate communication with stakeholders** by providing timely information and answering common queries. This can streamline the pre-negotiation exchange of information, ensuring all parties are operating from a common, verified data set.

### 2. Offer Matching Agent

This agent would likely automate the quantitative assessment of incoming settlement offers against predefined acceptance criteria or models.

*   **Quantitative Assessment:** The sources highlight that the **quantitative part is pretty straightforward**—how much is upfront, how much is deferred, how much equity is offered, etc.—and AI can run the necessary financial models to evaluate these terms.
*   **Optimization:** The concept of AI optimizing complex financial outputs is confirmed by its role in **automating the distribution waterfall** and calculating creditor payouts, where it finds the most efficient application of rules to numbers. An Offer Matching Agent could similarly optimize a settlement offer based on the known assets and repayment capacity.
*   **Personalized Solutions:** AI can help tailor **personalised insolvency solutions** based on specific financial and legal factors to create **more effective and customised plans**, directly supporting the creation of viable settlement proposals that consider personal income and assets.

### 3. Dispute Resolution Agent

This agent relates to the overall goal of achieving resolution, but the sources emphasize that the **actual execution of dispute resolution requires human elements** that AI cannot replicate.

*   **AI's Limitation (Human Judgment):** AI is proficient at analyzing data but **may struggle with the nuanced decision-making and contextual understanding** that human practitioners bring. Insolvency, by its nature, involves disputes.
*   **Emotional Intelligence and Trust:** Insolvency practitioners frequently deal with distressed clients and creditors, requiring the ability to **empathise and navigate emotionally charged situations**. **AI lacks this emotional intelligence**. Furthermore, **negotiation and communication skills are paramount**, and AI lacks the ability to build rapport.
*   **Strategic Thinking:** The professional must still execute the core strategic thinking. As one practitioner noted, **AI can’t do the thinking that fits behind complex schemes of arrangements or deal with the human dynamics** that lie behind commercial decisions and outcomes.
*   **Legal Research Support:** While AI cannot resolve the dispute itself, it can power legal research tools to find **relevant, up-to-date case law** and **extract legal points, quotes, and precedents to support an argument**. This provides the professional with the necessary legal ammunition for negotiation.

In conclusion, for voluntary settlements, AI excels as a **Negotiation Support Agent** and **Offer Matching Agent**, providing rapid, accurate, and benchmarked financial modeling. However, the success of the **Dispute Resolution Agent** ultimately rests on the human professional's **commercial wisdom** and ability to handle the "interpersonal dynamics" and "human dynamics" inherent in reaching a mediated solution.
