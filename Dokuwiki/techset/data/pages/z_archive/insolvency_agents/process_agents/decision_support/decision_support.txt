The sources strongly confirm that Artificial Intelligence (AI) is ideally suited for the **Decision Support** function, specifically to evaluate complex outcomes such as restructuring, merger, or sale, particularly during the critical stages of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP). AI's role is to act as a sophisticated analyst, providing data-driven insights that inform and strengthen the human judgment of the Committee of Creditors (CoC) and the Resolution Professional (RP).

## AI Agents in Decision Support and Evaluation

### 1. Scenario Simulator Agent (Evaluate Outcomes)

This agent leverages AI's computational power to model various possible futures for the corporate debtor, comparing outcomes of different strategic paths (restructuring vs. sale/liquidation).

*   **Financial Modeling and Cash Flow Analysis:** AI can **run the financial models** necessary to evaluate a plan. For instance, if a restructuring plan proposes debt repayment over three years, the simulator can look at the projected **cash flows** during that period to assess if repayment using internal accruals is feasible.
*   **Success Rate Prediction:** AI can model and compare **multiple debt restructuring scenarios**. Having a trustworthy AI model assess the success rates under various scenarios can make the decision faster and **"remove the risk of individual decision maker"** bias.
*   **Recovery Modeling:** The agent can conduct **recovery modeling** to forecast payouts under different options. For example, it can determine if accepting a resolution plan offering a recovery of 90 is better than proceeding to liquidation, where the revised market valuation might be **"much much lower"** than the initially estimated liquidation value of 100.

### 2. Restructuring Option Analyzer Agent (Evaluate Restructuring, Merger, or Sale)

This agent provides deep analysis of the viability and risks associated with each option, ensuring the decision is based on market realities and robust data.

*   **Benchmarking Plan Assumptions:** The agent can **benchmark plan assumptions against industry data**. If a plan proposes high market growth (e.g., 13%), the AI can flag this if the sector's Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is significantly lower (e.g., 8%). This is essential for understanding whether a business is viable or not.
*   **Highlighting Risks:** The agent can **highlight risks** inherent in any proposed option, such as an assumption of **unrealistically high market growth**.
*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** It can run **sensitivity analysis** on competing structures (e.g., bidder one's structure A versus bidder two's structure B) to evaluate the robustness of each option under varying financial conditions.
*   **Strategic Insights:** AI is described as providing **data-driven insights and recommendations** based on the analysis of complex financial and legal information, which enhances decision-making. It helps in **designing the optimal restructuring solution**.

## Limitations and The Role of Human Wisdom

While AI is transformative for decision support, the sources are clear that it assists, but does not replace, the final strategic decision-making:

*   **Human Judgment is Essential:** AI **cannot replace the commercial wisdom of the creditors**. Decision-making in insolvency involves **complex legal, financial, and interpersonal dynamics** that require human judgment.
*   **Strategic Thinking:** AI cannot do the complex strategic thinking involved in designing **complex schemes of arrangements** or negotiating the **human dynamics** that drive commercial decisions.
*   **Accountability:** Any output provided by these agents must adhere to the rule of **explainability and accountability**. The tool must offer **audit trails** showing the chain of reasoning used for scoring or recommending an outcome, ensuring credibility and judicial acceptance. The professional remains accountable for the AI's output.
