====== IBC Status: Bharat Papers Limited (Sample) ======

===== 1.0 Case Profile and Introduction =====

This document presents a formal case study of the insolvency proceedings of Bharat Papers Limited. An analysis of this case provides significant insights for legal and financial professionals, illuminating the procedural pathway from the initiation of a Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) to its eventual culmination in liquidation under the Indian insolvency framework. By examining the key stages, appointed professionals, and documented outcomes, this study serves as a practical illustration of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code in action.

The corporate debtor at the center of these proceedings is Bharat Papers Limited, a company operating in the paper manufacturing sector. The key details of the entity and its insolvency filing are summarized below.

* Corporate Debtor Name: BHARAT PAPERS LIMITED
* Corporate Identification Number (CIN): U21093PB2006PLC030592
* Sector of Business: MANUFACTURE OF PAPER AND PAPER PRODUCTS
* Initiating Applicant: Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd.
* Insolvency Commencement Date (ICD): 01-10-2020

The insolvency process was formally set in motion on this commencement date, triggering the appointment of an insolvency professional and the public announcement of the proceedings.

===== 2.0 Initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) =====

The initiation of the CIRP represents the first formal step in the insolvency process, where an external professional assumes control of the corporate debtor. This stage is critical for all stakeholders, as it halts existing legal proceedings and initiates a time-bound effort to find a viable resolution, preserving the company as a going concern if possible. The process begins with a public announcement to inform all creditors and stakeholders of the commencement of insolvency.

The first public announcement regarding the CIRP for Bharat Papers Limited was made in October 2020. The essential details of this announcement are as follows:

* Announcement Type: Public Announcement of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process
* Date of Announcement: 21-10-2020
* Last Date for Submission: 03-11-2020
* Appointed Insolvency Professional (IP): Mr. Navneet Gupta
* Role: Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)

According to the official record of associated professionals, Mr. Navneet Gupta was appointed to serve as both the Interim Resolution Professional (IRP) and the Resolution Professional (RP), tasked with managing the company's affairs and overseeing the resolution process. This appointment marked the beginning of the active phase aimed at formulating a resolution plan.

===== 3.0 The Resolution and Claims Management Phase =====

The core objective of the resolution phase is to determine the complete financial position of the corporate debtor and solicit a viable resolution plan from interested parties. This stage involves the crucial tasks of collating and verifying claims from all creditors and formally inviting expressions of interest from potential resolution applicants. The success of this phase is paramount to avoiding the eventual liquidation of the company.

A formal timeline for the submission of resolution plans was established in early 2021 under the authority of Resolution Professional Mr. Navneet Gupta:

* Date of Invitation of Resolution Plans: 03rd January, 2021
* Last Date for Receipt of Expression of Interest: 02nd February, 2021
* Last Date for Submission of Resolution Plans: 04th March, 2021

Concurrently, the management and verification of creditor claims were documented through a series of updates, reflecting the ongoing assessment of the company's liabilities.

Version	Date	Name of IRP / RP / Liquidator
Version 1	09-10-2020	Mr. Sumat Kumar Gupta
Version 2	07-07-2021	Mr. Sumat Kumar Gupta
Version 3	28-07-2021	Mr. Sumat Kumar Gupta
Version 4	16-09-2025	Mr. Vijay Kishore Saxena

A critical observation from this phase is the bifurcation of duties. While RP Mr. Navneet Gupta managed the solicitation of resolution plans in early 2021, the concurrent claims management, a foundational element of the CIRP, was being handled by another RP, Mr. Sumat Kumar Gupta, since October 2020. This division of responsibility is a key procedural detail in the administration of this case. The chronology of claims management also reveals a pivotal transition in professional oversight, with the final documented version being managed by Mr. Vijay Kishore Saxena. This change in stewardship is a clear procedural marker indicating that the case was progressing from resolution attempts toward liquidation.

===== 4.0 Transition to Liquidation Proceedings =====

The transition from a CIRP to liquidation proceedings is a significant development in any insolvency case. It signifies that a viable resolution plan to revive the corporate debtor was not approved within the legally mandated timeframe. Consequently, the process shifts from rescue and rehabilitation to the dissolution of the company's assets to repay creditors in accordance with the established order of priority.

For Bharat Papers Limited, the final process initiated was "LIQUIDATOR." This was formalized through a Public Announcement of Liquidation Process, signaling the end of resolution efforts. The details of this announcement are provided below:

* Announcement Type: Public Announcement of Liquidation Process
* Date of Announcement: 23-07-2025
* Last Date of Submission: 17-08-2025
* Applicant: Jammu and Kashmir Bank Limited
* Appointed Liquidator: Mr. Vijay Kishore Saxena
* Liquidator Address: 3rd Floor, 100, Kailash Hills, East of Kailash, New Delhi-110065 D-69, LGF, East of Kailash New Delhi-110065
* Process Email ID: irpbharatpapers@gmail.com

Notably, the "Auction Notice" section of the provided public record shows "No Result Found." This indicates that, within the scope of the available data, no specific notices for the auction of the company's assets had been published. The appointment of the liquidator officially commenced the final phase of asset disposal.

===== 6.0 Conclusion: Case Trajectory Analysis =====

This case study has chronicled the insolvency proceedings of Bharat Papers Limited from initiation to its final stage. This concluding section synthesizes the key events to provide a concise analysis of the documented progression, offering a clear view of the case's trajectory through the Indian insolvency system.

The case commenced with the initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process on October 1, 2020, following an application by Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd. An attempt to find a viable path to revival was made in early 2021, with a deadline for the submission of resolution plans set for March 4, 2021. However, the subsequent public announcement of liquidation proceedings in July 2025, under the oversight of a newly appointed liquidator, confirms that this effort was unsuccessful.

The case of Bharat Papers Limited serves as a textbook example of the IBC's structured yet unforgiving timeline. The inability to secure a resolution plan—despite the involvement of multiple resolution professionals and a formal invitation process—triggered the code's default mechanism, prioritizing creditor recovery through asset liquidation over a failed corporate revival. The transition from a resolution process to a liquidation process demonstrates that a viable resolution was not achieved within the CIRP framework, necessitating the commencement of asset liquidation as the terminal stage of the insolvency proceedings.
